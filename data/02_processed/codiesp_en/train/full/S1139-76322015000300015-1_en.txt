Previously healthy child. The only history is that he underwent surgery for left cryptorchidism; He is vaccinated correctly for his age. At the age of five, he presented bilateral parotid inflammation for the first time (with erythema, swelling and pain), accompanied by fever, which was managed on an outpatient basis with amoxicillin-clavulanic acid and oral anti-inflammatories. He had a second episode, also managed on an outpatient basis.
The third episode required hospital admission due to poor clinical progress and poor general condition after starting treatment with oral amoxicillin-clavulanic acid. The physical examination revealed a bilateral laterocervical tumor (initially right) that obliterated the mandibular angle, without signs of fluctuation or secretion or inflammatory signs of Stenon's duct. A blood test was performed, which showed a significant leukocytosis (24,100/mm3) with neutrophilia (78.7%), C-reactive protein (CRP) of 4.11 mg/dl and elevation of α-amylase (up to 1451 U /l). During admission, an ultrasound was performed that showed an increase in the size of the parotids, with increased density, heterogeneous and hypoechoic foci. The patient received treatment with amoxicillin-clavulanic acid for two days intravenously, completing eight more days orally, analgesia and anti-inflammatories, with good subsequent progress.
The Pediatric Infectious Diseases consultation has followed up the patient, from where the study has been completed. An immunity study was performed, which was normal, rheumatoid tests were negative, and a serological study was negative, except for the mumps vaccine serology, which was positive.
Currently he has not presented new episodes for more than a year.
