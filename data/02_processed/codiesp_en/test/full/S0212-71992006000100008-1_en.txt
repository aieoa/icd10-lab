This is a 40-year-old male of Bulgarian nationality who has a personal history of gastroesophageal reflux and incompetent cardia, who has not previously undergone surgery or transfusion, by profession employed in metal carpentry, a smoker of 15-20 cigarettes/day and a former moderate drinker until recently. 4 years. Among his family history are his mother, father and a brother with nephrolithiasis. He had been seen 21 days before admission for pain in the right flank with radiation to the ipsilateral testicle, which was classified as probable renal colic and treated with analgesia, with a favorable outcome; Later he began again with similar symptoms accompanied by a feeling of nausea without thermometered fever or voiding syndrome; He did not report loss of appetite or weight. He went again to the emergency room where an abdominal ultrasound was performed, which described the presence of grade II/IV hydronephrosis and a mass in the right reteroperitoneum of about 6-7 cm, for which he was admitted for study. The patient reports pain in the right flank/renal fossa that radiates to the ipsilateral testis without nausea or vomiting or thermomeasured fever. The examination only revealed positive right renal succussion. ECG at sinus rhythm at 76 bpm; chest x-ray without significant pathological alterations. The blood analysis revealed fibrinogen 478 mg/dL (150-450), ESR21 mm/hour (2-15), urea 52 mg/dL (10-50), creatinine 1.4 mg/dL (0.6- 1.2), LDH 446 U/L (0-288), human chorionic gonadotropin 7 IU/L (0-5) and alpha-fetoprotein 2.2 kU/L (0.5-7). An abdominopelvic CT was performed where a single "soft tissue" mass of rounded morphology (7 x 5.5 cm) with a uniform density and a focal area was described in the middle retroperitoneum (between the aorta and the inferior vena cava). less attenuation that "encompasses" the inferior vena cava and the right ureter with grade 2 hydronephrosis; The lesion is well delimited and no lymphadenopathy or accompanying satellite lesions are seen. An urgent consultation was made to Urology who performed a right nephrostomy. A trucut biopsy of the retroperitoneal mass was performed guided by ultrasound and the specimen was sent to pathological anatomy, which reported it as a neoformation made up of large cells that are arranged in nests, with little cohesiveness and a cytoplasm with imprecise limits, generally finely vacuolated. The nuclei are rounded or cleft, and have a large nucleolus and the cells are arranged forming nuclei and are surrounded by a large number of small lymphoid cells, corresponding to mature lymphocytes, the tumor cells being positive for C-Kit, being compatible with classic seminoma type germ cell tumor. Given this, an ultrasound of the testes was performed where a nodular-ovoid, isodense lesion with a peripheral hypodense halo, measuring 16 mm, was observed in the right testis, located in the upper half and anterior subcapsular, in addition to another small nearby hyperechoic nodule of approximately 5 mm compatible with right testicular tumor. He was subsequently referred to the Urology Service, after consultation with Oncology, to perform an orchiectomy and subsequently undergo cycles of chemotherapy in the Oncology Service.
