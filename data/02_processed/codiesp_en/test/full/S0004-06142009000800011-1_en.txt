A 56-year-old woman was initially evaluated for intestinal disorders. Physical examination and initial laboratory routine were normal. An abdominal ultrasound showed a solid mass 12 cm behind the left kidney that presented anomalous vascularization. The computed tomography showed a well-circumscribed mass behind the left kidney that enhanced after the administration of intravenous contrast and presented a clear fatty plane between the left kidney and the mass. Fine needle puncture was performed to obtain histology. The pathological report was of a benign mesenchymal tumor or one with low malignant potential. Laparoscopic exploration was decided.

Surgical technique: Under general anesthesia the patient was positioned in the right lateral decubitus after the placement of an orogastric and bladder tube. A single dose of 2gr cephalothin was administered intravenously. Pneumoperitoneum was performed with a Veress needle at 1 2 mmHg. Two trocars of 1,2 mm and 5 mm respectively were placed forming a rhombus as seen in Figure 3. The colon and spleen were widely mobilized towards the midline. After opening Gerota's fascia, the mass was clearly visualized on the outer edge of the kidney. By preserving the tumor boundaries, it was freed from its contact with the renal capsule. A 2cm area was observed firmly adhered to the renal capsule requiring resection of the same and subsequent fulguration of the bloody bed with argon laser. The surgical specimen was removed in a plastic bag by obliquely extending the trocar incision on the left flank. A Blake-type drain was left through the 5 mm trocar hole. The operative time was 135 minutes with an estimated bleeding of 200 ml. The patient progressed favorably and she was discharged on the third postoperative day. The final pathological analysis revealed a leiomyosarcoma of very low grade of malignancy. After a 6-month follow-up, the patient has completely recovered with no evidence of her underlying disease.
