A patient comes to our consultations with a large left cervical tumor. His background includes surgery for a branchial cyst in this same location 18 years ago.
It explains slow and painless mass growth of approximately 10 years of evolution.
The limits of the tumor include the left greater body of the hyoid at the anterior level, the mastoid region at the upper level, the clavicle at the bottom, and the external occipital protuberance as the posterior limit.

The consistency of the dough is soft and does not adhere to deep planes.
No lymphadenopathy or other masses were palpated and the intraoral examination was normal.
The rest of the physical examination was completely unremarkable.
It was decided to perform cervico-facial magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), which revealed a homogeneous, well-defined mass that did not invade neighboring structures and with a fatty density.

A sample was taken by fine needle aspiration (FNAB) which confirmed the initial suspicions of lipoma.
It was decided to perform surgical excision of the tumor. An approach was performed at the level of the posterior hairline, resecting a 5*10cm skin zone to improve direct closure.

During the intervention, a piece weighing 459.5g and measuring 25*16*4.8cm was obtained.
After the first postoperative day, the patient shows a rapidly growing hematoma at the level of the posterior suture that requires emergency evacuation and hemostasis of a small vessel at the level of the left trapezius muscle.
After the seventh postoperative day, the patient was discharged.
The anatomopathological studies confirmed the diagnosis of lipoma without atypia.
It is currently progressing satisfactorily and is being monitored in our external consultations.
