33-year-old patient who on 08-20-05 was taken to the emergency room due to a bleeding cut on the neck made when he fell on a store window. In the emergency room, an incised trauma was seen in the right anterolateral region of the neck with a clean dissection that concerned only the superficial tissues and without apparent damage to the rest of the structures, so it was sutured in planes. On 09-04-06 he went to the emergency room of our hospital to check the wound, reporting headache and blurred vision with the right eye (RE). In the ophthalmological examination, the uncorrected visual acuity with the RE was 1 and with the left eye (LE) was 1. The intraocular pressure was 12 mm Hg in the RE and 13 mm Hg in the LE. Extrinsic ocular motility was normal except for a 3 mm right eyelid ptosis with good levator function. The RE pupil measured 2.0 mm and the LE pupil measured 4.0 mm. External examination and palpation of the neck, except for findings of trauma, were normal. A cervical CT scan was performed, revealing a large hematoma with internal growth that was significantly compressing the trachea, so it was decided to explore and intrasurgical evacuation where a break in continuity was seen in the external jugular vein that was leaking into deep planes. Five months later Horner's syndrome had disappeared.
