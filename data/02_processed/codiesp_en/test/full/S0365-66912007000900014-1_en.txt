34-year-old male patient diagnosed with chickenpox three weeks earlier and resolved without complications. He went to the emergency room due to decreased visual acuity in his left eye.
In the ophthalmological examination, he had a corrected visual acuity of 1 in the right eye (RE) and 0.6 in the left eye (LE). The slit lamp study demonstrates in the LE a cellular tyndall of 4+, lower keratic precipitates (3+) and no fluorescein staining of the cornea, with the RE being normal. The intraocular pressure was 16mmHg in both eyes.
In the initial funduscopic examination of the LE, mild vitritis (1+) was observed without foci of retinitis.
Topical treatment with corticosteroids and mydriatics is instituted. After 2 days, a slight decrease in cellular tyndall (3+) was observed in the anterior chamber, but in the fundus a peripheral focus of necrotizing retinitis appeared in the temporal area associated with retinal vasculitis.
The patient is admitted and treatment is started with intravenous acyclovir (10 mg/kg/8 hours), antiplatelet therapy (acetylsalicylic acid 150 mg/24 hours) and topical treatment is maintained. Prophylactic photocoagulation with argon laser is also performed surrounding the area of ​​retinal necrosis.

After 48 hours and after verification of a good response with a decrease in the focus of retinitis, systemic corticosteroids are introduced at a dose of 1 mg/kg/d, presenting a rapid improvement in visual acuity, with a decrease in vitritis and a smaller size of the eye. focus of retinitis. After 2 weeks, the antiviral treatment (famciclovir 500 mg/12 hours) is switched to oral administration and a gradual decrease in corticosteroids is started at a rate of 10 mg each week. Antiviral treatment is continued at maintenance doses for 6 weeks, with periodic blood count and biochemistry laboratory tests performed to detect kidney involvement.
Six months after the onset of symptoms, the patient remains asymptomatic, with visual acuity of unity in LE, healing of retinal lesions and complete absence of ocular inflammatory activity in both eyes.
During admission, recent infection with varicella zoster virus was confirmed through seroconversion.
