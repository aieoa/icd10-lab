We present the case of a 64-year-old man with no relevant history, who consulted due to weight loss of 17 kg, asthenia, anorexia and anemia. A colonoscopy was performed which showed non-specific inflammatory changes of the colonic mucosa and a gastroscopy which showed gastric neoplasia and antral gastropathy, with biopsies taken. Histology of the gastric biopsy confirmed a neoplastic proliferation with a solid pattern suggestive of GIST, and immunohistochemistry showed positivity for CD117. AA amyloid deposits were observed in gastric mucosa, tumor, and colonic mucosa biopsies.
Subsequently, he began to have edema in his lower limbs and diarrhea. The analysis showed: hemoglobin 8.7 g/dl, hematocrit 28%, mean corpuscular volume 75, serum creatinine 1.3 mg/dl; total proteins 5.9 g/dl; albumin 1.36 g/dl; cholesterol 96 mg/dl; transferrin saturation index 13%. Proteinuria of 5.1 g/day and sediment with 4-6 red blood cells/field. Given the patient's poor condition, renal biopsy was not performed and it was assumed that the amyloidosis previously observed in gastrointestinal biopsies was responsible for the nephrotic syndrome and diarrhea. Cardiac amyloidosis was ruled out by echocardiogram.
Treatment was started with a selective cKit tyrosine kinase inhibitor, Imatinib (Glivec®), 100 mg daily. 15 months after diagnosis, surgical removal was decided after a decrease in the tumor mass was observed in the positron emission tomography/computed tomography study. The pathological anatomy confirmed the involvement of the removed stomach and splenic flexure of the colon. In this sense, it was classified as a high-risk GIST (> 5 cm in size, > 5 mitoses per high-power field)2.
Kidney function continued to deteriorate, so he finally entered a periodic hemodialysis program, one year and seven months after the discovery of the GIST, with the diagnosis of chronic renal failure secondary to AA amyloidosis.
