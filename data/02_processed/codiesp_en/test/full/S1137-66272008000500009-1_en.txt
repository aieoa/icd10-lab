A case is presented of a 26-year-old patient who came to the clinic due to pain in the right wrist of 7 years' duration with no history of trauma. The pain at rest was mild, but increased with manual activity and especially with forced extension of the wrist. She did not improve with symptomatic medical treatment.
The dorsal radiocarpal joint interline and the proximal pole of the scaphoid were tender on palpation. No palpable tumor was seen. Wrist mobility was slightly limited in the last degrees of flexion and extension.
The radiological image showed a well-defined, oval, radiolucent lesion located in the mid-dorsal third of the scaphoid. The CT scan showed the dorsal eccentric location of the lesion and stippling compatible with calcifications. The MRI showed signs of marked bone edema in the scaphoid, except in its distal third and adjacent synovitis. With the suspected diagnosis of a benign lesion (enchondroma, intraosseous ganglion), surgical treatment of the lesion was decided. This was performed through a dorsal approach to the scaphoid. After opening the joint capsule, the dorsal surface of the distal epiphysis of the radius and scaphoid was exposed, and in the middle third of the latter a tumor with a pearly appearance and a wine-red color was seen that occupied the dorsal crest of the navicular bone. An exhaustive curettage of the lesion was performed and the defect was filled with cancellous bone graft from the iliac crest.

The material submitted was formed by islets of cartilaginous matrix surrounded by abundant cellular tissue made up of pseudo-chondroblastic cells. These cells were round or ovoid with hyperchromatic nuclei, clear or moderately eosinophilic cytoplasm, and a clear plasma membrane. Cellular pleomorphism was minimal and the mitotic index was very low. Some cells contained iron. Among the pseudochondroblastic cells were abundant osteoclastic-type giant cells. In some areas there were areas of irregular calcification. Some areas of cystic transformation that resembled aneurysmal cysts were also observed. The pathological diagnosis was carpal scaphoid chondroblastoma.

After the surgical treatment, the wrist was immobilized with an antebrachial splint for 3 weeks and the patient was subsequently referred to the Rehabilitation Service. In the review carried out after 3 months, complete recovery of wrist mobility and radiological image of bone consolidation of the iliac crest autograft were observed.
Two years after surgical treatment there is no evidence of recurrence and a complete disappearance of pain persists with wrist mobility similar to that of the contralateral wrist, as well as a completely normalized radiological image of the scaphoid.
