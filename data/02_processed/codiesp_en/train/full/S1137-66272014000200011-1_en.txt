A 77-year-old woman with no relevant history consulted for a scalp lesion that had been going on for more than two years, with progressive growth in the last year and that was bothering her with friction. It was a 6 mm hard erythematous-violet papular lesion in the parieto-occipital region. This lesion was removed by slicing and silver nitrate, it bled a lot and it was not possible to obtain enough sample for anatomopathological (AP) study. After 6 months, the lesion with similar characteristics grew again in the same place; it was removed using elliptical excision and an electric scalpel. The AP analysis reported the presence of differentiated thyroid tissue in the skin. It was a tumor located in the middle and deep dermis consisting of follicular structures of variable sizes that contained colloid material with peripheral vascularization and covered by a row of cubic cells with a low number of mitosis. The immunohistochemical examination detected intense cytoplasmic and intracolloidal expression of thyroglobulin, keratin 7 and 19. With these findings, the patient was referred to us for evaluation of metastasis from previously unknown thyroid cancer.

In the anamnesis, the patient was asymptomatic and had no relevant medical history. On physical examination, a grade Ib goiter was palpable with a 2-3 cm nodule in LTD that ascended well with swallowing without palpable lymphadenopathy, the rest being normal. Complementary tests: in analysis TSH 0.55 mU/L (0.35-5.5), T4l 1.23 ng/dL (0.8-1.76), T3l 3.19 ng/mL (2.3 -4.2), undetectable anti-TPO and anti-TG antibodies and negative TSI that have remained the same throughout follow-up. The thyroid ultrasound showed multinodular goiter, with a dominant nodule measuring 25 FNAC and that she was informed of a follicular tumor. One month later, a thyroidectomy plus lymphadenectomy of the central compartment was performed. The intraoperative biopsy revealed papillary carcinoma. The AP analysis of the surgical specimen was reported to have mixed papillary and follicular carcinoma that was well differentiated (90%) and solid with 10% poorly differentiated, partially encapsulated with extensive infiltration of the capsule and its vessels. The size of the encapsulated portion was 15 mm and the complete tumor was 30 mm. No extrathyroidal extension was noted and the surgical margins were free, the closest being the posterior one at one mm. In the emptying of the central compartment, 4 lymph nodes were obtained that were free of tumor.

An extension study was performed before treatment with radioiodine using a metabolic study with 8.26 mCi of 18FDG, brain and pelvic MRI, and bone scan, which was negative. Seventy days after the intervention, 104.7 mCi of 131I was administered after rhTSH. Baseline serum TG was 351 µg/L (1.6-60). Stimulated TG 1788.7 µg/L (47-10000) with basal TSH of 117 mU/L (0.37-4.7). In the post-I131 RCT, post-thyroidectomy remains located in the left posterior chamber were observed, along with diffuse miliary involvement in both lung parenchymas compatible with metastasis. One month after radioiodine ablation and under treatment with 100 µg of levothyroxine, he presented TG 37 µg/L (1.6-60), TSH 1.96 mU/L and FT4 1.43 ng/dL, at which time he was increased the dose of levothyroxine to 125 µg. In the evaluation 6 months after radioiodine ablation, the TG was 2.78 with TSH 0.03 and FT4l 1.96, so another dose of radioiodine of 125 mCi was given; and 2 months later, TG <0.20 µg/L was detected with TSH 0.04 mU/L and T4 1.6 ng/dL. The patient will be re-evaluated in 4 months by quantification of stimulated TG after rhTSH and subsequent RCT to confirm cure.
