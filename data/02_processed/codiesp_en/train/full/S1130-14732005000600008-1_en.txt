45-year-old patient evaluated in September 2000 for left hemiparesis. His history included smoking, high blood pressure, hypercholesterolemia, peritonitis at the age of 18, and myocardial infarction in 1999. After CT and MRI, a cerebral angiography diagnosed occlusion of the right internal carotid artery and 60% stenosis. associated with pseudoaneurysm in the cervical tract on the left, with signs of dissection on MRI.

It was found that there was a contralateral substitution. Using a right common femoral approach, a monorail metal stent (Carotid wallstent, Boston Scientific Co.) was implanted in the area of ​​the stenosis that also extended over the pseudoaneurysmal area of ​​the cervical tract of the left internal carotid artery.

A microcatheter was introduced through the mesh of the endoprosthesis with which GDC coils (Boston Scientific Co.) were released without risk of migration.
A post-procedure angiographic control showed the recovery of the lumen in the stenotic area and the absence of filling of the pseudoaneurysm. During the procedure, 5000 IU of sodium heparin was administered. Sequential anticoagulant treatment was performed in the acute phase with sodium heparin in continuous perfusion, followed by dicoumarins and, subsequently, antiplatelet agents.

After three years of evolution, angiographic and ultrasound controls demonstrated patency of the treated artery without recurrence of the pseudoaneurysm; The patient has not presented neurological symptoms in the left carotid territory.
