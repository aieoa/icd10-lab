A 13-year-old boy, with a personal history of Asperger syndrome, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder and hypothyroidism, being treated with methylphenidate, risperidone and levothyroxine. He was sent to the Dermatology consultation due to a progressively growing lesion on his right lower limb that had been going on for a year. She was asymptomatic, without presenting any symptoms such as pain or pruritus. He had not received any treatment, nor did he report any triggering factor. During the examination, a plaque was observed, indurated on palpation, with a central dyschromic area and a violet halo, with a linear distribution in the middle-distal third of the external area of ​​the right thigh. He had no other skin lesions or accompanying systemic symptoms.

An analysis was requested that included a complete blood count, biochemistry, antinuclear antibodies (ANA) and serologies, a urine test, as well as a biopsy of the lesion. The results were normal, with ANA, anti-Sm, anti-RNP, anti-SSA, anti-SSB, anti-Jo1 and anti-Scl70 negative. Serology for Borrelia burgdorferi was negative and urinalysis showed no abnormalities. Histopathology was compatible with the diagnosis of morphea.
Given linear morphea in childhood without joint involvement or aesthetic deformity, it was decided to start topical corticosteroid treatment with fluticasone propionate, one application per day for 2 months, followed by topical calcipotriol, also one daily application. After almost a year of follow-up, the lesions have stabilized.
