A 39-year-old woman with a personal history of pulmonary tuberculosis, repeated renal colic, and stress urinary incontinence.
She goes to the emergency room due to low back pain and urinary frequency. The abdominal ultrasound performed revealed bilateral ureterohydronephrosis with preserved left renal parenchyma and right kidney with a thin and unstructured cortex, as well as renal function with a serum creatinine of 1.98 mg/dl. For all these reasons, a urinary diversion via percutaneous nephrostomy of the left kidney was decided.

Genitourinary tuberculosis was diagnosed by a positive Lowestein culture in the urine evacuated through the nephrostomy and medical treatment was instituted, with Rifampicin, Isoniazid and Pyrazinamide for 2 months and Isoniazid and Rifampin for 4 more months. During follow-up in outpatient clinics, a renal scintigraphy was performed in which a right kidney without function and the left kidney with preserved morphology and discrete ectasia of the upper calyceal system were observed. An abdominal ultrasound is also obtained that corroborates the findings of the renal scan. The patient abandoned antibiotic treatment and follow-up visits after 6 months, due to social problems, despite persistence of disease in cultures.
One year after diagnosis, medical treatment was restarted and continued for one year with adequate clinical follow-up. An intravenous urography was performed, with a diagnosis of right mastic kidney and stenosis of the last cm of the left ureter. A right nephrectomy was performed and a left uretero-vesical reimplantation was attempted, during which disinsertion of the left ureter occurred 1 cm below the pyeloureteral junction, so a permanent left urinary diversion (racket nephrostomy) was decided.

For three years she maintained good kidney function and periodic changes of racket nephrostomy with the obvious limitation and impact on her quality of life. After this time and given the good physical condition and age of the patient, reconstruction of the excretory tract with ileum was proposed. The preoperative evaluation confirmed complete bladder retraction, so simultaneous replacement of this organ was considered.

The surgical technique carried out consisted of releasing and defunctionalizing 60 cm of ileum with opening of the left mesocolon to allow the anastomosis of the proximal end of the ileum to the pelvis of the left kidney, with suture to loose stitches and a double J tutoring catheter. Subsequently, a cystectomy was performed. and hysterectomy of myomatous uterus due to making the anastomosis between neobladder and urethra difficult, with detubulization of 30 cm of the distal ileum in an isoperistaltic position, forming a "U" neobladder with a modified Camey-type fold of the final 20 cm.

The postoperative period was uneventful with good clinical evolution and removal of the ureteral tutor 21 days after surgery. Cystogram checking the tightness of the bladder suture, although the presence of vesico-ureteral reflux is observed, with high pressure. After removal of the nephrostomy and urinary catheter, she was discharged from the hospital with hourly urination and leakage between urinations, requiring compresses and the Credé maneuver to empty the bladder.

Six months later she had a cystography with a larger bladder capacity and little reflux, with nocturnal incontinence persisting.

During the periodic check-ups that are carried out every 6 months, the patient shows us her satisfaction with the results obtained after surgery and her improvement in quality of life, since she does not have urinary diversion and urine leaks are scarce and nocturnal.
Currently, 4 years after the last surgical intervention, he has urinations every 2-3 hours with daytime continence and a nighttime compress, bladder emptying at the expense of the Credé and Valsalva maneuver without subsequent residue, requiring 1 g daily for adequate metabolic control. of oral bicarbonate and a plasma Creatinine of 1.1 mg/dl7.
