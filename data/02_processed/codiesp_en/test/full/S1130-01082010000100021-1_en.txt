We present the case of a 43-year-old man, with a history of diabetes, who was admitted for moderate acalculous acute pancreatitis. Two weeks later, he reported acute pain in the left abdomen and nausea, with no other symptoms. On examination, a large occupation of the left hemiabdomen was palpable without peritoneal irritation. Laboratory tests: elevated triglycerides and lipase, rest biochemistry, blood count and normal coagulation. During the examination, he suffered syncope, lowering his hematocrit from 40 to 21%, requiring a transfusion of 6 packed red blood cells, and he remained stable. An imaging test was performed, which reported a hemorrhagic pancreatic pseudocyst measuring 17 x 27 cm in diameter. The patient is admitted to the ICU where he remains stable. After a week, the patient is scheduled for surgery by performing a cysto-jejunostomy. The postoperative period passed without incident, and he was discharged a week after the intervention.
