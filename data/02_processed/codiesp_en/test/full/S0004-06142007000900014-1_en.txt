A 58-year-old woman is being studied by the Digestive Service for pain in the right hypochondrium of 6 months' duration, accompanied by altered bowel habits. She does not present general syndrome or urological symptoms.
On physical examination, she appears to be in good general condition, normally hydrated and normally colored, and moderately obese. There is no cushingoid habit or virilization stigmata. The blood pressure figures are normal.
Cardiopulmonary auscultation is normal. The abdomen is globular, soft and depressible, making palpation of possible masses or organomegaly difficult, showing slight pain on deep pressure in the right hypochondrium, without presenting Murphy's sign.
Regarding complementary examinations, routine biochemical and hematological analysis, as well as adrenal function tests, are within normal ranges.
The ultrasound demonstrates the presence of a tumor in the right adrenal area of ​​approximately 6x6 cm. No liver metastases are observed.
The CT scan confirms the referred mass, in intimate contact with the wall of the inferior vena cava, which it apparently displaces, without being able to rule out its infiltration. There is no evidence of retroperitoneal lymph node involvement or metastasis at another level. The presence of tumor thrombosis is also not demonstrated.
Given these findings, it was decided to perform MRI for a correct definition of the involvement of neighboring tissues, reporting identical CT findings and without being able to definitively predict parietal infiltration.

With the diagnosis of adrenal mass, intervention was decided, which was carried out through a transperitoneal right subcostal incision. The right adrenal region was approached, revealing a voluminous tumor at that level. The dissection of the same begins, and no separation plane is found in the area of ​​contact with the lateral and posterior wall of the vena cava, so it is decided to excise it after proximal and distal venous control of the mass and with suture with polypropylene from the wall of the inferior vena cava.
The patient followed a favorable postoperative course, without developing complications. He was discharged on the seventh day post-intervention.
The macroscopic histological study shows a 6 x 8 cm piece of elastic consistency and easy to cut, with a fleshy appearance and grayish-brown color. Remnants of adrenal tissue are recognized in the periphery, apparently normal in appearance.
Microscopy informs us of a proliferation of cellular elements of stromal origin, with large pleomorphic nuclei, with multinucleated cells that adopt a fascicular pattern. The mitotic index is high (7 m/10 high power fields). There are areas of intratumoral necrosis and invasion of adrenal tissue present in the peripheral area.
The definitive diagnosis is LEIOMYOSARCOMA OF CAVA.
Regarding the evolution, the controls, both analytical and imaging, carried out in the early post-surgical period show normality. The case is referred to the Oncology Service, which decides on consolidation radiotherapy treatment on the surgical bed, starting 40 days after the intervention.
New controls were carried out at six, nine, twelve and twenty-four months, all of which were normal.

Currently the patient is alive and disease-free, after 28 months of follow-up.
