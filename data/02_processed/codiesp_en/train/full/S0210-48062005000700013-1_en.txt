A 35-year-old male, with a history of lues, unknown hepatitis and circumcision at the age of 4, presented with bifidity of the urinary stream. On examination, the meatus is normal and there are no fistulas. He draws attention to a penis larger than what could be considered normal and an erection of the corpora cavernosa, without participation of the glans..

Cross-examined, he claims to have been in this situation for as long as he can remember and that, as a child, he held his penis with a string around his waist so that his erection would not be noticeable. Regarding his sexual activity, he affirms his homosexual option and his satisfaction with the size of his penis and his permanent erection, which allows him successive and indefinite intercourse. He does not have any ejaculation problem.
He performed penile Doppler ultrasound, with time/velocity values ​​in corpora cavernosa compatible with erection (right cavernous systolic velocity of 51 cm/sec and 41 cm/sec on the left side). Probable arteriocavernous fistula in the proximal portion of the right corpus cavernosum.
MRI angiography demonstrates an arteriocavernous fistula on the right side, which is present in both the arterial and venous phases and confirms the diagnosis of high-flow arterial priapism.
