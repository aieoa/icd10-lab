B.A.M., is an 88-year-old man who is admitted to the Internal Medicine Service after being referred to the emergency room from the day center to which he attends daily, due to an episode of disconnection from his environment. The patient has been a widower for seven years, lives at home with a caregiver and has good family support.
Follow usual treatment with: atenolol (25 mg/day), acetylsalicylic acid (300 mg/day), omeprazole (40 mg/day), nitroglycerin (patch, 5 mg/day), simvastatin (20 mg/day), enalapril ( 20 mg)/hydrochlorothiazide (12.5 mg) 1-0-½, tamsulosin (0.4 mg/day), levothyroxine (50 mcg-75 mcg-0), bromazepam (1.5 mg/night), sertraline (50 mg/day from the previous day, previously 25 mg/day).
His personal history includes: HTN under treatment. Ischemic heart disease (AMI in 1995). Peripheral arterial disease in follow-up for vascular surgery. Prostatism with elevated-stable PSA. Hypothyroidism in replacement treatment. Long-term depressive syndrome, treated with benzodiazepines and different antidepressants for 10 years, always by his primary care physician (MAP). No other psychiatric history. The patient has had two previous admissions:
An admission three years ago to the Internal Medicine service due to hyponatremia (plasma Na 116 mEq/l) that resulted in functional deterioration. The family reported a sudden worsening of the patient two days before admission, with urinary incontinence, partial disorientation in time and space, and also associated a presumed worsening of the patient's depressive symptoms with the introduction of sertraline (75 mg/day) 13 days before. (depressed mood, feeling of imminent death, nighttime agitation). Fluid restriction was performed and treatment with the recently introduced diuretic hydrochlorothiazide (50 mg/day) and sertraline (75 mg/day) was suspended. He was discharged with a blood Na of 127 mEq/l. The main diagnosis at discharge was "Hyponatremia probably due to diuretics."
Another second admission 20 months ago to the Neurology Service after going to the hospital emergency department reporting a feeling of dizziness with spinning objects around the home, without nausea or vomiting, with a feeling of discomfort and a feeling of weakness in MMII. The patient was not being treated with any diuretic, and 15 days before admission, antidepressant medication had been reintroduced into his usual treatment (citalopram 30 mg/day) due to worsening of his depressive symptoms. He was admitted with a plasma Na of 114 mEq/l. Among other tests, a cranial CT scan was performed in which no intra- or extra-axial hemorrhagic lesions or space-occupying lesions were observed, and a cranial MRI showed moderate cortical atrophy and supratentorial white matter lesions. Fluid restriction and citalopram withdrawal were performed. He was discharged after 10 days with a Na of 125 mEq/l and a diagnosis of "Hyponatremia due to SIADH secondary to citalopram."
Four weeks before his current admission, his new PCP prescribed sertraline 25 mg/day due to low mood, anxiety, and apathy. The patient had been without antidepressant treatment since the last admission. According to the family, during these weeks they notice that he is progressively more clumsy on a motor, bradypsychic level and with psychomotor inhibition. The day before going to the emergency room, the dose is increased to 50 mg/day due to lack of improvement.
The patient is brought to the emergency room due to an episode of intense drowsiness lasting about ten minutes that only responds to painful stimuli. When he recovers later, he presents incoherent speech. In the emergency assessment, the patient is in a baseline situation, without motor focus and oriented in time and space. The physical examination does not show any significant findings and the neurological examination only shows motor slowness and a tendency to drowsiness. Complementary tests show hyponatremia of 116 mEq/l, increased urinary osmolarity (531 mOsm/kg) and decreased plasma osmolarity (254 mOsm/Kg). Chest x-ray and ECG, without relevant findings.
The patient is admitted to Internal Medicine with the diagnosis of severe symptomatic hypotonic hyponatremia secondary to thiazide treatment associated with SSRIs. Treatment with sertraline was suspended, fluid restriction with balance was indicated, and isotonic saline solution was administered until plasma sodium levels normalized.
After several days of admission, part of the consultation is carried out at the Psychiatry service for clinical assessment and decision regarding antidepressant pharmacological treatment. The patient presents a depressive condition with high levels of anxiety and intense hypochondriacal fears. Both he and his family demand another antidepressant treatment since in recent years, during the periods in which he had been without treatment, there had been a clear clinical worsening. It was decided to start treatment with mirtazapine 15 mg/day with an increase to 30 mg/day after the first week of treatment and to perform weekly controls of plasma sodium levels during the first 3-4 weeks and more widely spaced thereafter. Upon discharge, antihypertensive treatment with enalapril is also recommended, avoiding associations with thiazide diuretics.
