The case that our work describes is that of a 76-year-old woman with a history of high blood pressure and rectal carcinoma treated 15 years ago with radiotherapy, surgery and chemotherapy, in complete remission at that time. She was receiving outpatient treatment with opioid analgesics, under pain unit monitoring, for generalized osteoarthritis.
Last September 2013, said patient went to the Emergency Department presenting with acute chest pain that was diagnosed as NSTEACS-Killip III, evaluated by the Cardiology Service, loading doses of Prasugrel and Acetylsalicylic acid were started, and Primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention was decided through a right radial approach, with placement of two stents. Two hours after her stay in the Intensive Care Unit, the patient, in stable general condition, began to experience pain and hematoma formation on the volar aspect of the right forearm in relation to the catheterization puncture area.
The patient is evaluated by the Traumatology Service, and the physical findings found in the examination carried out are the following:
- Pain, exacerbated by passive extension of the fingers (extension of the forearm flexor muscles).
- Paleness.
- Inability to detect radial or ulnar pulse.
- Decrease in temperature with respect to the contralateral limb.
- Anesthesia and total paralysis of the affected limb.
Given the clinical findings, it was decided to make a confirmatory diagnosis by measuring the intracompartmental pressure with the Stryker® "Intra-Compartmental pressure monitor system", obtaining a record of 42mm Hg. The diagnosis of acute compartment syndrome in the right forearm was determined and urgent surgical treatment was proposed.
The surgical procedure was performed under balanced general anesthesia and airway control with a laryngeal mask. Antibiotic prophylaxis with 2 grams of intravenous Cefazolin. A skin incision was made on the midline of the volar aspect of the forearm, from the elbow flexure to Kaplan's line. The subcutaneous cellular tissue was intensely infiltrated by hematoma. A longitudinal section of the antebrachial fascia was performed, exposing the epitrochlear musculature of the forearm. In addition, a decompression of the median nerve was performed by sectioning the annular ligament of the carpus. No vascular lesions with signs of active arterial bleeding were observed. For surgical closure, the wound was covered with sterile compresses soaked in Nitrofural, and circumstantial closure was performed using mesh with vascular elastics (vessel loops) for containment, and a non-compressive bandage.

From the moment the fasciotomy was performed, recovery of the color and temperature of the affected limb, as well as the arterial pulse, could be seen. Three hours after surgery, the patient was re-evaluated, showing capillary refill of less than 2 seconds, mobility and distal sensitivity, and reporting no signs of pain. Nine hours later, the patient presented absence of pain in the right forearm, with good distal neurovascular status.
