A 73-year-old patient, with a history of high blood pressure and polyarthrosis, attended the emergency department due to distension and abdominal pain associated with constipation and low-grade fever. The symptoms had started three weeks earlier and worsened during the four days prior to admission. During this period she had undergone an upper digestive fiber endoscopy (esophagus, stomach and duodenum) as well as a colonoscopy (up to the splenic flexure), without finding any anomalies.
The physical examination revealed low-grade fever (37.6º C), a distended abdomen, diffusely painful on palpation, tympanic on percussion, with few borborygmi but no evidence of peritonism, pulmonary auscultation with decreased ventilation in the lower half of the abdomen. right hemithorax and the triggering of intense pain on palpation and percussion of the last three dorsal spinous processes.
Analysis showed 8.2 x 109 leukocytes/L, hemoglobin 136 g/L, platelets 186 x 109/L. Except blood glucose (123 mg/dl), the following laboratory parameters were normal or negative: urea, creatinine , bilirubin, transaminases, gammaglutamyltranspeptidase, sodium, potassium, chlorine, calcium, phosphorus, creatine phosphokinase, amylase, lactic acid hydrogenase (LDH), proteinogram, immunoglobulin dosage, alpha-fetoprotein, CA 19.9 and CA 125 antigens, as well as general urinalysis . ESR and C-reactive protein were elevated, with values ​​of 85 mm/1st h and 133 mg/L (normal < 5 mg/L), respectively. The Mantoux intradermorreaction (10 IU RT-23) was positive, with an induration of 25 mm. The chest x-ray showed an image compatible with atelectasis of the right lower lobe in the context of an ipsilateral pleural effusion. There were no signs suggestive of lymphadenopathy or alterations in the cardiopericardial silhouette. A thoracoabdominal CT confirmed the existence of a right pleural effusion and identified prominent degenerative changes along the thoracolumbar spine but, above all, erosions in the vertebral endplates adjacent to the D10-D11 disc space. A lumbar MRI showed hyposignal in the T1-weighted sequences and hypersignal in the T2-weighted sequences in these vertebrae and their corresponding disc, with morphological alterations typical of infectious spondylodiscitis D10-D11. Three serial blood cultures were negative. In the samples obtained by aspiration from the D10-D11 space, chains of gram-positive cocci were observed, which were later recovered and typed as Streptococcus pneumoniae sensitive to penicillin. Analysis of the pleural fluid showed pH: 7.55; leukocytes: 8.4 x 109/L (58% neutrophils, 26% eosinophils, 16% lymphocytes), proteins: 48 g/L (ratio to serum proteins: 0.65), glucose: 125 mg/dl, ADA : 25.92 IU/ml, LDH: 362 U/L (pleural LDH / serum LDH ratio: 0.8). Both auramine-rhodamine staining and culture in Löwenstein-Jensen medium of the pleural fluid were negative and cytology did not reveal neoplastic cells.

The patient was initially treated intravenously with the association of amoxicillin + clavulanic acid (1 g/200 mg, every 8 hours). After 21 days, she switched to oral administration (875/125 mg, every 8 hours) until completing 6 weeks. The evolution was favorable and she was able to begin walking with a thoracolumbar corset from the fourth week onwards. One month after the end of antibiotic therapy, a control chest CT still showed a discrete pleural effusion, but the patient only suffered mild mechanical back pain, the ESR had decreased to 21 mm/1 h and the CRP was 2.4 mg/h. L. Outpatient follow-up continued for three more years, during which the evolution was favorable and a D10-D11 vertebral block was formed.
