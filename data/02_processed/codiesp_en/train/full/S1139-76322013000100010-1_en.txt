This is a four-year-old male with no relevant history, correctly vaccinated, who started with a fever of 38.5 oC for 12 hours (h), painful submandibular lymphadenopathy, erythematous pharynx with exudate, without catarrhal symptoms. In addition, he had a raised micropapular rash with "goosebumps" that affected the trunk and the inguinal and axillary folds, which was slightly pruritic and slightly painful on palpation. Given the clinical suspicion of scarlet fever, a rapid detection test for Streptococcus pyogenes was performed, which was negative, so expectant management and clinical review were decided in 24-48 hours. At 24 hours, the general condition had worsened with fever up to 39 °C and persistence of rash, so, given the high suspicion of scarlet fever, it was decided to collect a throat swab and prescribe amoxicillin at a dose of 40 mg/kg/day distributed in one feeding every 8 hours. After 72 hours, the result of the throat swab was obtained, which was positive for Staphylococcus aureus (abundant growth) and sensitive to amoxicillin/clavulanic acid. At that time, the patient is clinically well, he is afebrile and the rash has subsided, so it is decided to complete treatment with amoxicillin/clavulanic acid for eight days. Two weeks after the onset of fever, the patient presented "glove-like" peeling of the soles and palms.
