Female patient, 62 years old, poorly controlled diabetic, obese, with a history of unresolved left staghorn lithiasis. Consultation due to fever and left low back pain of 7 days duration, with oral mycosis and clear deterioration of general condition. She presents leukocytosis with marked neutrophilia, hyperglycemia with ketoacidosis, and elevated urea and creatinine levels. On physical examination, she had a soft abdomen, with left flank pain and positive left renal fist percussion. Abdominal ultrasound and direct abdominal x-ray are performed, which guide the diagnosis by identifying air in the left renal fossa. Abdominal-pelvic CT scan shows air in the left kidney and perirenal tissues, which infiltrates the abdominal wall. Due to the rapid deterioration of his general condition, drainage of the renal fossa was performed by lumbotomy, to avoid contamination of the peritoneal cavity, evacuating the gas and debriding necrotic tissue. After an initial improvement, signs of sepsis reappeared, making it necessary to perform a simple nephrectomy a week later. Good postoperative evolution, 14 days later new sepsis of respiratory origin. It began with purulent discharge through the wound, a "toilette" was performed, but he died 48 hours later. due to multiple organ dysfunction with nosocomial pneumonia.
