A 59-year-old man was admitted in September 2008 with a diagnosis of post-surgical chylothorax. History of sigmoid cancer, with a liver metastasectomy performed in July 2008, and since then it has been necessary to perform several thoracenteses to evacuate chylothorax (approximate extracted volume: 9,000 mL).
Upon admission, he reported asthenia, discomfort in the right hemithorax, and slight respiratory difficulty. A pleural effusion was radiologically observed occupying two thirds of the right hemithorax. After placement of a pleural drain, 500 mL of a milky liquid was obtained in the first 24 hours, the analysis of which was compatible with chylothorax3: cholesterol (COL) 60 mg/dL, triglycerides (TG) 515 mg/dL, liquid COL ratio pleural/serum: 0.28; pleural fluid/serum TG ratio: 3.18. Conservative treatment was decided with an absolute diet and chest drainage, consulting with the Nutrition Area to initiate parenteral nutritional support.

The nutritional assessment highlighted a 4% weight loss in the last two months (usual 74 kg; current 71 kg), albumin 2.6 g/dL, transferrin 154 mg/dL, C-reactive protein (CRP) 16, 6 mg/dL. He had slight loss of subcutaneous fat and muscle mass, along with reduced functional capacity at home in recent weeks; absence of malleolar edema and signs or symptoms of specific vitamin deficiencies. Dietary history without recent changes.
The nutritional situation was compatible with mild energy-protein malnutrition, suggesting the following nutritional care plan:
• Objectives: Keep the gastrointestinal tract at rest and prevent a greater degree of malnutrition in a patient with high nutrient losses through pleural drainage.
• Nutritional requirements: caloric needs estimated using the Harris-Benedict equation (correction factor of 1.4), 2,200 kcal/day; protein needs 1.5-1.7 g/kg/day of amino acids; standard micronutrient needs.
• Composition of the parenteral nutrition (PN) formula: Volume 2,500 ml; 120 g of amino acids; 300 g of glucose; 60 g of lipids (MCT/LCT); 2280 total kcal; vitamins and trace elements according to AMA-ASPEN recommendations.
Despite treatment, pleural drainage remained stable in the first days, and the chylous appearance persisted. Ultrasound showed loculated pleural effusion, and 200,000 IU of urokinase were consequently administered for two consecutive days through the thoracentesis tube. Given the persistence of abundant chylous drainage, it was decided to start treatment with octreotide, at a dose of 100 mcg/8 h subcutaneously. The drainage progressively reduced over the following days, changing to a serous appearance. At the same time, there was a worsening of the patient's general condition, with abdominal distension, oliguria and hyperglycemia. Due to suspicion of an adverse reaction to octreotide, it was suspended on the 4th day of treatment. Biochemistry showed anemia (hemoglobin 9.4 g/dL; hematocrit 28.8%), leukopenia (2,390 leukocytes/microliters), thrombocytopenia (48,000/microliters), urea 59 mg/dL, creatinine 0.6 mg/dL, bilirubin total 1.4 mg/dL (direct 1.2 mg/dL), GOT 76 UI/L, GPT 61 UI/L, gGT 502 UI/L, FA 136 UI/L, CRP 29.8 mg/dL.

After discontinuation of octreotide, pleural drainage continued to decrease, until it was reduced to 50 mL/24h, maintaining the serous appearance. On day 19 of admission, mixed feeding was started using enteral nutrition (EN) orally with fat-free formula (Clinutren Fruit®; 1.25 kcal/mL; 13% proteins, 87% carbohydrates; 600 mL/day), along with with an oral diet with low fat content (boiled or pureed vegetables and potatoes, natural fruit, in juice or compote, infusions). On subsequent days, NE was progressively increased until reaching 50% of the estimated caloric needs (1,000 mL/day), the diet was progressed with the introduction of toasted bread, boiled or grilled white fish, salad and skimmed milk, and supplemented with MCT oil (up to 60 g/day, in salads and bread). At the same time, the contributions provided with PN were reduced. On day 22, after 48 hours without changes in pleural drainage, the thoracentesis tube and PN were removed.
The nutritional evolution was favorable, with a weight gain of 2 kg, although no improvement in visceral proteins could be observed due to the persistence of high CRP (15.7 mg/dL). Upon discharge, it was advised to follow the same diet supplemented with one tablespoon per day (10 g) of sunflower oil to prevent essential fatty acid (EFA) deficiency.
