A nine-month-old male presented with intense perianal erythema. A first rectal smear was performed with a negative streptococcal antigen rapid detection test result after 24 hours, so treatment with clotrimazole was started. As the patient did not progress well, it was decided to repeat the rectal and pharyngotonsillar swabs, finding a positive result for the rapid detection of streptococcal antigen in the rectal sample and a negative result in the pharyngotonsillar sample. The culture of the rectal sample was negative for fungi and showed growth of GABHS. Treatment with oral penicillin was started for ten days, with resolution of the condition.
