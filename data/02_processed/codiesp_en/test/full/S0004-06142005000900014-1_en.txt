A seventeen-year-old male with no relevant history presented with urinary frequency, burning urination, tenesmus, and self-limited hematuria.
In the urine analysis, 30-50 red blood cells per field are seen. Negative urine culture.
Abdominal ultrasound was performed, revealing a small lesion, half a centimeter in diameter, solid with anterior hyperechoic enhancement.

We performed cystoscopy and observed two small raised lesions on the right lateral side, outside the ureteral orifice, with apparently normal mucosa, similar to two igloi.
If an inflammatory lesion is suspected, treatment with NSAIDs is prescribed. for ten days without the lesions disappearing, deciding to undergo surgery.
TUR of both bladder lesions was performed, the pathological report being that of bladder leiomyoma, describing the lesion as "proliferation of spindle cells arranged in intertwined fascicles with blunt nuclei and eosinophilic cytoplasm without atypia, necrosis or significant mitotic activity. With the immunohistochemical study, demonstrated intense diffuse cytoplasmic positivity for smooth muscle actin.

In the follow-up carried out on the patient, no recurrence of these lesions has been observed.
