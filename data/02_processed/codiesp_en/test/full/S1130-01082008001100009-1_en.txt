A 79-year-old woman with no relevant history presented with diarrhea and general syndrome of one year's duration. The physical examination revealed a palpable mass in the right iliac fossa that was confirmed on a simple abdominal x-ray. An abdominal computed tomography was performed, revealing the presence of a large liver mass with exophytic growth towards the right iliac fossa suggestive of hepatocellular carcinoma. The pancreas, spleen, kidneys, uterus, and ovaries were normal. Serologies for hepatotropic viruses were negative and serum alpha-fetoprotein levels were within normal limits. After fine needle aspiration of the lesion, material was obtained whose histological study demonstrated a well-differentiated hepatocellular carcinoma. Surgical resection was ruled out due to tumor size and suspicion of vascular invasion, treatment with chemoembolization was initiated with good results. At present, after six months of follow-up, the patient is asymptomatic.
