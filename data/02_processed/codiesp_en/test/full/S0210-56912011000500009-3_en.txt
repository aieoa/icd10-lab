A 57-year-old man, with no relevant history, attended the hospital emergency department due to chest pain. Acute coronary syndrome with elevation of the ST segment in the anterior cardiac aspect was diagnosed and percutaneous coronary angiography was performed. The patient had a lesion in the middle portion of the anterior descending coronary artery, so angioplasty was performed and a stent was placed, without complications, after having administered 300 mg of acetylsalicylic acid, 300 mg of clopidogrel and 5,000 IU of sodium heparin. intravenous. At the end of the procedure the patient was in Killip III and presented with massive hematemesis. Arterial O2 saturation was 92%, with a 50% Ventimask 10 bpm. A digestive endoscopy with respiratory support with CPAP-B was performed, according to the described protocol, and a Mallory Weiss lesion was observed. Hemoglobin fell from 15.1 to 13g/dl and hematocrit from 43.6 to 39.1%. At the end of the procedure, the patient had 100% saturation and CPAP-B could be withdrawn (arterial blood gases were not performed due to the risk of bleeding after the administration of anticoagulants and antiplatelets).
