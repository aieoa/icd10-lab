A 64-year-old woman with a personal history of diabetes mellitus, arterial hypertension and atrial fibrillation anticoagulated with acenocoumarol. Seen in Outpatient Clinics due to a clinical picture of inability to walk and occasional urinary incontinence, with impairment of higher functions and emotional lability. Neuroimaging studies showed tetraventricular hydrocephalus. Given the suspicion of HCA, it was decided to place a DLE as a diagnostic test. After replacing acenocoumarol with low molecular weight heparin a few weeks before, the DLE was placed. During the transfer of the patient from the operating room to the hospital ward, the drainage was accidentally left open. Six hours after placement, after having drained 240 ml of CSF, the patient began to experience headaches, nausea, vomiting, and sweating that were initially attributed to intracranial hypotension. At no time did her level of consciousness deteriorate. Given the intensity of the symptoms, an urgent head CT was performed, which showed the presence of a hematoma in the vermis and left cerebellar hemisphere, with subarachnoid bleeding on the upper surface of the cerebellum and in the cerebellar foliae. The DLE was immediately removed and, given the patient's good clinical condition, it was decided to continue conservative treatment, with good clinical evolution. Neuroimaging studies (MRI and cerebral angiography) ruled out the existence of underlying pathology.

The patient recovered to her previous condition. Subsequently, a ventriculoperitoneal shunt was implanted, showing great clinical improvement.
