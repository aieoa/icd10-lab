31-year-old patient with traumatic spinal cord injury at 20 years of age and history of repeated urinary infections in relation to neurogenic bladder (self-catheterization) and episodes of urinary obstruction. In 2009 she was referred to a nephrology clinic for stage 2 chronic kidney disease, with proteinuria in the nephrotic range (creatinine 1.3mg/dl, proteinuria 20g/24h). The patient refused to perform a kidney biopsy and in the same year he stopped attending nephrology consultations.
In January 2012 he began having episodes of decreased level of consciousness. A cranial computed tomography and lumbar puncture were performed, both of which were normal. Electroencephalogram with diffuse slowing. The creatinine at that time is 3.3 mg/dl. The condition is suggested to be secondary to urinary infection.
In April 2012, the same situation was repeated, accompanied by temporal-spatial disorientation. Presents a new episode in the same month, but this time with behavioral change (infantilism, nervousness, aggressiveness). Magnetic resonance imaging of the brain was performed without findings of interest. After this episode, treatment with clonazepam was started.
In May 2012, due to laboratory tests and symptoms of end-stage renal failure, hemodialysis was started through a provisional catheter in the right jugular vein.
The patient presented, approximately once a month, in the first two hours after the start of hemodialysis, episodes of decreased level of consciousness, and on other occasions psychomotor agitation.
In November 2012 he was evaluated by Neurology and Psychiatry, with no relevant findings detected. They suggest the condition is secondary to hypoxic-metabolic encephalopathy.
Despite presenting correct KT and Kt/V, we increased the number of dialysis sessions to 4/week, with no clinical improvement evident.
In January 2013, we decided to discontinue treatment with baclofen (Lioresal®), replacing it with tinazidine and diazepam. After two weeks of treatment with tinazidine, the patient discontinued the drug due to drowsiness, and the spasticity was controlled only with diazepam.
Ten months after drug withdrawal, the patient has not presented any neurological symptoms again.
