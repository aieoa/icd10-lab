A 67-year-old woman consulted our service for swelling in the posterior region of the right hemipalate. A biopsy was performed under local anesthesia with the diagnosis of PLGA. On cervical examination, she had lymphadenopathy at the right submandibular level. The most relevant antecedent was treatment with radiotherapy in childhood for an unclassified pathology in the head and neck region.
Orthopantomography was normal. A cervicofacial computed tomography was requested and was reported as a stage IV lesion at the level of the right nasal fossa (T4N2CMx). A cytological puncture of the submandibular adenopathy was performed, which reported probable PLGA. MRI shows a well-defined lesion at the level of the nasal fossa with extension towards the pterygoid region.

Surgical intervention was decided under general anesthesia. The tumor was approached using a Lefort I type maxillotomy. A unilateral suprahomohyoid cervical lymph node dissection was also performed. Tumor resection and bone replacement of the maxilla were carried out using 1.5 mm preformed miniplates.

The definitive result of the pathological anatomy confirmed the diagnosis of PLGA, with invasion of surgical margins at the level of the right pterygomaxillary area. During dissection, metastatic lymphadenopathy (2/16) was detected on the right side (5).

The patient was presented in the Maxillofacial Surgery - Oncology session, deciding adjuvant radiotherapy in the right pterygomaxillary region and following imaging controls using resonance. Given the persistence of tumor remains, it was decided to widen the surgical margins at the right pterygomaxillary level and reconstruct the defect using a Bichat buccal fat ball.
Two years later, the patient is undergoing regular check-ups and is disease-free.
