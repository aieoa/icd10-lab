Patient: YSS Age: 32 years old Race: Black. Personal history: Does not report associated pathologies HEA: Patient who comes to consultation because of progressive increase in the external genital organs, lymph transudation and sexual impotence for two years.
Positive data on physical examination at the time of receipt:
External genitalia: Marked edema of the penis and scrotum with erythema and pain. Non-painful inguinal lymphadenopathy.

Complementary studies were performed to demonstrate possible etiology: Complete blood count: Normal figures. Serology: Non-reactive. Mantoux test: Negative Total and fractionated proteins: Normal Hip X-ray: Absence of bone lesion. Abdominal U/S: Thickened right psoas of 48 mm Simple CT of the hypogastrium and pelvis: Aseptic necrosis of the head of the femur with hypertrophy of the psoas at that level.
Different surgical procedures were performed: -Right retroperitoneal exploration. -Right iliac and cava adenectomy. -Needle lymphangioplasty. -Fasciotomy of the root of the penis.
This patient did not present any symptoms of chyluria or edema of the lower limbs, nor was a varicocele or hydrocele detected.
With the clinical manifestations, as well as with the findings found and ruling out another possible cause of lymphedema of the penis and scrotum in this patient, the following is diagnosed:
-Primary local inflammatory lymphedema of the penis and scrotum.
-Aseptic necrosis of the head of the femur.
An evaluation is then carried out with a multidisciplinary team made up of urologists, angiologists and plastic surgeons. It was decided to perform lymphangiectomy.
