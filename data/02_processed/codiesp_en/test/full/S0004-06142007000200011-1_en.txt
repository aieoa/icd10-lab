A 41-year-old patient, a smoker, diagnosed with lipoma in the right thigh who underwent an abdominal ultrasound due to an increase in catecholamines in the blood (norepinephrine and dopamine) without accompanying arterial hypertension. A hypoanechoic nodular lesion of about 3 cm in maximum dimension was observed at the adrenal level, compatible with a solid lesion with cystic, multi-seated degeneration. This injury was confirmed by CT and scintigraphy. The patient underwent surgery with the clinical judgments of pheochromocytoma versus adrenal gland adenoma. An irregular 9 cm fragment of adipose tissue was received, whose serial sections revealed a 1.2 cm yellowish-orange tissue along with a multilocular cystic formation with thin, whitish walls without papillary formations or excrescences inside. The histological study showed a large cystic formation without blood content covered by flat cells with central fusiform nuclei, sometimes protruding intraluminally, with immunohistochemical positivity for CD 31 and CD 34 and negativity for calretinin (endothelium). Cystic formations with characteristics similar to the one described, although smaller in size, were observed both in a location adjacent to the main one and randomly distributed within the adrenal parenchyma. With these findings, the diagnosis of cystic lymphangioma was made.
