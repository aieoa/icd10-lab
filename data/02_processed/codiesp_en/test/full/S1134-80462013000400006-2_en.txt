A 13-year-old girl with CRPS of the right lower extremity also secondary to a sprained ankle that occurred six months before her arrival at the Unit. The approach to follow was placement in the operating room, under general anesthesia, of an octopolar electrode (Octrode) in the posterior spinal cords at the level of T9-T12 and connection to an external electrical impulse generator. Once she recovered from anesthesia, the system was programmed with very good localization of the paresthesias on the affected area. It was reviewed a week later in the Unit, observing a good response for both pain and edema and other signs. It was reviewed again a week later in which maintenance of the pain control situation and allodynia, which had allowed the patient to begin the rehabilitation exercises that she had had to suspend due to the severe pain triggered by the mobilization of the limb. Given this situation and, in agreement with the parents, it was decided to connect, in the operating room and under general anesthesia, to a Genesis brand programmable electrical impulse generator.
It was evaluated 15 and 30 days after implantation of the generator, continuing in the same situation of pain relief and accompanying symptoms. Six months later, the patient went to the Unit for consultation, with both the pain relief and the rest of the symptoms remaining. With the allodynia and a large part of the edema and the rest of the accompanying symptoms having disappeared, the patient could now support the foot and continued with the rehabilitation, the family telling us that for two months they had not used the generator, which they kept disconnected, without any pain or accompanying symptoms having appeared.
Given this situation, it was decided to review the patient within four weeks, continuing with the rehabilitation exercises during that time. It was evaluated four weeks later and given the persistence of the previous situation, it was decided, by mutual agreement with the family, to explant both the generator and the spinal electrode, which was done again in the operating room and under general anesthesia. The patient currently continues to be asymptomatic and leading her normal life.
