A 45-year-old man with stage 5 CKD secondary to polycystic hepatorenal disease with a history of arterial hypertension and hyperuricemia. Given the progressive deterioration of renal function with the need for renal replacement treatment, and after the explanation of the different techniques, a 1-cuff non-self-positioning straight peritoneal catheter is inserted. One month later, after starting CAPD at his home with 4 exchanges of 2 liters of 1.36% glucose, the patient went to the Peritoneal Dialysis Unit, reporting inguinoscrotal swelling that had been going on for 48 hours. After ruling out orchiepididymitis, CT-peritoneography is performed, as in the previous case, confirming the passage of contrast to the testicles through a patent vaginal process. Peritoneal rest and transfer to hemodialysis were decided. One month later, the peritoneovaginal duct was surgically closed, restarting CAPD in June 2009 without incident or reappearance of the leak.
