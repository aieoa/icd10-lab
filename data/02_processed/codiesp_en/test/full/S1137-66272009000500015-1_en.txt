A 55-year-old man was referred to our hospital in November 2005 due to odynophagia and oral bleeding. The physical examination revealed a necrotic-looking ulcer measuring 4-5 cm in diameter on the tonsillar pillar and left tonsil, which was biopsied, resulting in squamous cell carcinoma. The CT scan showed ipsilateral laterocervical lymphadenopathy, both submandibular and jugulodigastric. The blood tests and the rest of the examinations were normal. Thus, the diagnosis of locally advanced squamous cell carcinoma of the oropharynx (T3-N2b-M0, Stage IVa) was established and chemoradiotherapy treatment was initiated. Treatment consisted of hyperfractionated radiotherapy (120 cGy per fraction, 2 fractions/day, up to 7,680 cGy), and cisplatin, 20 mg/m2/day in continuous infusion for 120 hours, between days 1 to 5 and 21 to 25 of treatment. . At the end of treatment, both the physical examination and CT showed a complete response.
The patient remained asymptomatic until March 2007 when he came to our hospital with symptoms of asthenia, instability and abdominal pain followed by massive hematemesis with clinical and hematological involvement (Hb 7.1g, hematocrit 21.5%). Gastroscopy showed an ulcerated tumor in the distal esophagus, over the cardia extending to the greater curvature and the anterior and posterior surface of the stomach. The biopsy result was squamous cell carcinoma compatible with metastasis of laryngeal carcinoma.

Throughout the hospital stay, several transfusions were necessary and treatment with somatostatin was started, despite the fact that the bleeding remained active. Palliative radiotherapy treatment with hemostatic intent was then initiated, administering 20Gy in 5 fractions of 400cGy with good digestive tolerance and achieving remission of the bleeding episodes. The patient was discharged and remained asymptomatic for another 5 months, after which he died due to metastatic spread.
