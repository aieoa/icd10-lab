A 13-year-old male is referred for ophthalmological evaluation due to poor vision. Phenotypically he was a short child with a height of 133 cm, brachymorphy and brachydactyly in all four limbs.
The patient had a corrected refractive error of -13.00 -6.50 at 1º in the right eye and -16.00-6.25 at 179º in the left eye. With this correction he achieved a visual acuity of 0.4 and 0.2 respectively. There was no monocular diplopia or findings in extrinsic and intrinsic ocular motility.
The horizontal corneal diameter was 12.0 mm in both eyes and the pachymetry was 613 and 611 microns, respectively. The anterior chamber was narrow, with bilateral iridophacodonesis noted. Microspherophakia was evident with anterior displacement of both lenses within the posterior chamber.
Intraocular pressure was 20 mmHg bilaterally. Gonioscopically, a symmetrical narrow angle was observed in both eyes, grade II according to Schaffer.
Examination using an Orbscan-II topographer (Corneal Topographer, Baush and Lomb, U.S.A.) and fundus examination showed no alterations.
To prevent angle closure, an Nd:YAG laser iridotomy was performed in both eyes at 10 and 2 hours, respectively. Given the stability of vision and the absence of diplopia, a conservative approach was adopted in accordance with the wishes of the patient's family by monitoring visual acuity, intraocular pressure, photographic documentation, and performing visual fields to detect the appearance of damage. glaucomatous.
Reviewing his family history, the patient was identified as the youngest of a family of eight siblings, in which - in addition to the aforementioned - a brother and two sisters presented spherophakia, brachymorphy and brachydactyly. The parents were healthy and none of the children have had children.
