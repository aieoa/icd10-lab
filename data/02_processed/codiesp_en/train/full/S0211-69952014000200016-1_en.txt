A 68-year-old woman, whose only history of interest is iron deficiency anemia studied by Gastroenterology since May 2013, with no nephrological history. After progressive anemia (hemoglobin 6.5g/dl), he was referred to a hospital for transfusion of packed red blood cells, observing a post-transfusion reaction and progressive deterioration of renal function, reaching maximum creatinine levels of 7.67mg/dl, urea 171mg/dl. dl and oligoanuria. It was decided to admit him to the Nephrology Service, where a complete study was carried out and hemoglobin values ​​of up to 4.2 mg/dl, LDH of 997 IU/l, indirect bilirubin of 1.1 mg/dl, reticulocytes 2.9%, haptoglobin were observed. of 36.40 mg/dl and in the blood smear: anisopoikilocytosis with polychromasia. A urine test strip showed hematuria and pyuria. The data suggested intravascular hemolytic anemia of autoimmune origin due to blood incompatibility.
The Hematology Service was consulted, which reported the presence of irregular antibodies with anti-Tja specificity at a titer of 1/16. These results are compatible with the absence of an antigen with a high incidence in the general population, the P antigen. During admission, kidney function improved. She required several sessions of hemodialysis, supportive treatment with intense serum therapy and alkalinization, and treatment for her anemia with erythropoietin and intravenous iron, transfusion of four packed red blood cells. She currently has a creatinine of 0.3 mg/dl and a hemoglobin of 10.6 g/dl.
