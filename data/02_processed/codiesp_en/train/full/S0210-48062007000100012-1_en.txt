A 25-year-old male, with no personal history of interest, presented with a tumor in both testicles that had been developing for two weeks.
Physical examination: Hard mass that occupies more than half of the left testicle. Right teste increased in consistency, especially in its posterior part.
Complementary explorations:
- Scrotal echo: Solid tumor measuring 37x21 mm, hypoechoic in the right testis. Two solid lesions in the left teste measuring 32x17 mm and 7 mm with ultrasound characteristics similar to those found in the right teste. All of this is compatible with multicentric bilateral testicular neoplasia. Left varicocele.

- Tumor markers: AFP: normal; ß-HCG: 6.15 mIU/ml (normal value: < 2.5 mIU/ml); LDH: normal.
Surgical treatment: Bilateral radical orchiectomy through the inguinal route. The patient ruled out the possibility of placing a prosthesis.
Histological diagnosis: seminoma with high mitotic index and vascular invasion (pT2) in both testes (anaplastic seminoma).

Extension study (thoracoabdominopelvic CT): adenopathic growths at the retroperitoneal level (N2) and liver metastatic lesions (M1).
Complementary treatment: systemic chemotherapy with the etoposide and cisplatin regimen (4 cycles).
Currently, and after 24 months of follow-up, there is negativity of tumor markers with absence of retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy and liver metastases (complete remission).
The patient declined the possibility of freezing semen prior to radical surgery.
