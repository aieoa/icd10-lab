This is a 14-month-old boy who came to the HUCA presenting with scaly skin lesions on the scalp and exudative skin lesions in the folds and perianal region.
A skin biopsy was performed, which gave the diagnosis of LCH. The condition is limited to the skin exclusively and topical treatment is performed locally.
In subsequent check-ups, localized gingivitis was diagnosed at the level of 83,84.

In order to determine if there is a relationship with the previous skin process, the corresponding 84 marginal gum biopsy was performed with the sample processed with hematoxylin-eosin, according to standard technique, for histopathological study.
At the same time, an immunohistochemical study is performed using the markers CD1a (surface antigen) and the S-100 protein specific to Langerhans cells.
The result of the pathological study determined the presence of a squamous epithelium with an infiltrate of polynuclear cells and eosinophils. At the level of the chorion, nodules composed of cells of a histiocytic structure with vesicular, clear, embossed nuclei were evident, some with an irregular "coffee bean" shape.

The immunohistochemical study left evidence of positivity for the CD1a surface antigen and for the S-100 protein, effectively determining the oral impact of the disease.

The patient was classified as having multifocal Langerhans cell histiocytosis.
