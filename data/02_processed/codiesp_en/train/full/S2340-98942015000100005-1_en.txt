We present the case of a 62-year-old patient who in December 2010 underwent surgery for papillary serous carcinoma in the left ovary with involvement of a lymph node in the aorto-caval chain (stage IIIC). She subsequently received adjuvant chemotherapy with carboplatin and paclitaxel, ending in June 2011. Later, she began follow-up, remaining asymptomatic until April 2013, when an elevation of CA 125 of 88.4 U/ml (0-35 U/ml) was detected. . The CT scan showed an image adjacent to the colon and an anterior resection of the upper rectum was performed with an intraoperative biopsy of a liver lesion that confirmed its metastatic nature.
A second line of treatment was instituted with carboplatin and paclitaxel, showing a CA 125 value of 123.3 U/ml. After a first cycle with good tolerance, we scheduled the second and during the first minutes of the carboplatin infusion, she presented a sudden episode of dyspnea, vomiting, loss of consciousness and SBP/DBP of 70/50 mmHg. The infusion was stopped and after the administration of corticosteroids the condition began to subside. The Allergology Unit recommended a desensitization scheme5 with slowing of the infusion rate. For this, three solutions were used with concentrations of 0.02, 0.2 and 2 mg/ml respectively, in addition to premedication the night before and half an hour before the infusion with:
- Cetirizine 10 mg - Prednisone 1 mg/kg - Ranitidine 150 mg - Montelukast 10 mg
Despite this, within a few minutes he began to experience dyspnea, sweating and blood pressure of 85/60 mmHg, forcing the infusion to be stopped and it being recommended not to use any platinum salt again.
In September 2013, it was decided to start the 3rd line of chemotherapy with trabectedin and pegylated liposomal adriamycin. After the third cycle, the markers normalized and the patient completed the sixth cycle, remaining disease-free at this time.
