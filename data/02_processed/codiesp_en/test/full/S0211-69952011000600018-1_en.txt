39-year-old man with no toxic habits, allergic to acetylsalicylic acid (ASA) and diclofenac. Initially consulted for soft tissue swelling associated with palpable purpura in both lower extremities in April 2001. An immunological study was performed that revealed positive cryoglobulinemia with cryocrit of 9.2% (monoclonal IgM kappa and IgG component). Serologies for hepatotropic viruses and human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) were negative, as well as antiphospholipid antibodies.
The physical examination revealed the presence of palpable purpura in the lower extremities; The rest of the exam was unremarkable. A thoracoabdominal computed tomography (CT) was performed, which showed no lymphadenopathy or visceromegaly. Based on cutaneous vasculitis, treatment with prednisone was started at a dose of 1 mg/kg/day, with a favorable initial evolution and the disappearance of the lesions.
One year after the first consultation, the patient reported paresthesia in the lower extremities, once again associated with petechiae in said location, presenting on this occasion nephritic syndrome. The analysis highlighted: hypocomplementemia, cryocrit of 17% and proteinogram with a weak abnormal band in the gamma zone. Serum immunoelectrophoresis: IgM kappa restricted mobility component (there was no element suspicious for monoclonality in the urine). Renal function showed a creatinine of 1.2 mg/dl, sediment with red blood cells +++ and proteinuria of 2.8 g/day.
The renal biopsy confirmed the presence of glomerulonephritis with a mesangiocapillary pattern. Azathioprine was added to the treatment, with maintenance of renal function and proteinuria around 1 g.

After six years of follow-up, a peripheral blood immunophenotype compatible with LLP was evident in an analytical control. This finding was later corroborated with a bone marrow aspirate in which marrow infiltration due to B-line small cell chronic lymphoproliferative syndrome was observed, compatible with quiescent PLL, so at that time it was not amenable to treatment with chemotherapy.
One year later, the patient presented persistent proteinuria of 4.7 g/24 h, so it was decided to perform a new kidney biopsy, which confirmed the presence of cryoglobulinemic glomerulonephritis and also showed lymphocytic infiltration compatible with B lymphoma. low grade. Given the kidney involvement due to the lymphoma, it was decided to start treatment with rituximab at a dose of 375 mg/m2 subcutaneous (s.c.) x 4 and a dose of plasma exchange.

Two years after having undergone anti-CD20 treatment, the patient has lymphoproliferative syndrome in remission and renal involvement remains in the form of residual proteinuria (4 g/day) with preserved renal function (Cr 0.8 mg/dl). , GFR: 100 ml/min) under treatment with double blockade of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone axis.
