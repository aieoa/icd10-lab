An 86-year-old patient was aphakic after cataract extraction in 1999, complicated by capsular rupture and vitreous leakage. Three years later, she comes to the clinic with a foreign body sensation and photophobia. She perceives light through her right eye; in her slit lamp, corneal edema is observed in the most peripheral 3 mm. Specular microscopy is performed. It was decided to perform penetrating keratoplasty due to the low density of central endothelial cells and the patient's discomfort. The patient's cornea is analyzed histologically, confirming the diagnosis of BML and showing detachment and rupture of Descemet's membrane and the endothelium in the peripheral area, with the central area being normal. At 18 months the corneal button remains transparent, without edema, achieving a corrected vision of 0.2 (due to cystic macular edema).
