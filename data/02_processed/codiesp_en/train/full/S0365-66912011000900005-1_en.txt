A full-term newborn who was sent for ophthalmological evaluation at 20 days of age due to the appearance of occasional nystagmus. Her mother, with no significant history during pregnancy, had undergone glaucoma surgery at 20 years of age with bilateral trabeculectomy and reconstructive surgery for hand syndactyly. The ophthalmological examination revealed microcorneas with a slightly blurred appearance, occasional horizontal nystagmus, increased digital ocular tone bilaterally, and fundus of the eye (FO) with papillae of preserved characteristics; and general physical examination revealed a narrow nasal pyramid with narrow nares and thin nasal wings, mild micrognathia, and syndactyly of the fourth and fifth fingers of both hands. Under sedation, tonometry revealed an intraocular pressure (IOP) of 35 and 40mm Hg in the right and left eyes, respectively, and epithelial edema and superficial corneal clouds were also verified.

A bilateral trabeculectomy was performed with good postoperative IOP control, without the need for additional topical drugs. In the last ophthalmological examination, at 14 months of age, he was in orthotropia, with good fixation and light tracking, a refractive error of - 4 diopters in both eyes, diffuse blisters and no inflammatory signs, and good control was confirmed. of the IOP. No alterations of interest were found in the FO.
