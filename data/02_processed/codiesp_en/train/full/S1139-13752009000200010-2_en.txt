A 42-year-old patient diagnosed with ulcerative pancolitis in 2003, corticosteroid dependent, with a first corticosteroid-refractory episode in August 2003 and with cytomegalovirus superinfection, which was treated with IV ganciclovir. In May 2004, he had a moderate outbreak of diarrhea with red blood (2 stools a day) and since then chronic iron deficiency anemia, which he treated with iron therapy. The endoscopic study revealed ulcerative pancolitis with severe involvement. He receives treatment with corticosteroids and immunosuppressants.
In June 2007 he had a new bout of disease activity with 10-12 liquid stools daily, abdominal pain and fever. Due to the progressive clinical and analytical worsening presented by the patient and the poor response to conventional treatment, it was decided to begin treatment with granulocytapheresis associated with IV cyclosporine.
While the patient was admitted, 4 sessions were performed, requiring the placement of a double-lumen catheter in the right jugular. The central catheter had to be changed due to MARSA infection and contact isolation was performed. The remaining 3 sessions were received on an outpatient basis. During these months, the patient has shown rapid clinical and endoscopic improvement. He is currently receiving treatment with azathioprine (Imurel®).
