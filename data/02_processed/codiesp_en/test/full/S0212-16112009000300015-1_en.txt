This was a 77-year-old man with a history of chronic atrial fibrillation who suffered massive intestinal ischemia with resection of the jejunum, ileum, ascending colon and half of the transverse colon, performing a jejunocolic anastomosis. After a two-month hospital stay he was sent to his home with NPD seven days a week, infusing over 10 hours. His usual caregiver was his 72-year-old wife, who suffers from incipient Parkinson's disease. He also had a close family very involved.
The NPD continued for 11 years, being suspended due to clinical deterioration caused by community pneumonia. The total duration was 3,940 days. During this period he had 5 catheter-associated infections (infection rate: 1.3 episodes per 1,000 days of HPN) and 1 accidental catheter exit. He needed to change the catheter on 4 occasions (catheter half-life: 788 days). Two years after the onset of DPN, he presented with mild, non-progressive hepatic steatosis. He was hospitalized four times due to complications from the technique. The functional situation at the beginning of NPD was one of active independent living and he transitioned to a sedentary but autonomous life at the end of it. During the first eight years of NPD he traveled once or twice a year to enjoy a few days of vacation on the beach.
