A 47-year-old woman with a history of glycogenosis type V (McArdle's disease), Chiari malformation, unrelated chronic renal failure, retinal atrophy and arterial hypertension.
A peritoneal catheter was implanted on 5/19/2000 in another center, and dialysis with a 2-liter infusion began 15 days later. From the beginning, the patient presented a feeling of abdominal discomfort, pleuritic pain in the right hemithorax, and dyspnea. A chest x-ray was performed, which revealed hydrothorax, which is why her PD was suspended and she was referred to our hospital.

In our center, drainage of the pleural effusion (transudate) was performed with normalization of the chest x-ray. PD was attempted again with a lower infusion volume and the pleural effusion reappeared shortly after. Subsequently, an arteriovenous fistula (AVF) puncture was attempted, which was complicated by extravasation and myositis.
The patient refused to perform hemodialysis or any other type of therapeutic option in order to resolve the pleuroperitonal communication, so automatic peritoneal dialysis (APD) with low volume and elevated head was started in September 2000, achieving good drainage and with Appearance on several occasions of small right pleural effusion.
The patient remained on APD until August 2002, at which time she underwent a kidney transplant, requiring a transplantectomy due to thrombosis. In September 2002 she was included again in the PD program in which she remained until she died at her home in October 2003.
