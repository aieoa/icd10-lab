We present the case of a 75-year-old patient with moderate Alzheimer's who presented with abdominal pain of three days' duration, with fever. On examination she presented diffuse abdominal pain and peritonism. A computed tomography (CT) scan was requested, which reported the presence of free fluid, pneumoperitoneum, with infiltration and thickening of the entire colon wall with the presence of calcified feces inside. The location of the perforation was not visualized (image). It was decided to urgently intervene on the patient, finding diffuse fecal peritonitis with stony fecalomas coming out due to perforation at the sigmoid level. A Hartmann intervention was performed, with a left hemicolectomy due to finding a new perforation at the level of the splenic flexure of the colon.
