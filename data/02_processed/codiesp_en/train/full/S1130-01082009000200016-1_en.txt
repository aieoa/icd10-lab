A 47-year-old woman, with no relevant history, presented with persistent pain in the right iliac fossa after performing an esophagogastroduodenal study with barium a year earlier due to gastroesophageal reflux symptoms. The analysis is strictly normal. She provides a colonoscopy that reaches the terminal ileum, with normal mucosa and caliber; An abdominal x-ray and ultrasound performed nine months after the barium study revealed the presence of barium inside the appendix, with no evidence of acute appendicitis. Due to the persistence of the symptoms, an exploratory laparoscopy was performed where, as the only finding, an appendix of normal size and slightly congested on its surface was found, so an appendectomy was performed. The patient progressed satisfactorily with the symptoms disappearing. The pathological study showed an appendix six centimeters long with a congestive surface and a hyperdense material inside (barium). Under the microscope, lymphoid hyperplasia of the submucosa with fibrosis of the mucosa and submucosa was observed, all compatible with chronic fibrous appendicitis.
