An 80-year-old woman comes to the hospital with diplopia of 7 days' duration. The diagnosis was complete paralysis of the right third cranial nerve with pupillary involvement. She did not report headache, jaw claudication, or stiffness in the neck and joints. Her personal history was only significant for high blood pressure. Her ophthalmological history was unremarkable. Ocular motility examination in the left eye (LE) was normal. The right eye (OD) had eyelid ptosis and limited mobility in all gaze positions except abduction. The pupillary light reaction in the LE was 3 mm to 2 mm, the pupillary response in the RE was slow from 5 mm to 4 mm. There was no afferent pupillary defect. Slit lamp examination was characteristic of bilateral cataract. The fundus was normal. A cerebral magnetic resonance angiography did not detect pathology. In the laboratory study, the erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) was 28 mm/h, C-reactive protein was 0.2 mg/dl (normal, <0.8 mg/dl) and platelets were 175,000/ml3. . Rheumatoid factor, antinuclear antibodies, antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies, antiacetylcholine receptor antibodies, and luetic serology were negative. The diagnosis of AT was considered and a biopsy of the temporal artery was performed. The pathological anatomy revealed stenosis of the arterial lumen with rupture of the internal limiting and inflammatory and giant cells.
