26-year-old male professional athlete (soccer player) with no other personal history of interest for the case at hand. He suffers a fall during sports practice from his own height, resting his left wrist and causing it to hyperextend. As a result of the trauma, he presents pain, deformity and functional impotence, with a normal distal neurovascular examination.
Anteroposterior and lateral radiographs of the wrist show a fracture of the radial styloid process, a fracture of the lunate bone, and a dislocation through the fracture line of the lunate. The dorsal fragment of the lunate is attached to the carpal bones and displaced dorsally, while the volar fragment is in its anatomical position. Given this image we diagnosed transstyloidradial-transelunar fracture-dislocation.

We urgently proceeded to closed reduction of the dislocation using traction and immobilization, checking the instability of the injury. We also performed post-reduction computed tomography, observing that the fracture line of the lunate bone was coronal-oblique and comminuted.

Seventy-two hours after the trauma, and after performing a supraclavicular block, the patient underwent surgery using a dorsal approach with opening of the dorsal retinaculum at the level of the third slide and capsulotomy according to the Berger technique (8). Afterwards, we proceeded to reduce the lunate fracture, stabilizing it with a 2 mm diameter miniscrew, and repair the scapholunate ligament with a harpoon in the lunate. Total limb ischemia time was 74 minutes.

After 6 weeks of immobilization with a cast, the patient was referred to rehabilitation until the condition was stabilized with a functional limitation: flexion 35o, extension 25o, complete prono-supination, and without radiocarpal or midcarpal instability, which allowed him to return to his normal position. sports activity prior to 5 months after the trauma. In the one-year review, the process is stabilized, without worsening.
