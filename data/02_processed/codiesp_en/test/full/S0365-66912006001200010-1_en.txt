A 56-year-old man with a history of laryngeal neoplasia, type 2 diabetes mellitus, ex-smoker and ex-drinker, diagnosed with hyperthyroid crisis. At that time he had an exophthalmos of 23 and 25 mm without limitation of eye movements, visual acuity of 0.7 with its correction in both eyes (AO), fundus of the eye with mild diabetic retinopathy and thickening of the internal rectus muscles in orbital CT scan.
One month later he presented retraction of both eyelids with limitation of superior and lateral motility in AO. Eye pressure (OT) was 26 mmHg in the right eye and 25 mmHg in the left eye. Treatment was initiated with topical beta-blocker and systemic corticosteroid treatment with 1 g/24 h of iv methylprednisolone in 3 pulses.
Exophthalmos and OT increased, so topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitors were added, and the patient was referred for orbital radiotherapy with 2,000 Gy.
The evolution was unfavorable; proptosis increased to 27 and 30 mm and CT confirmed increased thickening of the internal rectus muscles. Ophthalmoplegia occurred in AO and corneal exposure in the left eye, so bilateral orbital decompression was performed with fracture of the inferior, internal and external walls. Hormonal control revealed a severe hypothyroid crisis (TSH 44.5 uUI/mL (0.5-5.0), T3 42 ng/dL (60-180), Total T4 02.4 ng/dL (5.0- 12.0), Free T4 0.3 ng/dL (0.7-1.8). The exophthalmos continued to worsen, for which a lipectomy was performed in both orbits (1 cc of fat), showing muscles and tissues infiltrated orbitals, enucleation of the left eye due to absolute glaucoma, and external and internal tarsorrhaphy in the right eye. Subsequently, thyroid hormones normalized (TSH 14.3, Total T4 5.9 and Free T4 1.1), proptosis decreased manifested form and ocular motility increased, opening the tarsorrhaphies. The RE examination showed VA of 0.1 as a consequence of severe optic neuropathy, OT of 18 mmHg, and mild-moderate diabetic retinopathy. The patient remains stable after 9 years of evolution.
