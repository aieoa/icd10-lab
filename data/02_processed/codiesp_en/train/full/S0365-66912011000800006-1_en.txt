An 83-year-old man with advanced Alzheimer's disease, an insulin-dependent diabetic of 35 years' duration, who came to our emergency department due to ocular pain in the right eye for 2 days.
VA could not be objectiveized due to lack of collaboration. The evaluation of the anterior segment determined a corneal ulcerative lesion of 2.5 mm in diameter with a central perforating endothelial pore of approximately 1 mm, without a concomitant infectious or inflammatory process. The patient had no history of trauma.
With the diagnosis of sterile non-traumatic corneal perforation to be confirmed, the lesion was urgently closed.
Under topical anesthesia and in the operating room to facilitate collaboration, a Tachosil® patch was prepared and cut in such a way that it exceeded the edges of the ulceration, for complete coverage. After hydration with saline solution for 5 seconds, the active part (yellow part) was immediately applied to the corneal defect, gently pressing and molding the sponge for 3 minutes with a tweezer, until we ensured that it was firmly attached to the lesion.

Without removing the excess material, the eye was occluded. Upon uncovering, after about 36 hours of occlusion, it was observed that there were no remains of the sponge in the application area and there was a fibrinoid material that occluded the perforating endothelial pore. No displaced sponge remains were found in the conjunctival fornices. A therapeutic lens was applied for safety and topical antibiotic, cyclopegic, and medroxyprogesterone treatment was started. At 72 hours after application, corneal thinning persisted over the perforation area, with total closure and good anterior chamber depth. Subsequent controls after a week and a month confirmed total corneal closure, allowing the therapeutic lens to be removed and the medical treatment described.
