32 year old woman. She reports habitual constipation, which has increased during the last season. The physical examination is unremarkable except for the rectal examination, in which a rounded, soft, and painless protrusion is palpated with the fingertip on the right lateral aspect of the rectal wall. An abdominal ultrasound was requested, revealing a mass with a cystic appearance and doubtful etiology located in the right pararectal, measuring approximately 34 mm in longitudinal diameter. An MRI of the lower pelvis is requested to identify the cystic lesion found on the ultrasound, which is reported as a rounded formation (4.8 x 2.7 cm), well delimited, with a thin capsule and contents that show an intensity of homogeneous signal (faintly hypointense on T2 and with a greater signal on STIR) in the right pararectal space and producing compression on it, which could be a congenital anomaly. The patient is referred to the General Surgery service for scheduled surgical treatment with suspected retrorectal cystic hamartoma (RCH). A cystic-looking tumor with thin walls and several loculations is removed that are inserted between the muscle fibers of the levator puborectalis muscle to which it is attached. In the Pathological Anatomy service, two irregular fragments of tissue with a membranous appearance, brownish in color, are received, which together measure 3.5 x 3 x 1 cm. Microscopically, it is a cystic lesion covered mainly by mature squamous epithelium, focally by transitional and glandular epithelium, in whose wall there are disorganized fascicles of smooth muscle and acini of mucous glands. The definitive diagnosis is HQR.
