The patient is a 38-year-old man who attended the Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery Service of the Vall d'Hebron University Hospital, referred from another center, due to a mandibular tumor of 1 year's evolution.

The pathological history includes being formerly addicted to parenteral drugs, being HCV positive and HIV positive on antiretroviral treatment. Upon examination, a right mandibular tumor of approximately 5 cm in size, fixed and hard in consistency, maintained the integrity of the oral mucosa. The orthopantomography showed a mixed lesion with calcifications inside that affected the right hemimandible.

The CT showed a mixed expansive lesion in the body of the right mandible that destroyed the cortices measuring 4.5x3x3.3 cm.

The biopsy performed reported a calcifying epithelial odontogenic tumor.
The patient underwent a right hemimandibulectomy with microsurgical fibula reconstruction.

The pathological anatomy revealed a 4 x 2.5 cm mass with an irregular surface with a heterogeneous appearance, congestive areas and remains of teeth, which confirmed the diagnosis of calcifying epithelial odontogenic tumor of the jaw and infiltrating periosteal soft tissues. The mental nerve did not show invasion and the intraoperative biopsy of the left facial chain lymph node did not show metastasis.

The postoperative evolution was favorable and he was discharged tolerating diet and with monitoring of the wounds in outpatient clinics.
