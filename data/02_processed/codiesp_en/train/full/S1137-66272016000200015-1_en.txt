A 46-year-old man with no relevant history presented in a routine work checkup with recent-onset iron deficiency anemia (Hb 11.3g/dL; MCV 79.8 µ3; Urea 29 mg/dL; Creatine 0.95 mg/dL; Iron 29 µg/dL, Ferritin 12 µg/L). The patient denied ingestion of gastro-injurious drugs and digestive externalization of the bleeding, so a conventional endoscopic examination (gastroscopy and ileo-colonoscopy) was indicated, which was negative. After 30 days, the study was completed with an ECE that was also negative, ending the study and indicating symptomatic treatment with oral iron supplements. After 3 months, the patient went to the Emergency Department due to asthenia and melena in the last 48 hours. On physical examination he was pale and sweaty. He had a blood pressure of 90/60 mm Hg and a heart rate of 105 beats per minute, and the presence of melenic stools was observed during the rectal examination. The analysis at the Emergency Service (Hb 7 g/dL; MCV 77.2 µ3; Urea 57 mg/dL; Creatine 0.74 mg/dL) confirmed the suspicion of digestive bleeding, and it was decided to admit him for observation. In the first 24 hours, 2,000 ml of fluid therapy were administered and 4 concentrated red blood cells were transfused, with post-transfusion hemoglobin being 9.5 g/dL. Once the patient was stabilized and there was suspicion of upper gastrointestinal bleeding, an urgent gastroscopy was performed, which was negative, and a new ECE was repeated at that time. In this last examination, the origin of the bleeding was identified from a gastric lesion of submucosal origin and ulcerated on its surface that was hidden between the gastric folds. The definitive diagnosis was obtained after a new gastroscopy and after ruling out metastatic disease, it was resected. The histopathological study confirmed the submucosal origin of the lesion (gastric GIST).
