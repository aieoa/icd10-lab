39-year-old HIV positive patient, category C3, who consulted in the emergency room of our hospital due to abdominal pain. He had bilateral cervical lymphadenopathy due to Mycobacterium infection, for which he was undergoing treatment. The abdominal pain had begun eight hours earlier in the epigastrium and was later located in the right iliac fossa. Physical examination revealed slight abdominal distension with signs of peritoneal irritation. The only alteration found in the blood test was a mild leukocytosis (11.70 x 103/mL) with neutrophilia (78%). Bilirubin, amylase, and liver enzymes were normal. Abdominal ultrasound revealed the presence of hepatomegaly, splenomegaly, and peripancreatic, para-aortic, and celiac lymphadenopathy. The appendix and free peritoneal fluid were not visualized. The patient underwent surgery with a clinical diagnosis of acute appendicitis. During surgical exploration, the appendix was macroscopically normal, no lymphadenopathy was observed, and an appendectomy was performed. The immediate postoperative period was uneventful and the patient was discharged 48 hours later. The histopathological study of the specimen demonstrated the presence of leishmanias in the appendix and in two lymph nodes of the mesoappendix. With this result, medical treatment for Leishmaniasis was initiated.
