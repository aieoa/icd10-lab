A 50-year-old man weighing 79 kg with HCV cirrhosis (genotype 1b/fibroscan 22.6 KPa) diagnosed 20 years ago, with splenomegaly, portal hypertension and leukothrombocytopenia. History of parenteral drug addiction without current methadone replacement treatment. In 2009 treatment with interferon not effective. Rest of medical and family history without relevance.
In July 2015 he began treatment with Viekirax®, Exviera® and Rebetol®, 1,200 mg per day for 12 weeks, without any concomitant treatment. Two weeks later he went to the doctor reporting good tolerance. At the fourth week the viral load is already negative but the patient reports skin lesions, change in character, aggressiveness and thoughts of suicide, so both he and the family request to suspend treatment and he is referred to Psychiatry.
In December 2015, the patient came to the consultation with a Psychiatry report indicating that he had not been prescribed any psychotropic drugs and that at that time there was no contraindication to starting another treatment for HCV. In February 2016, treatment was started with ledipasvir 90 mg/sofosbuvir 400 mg (Harvoni®) and Rebetol® 1,200 mg for 12 weeks. At the fourth week, the patient reports good tolerance and the viral load is already negative. At the twelfth week the patient successfully completes the treatment.
