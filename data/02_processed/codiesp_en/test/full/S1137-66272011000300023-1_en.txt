The case of a 27-year-old woman of Chinese nationality is presented, who did not understand Spanish, had no known drug allergies, nor any relevant medical-surgical history, and was correctly vaccinated during childhood in her country of origin. The patient went to the emergency room of the health center for the first time due to a pruritic urticarial reaction that had been going on for twenty-four hours. In this first consultation, she was prescribed an intramuscular antihistamine and a delayed-release corticosteroid, and she was diagnosed with urticaria.
A day later the patient went to the emergency room again due to increased lesions, reporting intense itching on the face, scalp, trunk, upper extremities and genitals. Several polymorphic lesions with a widespread dispersed distribution are observed, with crusted hemorrhagic lesions and other vesicular-pustular lesions measuring two to five millimeters in diameter, some of them umbilicated in the central area. In the central-abdominal region she presents a crusty necrotic lesion that the patient reports having manipulated. After examination, she was diagnosed with chickenpox. Complications were ruled out by auscultation and a chest x-ray was requested, which was completely normal, so after taking smears of the lesions, she was sent to her home, explaining the respiratory isolation measures that should be taken with her non-immunized cohabitants. . She was prescribed oral acyclovir 800 mg every four hours, five times a day, with a night's rest for seven days and antihistamines.
Seven days later, our patient went to the Emergency Department of our reference hospital due to intense asthenia of 24 hours of evolution without other symptoms. The examination was completely normal, so in the absence of both analytical and radiological alterations, it was decided to discharge him home.
Twelve hours later, she went to the emergency room again due to suprapubic pain and asthenia. In the anamnesis she reported dysuria of two days' duration as well as oligoanuria, without concomitant febrile process. Cardiopulmonary auscultation was completely normal. Abdominal examination revealed suprapubic pain, palpable bladder globule with positive succussion in both kidney fossae. Given the suspicion of acute urinary retention, a bladder catheterization was performed, verifying an emptying of 1,200 cc. Blood and urine tests were performed with results within normal parameters. The abdominal x-ray performed revealed the presence of abundant retained fecal remains.
During her reevaluation in the observation room, the companion-translator reported a clear improvement in abdominal pain and stated that the patient had been suffering from loss of strength and progressive weakness in the lower extremities for four days. She was reevaluated, highlighting marked cervical rigidity, normal cranial nerves, and 5/5 distal and proximal motor balance in the upper extremities. In the lower extremities the appearance of a flaccid para-paresis with motor balance 3/5 proximal and 4/5 distal. She tendon reflexes with bilateral patellar hyporeflexia and abolition of Achilles reflexes. Plantar cutaneous reflex in bilateral flexion. Hypo-sensitivity in metameres below D5 (breast).
Given the presence of neurological alterations, a craniocerebral computed axial tomography (CT) scan was indicated, where no focal intracranial lesions were observed and the cerebrospinal fluid spaces, as well as the differentiation of gray matter and white matter, were normal.
Subsequently, a lumbar puncture was performed where clear fluid was observed and samples were taken for laboratory study, and he was admitted to the Neurology service with the diagnosis of suspected post-infectious incomplete transverse myelitis.
During his admission, a spinal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was performed, where an area of ​​hypersignal in T2 was observed located at the level of the cervical spinal cord (C5-D1) with a small dilation of the ependymal duct, most likely related to infectious myelopathy. . The lesion showed mild hypointensity on T1. All of this confirmed the diagnosis of infectious myelitis in the cervical spinal cord.

CSF serology results for Borrelia, Brucella, syphilis, Epstein Barr virus (EBV), Cytomegalovirus (CMV), Herpes simplex virus (HSV), and Varicella zoster virus (VZV) were negative. The results of serum serology were consistent with those of CSF, except for Varicella zoster virus, which was positive. Human Immunodeficiency Virus serology was also requested with a negative result. Therefore, his high probability etiological diagnosis was acute myelitis due to Varicella zoster virus (VZV) infection.
Since his admission due to the high suspicion of acute myelitis due to VZV, treatment with i.v. acyclovir was started. during the first ten days associating i.v. corticosteroid.
The patient experienced a slow but constant improvement; finally, after a month of admission, the catheter was removed without presenting any episode of acute urinary retention in the following days. Upon discharge she continued rehabilitative treatment.
