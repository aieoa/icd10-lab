We present a 63-year-old patient with a history of arterial hypertension and angor pectoris, referred to our service from a hospital in a rural area. The reason for the consultation was the accidental discovery in an ultrasound of a left renal mass, and the patient did not report any symptoms. The blood test only showed a slight eosinophilia. As complementary studies, intravenous urograms were performed in which a mass effect was observed at the level of the left flank, with a smooth and well-defined edge, dependent on the upper pole of the left kidney, with extrinsic compression of the pyelocalyceal system, measuring 16 x 10 cm. The CT scan revealed a mass measuring 17 x 12 x 19 cm in the upper pole of the left kidney, with cystic areas, compatible with multilocular cystic nephroma, without lymphadenopathy. The DMSA scan showed a differential renal function of 63% for the right kidney and 37% for the left.

A new ultrasound was performed showing an image compatible with a large hydatid cyst, with structures compatible with vesicles inside. The hydatid serology was negative, so it was decided to submit the patient to an MRI, which also did not definitively clarify the diagnosis: mass in the upper pole of the left kidney with a complex, well-defined internal structure, without contrast enhancement, with calcifications. , suspected of renal hydatid cyst or multilocular cystic nephroma.
In view of the previously presented results, the next step was ultrasound-guided puncture of the renal mass, the serology for hydatidosis being again negative, although a crystalline-appearing liquid was obtained.
Finally, the patient underwent surgery in which the diagnosis of renal hydatid cyst was confirmed and a partial cystectomy was performed, preparing the operating field using compresses dipped in rivanol.

The postoperative evolution was favorable, and treatment with albendazole 400 mg every 12 hours was instituted for one month after surgery.
At present, the patient remains asymptomatic (the arterial hypertension that she already had before the diagnosis remains) and the control serology was again negative, with minimal eosinophilia persisting.
