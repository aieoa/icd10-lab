A 67-year-old male patient, with no relevant history, who attended a urology consultation for the first time, referred by his primary care physician, due to symptoms of prostatism. On physical examination, a prostate of adenomatous consistency and increased size was found (III/IV). In the transrectal ultrasound, a heterogeneous parenchyma was observed, with isolated calcifications in the right lobe, without hypoechoic images and with a prostate volume of 101.7 c.c. In the analysis, elevation of PSA (13.08 ng/ml.). A transrectal prostate biopsy is indicated.
The result of the pathological anatomy was reported as prostatic blue nevus. A prostate parenchyma is described with chronic inflammation and a foreign body-type granulomatous reaction, with the presence of intracytoplasmic granules and spindle-shaped dendritic melanocytes. There is no evidence of malignancy in any of the samples.
