This is a 29-year-old woman undergoing a control pelvic ultrasound study after laparoscopic tubal ligation. During the study, a 20 mm tumor was detected on the right lateral aspect of the bladder, well-defined and hypoechoic. The patient did not present voiding symptoms, as she reported in the subsequent interrogation.

She underwent an intravenous urography, in which no alteration of the upper urinary system was detected. The cystogram revealed a rounded filling defect with a smooth surface, located in the right bladder wall. Blood and urine tests were within normal limits. A cystoscopy was performed on the patient, which revealed the presence of a "hump"-like tumor, with a smooth surface and preserved mucosa, on the right lateral aspect of the bladder, immediately above and in front of the ipsilateral ureteral meatus.

With the presumptive diagnosis of bladder leiomyoma, transurethral resection of the tumor was performed. The resected fragments had a whitish, solid and compact appearance, similar to those of a prostatic adenoma, with little bleeding.
The material obtained from the transurethral resection was formed by a proliferation of spindle cells with elongated cytoplasm, like the nucleus, and slightly eosinophilic. No mitosis or atypia were observed. The immunohistochemical study demonstrated positivity for muscle-specific actin (DAKO, clone HHF35) in the proliferating cells.

Three months after the transurethral resection, a control cystoscopy was performed, observing a raised calcareous plaque over the area of ​​previous resection, compatible with encrusting cystopathy that was treated by transurethral resection of this and leiomyomatous remains and subsequent urinary acidification.
