38-year-old patient, with no relevant history, diagnosed with depressive syndrome and treated with venlafaxine (75 mg - 150 mg - 150 mg p.o.) and alprazolam (1-2 mg p.o. /8 h). He goes to the Primary Care doctor after an episode of oppressive chest pain, not radiating and without vegetative cortex, which fluctuates in intensity during the day but does not subside despite rest. An electrocardiogram (ECG) was performed, showing sinus tachycardia around 120 bpm with alterations in repolarization.
Transferred to the hospital, he was admitted to the Intensive Care Unit, where a new ECG confirmed persistent sinus tachycardia with very diffuse alterations in repolarization, as well as negative T waves on the anterior side and a prolongation of the QT space around 500 ms. It was diagnosed as an Acute Coronary Syndrome and conventional treatment was started with double antiplatelet therapy (acetylsalicylic acid 100 mg PO/24 h and clopidogrel 75 mg PO/24 h), anticoagulation (enoxaparin 80 mg SC/24 h) and intravenous infusion of nitroglycerin at 10 ml/hour. As sinus tachycardia continues, treatment is started with low doses of beta-blockers (atenolol 25 mg PO/12 h).

The absence of alterations in cardiac markers was the reason for a new ECG that indicated regional alterations in contractility. A coronary angiographic study showed a normal left ventricle with an ejection fraction of 65% and a normal segmental analysis, showing epicardial coronary arteries without significant angiographic stenosis, ruling out the existence of active coronary disease.
Suspecting that cardiovascular effects may be produced by venlafaxine, the drug is withdrawn and treatment with sertraline 50 mg PO/day is instituted, decreasing the dose of nitroglycerin to 5 ml/h until its subsequent suspension. The patient progressed adequately and continued treatment with sertraline and atenolol, shortening the QT interval and maintaining heart rates of around 80 bpm, with the ECG evolving towards normalization of repolarization.
