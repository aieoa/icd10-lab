A 72-year-old man presented to the Ophthalmology service due to loss of vision in the left eye (LE) that had been developing for one month. As background, the patient had been treated for prostate carcinoma 10 years ago with radiotherapy without relapse at the time of the study. Visual acuity in the right eye (RE) was 5/10 and in LE there was no light perception. The intraocular pressure was 18 mmHg in the RE and 12 mmHg in the LE. At slit lamp he showed a ++ nuclear cataract in both eyes. There were no alterations in the fundus of the eye in the RE, but in the LE it was not visible due to a hemovitreal. The ultrasound showed a lobulated lesion that occupied 4/5 of the vitreous chamber. The hypothesis of MC or metastatic lesion was raised, so a scan was performed in search of a primary lesion. Thoraco-abdominal CT and liver tests were normal. Given the suspicion of a malignant lesion, a PET/CT was performed, which was positive at the ocular level (SUVmax: 7.2), liver (SUVmax: 5.7) and right pulmonary hilum (SUVmax: 3.5). After the PET/CT, we reviewed the abdominal CT, without identifying liver lesions, only a doubtful nodular alteration of the density that was poorly defined and difficult to interpret due to its small size, not accessible to biopsy using ultrasound or CT. A liver magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was considered, but was ruled out due to the small size of the lesion. Enucleation was decided due to the need for pathological anatomy to confirm the diagnosis prior to chemotherapy. The severity of the pathology, type of surgery and no possibility of visual recovery in the affected eye were reported. The pathological anatomy showed a large MC (17*17 mm) of spindle cells and in the immunohistochemical study intense cytoplasmic positivity for MELAM-A and HMB45.

The patient, who at the end of the diagnostic process had an ECOG-1 (quality of life according to the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group scale) due to vision loss, began palliative treatment with dacarbazine at a dose of 850 mg/m2 every 3 weeks in April 2011, with excellent tolerance. In June 2011, he was re-evaluated, presenting radiological liver progression, not detected on CT, but detected on PET/CT.
In June 2011, he began 2nd line treatment with ipilimumab (3 mg/kg every 3 weeks-4 doses), with conservation of general status (ECOG1), completing the treatment without side effects. Hepatic progression was again detected (in weeks 12 and 16 of treatment, as stipulated in the ipilimumab evaluation protocol) identified on CT and PET/CT.
In November 2011, he started the 3rd line of treatment with fotemustine at 75 mg/m2 with induction dose, due to conservation of general condition, without completing the planned treatment until the planned radiological evaluation, due to general deterioration. He died in February 2012 (11 months after diagnosis).
