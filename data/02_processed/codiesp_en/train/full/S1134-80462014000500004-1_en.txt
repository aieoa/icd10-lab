A 27-year-old woman, who has been monitored in our Unit since February 2004, when she came at the age of 18 sent by her traumatologist.
Background
- Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva. Diagnosis at 6 years of age. Fusion at the level of the vertebral bodies of the cervical spine, multiple ossifications at the level of the cervical, dorsal, lumbar paravertebral musculature, bilateral shoulder girdle, coxofemoral joints, thighs, knees and abdomen, which cause intense pain and inability to ambulate, requiring a wheelchair. for travel.

- Surgical intervention of exostoses at the level of the right knee, and in the first metatarsophalangeal joint bilaterally.
- Bilateral otosclerosis operated on at 6 years of age. Hearing aid wearer.
- Iron deficiency anemia probably secondary to chronic gastric bleeding due to treatment with NSAIDs and corticosteroids. In treatment with oral iron.
- Obesity, cushingoid appearance.
- Intolerance to metamizole.
- Reactive anxious-depressive syndrome.
-Episodes of hypercapnic respiratory failure secondary to restrictive ventilatory disorder due to the underlying disease, which have occasionally required treatment with home NIV since 2004.
The latest complementary explorations carried out have been:
- Thoracic CT (February 2010): gross calcifications in soft tissue tissues of the thoracic cavity in relation to the patient's underlying pathology. Cardiomegaly.
-Abdominal CT (April 2010): multiple muscle calcifications are seen in the pectoral, periscapular, dorsal, paravertebral territory and mainly in the left gluteal territory, suggesting that they correspond to her underlying disease.
- PFR (March 2011) FVC 1.57 (40%); FEV1 1.36 (42%); FEV1 86%, ABG: (March 2011) P02 70, PC02 55.
Sociofunctional situation: she lives with her parents. Business administration student. She moves around in a wheelchair.
On her first visit, she came on a slow-release oral morphine regimen, plus a permanent prednisone regimen. Previously, various regimens of NSAIDs and paracetamol had been established that were progressively ineffective, later alternating different types of opioids and different forms of presentation. Since February 2004, she has been periodically monitored, both for her basal pain caused by the multiple heterotopic ossifications she presents, and also and fundamentally, for her painful thrusts secondary to inflammatory conditions in soft tissues that give rise to new bone formations, thrusts caused by trauma. mild, abnormal postures, viral symptoms, etc.
She progressively withdrew her corticosteroids, leaving them confined only to the painful episodes due to neo-ossification, largely improving the cushingoid elements that she presented. Baseline control with transdermal fentanyl plus sublingual fentanyl for breakthrough pain. Lately, with transdermal fentanyl 100 mcg/h every 3 days, the pain was controlled.
A few weeks ago she presented her most severe bout of pain since we assisted her: due to a change of address, the 2nd day of family vacation, and with no other apparent cause, severe pain began at the level of the left scapula, without fever and without any parallel respiratory symptoms. . She went to the nearest hospital where they studied her, not finding any respiratory or diffuse infectious component, attributing the condition to her underlying illness. She is medicated with NSAIDs, pyrazolones and morphine, all via IV. After 24 hours, as they see no improvement, they refer her to our Center. Upon arrival, 100 mm EVA. The X-ray shows bone neoformation in the form of a bridge between the left scapula and rib cage, as well as accentuation of right paracervical bone neoformation. We started a regimen of IV dexamethasone 4 mg every 6 hours + IV morphine 5 mg every 6 hours + desketoprofen 50 mg IV every 8 hours + oral diazepam 10 mg hour 22. Coverage with omeprazole, 40 mg/day. After 24 hours it remains practically the same, so we increased the dexamethasone dosage to 12 mg every 6 hours. After 24 hours there was improvement, VAS of 40 mm, we increased the dosage of dexamethasone in the morning to 20 mg, leaving the other 3 administrations at 12 mg. Furthermore, as some dysesthesias and paresthesias occur when mobilizing the painful region, we added oral pregabalin: 75 mg-5 mg-150 mg. Progressive improvement. After 3 days of this last regimen, EVA 0 mm. We began decreasing the dosage of dexamethasone, switching it to oral administration, and morphine. Five days later discharged home, with oral regimen of paracetamol 1 gram every 8 hours + naproxen 500 mg 1-0-0 + pregabalin 75 1-0-2 + ​​diazepam 10 mg 0-0-1 + montelukast 5 mg 0- 0-1. Six days after starting corticosteroids, the blood tests were normal.

Since discharge we carried out telephone follow-up, showing good progress. Without pain. He has resumed his university studies remotely.
