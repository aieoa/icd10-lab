This is a 43-year-old woman, originally from Bolivia, who went to the Emergency Service of the Hospital of Navarra after presenting at home with a generalized seizure, witnessed by family members, with a subsequent period of confusion and an intense bilateral headache. Before the presentation an hour before, she began to have paresthesias in her right hand. She had no family history or personal history of interest.
On physical examination, she had normal pressure, rate, and temperature constants and a Glasgow score of 15. She did not present neck stiffness or meningeal signs. She did not find neurological focality. The patient was sweaty, with an associated anxiety disorder, which was successfully resolved with 0.25 mg of alprazolam. The rest of the examination was normal and she did not present a new seizure.
In the Emergency Department, an ECG and chest X-ray were performed, both normal, and analysis with biochemistry, blood count, and coagulation tests, also within normal parameters. An urgent cranial CT scan was performed, which revealed a cystic lesion measuring 3 cm in diameter in the left fronto-parietal region, which did not capture contrast, with nodular calcifications on its periphery. It was decided to admit him to neurosurgery with a suspected diagnosis of NCC.

During his admission, the study was completed with an electroencephalogram (EEG), which was normal, a cranial magnetic resonance imaging, which confirmed the existence of cysticerci in the vesicular phase, and an x-ray of the thigh, with several small calcifications and morphology compatible with cysticerci. Treatment was started with dexamethasone, albendazole and oxcarbazepine, with good tolerance, and he was discharged with the diagnosis of left rolandic epilepsy secondary to systemic cysticercosis and he completed outpatient treatment for one month with albendazole, maintaining antiepileptic treatment indefinitely. without presenting new crises.
