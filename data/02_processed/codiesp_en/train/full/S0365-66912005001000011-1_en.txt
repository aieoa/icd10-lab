Triamcinolone-assisted PPV was performed on a patient who had dislocation of a quadrant of the lens into the vitreous cavity with secondary macular edema in her left eye (LE). After phacofragmentation, when peeling the internal limiting membrane, marked macular edema was observed. Due to this edema, 5 mg/0.1 ml of triamcinolone acetonide (Trigon depot® Bristol-Myers Squibb SL, Anagni Frosinone, Italy) was injected at the end of the intervention.
After a good immediate postoperative evolution, she attended the clinic four days later with painless loss of visual acuity (VA) and a sensation of a foreign body in the LE. The VA was less than 0.05, presenting a 3.4 mm hypopyon formed by the deposit of fine crystalline opacities without inflammatory signs, intraocular pressure of 10 mmHg and a well-positioned anterior chamber lens. Marked vitreous turbidity was observed in the fundus.
The clinical picture presented by the patient suggested infectious, toxic endophthalmitis or pseudoendophthalmitis. Given the early onset, the absence of inflammatory signs, except for the large pseudohypopion, its crystalline nature, and that the patient had an anterior chamber lens that facilitated the diffusion of triamcinolone, the condition was oriented towards pseudoendophthalmitis and it was decided monitor it closely with ciprofloxacin eye drops every 2 hours (Oftacilox®, Alcon Cusi, El Masnou, Barcelona, ​​Spain) and Poly-pred eye drops® every 8 hours (Allergan, Wesport, Co. Mayo, Ireland). Four days later, she was asymptomatic, presenting bullae in the corneal epithelium and a 4.3 mm hypopyon. As no improvement in the corneal epithelium was observed, surgical cleaning of the anterior chamber with serum was performed after seven days. Three months later she was asymptomatic, her best corrected visual acuity was 0.3, and her cornea was transparent with no traces of triamcinolone in the anterior chamber.
