A 37-year-old woman diagnosed with LAM in 2003 as a result of spontaneous right hemopneumothorax that required surgery with evacuation of the hemothorax and resection of bullous dystrophy. She underwent outpatient follow-up without incident until 2009 when she presented chylous ascites and a large retro-peritoneal cystic lymphangioma was detected in the abdominal computed axial tomography (CT).
In February 2011 she was admitted due to dyspnea on exertion and extensive right pleural effusion. The pleural fluid showed characteristics of chylothorax: pH 7.43; triglycerides 1,216 mg/dl; cholesterol 73 mg/dl, leukocytes 2,700 cells/μl (mononuclear 92%), proteins 4.5 g/dl, LDH 142 U/l. The patient weighed 57 kg, height 169 cm, BMI: 19.2 and she had normal blood protein and lymphocyte values.
Initially, conservative treatment was performed with evacuating thoracentesis, a fat-free diet, and oral nutritional supplements (ONS) rich in medium chain triglycerides (MCT). After a week, the patient had respiratory worsening, so a right chest drainage tube was placed, obtaining 2,000-4,000 ml/day of pleural fluid. Given the amount of pleural discharge, parenteral nutrition (PN) was started and treatment with octeotride was initiated in an increasing dose up to 100 μg/8 h, which was suspended after a few days due to digestive intolerance and severe hydroelectrolyte alteration.
Despite digestive rest, the pleural effusion increased, becoming bilateral and requiring bilateral chest drainage. Three talc pleurodeses were performed, which were partially effective, not allowing removal of the drain.
Despite the treatment instituted, the patient presented a deterioration in her nutritional status with a weight loss of 5 kg, hypoalbuminemia of 1.8 g/dl and lymphopenia of 700 cells/μl.
During treatment with PN, she developed a catheter-associated infection that required removal of the central line and its suspension. The fat-free oral diet with MCT-rich OS was progressively reintroduced. The output of both chest drains progressively decreased and after two and a half months of admission, the patient was discharged from the hospital carrying the two chest drainage tubes and having evacuations every 3 days with an output of 500 ml/day, with an albumin. 2 g/dl and lymphocytes 700 cells/μl.
The subsequent evolution was good and the pleural drains were able to be removed 4 and 6 months after discharge with a progressive improvement in the patient's nutritional status. Currently, minimal bilateral pleural effusion persists, predominantly on the right, which has not changed in the last 4 months. The patient does not have dyspnea at rest, although she requires home oxygen therapy and has a BMI of 18.7, with normal nutritional parameters.
