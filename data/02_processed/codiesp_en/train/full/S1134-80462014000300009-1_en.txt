42-year-old patient referred to the Colombian Institute of Pain (INCODOL) with 11 months of neuropathic pain rated on the visual analog scale (VAS) 10/10 and allodynia in the left groin, after an ipsilateral inguinal herniorrhaphy, irradiation to the testicle and thigh , managed with acetaminophen. Stepped and multimodal pharmacological treatment was initiated with antiepileptics, opioids, lidocaine patches, tricyclic and dual antidepressants, in appropriate doses and schedules, without improvement of at least 50% at two months of follow-up, deciding to perform an ultrasound-guided block. the left ilioinguinal, iliohypogastric and genitofemoral nerves with local anesthetic associated with steroid. Four months later, the pain was rated 8/10, with exacerbation on movement, hypersensitivity in the left inguinoscrotal region, positive cremasteric reflex, negative percussion on the anterior superior iliac spine, and a new block was performed, with which the patient reported an improvement of more than 50%. Given this evolution, it was decided to perform pulsed radiofrequency with a cannula with a 10 mm active tip on the left ilioinguinal, iliohypogastric and genitofemoral nerves, after a positive sensory test at 0.2 mV, with the following parameters: 2 Hz, active phase of 20 milliseconds , temperature control < 42oC, for 10 minutes, also applying local anesthetic and steroid to each nerve. After a week he rates the pain 2/10; per month he is rated 1/10, without adverse effects, returning to his work and family activity. Sustained improvement in controls at 6 and 9 months (VAS 2-3/10).
