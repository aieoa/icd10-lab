A 46-year-old male, with no relevant medical history except for a vasectomy at age 42, who attended the emergency department due to a perineal tumor of insidious onset and progressive increase in size in recent days, without impact on erectile capacity or urination. . He flatly denied any traumatic history. On examination, there was a perineal tumor located in the left paracavernous region, non-fluctuating, non-painful, with a fibroelastic consistency.
Soft tissue ultrasound was performed with diagnostic orientation toward perineal hematoma dependent on the proximal portion of the left corpus cavernosum.
Nuclear Magnetic Resonance showed a hypointense area of ​​approximately 8x2x0.8 cm. located in the posterior region of the left corpus cavernosum with integrity of the tunica albuginea and Buck's fascia.

A bacteriological study was carried out on material obtained by puncture with a negative culture.
Oral antiplatelet treatment and physical measures were instituted, showing resorption of the hematoma and complete resolution after 4 months.
