An 82-year-old woman with a history of high blood pressure, atrial fibrillation on anticoagulant treatment, diabetes mellitus, and chronic kidney failure.
She was admitted with obstructive jaundice secondary to choledocholithiasis. Therapeutic ERCP is performed with conscious sedation (midazolam-propofol and remifentanil), technically laborious, lasting 150 minutes. Suddenly at the end of the intervention she presented a picture of hemodynamic and respiratory instability. An urgent abdominal computed axial tomography (CT) with direct intravenous contrast was performed, in which a hypervascular image was observed in the anterior pole of the spleen compatible with an area of ​​splenic contusion, with intra-abdominal free fluid, mainly in the perisplenic region. , left paracolic gutter, perihepatic and also in the pelvis with intermediate density compatible with blood. The patient was transferred to the operating room with deterioration in general condition. An urgent laparotomy was performed, evacuating 2000 ml of hemoperitoneum due to a tear in the splenic anterior border in the area visualized by CT. Splenectomy, cholecystectomy, duodenostomy and extraction of the biliary mold were performed with subsequent placement of a Kher tube. No evidence of duodenal perforation.

In the postoperative period, she presented a picture of septic shock due to biliary peritonitis, treated empirically with piperacillin-tazobactam, and the need for relaparotomy, finding a biliary fistula due to the exit of the Kher tube. She was discharged from the Resuscitation Unit 12 days after admission.
The pathological anatomy report of the splenectomy specimen revealed the existence of a break in the continuity of the capsule on its internal surface. The rest of the parenchyma did not present alterations.
