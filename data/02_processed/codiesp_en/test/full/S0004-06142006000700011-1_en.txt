We present the case of a 61-year-old man diagnosed, operated on and treated for rectal adenocarcinoma 18 months previously. He consulted due to a painful increase in penile size, along with the appearance of erythematous, excrescent and bleeding lesions on the preputial skin. Simultaneously, hypogastric lymphedema, penile skin and scrotum lymphedema was observed during the examination.

He was excised by circumcision. The pathological study reported preputial subcutaneous metastases secondary to intestinal adenocarcinoma. In the macromicroscopic image, tumor infiltration of the flat squamous lining epithelium was seen, giving rise to a carcinomatous excrescence. The scarce inflammatory component stood out. At high magnification it is observed that the infiltrating tumor is a moderately differentiated, high-grade adenocarcinoma with a high mitotic index.

Despite the oncological treatment received, the patient died 4 months after diagnosis.
