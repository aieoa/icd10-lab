A 40-year-old woman who attended the emergency department of the health center due to unilateral right otalgia and headache of 3 days duration. It was treated as otitis externa with beclomethasone + clioquinol drops. The next day she returned to the emergency room due to headache that resolved almost completely with intravenous analgesic therapy, associating dexketoprofen 25 mg/8 h with the previous treatment. Two days later he came to the consultation complaining of stabbing headache, oppressive sensation at the periorbital level and tinnitus in the right ear, the examination showing no inflammation in the external auditory canal (EAC), selective hyperemia of the ossicular chain without tympanic alteration and discreet horizontal nystagmus, indicating treatment with levofloxacin 500 mg/24 h, betahistine and sick leave. Two days later she reported temporary disability reporting improvement in ear pain, but a certain feeling of instability, and betahistine was replaced by sulpiride 50 mg/8 h. Forty-eight hours later she consulted for "not feeling better"; He noticed paresthesias in the right hemiface since that same morning, ageusia in the right hemilegue and during the interview we observed blepharospasms in the left eye, confirming in the examination a right peripheral facial paralysis (facial asymmetry when smiling and when blowing, as well as a slight opening right eye by telling him to close his eyes), so he was referred to the hospital emergency room for otorhinolaryngological evaluation. Audiometry showed a slight drop in treble in the right ear. The cranial nerves were normal except VII. She did not have spontaneous nystagmus. Otoscopy showed hyperemia in the upper portion of the EAC and small vesicles were seen in the right auricle, so she was admitted with the diagnosis of "Right Ramsay Hunt Syndrome" to begin treatment with intravenous acyclovir and follow-up.
