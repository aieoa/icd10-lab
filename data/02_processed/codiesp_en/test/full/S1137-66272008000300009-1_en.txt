A 39-year-old woman with a history of diffuse normal functioning goiter, calcified uterine myoma, normal menstrual periods, an uneventful pregnancy and a vaginal delivery, presented to the emergency room due to crampy abdominal pain along with abdominal distention and several liquid stools for 48 hours. evolution. The patient reported two similar episodes in the last year, which resolved spontaneously in a few hours, without other accompanying symptoms. On physical examination, he was in good general condition, with hemodynamic stability, an abdomen without scars, distended and tympanic, painful on palpation in the right hemiabdomen, especially in the right lower quadrant, without signs of peritoneal irritation and without masses or visceromegaly being palpated, decreased peristalsis. . In the requested analysis, she had a slight leukocytosis with granulocytic predominance and CRP 3.7mg/dl, with the rest of the analysis (glucose, ions, kidney function, liver enzymes, amylase, urine system...) being normal. In the abdominal Abdominal ultrasound shows abundant gas and hyperperistalsis in the ID and a minimal amount of free fluid between the intestinal loops. The abdominal CT scan shows a marked dilation of the ID loops that respects the terminal ileum, with a change in caliber being seen in the preterminal ileum at the level of the FID, a non-dilated colon with opacification with oral contrast, and accumulation of free fluid at the level of the sac. Douglas, myomatous uterus with lumpy calcifications.

Initially, conservative treatment was applied with a nasogastric tube, fluid therapy and analgesia, with no clinical or radiological improvement evident in the following hours, so surgical intervention was decided. The approach was performed through infraumbilical midline laparotomy, revealing a moderate amount of free serous fluid, dilation of ID loops and stenosing thickening in the terminal ileum, about 10 cm from the ileocecal valve, with a tumor-like appearance, with retraction of the serous surface and mesenteric lymphadenopathy. The pelvic examination revealed a myomatous uterus. Ileocecal resection and mechanical side-lateral anastomosis were performed, as well as drainage of fluid from the Douglas cul-de-sac. Samples were sent to Pathological Anatomy for histological study, reporting that abundant adhesions and fibrosis were observed in the wall at the level of the terminal ileum and cecum. In depth, islets and nests of endometrioid-type glandular epithelium are observed, as well as endometrial stroma. Focally, an increase in inflammatory infiltrate and fibrosis is observed. The intestinal wall shows hypertrophy of the muscular layers, lymph nodes without obvious histological lesion. With the diagnosis of ileocecal endometriosis, and after a favorable postoperative course without complications, the patient was referred to Gynecology and Obstetrics consultations to assess initiation of treatment with gonadotropin-releasing hormone analogues and evolutionary follow-up.
