An 80-year-old man came to the emergency room due to a 2-day history of decreased visual acuity (VA) of the right eye (RE).
His medical history includes: hypertension and dyslipidemia under treatment, chronic bronchitis and a transient ischemic attack that required antiplatelet therapy with aspirin.
The ophthalmological examination in the emergency is as follows. AV by OD: light perception (PL); per left eye (LE): 1; OD afferent pupillary defect 3+/4+; intraocular pressure OD: 16; OI: 17; The biomicroscopy is normal and the funduscopy shows a subretinal hemorrhage of 6 × 10 papillary diameters at the level of the posterior pole, including the macular area.

An angiofluoresceinography (AFG) was performed, confirming the subretinal location of this hemorrhage, along with an inferior juxtafoveolar hyperfluorescent area at late times that indicates the presence of a neovascular membrane (NVM) at that level.

Two days later, 25 μg of r-TPA in a volume of 0.1 ml is injected intravitreally, followed by an injection of 0.3 ml of pure sulfur hexafluoride (SF6) and an anterior chamber evacuation paracentesis of 0.2 ml.
After 3 days he went for a check-up and it was observed that the blood had partially moved from the posterior pole, along with areas of fibrin.

One month after this procedure, he returned for review with a VA of 0.05 and in the funduscopy a decrease in hemorrhage, with blood persisting in the macular area and fibrin in the lower area.

One month later, the patient went for a check-up and the funduscopy revealed a subretinal hemorrhage of similar extent to that presented at diagnosis, so he was diagnosed with hemorrhagic AMD and a cycle of 3 doses of intravitreal ranibizumab was prescribed.

After the ranibizumab cycle, he went for a check-up at the hospital and presented a VA in the OD of the LP and in the LE of 1. The funduscopy revealed the presence of hemovitreum, which prevented adequate visualization of the structures of the posterior pole.
At the present time it is still being studied to evaluate the next therapeutic step.
