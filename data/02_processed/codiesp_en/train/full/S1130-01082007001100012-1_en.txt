80-year-old woman with HTN, CRF and hypothyroidism. She was admitted to Geriatrics due to cognitive impairment, she developed an acute abdomen with septic shock. Abdominal CT showed free fluid and thickening of the colonic wall. After emergency surgery, she underwent total colectomy with terminal ileostomy for ischemic colitis and splenectomy for intraoperative bleeding. The postoperative period progressed favorably with resolution of the shock. He tolerated enteral nutrition (EN), with the ileostomy functioning well. On the 8th postoperative day, given the appearance of abdominal blood drainage, a CT scan was performed, revealing hematoma of the surgical bed. Subsequently, the drainage had a milky appearance, with a discharge greater than 1,000 cc/d. When analyzed, he had: TG 166 mg/dl (in blood: TG 42, albumin 1.8), glucose 100 mg/dL, proteins 0.83 g/dl, amylase 133, cells 145 (PMN 65%, M 32%) , negative bacteriological culture, confirming the presence of FL.

TPN and somatostatin 3 mg/12 h i.v. were instituted. The drainage output decreased progressively (BQ: TG 3 mg/dl, protein 2.2 g/dl), subsiding after 5 days. Therefore, it was not considered necessary to perform lymphoscintigraphy. Low-fat NE rich in medium-chain fatty acids (MCFA) and high protein was added, with good tolerance.
