A 66-year-old woman underwent hysterectomy plus bilateral adnexectomy and lymphadenectomy for endometrial adenocarcinoma. History: myotonic dystrophy type I (DM type I) or Steinert's disease, pacemaker due to complete BAV, restrictive lung disease requiring nocturnal non-invasive mechanical ventilation (NIMV), non-viral chronic liver disease with hypertransaminasemia in digestive follow-up, hypothyroidism in treatment with levotyrosine and HBP in treatment with candesartan. Previous surgical interventions: laparotomy cholecystectomy 20 years ago and bilateral cataract surgery, both without complications.
- ECG: sinus rhythm. Pacemaker ventricular capture. Complete left bundle branch block.
- Chest x-ray: restrictive pattern. Slight left pleural effusion.
- Spirometry: FEV1 0.88 L (45.3%), FVC 1.16 L (49.6%), FEV1/FVC = 0.76.
- Arterial blood gases: pH 7.44, PO2 53 mmHg, PCO2 51 mmHg, HCO3 34.6 mmol/l
- Blood count: Hb 11.4 g/dl, Hct 35.6%, Platelets 85x1000/μl, Leukocytes 4.6x1000/μl
- Coagulation: prothrombin activity 80%, APTT 24.8 s.
Upon her arrival to the operating room, the patient was normotensive, normothermic, and had an arterial oxygen saturation of 99% with 02 in nasal cannulae at 3 liters per minute. The administration of benzodiazepines in premedication was avoided.
It was decided to perform intraspinal anesthesia with 0.5% hyperbaric bupivacaine, 9 mg, plus 5 μg of fentanyl; The technique was performed without incident. In the first hour, paracetamol 1 g IV, metamizole 2 g IV, dexamethasone 4 mg IV, dexketoprofen 50 mg IV and fluid therapy with a heater were administered. During this first hour, the resection of the uterus and adnexa was performed. After 100 minutes and coinciding with the iliac and para-aortic lymphadenectomy, the patient experienced pain, so it was decided to inject 10 mg of ketamine as a bolus (avoiding the use of benzodiazepines, morphine and propofol). A total of 40 mg of ketamine was used, providing satisfactory analgesia and no side effects. Ventilatory mechanics and O2 saturation did not change during the intervention, maintaining the oxygen therapy that she was already receiving on the ward (O2 in nasal cannulas at 3 l/min) as the only respiratory support.
The surgery ended after 150 minutes without any incident. In the resuscitation unit, NIV (CPAP at 5 cmH2O) was established, and a bilateral percutaneous transabdominal block (TAP) was performed (7.5 ml of 0.5% ropivacaine and 7.5 ml of 1% mepivacaine in each hemiabdomen). ) in addition to a rectus abdominis muscle block (5 ml of 0.5% ropivacaine on each side), both being effective. Analgesia was prescribed for the hospitalization ward, avoiding opioids, where he arrived three hours after being admitted to resuscitation.
During her stay in the ward, the patient remained stable and her pain was controlled, and she was discharged from the hospital on the fifth day after the intervention.
