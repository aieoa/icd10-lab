84-year-old female patient with a history of HTN, Hyperthyroidism, Type II Diabetes Mellitus and anxiety syndrome. Referred to the External Unit of Urology from the Emergency Department due to a case of frank monosymptomatic hematuria of several weeks of evolution without other associated symptoms or alteration in general condition.
Physical examination revealed the presence of a palpable mass at the level of the right lumbar fossa. Her abdomen was soft and depressible.
The requested ultrasound study showed a large heterogeneous solid mass in the lower pole of the right kidney, with the contralateral kidney being normal. The bladder presented internal echoes compatible with clots. The cystoscopic study was unremarkable.
Abdominopelvic CT, chest X-ray, blood chemistry and blood count were also performed.
CT showed a large right tumor mass measuring 9x10 cm, heterogeneous and with hypodense areas (areas of necrosis) with distortion of the renal parenchyma and its contour, located in the lower pole and with attenuation values ​​lower than those of the healthy parenchyma. The renal vein was thrombosed and there were retroperitoneal locoregional lymphadenopathy.

At the analytical level, the presence of iron deficiency anemia, increased ESR and normocalcemia stood out.
Radical surgery was proposed, which the patient and family rejected, and selective embolization was accepted as an alternative.
By puncture in the right common femoral artery, abdominal aortic arteriography was performed and subsequently the right renal artery was selectively catheterized with a 5 Fr Cobra 2 visceral angiographic catheter (Angiodinamics®). The artery that supplies the tumor is then embolized with polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) particles of 355 to 500 microns (Contour®, Boston Scientific), completely occluding its flow and without immediate complications.

After 24 hours of hospitalization, given the patient's good progress, it was decided to discharge her from the hospital pending subsequent check-ups.
Two weeks after embolization, the patient consulted due to headache, and a brain CT scan showed no findings of pathological significance. After 28 months of follow-up, no episodes of hematuria have occurred again and the only notable symptoms are insomnia and musculoskeletal pain, both controlled with symptomatic treatment. Blood pressure values ​​have not been modified, maintaining acceptable figures with ACE inhibitors and calcium antagonists.
