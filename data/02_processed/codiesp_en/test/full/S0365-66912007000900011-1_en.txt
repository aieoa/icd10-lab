A 42-year-old, asymptomatic man was evaluated ophthalmologically to review his type II diabetes of 3 years' duration. On physical examination, corrected visual acuity was 1.0 in both eyes. The anterior pole was within normal limits and intraocular pressure was 16 mmHg in both eyes. Intrinsic and extrinsic motility were normal. Indirect ophthalmoscopy showed a right optic nerve with band or “bow-tie” atrophy and a left papilla that was described as pale; there were no signs of diabetic retinopathy.

A visual field was performed (Humphrey 30-2 test Allergan Humphrey, San Leandro, California, USA) where a right homonymous hemianopia was noted. Given the suspicion of a lesion occupying intracranial space, a brain computed tomography (CT) was performed in which a calcification of 2 cm in diameter was observed in relation to the amygdala and head of the left hippocampus. Brain MRI showed a heterogeneous lesion in the head of the left hippocampus, with calcification and an adjacent hemorrhagic area.

The color test was normal. In the cerebral arteriography and presurgical vascular mapping, the patient presented an allergic reaction to the contrast with cutaneous manifestations, which subsided after treatment with antihistamines.
Surgery was performed using a craniotomy and a complete resection of the lesion was performed. The pathological study revealed a subependymoma. The final diagnosis was temporal horn subependymoma of the left lateral ventricle with involvement of the retrochiasmatic pathway and optic atrophy in the left band.
In the last ophthalmological examination, the patient remains unchanged, with right homonymous hemianopia maintained in campimetry, the optic nerves with the same degree of atrophy, and the rest of the examination is normal.
