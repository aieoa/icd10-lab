42 year old female. He has had recurrent herpes in the mouth for 8 months.
He was admitted for a 2-month illness with dry cough in fits and rapidly progressive dyspnea. In addition, bilateral chest pain, fever up to 39oC and weight loss of 14kg. The recurrence of the herpetic lesion caused dysphagia and odynophagia.
On physical examination he had whitish plaques in the oropharynx; examination of the chest, with decreased respiratory sounds and fine crackles. In room air the oxygen saturation was 86%. The arterial blood gas report with 70% supplemental oxygen was: pH 7.30, pCO2 40.5mmHg, pO2 132mmHg, HCO3 19.5mmol/l, base excess -5.8mmol/l, oxygen saturation 97, 9%. Oxygenation index (OI) of 188. Laboratory tests on admission highlighted: lymphopenia of 600 cells/mm3, Hb 11.8gr/dl, lactic dehydrogenase of 971IU/l and albumin 3.3gr/dl.
The chest X-ray showed bilateral patch opacities with ground glass and pneumomediastinum, therefore, the differential diagnosis included immunosuppression associated with HIV and P. jirovecii pneumonia (PJP). The analysis for HIV by ELISA was POSITIVE, confirmed by Western Blot.
Bronchoscopy with transbronchial biopsy and bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) was performed. The histopathological study is reported in figure 1. He received treatment with Trimethoprim / Sulfamethoxasol and Prednisone in reducing doses for 21 days.

On the 7th day of treatment, she presented respiratory deterioration and the IO decreased to 110, for which she was admitted to intensive care in a state of shock and supported with invasive mechanical ventilation. Upon admission, laboratory tests revealed leukocytes of 24,300 cells/mm3, Hb 10.8gr/dl, lactic dehydrogenase 2033IU/l and albumin of 2.26gr/dl. Imipenem was added due to suspicion of hospital-acquired pneumonia and after 12 days the leukocyte count improved to 5800 cells/mm3, Hb 8.7gr/dl, lactic dehydrogenase 879IU/l, albumin 2.41gr/dl and IO at 243.5, leading to extubation.
In the next 24 hours, he presented massive hemoptysis (volume 250 ml). The Hb level drops to 6g/dl, the IO decreases to 106 and is supported with invasive mechanical ventilation. Using Swan-Ganz, a POAP of 10mmHg is measured. The chest x-ray is shown in Figure 2A. He underwent BAL, whose pathology study confirmed the presence of recent and active alveolar hemorrhage. Furthermore, CMV infection was documented by rt-PCR and treatment with Ganciclovir 350 mg/d was initiated for 14 days.

He has a good evolution, OI improves to 277, withdrawing from invasive mechanical ventilation 9 days after the event. After 37 days he returns home. During follow-up, the CD4 count is 109 cells/µl and the viral load <40 copies/µl.
