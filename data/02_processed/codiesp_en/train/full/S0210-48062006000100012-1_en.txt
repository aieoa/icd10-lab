We present the case of a 41-year-old patient who consulted for pain and dorsal induration of the penis at the level of the proximal third for one week. His personal history included smoking 20 cigarettes a day and excision of cysts in both breasts. There were no coagulation disorders or diseases of interest.
The patient reported that a week ago he began to have pain on the dorsum of the penis at the root level where he could palpate an indurated and mobile nodule.
On physical examination, the external genitalia were normal, except for the painful palpation of the nodule described.
A Doppler ultrasound was performed in which a dorsal induration corresponding to segmental thrombosis of the superficial dorsal vein of the penis was observed, with the rest of its course being patent.

The patient received treatment with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and antibiotics for four weeks, experiencing clinical improvement in pain and a decrease in induration.
A follow-up pelvic MRI was also requested after six weeks, in which no injury data suggesting thrombosis or other type of pathology in the location of the dorsal vein of the penis was evident.
