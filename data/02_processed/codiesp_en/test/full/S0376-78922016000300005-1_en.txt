Neonate patient, 8 days old, female, preterm at 36 weeks and high weight (3,600 g), suffering from peripartum asphyxia with moderate-severe neonatal encephalopathy. Due to peripartum asphyxia, treatment with systemic hypothermia was performed at the regional hospital of origin using the CritiCool® system (MTRE, Mennen Medical Corp, USA). After 8 days of admission, he was transferred to the Pediatric Surveillance Unit of the 12 de Octubre Hospital. During his stay in this unit, he was consulted to the Plastic Surgery Service for an early-onset dorsal skin lesion. During the evaluation we observed a tumor on the back, in the midline, measuring 11x13x4 cm, partially fluctuating, with a central blackish eschar approximately 5 cm in diameter.
We requested a soft tissue ultrasound to try to establish the diagnosis, which reported liquefactive fat necrosis. We performed 2 punctures to evacuate the contents without success, so we decided to perform surgical debridement and application of vacuum therapy (Renasys®, Smith&Nephew®, London, United Kingdom), on the 11th day of admission. The therapy was administered at a pressure of 100 mmHg, performing continuous cycles and dressing changes every 72 hours.

The patient underwent surgery after 21 days of treatment with vacuum therapy, presenting at the time of surgery a good-looking wound, with a bottom with the presence of granulation tissue and no exudate. The tissues were advanced, leaving a bloody central area of ​​1 cm2. The patient remained admitted to the Neonatology Service of our hospital and was finally discharged 5 days after surgery, at 45 days of age.
After 8 months of evolution, the patient presents a stable wound, without pain, which allows her to lead a normal life, scoring 2 points based on the Vancouver Scar Scale.
