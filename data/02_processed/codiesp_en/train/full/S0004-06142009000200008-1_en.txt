A 32-year-old male presented with long-standing low back pain that worsened in recent days, as well as a loss of thirty pounds of body weight in a period of approximately three months. No symptoms and signs of adrenal hyperfunction were detected.
During the abdominal examination, a hard tumor was palpated in the left hypochondrium that reached the mesogastrium and left iliac fossa. The imaging studies performed were: abdominal ultrasound, chest x-ray, descending urogram and computed axial tomography.
No metastases were detected in the chest x-ray. The findings obtained in the other imaging studies indicated the presence of an extensive, well-defined mass, with regular contours and with heterogeneous signals suggestive of hemorrhagic and necrotic areas. There was displacement of neighboring structures with infiltration. No infiltration of the liver or inferior vena cava was demonstrated. The treatment consisted of excision surgery through the thoraco-abdominal route with clamping of the thoracic aorta to control intraoperative hemorrhage. In addition to tumor resection, left nephrectomy and en bloc splenectomy with bundles of hilar lymphadenopathy were performed.

The excised tumor was well encapsulated, weighing 4350 g and with a maximum diameter of 24 cm. Its macroscopic study showed a solid mass with hemorrhagic areas. The tumor infiltrated the capsule and perinephric fat of the entire upper pole of the left kidney as well as the spleen. In the histological study, the diagnosis of a carcinoma of the adrenal gland was confirmed. The postoperative evolution was satisfactory and he was discharged on the tenth day after the operation. Survival was six months. Death occurred due to progression of the disease.
