A 26-year-old female patient came to our service for bilateral upper eyelid edema of 2 years' duration.
In the ophthalmological examination, lesions with imprecise limits (infiltrative appearance of the epidermis) were observed in the nasal part of both upper eyelids, with an orange hue in the temporal area, of firm consistency, and that extended along the eyelid fold. . They were non-mobile, non-painful masses that did not exceed the orbital rim. The rest of the exploration was normal.
To try to make a differential diagnosis between entities such as orbital pseudotumor, collagen disease, sarcoidosis, etc., a systemic examination was performed, with all studies being normal.
In the orbital Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), these masses were practically symmetrical and infiltrated the levator muscle of the upper eyelid, without affecting the eyeball.
Corticosteroid treatment was instituted (thinking of an inflammatory process located at the eyelid level), with high doses of methylprednisolone (1 g of Urbason®, HOECHST FARMA), which reduced the size of the tumors but with recurrence of the tumors when said medication was suspended. . Given this, and not having reached a diagnosis, an incisional biopsy was performed, obtaining three pieces of pinkish-yellowish tissue. The histological study showed loose connective tissue that was almost entirely infiltrated by histiocytic cells characteristic of xanthogranulomatosis, which tended to form a granulomatous pattern. Immunohistochemical stains for histiocytic cells (CD 68) were positive.
With all this, the diagnosis of eyelid xanthogranulomatosis was reached.
More tests were performed to rule out multiorgan involvement: abdomino-thoracic CT, cranial and orbital CT, chest X-ray, abdomino-pelvic ultrasound and bone scan with Tc 99m diphosphate. They were all normal.
It is, therefore, a case of pure orbital xanthogranulomatosis, or Erdheim-Chester disease, without systemic manifestations.
Currently, after a new cycle of corticosteroids (methylprednisolone 80 mg/24 hours, Urbason®) and with a maintenance dose (methylprednisolone 4 mg/48 hours, Urbason®) on a continuous basis, the process remains relatively stable.
