This is an 18-year-old patient, diagnosed (DSM-IV-TR) with Social Phobia and Dependent Personality Disorder, referred from the Child and Adolescent Mental Health Service (CSMIJ) for follow-up in the adult service, who attends accompanied by her mother.
She is the eldest of two sisters. Separated parents. She lives with her mother and sister. During her childhood, she highlighted attention deficit and poor academic performance without repeating a grade. At the moment she begins to study a higher module and collaborates in the family business. As medical history of interest, she highlights an isolated growth delay that required treatment with growth hormone (GH) from 12 to 17 years of age. Fructose intolerance associated with digestive bleeding and lactose intolerance. In her childhood, a genetic examination was performed, without observing any genetic, numerical or structural alteration. She denies drug use. Among her family psychiatric history, her mother reports long-standing anxious symptoms. They describe the father as a lonely, introverted, and "unemotional" person, characteristics that several paternal-line relatives present. From a psychiatric point of view, the patient came into contact with the CSMIJ at the age of 14, and she was guided by the diagnosis of Social Phobia. At the age of 17, she was admitted to the Day Hospital for 2 months due to difficulties relating to relationships, school absenteeism and great dependence on the family environment. Her diagnostic orientation was Social Phobia and Dependent Personality Disorder (according to evolution) which after discharge evolved towards partial improvement. Among the premorbid personality traits, high introversion stands out. Her mother describes her as an isolated, "different" girl, with little social interest.
From her initial examination, distant contact stands out, with little spontaneous language without alterations at a formal level. She has moderate psycho-physical anxiety, hypochondriac-type obsessive ideas, onychophagia and nocturnal bruxism. No symptoms of the emotional or psychotic sphere are observed. No alterations in the course or content of thought. No alterations in sensory perception. Partial consciousness disorder.
The picture is oriented as a Cluster C Personality Disorder (phobic, obsessive and dependent traits). Pharmacological treatment prescribed by CSMIJ (Sertraline, 100 mg) is maintained, and he is referred to psychology to work on social difficulties. She made few visits with poor compliance with the proposed strategies (cognitive-behavioral model), and was disengaged due to incompatibility with studies.

Second contact after two years
The patient is reconnected after almost two years (20 years) of not following up. Social difficulties persist. Exploratory visits are made by psychiatry and psychology, including a visit with her mother. They express the decision to abandon SSRI medication. They do not accept pharmacological treatment, although they do accept psychological treatment. During the assessment, the patient was impressed by a "peculiar" contact rather than the usual phobic-anxious contact. She presents a strange -although subtle- alteration in eye contact. Her language continues to be unspontaneous. In addition to the difficulties at a social level and the little interest in the environment, obsessive ideas of a hypochondriacal type and an absolute rejection of physical and sexual contact stand out. In order to assess the patient's difficulties/abilities at an interpersonal level, simple social exposure exercises are proposed outside the consultation. The patient does not perform any of the proposed exercises/strategies. There is also no concern to resolve these difficulties. On the contrary, she impresses with a certain indifference in this regard. Given this, doubts begin regarding social avoidance (uncomfortable vs. anxious?), and regarding the desire to relate socially (learned social discourse?). Given these doubts, the suspicion arises that these symptoms could be explained by other diagnoses: schizoid traits? autism spectrum traits? Therefore, a new evaluation of the patient is proposed.

Evaluation protocol and results thereof:
• Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-IV Axis II Disorders (SCID II):
- Schizoid Personality Disorder Criteria: 4/5. Close to cut-off point.
- Avoidant Personality Disorder Criteria: 5/5. Meets cutoff point.
• Theory of Mind Tests (TOM):
- "Happé Stories Test": Shows an adequate ability to infer mental states in others, understand metaphors and double meanings.
- "Mind in the Eyes Reading Test": You are able to attribute to most elements (23/36) the appropriate feeling or emotion shown in the photograph.
- "Gaffe Test": Correctly identifies "gaffe" stories but shows difficulties in attributing emotional states and intentionality to the characters.
• Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale (WAIS-III): Verbal IQ= 99 Manipulative IQ= 94 Total IQ= 97 Range: Medium
• Autism Diagnostic Interview Revised (ADI-R): Alterations in reciprocal social interaction are identified. Limited variety of facial expressions to communicate and regulate social interaction. Inability to develop relationships with equals. A lack of socio-emotional reciprocity, inadequate social responses and a deficit in the search for sharing pleasure with others are observed. Retrospectively, qualitative alterations in communication are identified, such as lack of spontaneous and varied symbolic play.
Some restricted behavioral patterns are also identified.
• Magnetic Resonance (MRI): Cranial examination within normality.

Overall Rating:
Throughout the examination, the patient appears cooperative but unmotivated. The information obtained allows us to identify signs of an autism spectrum disorder (ASD), which includes poor social interest, inappropriate expressions and behaviors, difficulties establishing social relationships with peers, facial expressions and restricted affectivity, and limited interests/activities. The cognitive profile and performance in the 'TOM' domains (non-significant number of errors, although difficulty in attributing emotional states and intentionality to the characters in the 'Mistakes' test) are consistent with the profile observed in patients. High-functioning TEA (1).
