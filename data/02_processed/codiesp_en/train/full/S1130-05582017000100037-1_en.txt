A 72-year-old man, with no medical history of interest and under treatment with anti-inflammatories since 2011 for chronic neck pain, who attended the emergency department reporting a significant alteration in gait and progressive loss of strength in extremities during the last 6 months accompanied by Dysphagia for solid and moderate dyspneic symptoms. Physical examination revealed spastic left hemiparesis, generalized hyperreflexia, positive Romberg sign, and ataxia. The MRI shows a thickening of the soft tissues around the odontoid process, causing a severe stenosis of the spinal canal, especially in the left half, with compromise of the bulbomedullary junction and voluminous anterior syndesmophytic proliferations at the level of C2-C6, predominating in C4. -C5, which deform and partially obliterate the airway.

Under the diagnosis of Forestier disease, the patient initially underwent transoral surgery, performing resection of the anterior arch of the atlas and the odontoid process without neurological improvement, so an external anterolateral cervical approach and resection of the osteophytic projections were performed. anteriors of C3-C4-C5. The postoperative course showed progressive clinical improvement of dysphagia and hemiparesis with absence of respiratory symptoms.
