This is a 60-year-old man with a personal history of ischemic heart disease (he suffered an acute myocardial infarction in 2004) and is an ex-smoker. The patient had no known allergies to medications or foods. In July 2008 he was diagnosed with primary amyloidosis and nephrotic syndrome. Upon discharge from the hospital, he received nutritional recommendations from the Nephrology service to follow a diet low in sodium and fat, as well as to exclude protein foods. To do this, the patient had to cook all foods, not use table salt, and could not use any other culinary technique (frying, grilling, roasting, microwave, etc.), as well as not eating main courses (meat, fish and eggs). ). The pharmacological treatment he currently receives is omeprazole, candesartan, enalapril, atorvastatin, acetylsalicylic acid, ezetimibe, furosemide, barnidipine and spironolactone. He goes to the diet therapy consultation to prescribe a diet adjusted to his nutritional needs.
