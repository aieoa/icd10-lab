In June 2003, a 40-year-old female patient, originally from and residing in a town near Guatemala City, presented to a consultation referred by her general dentist with an asymptomatic, dark brown pigmented macule, discovered during a clinical examination. routine. The lesion was located in the anterior mucosa of the hard palate, measuring 0.5 cm in diameter. The patient had no significant medical history and was in good health. She reported not taking any type of medication. The patient had lost her upper incisors due to extractions and used an inadequately designed removable acrylic partial prosthesis that caused erythema due to pressure on the underlying mucosa. Candidiasis was ruled out by exfoliative cytology.

An excisional biopsy was performed under local anesthesia, using the punch technique with a 0.6 cm diameter instrument. The surgical site healed by secondary intention without complications.
The tissue obtained from the biopsy was fixed in 10% buffered formalin and processed routinely. The microscopic study of the same, with hematoxylin-eosin staining, revealed a fragment of mucosa covered by slightly acanthotic stratified squamous epithelium, with spongiosis, which contained numerous dendritic melanocytes. The underlying connective tissue showed the presence of melanin pigment admixed to a mild inflammatory infiltrate of lymphocytic predominance. The definitive diagnosis was melanoacanthosis.
