A 48-year-old Caucasian man with CKD secondary to renal hypoplasia began hemodialysis in 1984. That same year he received his first kidney transplant from a living donor, his father, which remained functional until 1997, when he returned to hemodialysis for chronic kidney disease. of the graft and transplantectomy was performed. In 1999, he received his second kidney transplant from a deceased donor, returning to hemodialysis in November 2002 after the development of membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis secondary to HCV on the kidney graft. “Non-A, non-B” hepatitis was diagnosed in 1984 and a liver biopsy in 2000 showed chronic periportal hepatitis (P3L3F2) with positive HCV RNA (genotype 3). After the patient returned to hemodialysis to be included again on the kidney transplant list, treatment with IFN-α, 3 million units three times a week, was started in March 2003. In April 2003 he began to experience pain at the level of the kidney graft, hematuria and fever, so INF-α was suspended and a scheduled transplantectomy was performed, with intense parenchymal lesions of acute and chronic rejection being observed on histology. In August of that same year, HCV-RNA negativity was confirmed despite having received a month of antiviral treatment. However, in January 2004 he had a relapse, so antiviral treatment was reintroduced, which was continued for 12 months, achieving an SVR without a new relapse. In February 2008 he received his third kidney transplant from a deceased donor. He currently has normal kidney function and maintains an undetectable viral load five years after completing treatment.
