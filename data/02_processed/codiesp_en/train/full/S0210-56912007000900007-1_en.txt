A 58-year-old woman came to the hospital due to pain in the popliteal fossa and vomiting that lasted 48 hours. Her history included DM, high blood pressure, dyslipidemia, hyperuricemia, congestive heart failure, and depression. On admission, vital signs were normal and examination showed signs of deep vein thrombosis. Blood analysis showed normal ionic, hematological, and renal and hepatic function. The patient was diagnosed with deep vein thrombosis of the lower extremities by ultrasound and she received treatment with NPH insulin, metformin (850 mg/12 hours), enoxaparin, torasemide, enalapril, clorazepate dipotassium, allopurinol, mirtazapine and digoxin. During the first two days the patient continued to vomit and barely ingest fluids and, 24 hours later, she reported abdominal pain with asthenia, vomiting, visual disturbances and oliguria. On the fifth day of admission, the patient suffered cardiac arrest due to ventricular fibrillation and, after being resuscitated by the intensive care physician, she was left mydriatic, anuric, and with a score of 3 on the Glasgow scale. During the first hours he received fluids, 500 mEq. of sodium bicarbonate and norepinephrine, which restarted diuresis, but arterial blood gases did not improve (pH 6.90 and bicarbonate 7.1 mEq/l). Performing a hemodialysis session improved metabolic acidosis (pH 7.28 and bicarbonate 16 mEq/L) and hyperkalemia (5.2 mEq/L). After overcoming aspiration pneumonia, the patient was transferred to the ward in a vegetative state and died on the nineteenth day after admission.
