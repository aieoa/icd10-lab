A 74-year-old female patient was referred from the Gynecology Outpatient Clinic to Urology due to repeated urinary tract infections (UTIs) with hypogastric pain of at least six months' duration.
Her personal history highlights a vaginal hysterectomy due to uterine prolapse seven months ago in another center. She required readmission 18 days after surgery due to fever and pain, with a diagnosis of vaginal vault abscess that drains spontaneously, the clinical picture disappearing; The tests performed on said admission showed: ECO and CT abscess in the vaginal vault without any other findings. Not DM. Not HTA. No known allergies. No hematuria. No colic. Sporadic urine infections. G2 A0V2, don't escape.

When she was referred to our clinic for the urinary and UTI clinic, she was accompanied by a simple urinary system X-ray, which was reported as normal, although a weakly calcium contour was clearly seen in the pelvis in the bladder area, and a transvaginal ultrasound that reported probable bladder stones, a diagnosis by the which, together with repeated urinary infections, is referred to the Urology Service of our center.

Given the finding on the X-ray. accompanying simple and transvaginal ultrasound with intravesical lithiasis, not mobile and with the surgical history referred to, the presence of an intravesical foreign body is suspected from the beginning due to probable loss of material used in the surgery.
Urethrocystoscopy is performed on the same day of the consultation to confirm the suspected diagnosis, which is how it occurs, revealing a large "ball of gauze" adhered to the bladder wall that is not mobilized because it is calcified; Pelvic CT is requested to assess extension and size as well as relationships with the area.

With the definitive diagnosis of intravesical lithiasis due to a foreign body, probably abandoned in the hysterectomy surgery, it is proposed to extract it by cystoslitectomy or more correctly "cystogasomectomy" by opening the bladder openly, finding the foreign body described.
After good progress, the patient is asymptomatic, the repeated urinary infections have disappeared, although in the two years of follow-up she has had an isolated episode, the patient leads an absolutely normal life.
