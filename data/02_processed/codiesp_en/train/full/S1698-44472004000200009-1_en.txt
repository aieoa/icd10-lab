46-year-old man, with no relevant medical history, non-smoker. A fixed rehabilitation was performed on a single implant (ITI® SLA. Straumann. Walderburg-Switzerland) placed in zone 3.6. Six months after the crown was cemented, he went for a follow-up without reporting associated symptoms. The clinical examination revealed slight redness of the mucosa adjacent to the implant and a 6 mm deep peri-implant pocket, with slight bleeding on probing. The articulating paper showed premature contact on the prosthetic crown. In the panoramic radiograph, a radiolucent area was observed in the marginal bone at 3.6.
The occlusal carving of the prosthetic crown was carried out. Subsequently, a mucoperiosteal flap was raised from 3.5 to 3.7, revealing a fibrous-looking soft tissue rim that occupied a marginal peri-implant bone defect at 3.6. The pathological tissue was removed with plastic curettes and sent for pathological study. The implant surface was decontaminated with 0.2% chlorhexidine gel for 2 minutes and irrigation with saline. The flap was expanded to access a lingual mandibular torus in the ipsilateral premolar area, which was extracted and particled to serve as an autograft. The flap was repositioned and sutured with 3.0 silk. The patient was reinstructed in oral hygiene, ibuprofen 600 mg every 8 hours for 4 days and mouthwashes with 0.12% chlorhexidine digluconate 2 times a day for 2 weeks were prescribed.
The histopathological analysis showed an epithelial-connective tissue, with abundant juxtaepithelial lympho-plasmacytic infiltrate. Under the superficial area, a dense fibroconnective tissue with few inflammatory cells was seen.
Twelve months after the surgical treatment, an x-ray showed marginal bone recovery and a clinical appearance of normality, with the absence of symptoms.
