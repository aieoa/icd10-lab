A 40-year-old man was followed up by the Dermatology Service for presenting painful nodular gluteal lesions with purulent drainage. He was histologically diagnosed with deep suppurative cystic folliculitis, compatible with acne conglobata, with a positive culture for Staphylococcus aureus. He was treated with prednisone (15 mg/day), isotetricin, and rifampicin without improvement. Furthermore, he associated a one-year history of weight loss of 25 kg and abdominal pain, with the appearance of fever in recent weeks. The patient was referred for admission. On physical examination, he was cachectic, and had multiple nodular lesions in the gluteal and perianal region with scar areas between them, which drained purulent material. The analysis revealed severe iron deficiency anemia (Hb: 7.2 g/dl), an increase in acute phase reactants (CRP: 13) and signs of malnutrition (albumin: 2.49 g/dl, cholesterol: 8.3). mg/dl). A colonoscopy was performed, observing pseudopolyps, ulcers and a cobblestone mucosal appearance in the rectum and transverse colon, as well as two fistulous orifices; The histological diagnosis was Crohn's disease (CD) type inflammatory bowel disease. A pelvic MRI revealed a left perianal fistula extending to the root of the left thigh. Treatment was prescribed with high-dose steroids, 5-ASA, antibiotics and azathioprine, with notable clinical and analytical improvement and virtual resolution of the skin lesions. In this case, the existence of another diagnosis (acne conglobata) together with the presentation of CD as perianal disease (PAD) led to a delay in diagnosis, which could have been decisive in the development of these serious lesions.
