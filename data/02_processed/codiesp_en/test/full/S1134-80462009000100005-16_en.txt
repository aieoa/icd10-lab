A 61-year-old male patient is being evaluated by the pain clinic for grade III obliterative arteriopathy affecting the lower limbs. He has been prescribed Durogesic® Matrix analgesic treatment at 50 μg/h for 18 months with acceptable pain control (VAS, 3-4). As rescue medication he is using paracetamol 1 g, (maximum 1 or 2 a day).
He makes an emergency visit to the pain unit because for the past 15 days he has had an increased pain threshold with a certain state of agitation and nervousness. This whole condition occurred after going to his family doctor for his medication and he prescribed generic transdermal fentanyl at 50 μg/h, requiring paracetamol as rescue 1 g every 6 h, with little pain relief, increasing the VAS to 7 -8. After taking a history with the patient, we again prescribe Durogesic® Matrix 50 μg/h. When the patient is reviewed after 2 weeks, he again shows better pain relief, with VAS again 3-4, disappearing the state of nervousness that he had previously presented. Currently, 3 months after the last visit, the patient continues with Durogesic® Matrix 50 μg/h and, sporadically, paracetamol 1 g.
