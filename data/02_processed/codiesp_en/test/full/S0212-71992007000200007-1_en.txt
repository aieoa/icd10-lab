A 64-year-old Italian man was admitted in March 2005 due to intense headache, continuous fever and pancytopenia. In the history he turns out to suffer from type B NHL grade II (Real) already treated with chemotherapy according to the CHOP-R scheme and successively with high doses of cyclophosphamide with the aim of mobilizing stem cells in anticipation of autotransplantation, on the other hand, he never effected. A recent total body CT scan described a remission of the lymphomatous disease while the osteomedullary biopsy showed secondary myelodysplasia in the absence of neoplastic infiltration.
Upon his admission to the hospital, the physical examination revealed a fever of 39 ° C, pale mucous membranes and splenomegaly, BP 110/70 mmHg, and heart rate 128 beats per minute. The rest of the initial physical examination is compatible with normality.
Complementary explorations. The first analyzes show, among other things, pancytopenia with neutropenia of 830/ul. Virological tests (negative) and serial blood cultures were performed. A CT scan of the head showed no acute focal lesions or signs of cerebral hypertension. The patient is treated with supportive therapy, granulocyte growth factors, antifungals and broad-spectrum antibiotics, with remission of the hematological condition and resolution of the fever. Due to the persistence of the headache and the successive appearance of meningeal signs, a diagnostic lumbar puncture was performed. Microscopic analysis of the CSF shows an increase in the total number of cells, consisting of a large number of neutrophils, megakaryocytes and myeloid cells with intermediate maturation; representative table of a core population. The immunohistochemical cytological analysis was compatible with myeloid metaplasia: factor VIII and myeloperoxidase were positive in the morphologically compatible elements. Repeated analysis of CSF compared to peripheral blood and the fact that no rachicentesis was traumatic or difficult to perform excludes contamination. Meanwhile, the blood culture was positive for Sterotrophomonas maltophilia, sensitive to the current antibiotic treatment. Two weeks after admission the patient's neurological conditions worsen, he enters a cerebral coma and dies.
