A 78-year-old woman with a history of hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, generalized osteoarthritis, NYHA stage II congestive heart failure, and chronic atrial fibrillation. She was being treated with metformin (850 mg/8 hours), glipizide (5 mg/day), lercanidipine (10 mg/day), lisinopril-hydrochlorothiazide (20/12.5 mg/day), acenocoumarol and occasionally ibuprofen . Two days before admission due to increased bone pain, she received ibuprofen (600 mg/8 hours), subsequently beginning with general deterioration, vomiting and decreased level of consciousness.
In the emergency room she was hypotensive and poorly perfused in a coma (Glasgow 6/15) and the analysis showed plasma creatinine of 1.79 mg/dl, blood glucose of 215 mg/dl and prothrombin time of 7%. Arterial blood gas analysis had a pH of 7.03 with bicarbonate of 10 mEq/L and plasma lactic acid of 14 mmol/L. Chest x-ray revealed an infiltrate at the left base.

She was transferred to the intensive care unit (ICU) and underwent orotracheal intubation with mechanical ventilation and administration of vasoactive drugs and antibiotics. Lumbar puncture was indicated, the result of which was compatible with pneumococcal meningitis. She underwent hemodialysis with a bicarbonate bath, subsequently having analytical improvement with plasma creatinine (Cr) of 1.2 mg/dl and serum bicarbonate of 22 mEq/l. However, the patient remained in a coma, with EEG with low voltage waves, dying 72 hours after admission.
