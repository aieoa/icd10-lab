19-year-old patient admitted from the emergency room due to gunshot wound (hunting shotgun) after attempted self-lysis (impact distance less than one meter). He had a wound in the lower third of the lateral region with significant bone loss in the region from the first left premolar to the left mandibular angle. Significant cutaneous-mucosal loss of the left jugal region and left cheek. Initially, we proceed to perform a tracheostomy, debridement of devitalized tissues, dental splinting, intermaxillary block and placement of a mandibular reconstruction plate.
In this first surgical stage, a direct intraoral closure and a skin closure with a cervicofacial advancement flap were carried out.
In the immediate postoperative period, there is suffering and loss of the cervico-facial advancement skin flap, which leaves a significant left facial granulating area. Scar retraction of the oral mucosal tissue produces a large limitation of the oral opening (0.8 cm). Orofacial communications and salivary fistulas appear.
Secondary intervention was decided by means of a scapular-parascapular osteomyocutaneous flap of the left arm, not dominant, but with a history of recurrent dislocation.
The intervention begins with the new tracheostomy and the preparation of the recipient vessels: facial artery and branch of the thyrolinguofacial venous trunk. It is necessary to remove a large amount of cell-ganglionic tissue from level I and III to reach the recipient vessels.
Afterwards, the scapular-parascapular osteocutaneous flap is dissected, and the donor area is closed, leaving aspiration drainage, and the left upper limb is immobilized with a sling.

The bony part of the flap is then adapted to the mandibular defect with ostosynthesis using the reconstruction plate placed in the initial intervention. The skin part of the flap is folded as if it were a book, leaving one of the skin lobes intraoral and another extraoral (cervical facial). The back of the supposed book is de-epithelialized and sutured to the remaining lower lip. The intraoral part reconstructs the entire buccal mucosa up to the anterior pillar of the soft palate. The external part reconstructs the left mandibular and submandibular skin area. The posterosuperior region of the skin defect remains for dermal-epidermal grafting. The blockage and dental splint are removed and the mouth is left "open."

Adaptation of the flap requires submaxillectomy and removal of part of the subcutaneous tissue in the posteroinferior area.
The anastomosis was made end-to-end from the scapular circumflex artery to the facial artery; and from the largest of the scapular circumflex veins to the thyrolinguofacial trunk.
The duration of the intervention was 14 hours; with an ischemia time of 2.45 hours.
Satisfactory evolution regarding the vitality of the scapular flap. Local complications: parotid salivary fistula in the posterosuperior area due to loss of the dermoepidermal injero over the masseteric region.

Complications in the donor area, disinsertion of the long arm of the triceps and wound dehiscence. To treat this dehiscence, the patient underwent surgery by performing a local transposition flap. Postural complication: paralysis of the radial nerve on the contralateral (right) side.

The patient follows rehabilitative treatment to regain mobility in his left arm.
