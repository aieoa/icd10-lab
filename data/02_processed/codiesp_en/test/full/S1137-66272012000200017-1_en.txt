A 43-year-old man, previously healthy and asymptomatic, attended the Thoracic Surgery service in order to complete the study of a middle mediastinal tumor identified in a routine study.

In the pharyngo-esophageal study, after the administration of barium contrast, a stenosis of about 3 cm in length is seen in the middle third of the esophagus with well-defined contours and which reduces the esophageal lumen by 50%. These findings are compatible with an extrinsic compressive process.

Right video thoracoscopy is performed through three routes. The presence of a tumor is identified in the middle mediastinum at the level of the azygos vein (cephalic to said vein), mobile, hard, without adhesions to the adjacent structures. The dissection begins by opening the mediastinal pleura and separating the tumor from what appear to be muscle fibers of the esophagus (the latter is performed using a videothoracoscopy hook and electric scalpel). In this way, the tumor, which measures approximately 3 cm in diameter, is enucleated. At its deep edge, the esophageal mucosa is identified, where its tightness is checked.
The postoperative course was uneventful, and the patient was discharged after 48 hours. In the review carried out 6 months after surgery, the patient was asymptomatic and living a completely normal life, without dysphagia or symptoms of gastroesophageal reflux, afebrile and with normal respiratory function tests.
The pathological report of the fragment was diagnosed as esophageal leiomyoma. Macroscopically, it had a size of 5 cm, an irregular shape, a multi-lobed surface, and a certain tendency to form swirls. Under the microscope, the tumor is observed consisting of bundles of smooth muscle fibers strongly positive for anti-actin, with a multidirectional arrangement, without cellular atypia or mitosis. From time to time, hyaline amorphous material appears, in the form of irregularly distributed droplets.
