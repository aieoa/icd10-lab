A 50-year-old Caucasian woman with CKD secondary to polycystic kidney disease began hemodialysis in 1987, receiving her first kidney transplant from a deceased donor in 1988. Complications of the transplant included acute rejection treated with steroid shocks and antithymocyte globulin ( ATG) and anti-CD3 monoclonal antibody (OKT3), returning to hemodialysis in June 2009 after loss of the kidney graft due to chronic kidney disease. She was diagnosed with "non-A, non-B" hepatitis in 1987 in relation to multiple transfusions of blood products, subsequently confirming positive HCV RNA (genotype 1a). In November 2009, five months after returning to dialysis, treatment was started with pegylated IFN α2a, 135 μg weekly, and RBV 200 mg every 48 hours, with oscillating erythropoietin support throughout the treatment. In February 2010, she developed fever and hematuria, so the dose of antiviral treatment was reduced and embolization of the kidney graft was performed due to suspicion of acute rejection of the non-functioning graft. Treatment with IFN and RBV was resumed at the initial dose, which had to be suspended at week 40 due to the appearance of exudative erythema multiforme without response to corticosteroid treatment. The patient presented a rapid viral response with undetectable load in the fourth week of treatment and subsequent SVR. In July 2011, she received her second kidney transplant from a deceased donor. She currently maintains normal kidney function and a negative viral load.
