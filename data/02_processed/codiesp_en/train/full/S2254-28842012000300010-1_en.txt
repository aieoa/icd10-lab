A 72-year-old man in an HD program since December 2002, due to renal failure secondary to bilateral nephrectomy for renal neoplasia (1999 and 2002).
History of high blood pressure, appendectomy, cholecystectomy and right inguinal herniorrhaphy. Splenectomy for injury to the spleen during right nephrectomy (2002). Venous thrombosis in the right lower limb caused by femoral cannulation (dialysis catheter). Ischemic colitis, colon polyposis and lower gastrointestinal bleeding in 2007. In December 2008, a new episode of lower gastrointestinal bleeding due to angiodysplasia. Diagnosed in 2009 with moderate-severe aortic valve stenosis.
History of vascular access:
• Treatment began in December 2002 using a right femoral catheter. He retired in February-03 (venous thrombosis).
• In January 2003, a left humerocephalic AVF was performed. It began to be used after 4 weeks. He developed aneurysmal dilation, presenting a partial thrombosis (December-03), which allowed its use in single puncture.
• In December 2003, a right humero-basilica AVF was performed that did not work.
• The same month, a right humero-axillary PTFE graft was implanted, with thrombosis 24 hours later. He had to have it removed due to infection in January-04.
• Due to dysfunction of the left fistula, in January-04 a tunneled catheter was implanted in the left internal jugular. He was used as VA until July-04, leaving due to infection refractory to antibiotic therapy.
• On 7-22-04 there was a failed attempt to canalize the right internal jugular, placing a catheter in the right femoral vein: he retired in September 04, having a functioning AVF.
• Again, on 7-23-04 an attempt was made to canalize both the right and left internal jugular veins without success.
• On 4-8-04 a PTFE prosthesis was implanted between the left femoral artery and vein. It was used from September until 10-22-04 (non-recoverable thrombosis).
• On the same day, 10-22-04, a tunneled catheter was implanted in the right internal jugular:
- In October 2005, he presented an episode of bacteremia due to E. Coli, which was resolved with antibiotic therapy.
- Subsequently, the catheter caused insufficient flow problems, frequently requiring both local and systemic fibrinolytic treatment.
- For this reason, in November 2006, anticoagulation with acenocoumarol (Sintrom®) was indicated, with slight functional improvement.
-In February 2007 she suffered an episode of lower gastrointestinal bleeding, due to ischemic colitis and polyps in the colon and caused by anticoagulant treatment.
- After removing the acenocoumarol, the catheter stopped providing flow.
- On 2/22/07 an attempt was made to replace the catheter through the same venous tract, making it impossible to introduce the new catheter due to complete thrombosis of the superior cava.
• On the same day, 2-22-07, a temporary catheter was placed in the left femoral vein. He was removed on 2-3-07 as he had a functioning central catheter.
• On 2-28-07, a tunneled Split-Cath catheter was placed directly in the right atrium, through a right anterior mini-thoracotomy.
- From the first dialysis session there was minimal blood leakage, through the union of the catheter with the connection extensions. It was not an impediment to continue using it.
- Hospitalization in October-07 due to catheter infection, resolved with antibiotic therapy.
- In November 07, the blood leak increased significantly, which was solved with a sterile silicone seal and placing a PTFE sleeve around the leak point.
- In March 2009 the blood leak occurred again, resolved with the same sealing procedure and PTFE sheath.
- On 11-30-09 he escaped again, a new sealing attempt failing.
• On 3-22-09 he required the insertion of a temporary catheter in the right femoral artery for a few days.
• On 12-17-09 a new transthoracic replacement was performed by thoracotomy, placing another Split-Cath catheter.
-As a complication, he presented post-surgery cardiogenic shock, and was then diagnosed with moderate-severe aortic valve stenosis.
- The catheter gave an inadequate flow, demonstrating that the distal ends were located in the suprahepatic vein.
- On 3-25-10 it was repositioned in hemodynamics, leaving the distal ends of the catheter in the inferior cava.
- On 9/24/10, again due to catheter dysfunction, it was repositioned in hemodynamics, leaving the distal ends lodged in the right atrium.
• On 3-22-10 he required the insertion of a temporary catheter in the right femoral artery for a few days.
Since the last episode on 9-24-10 and until today (02-10-2012), the patient is dialyzed at his dialysis center through the transthoracic catheter, without incident and with adequate catheter function.
