A 73-year-old woman whose personal history reported only bronchial asthma that she treated with bronchodilators as needed. She did not report cardiovascular risk factors. The patient went to the hospital due to bronchial hyperreactivity secondary to respiratory infection. In her anamnesis she reported oppressive back pain with sweating and dyspnea for 8 months that was triggered by moderate exertion, which subsided with rest and which had not changed in all this time. On auscultation her heart was rhythmic, she had a third heart sound, a fluctuating systolic and diastolic murmur, and signs of heart failure. Lung auscultation revealed rhonchi and wheezing compatible with the bronchial hyperreactivity that led to admission. The analysis showed anemia with a slight increase in acute phase reactants. Cardiac biomarkers were negative. The ECG was normal and the chest x-ray showed cardiomegaly, not present in a previous one from a year ago. With the initial suspicion of bronchial hyperreactivity, stable exertional angina and secondary heart failure, an echocardiogram was requested, which showed a 4 x 5 cm mass dependent on the left atrium and that, starting from the fossa ovalis, protruded in diastole towards the ventricle, producing a stenosis. mitral. Subsequently, a cardiac catheterization was performed in which no angiographically significant coronary lesions were observed. With suspicion of atrial myxoma, the patient underwent surgery, confirming the diagnosis by histology. Four months after the intervention, the patient is asymptomatic and without signs of recurrence.
