Patient EJO, brunette, female, 23 years old, was referred to the Oral-maxillo-facial Surgery Service of the Faculty of Dentistry of Piracicaba (FOP/UNICAMP) for removal of a lesion in the lingual region of lower anterior teeth. She herself had a clinical diagnosis of neurofibromatosis.
On physical examination, facial asymmetry and hundreds of skin nodules were observed throughout the body, including the face and neck. Café au lait spots were present on the trunk and upper limbs.

The intraoral examination revealed malocclusion, diastemas, missing teeth, and a neurofibromatous-looking mass in the region of the lower alveolar ridge, lingual to the incisors. The lesion was firm and painless to palpation, with normal mucosal color, not ulcerated, with a sessile base and approximately 2.5 cm in extension. Due to its size and its relationship with the teeth, it did not allow adequate hygiene of the region, favoring the accumulation of calculus. The tongue also showed increased volume, with the presence of a neurofibromatous lesion with diffuse limits.

The radiographic examination revealed the presence of retained element 23, as well as right mandibular hypoplasia.

The procedure adopted for the neurofibromatous lesion present in the lower anterior region was surgical excision. The lesion in the lingual region was not treated surgically due to the fact that it did not present functional or aesthetic compromise for the patient. The diagnosis of neurofibromatosis was confirmed by pathological analysis.

The patient has six months of post-operative follow-up, with no complaints and no apparent sign of lesion recurrence. Currently, the patient is undergoing periodontal treatment, with a significant improvement in the conditions of the local supporting tissues.
