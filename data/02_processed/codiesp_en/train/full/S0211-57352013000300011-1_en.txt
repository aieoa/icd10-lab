History and appearance of Capgras delirium after Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH)
59-year-old patient, graduate in exact sciences and former high school teacher. She is the eldest of three siblings. She describes her happy childhood, she was very close to her father, she was an outgoing, sociable and independent child. Intellectually brilliant, she soon lived independently, moving cities several times according to professional requirements. She debuted with a psychotic break with paranoid ideation at the age of 30 after her wedding plan was suspended. The manic and depressive episodes alternate, requiring some of them to be admitted to hospital, although they are able to lead a normal life without being affected by work between periods up to the third of their admissions in 2005, motivated by a depressive episode and coinciding with the recent death of their father. moment at which the patient requests work incapacity (absolute ILP granted in 2006). Her relapses always had to do with abandoning Lithium treatment. Abusive alcohol consumption is also observed during this time. The diagnosis until December 2009: "Bipolar Disorder with congruent and non-congruent psychotic symptoms, with remission" and "Alcohol abuse." Since she became independent she barely has any relationship with her parents and siblings, due to her lifestyle and manic symptoms she is considered the "black sheep" of the family.
In 2009 she suffered a Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH). The CT scan reveals left temporoparietal involvement that is surrounded by extensive edema; mass effect on the ipsilateral ventricular system and acute left parietal hematoma with severe impairment of the level of consciousness, sensitivity, motor skills and speech. When she wakes up from her coma, she says that the first thing she sees is the face of a former fellow teacher with whom she had a relationship of affection. After this she talks about how her brothers "are not her brothers," attributing the change to the professor's friend. The DC is extendable to friends and acquaintances. Abandoned by her partner at that time, it will be her brother who takes responsibility for her care.
Evolution of the case after HSA. She subsequently required several hospital admissions due to decompensation of psychiatric symptoms; she practically stops having asymptomatic periods. In the acute care unit prior to admission to the EMU, she presented: potomania, psychotic decompensation with paranoid-type delusional ideation, kinesthetic hallucinations, social isolation and disorganized thinking, hyperthymic mood, insomnia, and slight disinhibition. She was withdrawn from lithium salts due to nephropathy secondary to diabetes insipidus, and valproic acid was instituted. The diagnosis: 'Mixed Schizoaffective Disorder, with congruent and non-congruent psychotic symptoms'; 'Alcohol abuse'. In February 2012 she entered the UME to start an 'individualized rehabilitation plan'.
• The doctor-nurse evaluation confirmed moderate functional limitation with generalized hypotonia. History of hypothyroidism and NID-DM, both in remission. Potomania and enuresis secondary to episodes of nephrogenic diabetes insipidus that were corrected with self-recording and thiazides.
• The neuropsychological examination (WAIS IV) showed cognitive impairment in both working memory and processing speed, although the levels reflect a "normal" level, around an IQ of 100, which was previously assumed to be much higher based on their academic history. and professional. He exhibits socially inappropriate verbal behaviors that would suggest frontal involvement, perhaps consistent with the long history of abusive alcohol consumption.
• Psychopathological evaluation: Presence of several delusions, of body influence, of telepathy and CD, and some simple hallucinatory phenomenon. The same teacher who causes identity theft also throws things at him and pushes him. It should be noted that although at the beginning he expressed some hostility towards the "brother's double", the feelings became positive, in fact he now gets along much better with him than before the episode of the HSA. Psychopathological stabilization is achieved with Depakine 1300 mg/day, Olanzapine 20 mg/day, Risperidone 6 mg/day, Vitamin B Complex. He participates in the "treatment adherence group". Discharged in December 2012. Residual positive psychotic symptoms and partial insight.
