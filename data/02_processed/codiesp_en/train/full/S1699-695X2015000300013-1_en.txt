This is a 46-year-old male patient with no relevant history, who came to the clinic due to sudden loss of strength in his lower limbs. This condition occurs repeatedly and episodically, self-limiting within a few hours. It generally affects the lower limbs, although it can be generalized. He refers to previous episodes that have been increasing in frequency and intensity in the last year. During the directed interrogation, he reported a weight loss of 20 kilos in the last four months without a decrease in intake, occasional tremors, irritability and nervousness.
During the physical examination, no decrease in strength in the limbs or alterations in deep tendon reflexes was detected. He had a stable, non-pathological gait, without dysmetries. According to what he said, he had progressively recovered muscle strength a few minutes ago. A fine distal tremor was discovered, without exophthalmos. The heart rate was 120 beats per minute, and upon palpation there was an enlarged thyroid without palpable nodules. An emergency analysis was performed which detected: creatinine 0.59 mg/dl (normal 0.60-1.35), sodium 142 mEq/L (135-145), potassium 2.07 mEq/l (3 .5-5), magnesium 1.64 mg/dl (1.7-2.5), calcium 8.01 mg/dl (8.50-10.50), ionic calcium 3.95 mg/dl (4 ,1-5.3), total proteins 6.47 mg/dl (6.00-8.50). A picture of loss of strength coinciding with hypokalemia guided us towards the diagnosis of periodic paralysis. Given the presence of characteristic semiology of thyroid pathology, a hormonal study was requested, finding primary hyperthyroidism of autoimmune origin with the following parameters: FT4 4.07 ng/dl (0.9-1.7), TSH < 0.008 mcgU/ l (0.4-4.8), FT3 15.58 pg/ml (2.3-4.2), antithyroglobulin antibodies 62.7 IU/ml (0-60), antiperoxidase antibodies 1269 IU/ml (0 -60), TSI 5.7 IU/l (0-1.75). Vitamin D and PTH were normal (28 ng/mL [20-55] and 26 pg/mL [11-80], respectively). The first complementary test that we should request in case of hyperthyroidism is a thyroid scan, which in our case showed diffuse hyperuptake. All these data pointed us to Graves' disease as the cause of the condition. In the acute phase, treatment is started with 40 mEq of intravenous potassium, achieving normalization of the figures (4.1 mEq/L), and at home, propranolol 10 mg and methimazole 30 mg daily are proposed with progressive dose reduction. At present, he maintains treatment with methimazole 5 mg per day with partial remission of symptoms and normal thyroid function, without having presented new episodes of paralysis.
