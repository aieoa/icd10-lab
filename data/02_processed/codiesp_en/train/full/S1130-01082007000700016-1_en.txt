A 60-year-old male patient with a history of several episodes of acute pancreatitis of alcohol origin. He presented with fever, general condition, abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting. He was admitted to the Emergency Department where the existence of hemodynamic instability with sinus tachycardia at 130x' and arterial hypotension (BP: 80/50 mmHg) was observed, which is why he was transferred to the Intensive Care Unit (ICU).
In the physical examination upon his arrival to the ICU, he highlighted the existence of a large abdominal distension, with a diffusely painful abdomen and the palpation of a mass at the epigastric level that extended towards the left hypochondrium. In the blood tests carried out, leukocytosis with a significant left shift, hyperglycemia, and a slight elevation of both bilirubin levels and liver enzymes were observed. After his stabilization, an abdominal CT scan was performed, observing a pancreatic pseudocyst measuring 17 x 8 centimeters in diameter that displaced the intestinal loops and compressed the stomach.

Transgastric drainage was attempted using endoscopic ultrasound but was not possible since the compression exerted by the mass made it impossible for the endoscope to progress. Likewise, several attempts at percutaneous drainage were made, all of which were unsuccessful. For this reason, it was decided to perform a surgical evacuation by proceeding to marsupialization of the pseudocyst through cystogastrostomy, obtaining a total of 4 liters of purulent material. The subsequent evolution was satisfactory and he was discharged from the ICU 6 days after admission.
