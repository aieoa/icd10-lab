A 76-year-old man with stage 4 CKD secondary to polycystic hepatorenal disease with a history of long-standing arterial hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, hyperuricemia, dyslipidemia, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. Given the situation of advanced CKD and after explaining the different dialytic techniques, a 1-cuff non-self-positioning straight peritoneal catheter was inserted through open surgery without immediate incidents and with good functioning during the training period. One month after catheter placement, continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) was started at home with a schedule of 3 exchanges of 2 liters of 1.5% dextrose, initially with neutral or negative balances of 200-300 ml. Four days after starting treatment at home, he went to the Peritoneal Dialysis Unit reporting symptoms of genital edema, with no other associated symptoms. After a testicular ultrasound was performed, pathology at that level was ruled out. If a leak is suspected, a computed axial tomography (CT)-peritoneography is performed, after the administration (through the catheter) of 100 ml of hypoosmolar iodinated contrast (Optiray® 300 mg/ml), confirming the passage of peritoneal contrast material to the through the spermatic cord to the scrotum due to the presence of a patent, although not dilated, peritoneovaginal process. Likewise, the existence of an ipsilateral left indirect inguinal hernia with a sac up to 58 mm in diameter is also noted. Given these findings, it was decided to perform peritoneal rest and surgery was indicated to correct the inguinal hernia and close the peritoneovaginal duct. With these measures, and after restarting peritoneal dialysis with low volumes, one month after the reintervention no new leak was observed.
