A 65-year-old man with a history of two sessions of photodynamic therapy with Verteporfin (PDT) and proposed for a third comes for evaluation. His current visual acuity is 0.7 in the right eye (OD). It is a unique eye since the left eye (LE) is in phthisis bulbi due to a traumatic history that occurred in his childhood. The patient reported no changes since the onset of the condition and had not noticed a change in his visual acuity before having the PDT sessions performed. The funduscopic image reveals a raised vitelliform lesion in the foveal area with hard drusen in the posterior pole. Fluorescein angiography (FGA) shows a progressive and intense ring hyperfluorescence in late periods with a central hypofluorescent area. No interesting alterations were observed in indocyanine green angiography (IGB). The OCT shows the presence of a wedge-shaped structure of medium-high reflectivity between the pigmented epithelium and the neurosensory retina with a detachment of the neurosensory retina bordering the lesion. An electrooculogram was performed and its results were within normal limits.
The patient is diagnosed with DFVA and it is decided to perform periodic check-ups. In the controls at 3, 6 and 12 months, the test results show no variation compared to the first visit and his visual acuity remains stable.
At the 24-month checkup, his visual acuity is 0.02. In the fundus, the vitelliform lesion is not observed but rather a rounded orange lesion in the foveal area. In both AGF and AVI there is intense hypofluorescence maintained throughout the angiographic time. The OCT shows a normal foveal depression with absence of the medium reflectivity mass seen in the first visit and an increase in reflectivity later at the level of the pigmented epithelium-choriocapillaris complex.
