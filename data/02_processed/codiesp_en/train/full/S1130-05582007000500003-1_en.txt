A 59-year-old Caucasian male was referred to our Service complaining of lancinating pain in the left mandibular body as well as lip-mental hypoesthesia on that same side for a month. The intraoral examination was normal. A radiographic study was performed using orthopantomography and CT that revealed the presence of a well-defined osteolytic lesion of about 2.5 cm in diameter located at the level of the left mandibular ascending ramus. A biopsy of the lesion was performed. The histological study showed an image compatible with a metastatic lesion of adenocarcinoma, probably of the mucosecretory type. A bone scan was then performed which showed areas of hyperuptake in the left mandibular ascending ramus, left vertebral body at the level of T12, right 8th rib and in the costochondral junction of the right 6th rib. Thoraco-abdominal CT showed a large mass affecting the body and gastric antrum, with regional lymphadenopathy and a nodule in the left upper pulmonary lobe. A combined treatment with radio and chemotherapy was administered. The patient died 2 months later. The autopsy confirmed the gastric origin of the metastasis.
