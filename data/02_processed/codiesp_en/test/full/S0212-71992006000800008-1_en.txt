A 50-year-old man with a history of high blood pressure under treatment and an appendectomy at age 25, consulted for epigastralgia, melena, and asthenia for the past fifteen days. He did not report dysphagia, heartburn or altered bowel habits.
The physical examination was normal, no masses or visceromegaly were palpable in the abdomen, nor were there any signs of malnutrition.
The analysis revealed the presence of hypochromic microcytic anemia with Hgb of 10 gr/dl and Hct of 33%. Chest and abdominal radiographs showed no interesting changes. In the upper digestive endoscopy, a normal esophagus was observed, a gastric cavity without blood remains with a normal mucosa and erosions on an edematous mucosa in the duodenal bulb. Biopsies were taken in the antrum and body, and the biopsies were reported as chronic gastritis without activity, and no activity was detected. helicobacter pylori. In the abdominal ultrasound, the liver, gallbladder and pancreas were normal.
With the diagnosis of upper gastrointestinal bleeding due to erosive duodenitis and anemia secondary to digestive losses, he was discharged for outpatient follow-up with treatment with antisecretors. The patient was readmitted three weeks later due to a new episode of melena, a new upper digestive endoscopy was performed, revealing a normal esophagus, mucosa of the gastric cavity and duodenal bulb, with isolated blood remains at the level of the second portion of the duodenum, so He decided to perform a pulse enteroscopy, observing a friable and ulcerated tumor in the proximal jejunum that partially narrowed the lumen of the jejunum and did not allow the passage of the endoscope, but multiple biopsies were taken at the edges of the tumor. An intestinal transit was performed in which a short stenosis was evident in the proximal jejunum, which allowed the passage of contrast.

The extension study was completed by performing an abdominal CT in which the presence of a mass in the proximal jejunum was revealed without detecting locoregional or retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy of significant size, no focal liver lesions suggestive of metastasis, nor other evaluable radiological alterations were observed. . With the diagnosis of low-grade leiomyosarcoma of the small intestine and after ruling out distant tumor extension or local invasion in imaging studies, surgical treatment was decided with tumor resection and subsequent duodenal-jejunal anastomosis. The patient's evolution was satisfactory, requiring no adjuvant treatment.
