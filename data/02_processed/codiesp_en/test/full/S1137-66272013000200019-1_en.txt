A 12-year-old girl with a history of prominent ears treated by otoplasty at age 8. A keloid progressively developed in both ears one year after the intervention. The keloids invaded the upper edge and posterior surface of the upper poles. The right one had a horizontal size of 3.2 cm and a vertical size of 2 cm, and the left one had a size of 2.7 cm x 1.5 cm respectively. The patient did not report pain or pruritus. Excision was indicated given the large size of the lesions, with aesthetic and psychological repercussions. The intervention was performed under general anesthesia. The surgical treatment consisted of the following technique: an incision was made on the posterosuperior surface of the keloid, from which a thin flap of the keloid skin covering was dissected, maintaining a pedicle attached to the normal skin on the anterior surface of the keloid. ear. After raising the flap of an adequate size, the rest of the keloid was removed. The flap was sutured to the defect without tension. The size of the flaps was 2.5 cm x 1.5 cm in the right ear, and 2 cm x 1 cm in the left ear. In the pathological study, it was found that the keloid nodules were covered by epidermis, and atrophy of the skin annexes was observed. The surgery was associated with other treatments, commonly used to prevent recurrence: intraoperative injection of corticosteroid (triamcinolone acetonide) into the surgical bed, application of silicone gel to the resulting scars, and pressure therapy with an elastic band. Silicone gel and pressotherapy were used for one year after the intervention. The patient was monitored periodically.

The right ear flap presented a small suture dehiscence, which healed by secondary intention a few weeks later using topical treatment. No necrosis or other complications developed. A satisfactory repair of the defects was achieved, with restoration of the normal contour of the ears. A recurrence developed in the right ear 5 years after the intervention, forming a 1.5 cm x 1 cm keloid in the operated area, which was treated with the same surgical procedure. There has been no recurrence in the left ear. At 18 years of age, both ears look good and the patient undergoes periodic check-ups.
