A 58-year-old woman diagnosed with seropositive, erosive and nodular rheumatoid arthritis (RA) with high titers of rheumatoid factor for 15 years. She had received previous treatment with different disease-modifying drugs (DMARDs): hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine, cyclosporine and leflunomide, which were withdrawn due to ineffectiveness. She was following background treatment with methotrexate 25mg/weekly/SC, prednisone 5-10mg/day and infliximab 5mg/kg/IV/8 weeks with acceptable control of the disease in the last 2 years with low activity without achieving complete remission. .
During her evolution, she presented septic arthritis of the right ankle with a positive synovial fluid culture for Staphylococcus aureus. She was withdrawn from infliximab and started on intravenous antibiotic therapy.
At 3 months she consulted for red eye, intense periorbital pain and photophobia. She presents with a crescentic upper peripheral corneal infiltrate with extreme stromal thinning associated with scleritis. Culture of corneal scraping negative. Given the suspicion of severe QUP, treatment was started with oral prednisone 1 mg/kg/day without response. Megadoses of methylprednisolone 1g/IV/3 days are prescribed. In addition, a surgical resection of the conjunctival tissue adjacent to the PUC and cyanoacrylate adhesive were performed to prevent ocular perforation. The patient responded poorly to treatment, with persistent pain, scleritis, and corneal infiltrate. As there was also no optimal control of her underlying disease, we decided to start perfusion of rituximab 1g/i.v. two infusions 15 days apart. Three weeks after the start of treatment, significant clinical improvement was observed, presenting inactive-looking corneal leukoma without scleritis. She is currently on treatment with rituximab i.v. on AR regimen every 8 months with good clinical control.
