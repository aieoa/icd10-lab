A 38-year-old man, with hepatitis B as the only personal history of interest, presented with 11 months of urinary frequency and urgency accompanied by terminal hematuria without clots.
On physical examination, his abdomen was soft and depressible without visceromegaly or other alterations. On rectal examination, the prostate had volume I/IV, was elastic, poorly defined and almost flat. Normal genitalia.
Blood analysis with hemogram, biochemistry, coagulation and PSA within normal parameters. Urine analysis with pH 5.5. Urinary sediment with 15-20 leukocytes/field and abundant mucus. Sterile urine culture.
Urine cytology was negative for malignant tumor cells and no alterations were found in the renal, bladder and prostate ultrasound, with the prostate being homogeneous, with well-defined contours, measuring 17 x 20 mm.
Intravenous urograms showed normal kidneys and ureters, and displacement of the bladder to the left and superiorly by a subvesical pelvic mass (without noticeable bladder filling defects). Cystourethrography revealed extrinsic bladder compression.

In the cystoscopy performed, no endovesical alterations were observed. The abdominopelvic CT scan revealed a large pelvic mass anterior to the bladder, measuring 12 cm in diameter, with well-defined contours, which displaced the bladder backwards and to the left, and the intestinal loops cranially, with a fatty plane of separation between the mass and bladder. After the administration of contrast, the mass enhanced its density.

Since its origin was not clear, MRI was performed, confirming that its measurements were 8x12x13cm, well delimited, located in front of the bladder and rejecting these towards the back and to the left. The mass formed lobulations and was delimited by a thin capsule. The suspected diagnosis in the MRI was urachal fibroadenoma due to its lobulated appearance and the presence of fibrous tissue with nodular areas that enhanced with IV contrast injection.

The FNA diagnosis was 'morphologically benign tumor, probably adenomatoid'.
Given the findings described above, it was decided to remove the tumor. In bimanual examination, the mass was mobilized to prostatic compression. Through a mid-infraumbilical incision and extraperitoneal access, a pelvic mass was identified located to the right of the bladder and that seemed to depend on the anterior prostatic surface. After opening the endopelvic fascia, the thin prostatic pedicle on which the mass depended was sectioned. The intraoperative biopsy was reported as a malignant epithelial tumor with intense reactive fibrosis. Prostatectomy was not performed pending a definitive diagnosis, since macroscopically it appeared benign.

The histopathological study described, macroscopically, a nodular tumor with a smooth surface measuring 12.5x12.5x7 cm, weighing 50 g. On one of its edges there was a thin pedicle 0.7 mm long. Microscopically, it was composed of a proliferation of spindle cells without atypia in variable amounts, adopting in some areas a hemangiopericytoid type pattern, associated with a great differentiation of thick collagen bundles that predominate in the tumor. There were very isolated foci of atypical cells with <4 mitoses/10 high-power fields. The pedicle presented arterial and venous structures surrounded by connective tissue within which nests with fibroblastic differentiation were identified. The immunohistochemical study showed positivity for CD34, D99 and vimentin, focally positive antidesmin and negative broad-spectrum keratins, cytokeratin 7, EMA, actin, enolase, S-100, chromogranin and p53. The tumor proliferation index expressed by Ki67 was less than 1%.
The final diagnosis was a solitary fibrous tumor.
After three years of follow-up, the patient has no recurrence of the disease on MRI and is asymptomatic.
