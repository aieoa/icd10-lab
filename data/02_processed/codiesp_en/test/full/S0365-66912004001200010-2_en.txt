A 62-year-old man presented to the emergency department with painless loss of vision in his left eye. His background highlights being under hematological treatment with hydroxyurea (Hydrea®, 500 mg, Bristol-Myers, Squibb) and acetylsalicylic acid (Adiro®, 300 mg, Bayer) for a year due to essential thrombocythemia with 1,200,000 platelets/mm3. In the ophthalmological examination, the corrected visual acuity of the RE was 0.6 and that of the LE was 0.02. Anterior pole biomicroscopy was normal, with applanation stress of 18 mmHg in the RE and 20 mmHg in the LE. The fundus examination of the LE revealed venous tortuosity with multiple cottony exudates juxtapapillary with the edematous optic nerve papilla and diffuse hemorrhages. Macular edema was also evident. The platelet count at that time was 556,000 platelets/mm3, the lowest figure reached during his evolution, with 9000 white cells/mm3, a hematocrit of 41% and a hemoglobin of 14%.
The bone marrow biopsy demonstrated hyperplastic erythropoiesis and myelopoiesis with mature and complete forms, highlighting an increase in dysplastic and atypical megakaryocytes.
A month later, the patient was admitted to the emergency room with a massive hemorrhage in the right cerebral hemisphere, dying 14 hours later.
