A 13-year-old female patient with no personal history of interest presented with bilateral eyelid edema of three days' duration. In the subsequent anamnesis, she reported accompanying headache with mild odynophagia, without associated fever. The physical examination revealed bilateral eyelid edema with no other ocular involvement, no edema in other locations and 2/4 tonsillar hypertrophy without exudates. In the following days she consulted again up to three times due to persistence and increase in eyelid edema, without the appearance of other symptoms. Urine dipsticks were performed on several occasions without detecting proteinuria and pharyngeal exudate culture which was negative. Given the clinical findings and the negativity of the tests carried out to date, an analytical study was requested with a complete blood count, blood biochemistry, liver profile and urine sediment with normal results except for leukocytosis (18,460/µl) with lymphocytic predominance (61.8%). ) and increased transaminases (GOT 139 IU/l, GPT 184 IU/l). Ten days after the onset of symptoms, the eyelid edema resolved without fever, lymphadenopathy or hepatosplenomegaly being evident throughout the course. Analytical control is performed after three weeks, finding a blood count without alterations and a decrease in transaminases to normal values ​​(GOT 22 UI/l, GPT 16 UI/l). Given the suspicion of mononucleosis syndrome, EBV and cytomegalovirus (CMV) serology is requested, obtaining positive IgM against EBV, which allows diagnostic confirmation.
