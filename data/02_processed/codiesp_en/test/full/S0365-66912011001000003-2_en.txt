The second case is that of a 10-year-old girl who was referred from another center for low vision with no apparent organic cause. The patient presented a report showing visual acuity of 0.01 in both eyes with a refraction under cycloplegia of + 1.00. All ophthalmological examinations were normal. In the complementary tests presented visual evoked potentials (VEP), electroretinogram (ERG), angiofluoresceinography (AFG), computerized axial tomography (CAT), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), no alteration was found either. Given the suspicion of malingering, we delved into the patient's family, school and social situation. She was the youngest of 4 siblings, with a large age difference, and in recent months she had begun to have bad behavior, with low grades at school, difficulty integrating with her classmates and, in addition, a sprain that He forced him to use crutches. It was decided to perform the confusion test with lenses: a + 6.00 lens was placed in a test frame and then we progressively neutralized it with negative lenses. Monocular VA was 0.8 and 0.6, respectively; and 0.8 in binocular vision. The stereopsis values ​​were normal, 40". Her parents were spoken to reassure them about the girl's situation, and she was referred back to the reference center for control and follow-up.
