We present the case of a 67-year-old woman who came to the emergency room due to bilateral conjunctival edema of 3 weeks' duration, after having been treated with artificial tears and topical antibiotic-corticoid ointment during this period of time without improvement. Her personal history included high blood pressure under pharmacological treatment, diabetes mellitus that was difficult to control, and recently diagnosed oral candidiasis.
The ophthalmological examination revealed bilateral conjunctival chemosis with intense tearing, and the rest of the ophthalmological examination was normal. The patient reported having lost 5 kg in the last 2 weeks, a feeling of generalized swelling especially in the face along with asthenia and generalized itching.

After a systemic examination she was diagnosed with suspected Cushing's syndrome. Hormonal analyzes revealed an ACTH of 3 pg/mL (normal 10-55), basal plasma cortisol of 35.3 mcg/dL and urinary free cortisol (ULC) of 632 mcg/24h (normal < 180). Chest x-ray and computed tomography (CT) were normal. The abdominal CT scan revealed a left adrenal mass measuring 8.2x5.4cm, suggestive of adrenal carcinoma, without locoregional lymphadenopathy.

Given the clinical evolution, it was decided to start treatment with ketoconazole 400 mg/day prior to surgery with the objective of inhibiting the secretion of adrenal cortisol, performing excision of the adrenal tumor together with dissection of the surrounding fat, presenting a complete resolution of the chemosis.

The histology of the adrenal tumor was reported as "carcinoma of the adrenal cortex with an extensive area of ​​necrosis with proliferative activity, which infiltrates the surrounding capsule, invading the pericapsular lymphatic vessels." After a first course of chemotherapy with cisplatin-etoposide, he was diagnosed, 3 months later, by a thoraco-abdomino-pelvic CT scan with multiple bilateral lung metastases in both hemithorax and a local recurrence at the left adrenal level, for which he received three cycles of chemotherapy. with cyclophosphamide, adriamycin and vincristine, without improvement, with progression of respiratory and cardiac failure, starting treatment with weekly taxol for 2 months (antineoplastic chemotherapy), dying due to the progressive respiratory failure he presented.
