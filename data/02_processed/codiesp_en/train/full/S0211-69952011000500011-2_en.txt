A 76-year-old woman with a history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, and depressive syndrome. She was being treated with metformin 850 mg/8 hours, escitalopram 20 mg/24 hours, mirtazapine 30 mg/24 hours, and simvastatin 40 mg/24 hours. In an analysis carried out six months earlier, she had a Cr of 0.81 mg/dl.
Two weeks before admission, she began treatment with ibuprofen (600 mg/8 hours) for low back pain. A week before, she had nausea, vomiting and diarrhea, so she went to the emergency room. On examination he had dry skin and mucous membranes and on analysis plasma Cr was 9 mg/dl, glucose 189 mg/dl, urea 196 mg/dl, hemoglobin 10.9 g/dl, sodium 125 mEq. /l, potassium of 8.6 mEq/l and lactic acid of 6.3 mmol/l. On arterial blood gas analysis she had a pH of 7.23 and bicarbonate of 15 mEq/l. An abdominal ultrasound was performed which revealed bilateral ureterohydronephrosis due to bladder neoplasia. Given the patient's hemodynamic instability, hemodialysis was performed for two hours, subsequently having a Cr of 5.9 mg/dl, potassium of 6.1 mEq/l, pH of 7.39 and bicarbonate of 22 mEq/l. Subsequently, a bilateral nephrostomy was placed with improvement in clinical symptoms and normalization of laboratory values ​​with Cr of 1.1 mg/dl, pH of 7.31 and bicarbonate of 23 mEq/l.
