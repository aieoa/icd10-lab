84-year-old woman diagnosed with depression; severe colonic diverticulosis; D8-D10 vertebral crushes; vitamin D deficiency; arterial and pulmonary hypertension; atrial flutter and mitral, tricuspid and aortic insufficiency, treated with sertraline (50 mg/day PO), furosemide (40 mg/day PO), bisoprolol (5 mg/day PO), acenocoumarol according to hematology guidelines, omeprazole (20 mg /day PO), simvastatin (20 mg/day PO), lormetazepam (1 mg/day PO), vitamin D (1 ampoule PO/month), domperidone (10 ml/8 hours PO) and home oxygen therapy.
In January 2016, she went to the hospital emergency room due to an episode of hematochezia, where she was diagnosed with bilateral lung pathology. A computed axial tomography (CT) of the chest was performed, which revealed the presence of extensive bilateral parenchymal pulmonary infiltrates, a calcified granuloma in the middle lobe of the lung, and subcarineal adenopathy (15x20 mm). Suspecting community-acquired bilateral multilobar pneumonia, she was prescribed clarithromycin (500 mg/day PO) and was discharged from the hospital.
In February 2016, the patient was admitted to the internal medicine service due to dyspnea and for the study of pulmonary infiltrates that had not resolved since hospital discharge despite antibiotic treatment. During admission, the Mantoux test and a bronchoscopy were performed, both with negative results, in addition to a CT scan that reflects bilateral airspace condensation, an interlobular adenopathy (15 mm) of possible malignant origin, several irregular nodules and a nodule. calcified (6.2 mm).
It was decided to withdraw treatment with sertraline and clarithromycin and the tests were repeated two weeks later, obtaining results that showed a decrease in pulmonary infiltrates and less air condensation. The patient experiences both objective and subjective clinical improvement, for which she is discharged.
Five days after discharge, the patient restarted treatment with sertraline at the direction of her primary care physician due to a possible reappearance of her depressive symptoms.
The patient went to the emergency room again in March 2016 due to dyspnea on minor exertion and oppressive centralthoracic pain that increased with deep inspiration. Through lung auscultation, a vesicular murmur with crackles at the right base was detected.
She is readmitted to internal medicine, where a chest x-ray is performed, which reveals the presence of smaller pulmonary infiltrates than in the previous admission. It was decided to withdraw sertraline for the second time, with which the patient regained her autonomous capacity, dyspnea and chest pain disappeared, and oxygen saturation increased to 93%.
After two weeks of interruption of treatment with sertraline, a control chest x-ray was performed, which confirmed the decrease in the extent of pulmonary infiltrates.
It should be noted that throughout the entire period the patient presented eosinophil levels within the normal range.
The patient's clinical evolution allowed the treatment of sertraline, simvastatin, omeprazole, domperidone and vitamin D to be definitively suspended, while maintaining the rest of the medication.
