This is a 53-year-old man with no relevant history who was admitted from the emergency room with pulmonary thromboembolism.
Given the suspicion of occult neoplasia and the presence of hematuria not previously evident, an abdominal ultrasound is performed where a right renal mass is evident and the study is completed with CT and MRI.

Both studies confirm the presence of a heterogeneous tumor that infiltrates the lower two thirds of the right kidney measuring approximately 10x10 cm. with involvement of the renal sinus and hilum, also showing tumor thrombosis of the right renal vein and infrahepatic cava. No lymphadenopathy or metastasis was evident.
He underwent surgery, performing an anterior radical nephrectomy with cavotomy to remove the thrombus and an extensive right aorto-caval lymphadenectomy.
The anatomical-pathological result was Fuhrman grade 2 clear renal cell carcinoma measuring 9 cm. with invasion of the renal hilum, perinephric fat and renal vein, without metastatic involvement of lymph nodes or the edges of said fat or the hilum, as well as free ureter. (Stage III, T3N0M0). The patient was discharged from the hospital on the sixth day.
Three months after the intervention, the patient reported mild pain and induration in the penis that had recently appeared. On palpation an indurated mass was observed.
An MRI of the pelvis was performed, which informed us of the existence of a mass that occupies and expands the left corpus cavernosum, compatible with metastasis from previous renal carcinoma.

A biopsy was taken of said lesion, the result of which confirmed the suspicion, with isolated nests of tumor cells compatible with metastasis of clear cell carcinoma being evident in the histological sections.
Given this diagnosis, we considered what would be the best therapeutic attitude for the patient, and we took into account the progressive increase in local pain, the patient's age and his general good condition. Therefore, a total penectomy was chosen until a disease-free surgical margin was confirmed intraoperatively.
One week after discharge, he was admitted to the Oncology service with symptoms of obtundation and motor and sensory alterations, and on the cranial CT he presented lesions in the cerebellum and right cerebral hemisphere compatible with metastasis. A chest CT was performed and multiple pulmonary nodules and bilateral paratracheal microadenopathy in relation to metastases also appeared.
The patient died nine months after the first intervention for his renal carcinoma, that is, six months after the diagnosis of the metastasis to the penis.
