23-year-old patient diagnosed with ileal Crohn's disease. Initially, she presented an inflammatory pattern that evolved to stenosis (occasional subocclusive crises) and later to perforation, leading to an abdominal perforation with fecaloid peritonitis that led to 3 surgical interventions during a stay abroad.
Due to numerous postoperative complications, short bowel syndrome and enteroenteric and enterocutaneous fistula, she was transferred to our hospital. Upon admission, she presented with low-grade fever, wound infection with skin dehiscence, and severe malnutrition. She has a protective ileostomy, fistulization directed at the ileocolic anastomosis with a Petzer catheter and abdominal drains.
Since admission, she is started on Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN), gradually introducing the contributions. Throughout the period, monitoring is carried out by the Nutrition Section of the Pharmacy Service. The NPT is prepared daily, adjusting the contributions according to the biochemical parameters and their clinical evolution.
After 7 days, she developed cholestasis, so it was decided to reduce and subsequently eliminate the lipid intake in TPN.
On day 68, TPN was withdrawn due to the onset of oral tolerance, and was reintroduced after 5 days due to persistent enterocutaneous fistula that caused fever, abdominal pain, and significant weight loss.
After removal of drains and the Petzer catheter and introduction of treatment with Infliximab, on day 85 she was discharged from the hospital, continuing with TPN at her home until she recovered her nutritional status necessary to undergo an intestinal reconstruction intervention. She is followed up on an outpatient basis by the Home Hospitalization Unit (HADO) and the Parenteral Nutrition Section of the Pharmacy Service.
The NPT is prepared daily in the Pharmacy Service, and staff from the HADO Unit are responsible for its administration, maintenance of the infusion line and clinical monitoring of the patient. Likewise, the patient and her family received instructions for handling the administration team. This allowed the patient to adapt the administration of TPN to her daily activity, despite intrinsic limitations. In addition, a general biochemistry was performed every week, the results of which have allowed us to monitor the patient and modify her contributions according to her needs.
During this period, the daily intake (mean ± standard deviation) of macronutrients was 12.2 ± 0.62 g of nitrogen and 1,400 ± 71 kcal non-protein. Regarding micronutrients, the average contributions were: sodium 92 ± 14.2 mEq, chlorine 82.6 ± 14.6 mEq, potassium 100 ± 36.4 mEq, phosphorus 10.6 ± 1.3 mEq, calcium 7 ± 0.03 mEq , magnesium 11.7 ± 0.06 mEq and zinc 3 mg. Once a week, trace elements (Mn, Cr, Cu, Se) are incorporated into the mixture.
Regarding the evolution of biochemical parameters (mean ± standard deviation): glucose 95 ± 15.1 mg/dl (range 70-100), urea 56 ± 25.1 mg/dl (range 10-50), creatinine 1 ± 0.18 mg/dl (range 0.6-1.2), natremia 136 ± 1.7 mg/dl (range 135-145), chloremia 94 ± 5.5mg/dl (range 96-110), potassium 3 ± 0.47 mg/dl (range 3.5-5.1), albumin 4 ± 0.3 mg/dl (range 3.55.2), total proteins 7 ± 2.9 mg/dl (range 6 .2-8.3), phosphataemia 4 ± 0.57 mg/dl (range 2.6-4.5), calcemia 10 ± 0.43 mg/dl (range 8.4-10.4), magnesemia 2 ± 0.49 mg/dl (range 1.6-2.5), GOT 70 ± 27.7 mg/dl (range 4-37), GPT 150 ± 89.4 mg/dl (range 10-50), alkaline phosphatase 291 ± 163.5 mg/dl (range 80-300).
After 7 and a half months at home with TPN, the patient with a BMI = 18.63 and a prealbumin of 32.9 mg/dl (range 20-40), underwent bowel reconstruction, progressing favorably and suspending the TPN at 9 days.
