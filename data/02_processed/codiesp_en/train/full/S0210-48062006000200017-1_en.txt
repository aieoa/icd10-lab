The patient was a 56-year-old man, with no relevant personal history, who consulted for self-limited episodes of monosymptomatic macroscopic hematuria lasting 2 months.
On rectal examination, the prostate was well delimited and had an adenomatous consistency (grade II/IV).
The complementary examinations carried out were the following:
- Hemogram, biochemistry, urine culture, urine cytology and sediment: within normality. PSA: 1.46 ng/ml.
- Urological ultrasound: bilateral renal sinus cysts. Normal bladder.
- Intravenous urography: pyelocalyceal distortion due to the aforementioned cysts. Patent ureters. Normal bladder. In the post-void film, the presence of a 'contrast leak' into the abdominal cavity was striking.
- Cystoscopy: small solid-looking mass located in the bladder dome.

Given these findings, transurethral resection (TUR) of the lesion was performed, with the histopathological result of adenocarcinoma infiltrating the bladder wall.
After this diagnosis, an extension study was performed using a chest x-ray (which was reported as normal) and an abdominopelvic CT scan, which revealed a tubular structure that extends from the bladder dome to the umbilicus with a thick wall, especially close to the bladder (persistent urachus with possible infiltration of the wall by tumor) in addition to a moderate amount of free fluid in the peritoneal cavity.

One week before definitive surgical treatment, the patient was admitted with high fever accompanied by urinary symptoms, abdominal pain, vomiting and anorexia.
On physical examination, the patient had abdominal distension with a certain degree of peritoneal irritation. The analysis revealed the presence of leukocytosis with neutrophilia. After starting broad-spectrum parenteral antibiotic treatment, the patient's symptoms improved except for abdominal distention. The abdominal ultrasound performed revealed a large amount of ascitic fluid with septa inside.
With the suspected diagnosis of urachal adenocarcinoma, a wide partial cystectomy was performed that included the bladder dome, a large mass at the level of the urachus and umbilicus, in addition to a pelvic lymphadenectomy. During the surgical procedure we observed a large amount of mucoid material infiltrating the entire peritoneal cavity (several samples were taken from different locations for histological study). The pathological diagnosis of the surgical specimen was mucinous adenocarcinoma of the urachus with extension to the urinary bladder and peritoneum. The histological report of the mucoid material sent was pseudomyxoma peritonei.

Currently, after 1 year of follow-up, the patient is asymptomatic and has a disease-free extension study.
