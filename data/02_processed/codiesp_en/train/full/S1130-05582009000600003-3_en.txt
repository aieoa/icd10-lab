The third patient is a 25-month-old male, who went directly to the maxillofacial surgery emergency room referred by his local pediatrician, presenting with the inability to close his mouth and drooling, as well as refusal of breakfast. The day before, while playing in the pool, the boy had suffered a fall and hit his nose, causing erosion in the nasal root. Based on the clinical symptoms and this history, the child was diagnosed with traumatic TMJ dislocation. During our examination we noticed pain in the preauricular region on both sides that worsened when closing the mouth. However, complete closure was possible without reduction maneuvers, and occlusion was good. The otoscopy was very painful, the "tragus sign" was positive, and the EAC was inflamed, presenting discrete peeling and suppuration. An x-ray of both TMJs was performed, which was normal. The patient was referred to the ENT Service, where after appropriate treatment he resolved the condition.
