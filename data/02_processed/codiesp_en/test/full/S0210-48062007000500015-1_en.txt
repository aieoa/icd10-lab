A 65-year-old woman with no personal history of interest was admitted for a 1-week history of high fever with no apparent focus, which is why a thoracoabdominal CT was performed, observing a large mass (9x8x7 cm) in the upper right renal pole, in the intimate area. contact with the liver. He did not report any other symptoms except pain and functional impotence in the left lower limb that had been ongoing for months and was followed up by the Neurosurgery Service and with a diagnosis, after spinal CT and Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (MRI), of degenerative pathology, L4-L5 canal stenosis. and spondyloarthrosis. She does not present constitutional syndrome or abdominal pain.

On physical examination, she had a soft, depressible, globular abdomen, without palpation of masses or megalias. No pain upon exploration. Negative bilateral renal fist-percussion.
Analytically, the biochemistry showed no alterations with a liver profile, total proteins and calcium in the normal range. Complete blood count with hemoglobin of 9.4 g/dL, and 22,500 leukocytes (N: 72.9%). Hemostasis without findings. Sterile blood cultures and urine cultures <10,000CFU/ml.
Broad-spectrum antibiotic and antipyretic treatment was instituted, despite which he had daily feverish peaks with clinical repercussions in the form of asthenia.
Given the CT findings, it was decided to perform MRI, confirming a solid mass in the upper right pole measuring 14X10X10 cm in diameter, with great signal heterogeneity and necrosis inside. The lesion extends upward towards the liver with signs of hepatic infiltration. The right renal vein and vena cava are free. There are retrocaval lymphadenopathy, pleural effusion and diaphragmatic thickening.

Given the liver involvement, the case was discussed with the General and Digestive Surgery service, agreeing to perform Multislice CT to more accurately delimit the level of involvement of said organ and planning the surgical intervention, appreciating a heterogeneous mass with a necrotic appearance and a maximum diameter of 11 cm in the upper half of the right kidney without plane of separation of the postero-superior segments of the right hepatic lobe, with involvement due to their contiguity.

After renal embolization, surgical intervention was performed through supra- and infraumbilical median laparotomy. The examination shows a renal tumor infiltrating the liver (segment VII) and diaphragm, observing pathological lymphadenopathy in the renal hilum and free peritoneal fluid with no evidence of peritoneal implants. Peritoneal fluid samples were taken for culture, cytology and gram with negative results. Right radical nephrectomy was performed, including hepatic segment VII, right adrenal, and portion of the diaphragm en bloc. Cholecystectomy and lymphadenectomy.
The pathological analysis reveals a conventional renal cell cancer of high cytological grade (Fuhrman grade 4), with foci of sarcomatoid transformation and extensive areas of tumor necrosis. Two satellite nodules on the peritoneal surface of the diaphragm and metastasis in a lymph node of the renal hilum. No involvement of the renal venous or excretory system or the adrenal gland. pT4G3N1 stage according to the UICC.
After a satisfactory postoperative period with a progressive decrease in leukocytosis, oral chemotherapy treatment with angiogenesis inhibitors was started. In the second control CT performed two months after the intervention, right pleural effusion with basal and posterior pleural nodular lesions was evident. Pleural fluid cytology is compatible with adenocarcinoma.
