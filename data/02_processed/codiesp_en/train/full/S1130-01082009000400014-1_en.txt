A 73-year-old woman was admitted for elective surgery for gastric adenocarcinoma after neodjuvant chemotherapy, undergoing a subtotal gastrectomy with reconstruction using Roux-en-Y gastrojejunostomy. As a previous history, there was a probable allergy to vitamin B (Neuromade®). In the postoperative period, she presented a fistula of the gastrojejunal anastomosis managed conservatively, with cutaneous externalization that required the initiation of parenteral nutrition (PNT) without thiamine intake. One month later she presented a neurological deterioration consisting of disorientation, bradypsychia, tendency to sleep, horizontal nystagmus and ophthalmoparesis that forced her admission to the intensive care unit for intradermal thiamine testing. This was negative and controlled administration of thiamine was carried out with complete recovery of the clinical picture.
