The case of a 40-year-old patient, polydrug addict since adolescence, UDVP, with a first admission to prison at the age of 20, and with repeated subsequent admissions, is presented. In 1993, coinfection with HIV and HCV was detected. His medical history records the prescription of the first ART in 1998, punctuated by constant abandonment, especially during the periods spent in freedom. Other pathologies of interest:
- pneumonia due to pneumocystis jirovecii (formerly called carinii) in 1998
- recurrent community-acquired pneumonia
- oropharyngeal and probably esophageal candidiasis, of a repetitive nature
- Pulmonary TB and brain lesions due to toxoplasmosis and/or TB
- practically constant seborrheic dermatitis
In August 2009, he presented crusty, scaly, non-pruritic lesions that initially appeared on the scalp, ears, and armpits. These lesions subsequently extended to the trunk and extremities, including the soles and palms of the feet and hands. Similar lesions also appeared in the intergluteal fold and genitals.
The dermatologist diagnosed the condition as psoriasis, prescribing treatment with calcipotriol plus betamethasone, salicylic petrolatum and tracolimus 0.1%. This treatment was not started until a new admission to prison, at the beginning of 2010. In recent weeks, a slight improvement has been observed. At the beginning of the condition, the number of CD4+ lymphocytes cells/ml was 64 with an HIV VL of 1,090,000 copies/ml (6.04 log). He also had significant cachexia (BMI < 16) and anemia (Hbne 10 g/dl; Hct. 30%). The patient has refused ART. Currently, he is admitted to hospital to study the possible etymology of brain injuries.
