A 58-year-old male with a body surface area of ​​2.02 m_ with ischemic heart disease and chronic renal failure secondary to nephroangiosclerosis. Informed about dialytic techniques, he opted for PD as he desired home treatment. On January 23, 2004, a self-positioning PD catheter was surgically implanted without incident. A preoperative chest x-ray showed no pleural effusion or other notable abnormalities.

On March 30, 2004, continuous outpatient PD (CAPD) was started with 3 daily exchanges of 2,000 cc at 1.36%. At 48 hours the patient reported drainage of 2,500 cc after 8 hours. of stay, so the regimen was modified to 2 daily exchanges with the same volume and glucose to minimize the negative effect of excessive ultrafiltration on residual renal function (RRF). After 1 week in CAPD, on April 6, 2004, he consulted for dyspnea on medium exertion with orthopnea of ​​48 hours duration. He had abundant urine output, drainage of 2,300-2,400 cc per exchange, and stable weight. The examination revealed a decrease in vesicular murmur at the right lung base, compatible with the existence of pleural effusion at that level, which was confirmed on a chest x-ray.
With a suspected diagnosis of hydrothorax in CAPD, a right diagnostic thoracentesis was performed, which showed a light yellow fluid with proteins < 1 g/dl, 17 leu/mm3, 40 heme/mm3 and 197 mg/dl of glucose for a blood glucose level of 108 mg/mm. dl. Upon confirmation of the diagnosis of hydrothorax, CAPD was suspended and the patient was kept ambulant and off dialysis given his good RRF. The symptoms improved the next day and a check-up carried out a week later confirmed the absence of pleural effusion.
On April 20, 2004, after 2 weeks without PD, the patient was admitted and CAPD was restarted in the recumbent position with 6 exchanges/day of 1,000 cc at 1.36% to minimize intraperitoneal pressure, without initial recurrence of hydrothorax. . After 4 days without problems, the infusion volume was increased to 1,500 cc and ambulation was allowed. On April 27, 2004 he was discharged. After 3 days at his home and 10 after restarting CAPD, he went to the hospital again with a recurrence of the right hydrothorax. On this occasion, and after stopping CAPD immediately, a hemodialysis (HD) catheter was implanted in the right femoral vein. The good evolution of the case during the first days after restarting CAPD encouraged us to make a new attempt to solve the problem through peritoneal rest, this time longer.
On May 31, 2004, after a month of peritoneal rest, he restarted CAPD again with 5 daily exchanges of 1,000 cc at 1.36% and absolute bed rest, this time the hydrothorax recurred only 48 hours later. CAPD was again suspended and HD was restarted. Given the ineffectiveness of conservative treatment, and in agreement with the patient, pleurodesis with talc was indicated, which the thoracic surgeons performed on June 18, 2004 without subsequent incidents or problems. A chest x-ray performed 4 days after thoracentesis demonstrated the absence of pleural effusion. After keeping the patient on HD for 5 more weeks as recommended by the thoracic surgeons, on July 26, 2004 he restarted CAPD with 4 exchanges/day of 1500 cc at 1.36%, this time without incident. Currently, 15 months after restarting CAPD after pleurodesis, the patient continues on CAPD without recurrence of hydrothorax, uses 4 exchanges/day of 2 liters at 1.36% and is truly satisfied with being able to maintain the type of dialysis he had. chosen one.
