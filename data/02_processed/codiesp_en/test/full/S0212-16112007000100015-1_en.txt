A 72-year-old male patient whose personal history included having been diagnosed with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), high blood pressure, type II diabetes mellitus, and chronic ischemia in the lower extremities. He was being treated with metformin, acarbose and glinclazide. He presented a 72-hour history whose onset was characterized by the existence of diffuse abdominal pain accompanied by diarrhea and anorexia, with subsequent deterioration in the level of consciousness. He was treated at a secondary hospital where the existence of evidence of acute renal failure, significant metabolic acidosis and hemodynamic instability was observed, which is why it was decided to transfer him to the ICU of our hospital.
Upon arrival at our Unit, the patient was conscious although prone to sleep, tachycardic (heart rate of 120 beats per minute in sinus rhythm), hypotensive (blood pressure 80/40 mmHg) and with indirect signs of low cardiac output in the physical exploration. Among the analytical determinations carried out upon admission, the existence of acute renal failure with creatinine and urea levels of 6.6 mg/dl and 197 mg/dl respectively, and severe lactic acidosis with pH: 6.8, deficiency of bases -29 mmol/l, GAP anion of 41 and lactate determination of 176 mg/dl.
Suspecting that it was acute metformin poisoning, a blood sample was taken to determine its plasma levels and treatment was initiated consisting of hydroelectrolyte replacement, hemodynamic stabilization through the use of norepinephrine at a dose of 0. 4 mcg/kg/minute and dobutamine at a dose of 10 mcg/kg/minute and correction of the acidosis by intravenous administration of 1 M bicarbonate. Likewise, and in agreement with the Nephrology Service, it was decided to perform hemodialysis.
The patient's evolution was favorable, with a progressive disappearance of acidosis and normalization of renal function parameters that allowed the patient to be discharged on the ninth day of his admission to the ICU. The determination of metformin levels was 70.40 mg/l at the plasma level and 53.10 mg/l at the erythrocyte level.
