A 74-year-old male patient presented with recurrent symptoms of painful red eye in the LE, associated with a progressive loss of vision that had been going on for several months. He does not report any relevant systemic personal history. The ophthalmic history included accidental exposure to a cleaning product 15 years previously.
The ophthalmological examination revealed visual acuity (VA) of 8/10 in the right eye (RE) and 1/10 in the left eye (LE). The biomicroscopic examination revealed in LE the existence of an area of ​​approximately 210º of corneal conjunctivalization that affected the visual axis, with superficial vascularization and mixed hyperemia. There was a sector of limbus approximately 5 hours long that preserved the normal architecture of Vogt's palisades and an absence of staining with dyes. The rest of the examination showed no relevant findings.

With the clinical diagnosis of partial LI with involvement of the visual axis, an ECS of the fibrovascular tissue that invaded the corneal surface, including the affected limbal area, was performed, respecting the nasal limbus sector with normal characteristics. In the denuded area, an amniotic membrane (AM) graft was placed that was sutured with the epithelium facing up and subsequently another circular fragment of AM of 15 mm in diameter that was sutured as a patch, with the epithelium facing down. During follow-up, progressive reepithelialization occurred with an epithelium with corneal characteristics on the AM graft that remained integrated under it. Three weeks after surgery there was a smooth and regular epithelial corneal surface and a significant reduction in the associated mixed hyperemia. LE VA improved to 4/10. With a follow-up of 8 months, no recurrences have been observed.
