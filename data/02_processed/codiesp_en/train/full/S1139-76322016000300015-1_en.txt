Eight-day-old infant whose parents consulted for a poorly healed navel. As a personal background, she is the first daughter of healthy parents. She was born at 39 weeks of gestation by eutocic delivery; Her Apgar 9/10, weight 2830 grams. They do not report any history of interest, nor do they provide the hospital report.
The examination revealed a wide implantation of the umbilical cord, which had a mucous appearance, and a small tear at its base, with no signs of local infection. The rest of the physical examination is normal. She did not report any accompanying clinical manifestations. Silver nitrate is applied and an appointment is made for control in 48 hours.
During the control visit, they provide the discharge report after the birth. It reflects that the umbilical cord had a cyst, diagnosed in the prenatal ultrasound of the second trimester and described as a Wharton jelly cyst measuring 49 x 39 mm. At birth she was evaluated by a surgeon, who placed a clamp proximal to the cyst, which was resected. Two days after birth, an abdominal ultrasound was performed, which was normal. During admission she remained asymptomatic and was discharged with clinical normality, except for a small tear at the base of the cord, which presented no complications.
During the outpatient follow-up visit, an abundant flow of clear fluid from the center of the navel is observed, without signs of infection. He was referred for evaluation by pediatric surgery. The surgeon ligates the umbilical cord, which is almost detached and looks wet. The existence of a fistulous tract is confirmed. A new abdominal ultrasound reveals a tortuous tubular structure that communicates with the upper bladder wall and a diagnosis of persistence of the urachus (patent urachus type) is made and observation and subsequent reassessment are decided. At eight months of age, spontaneous closure occurred, and in subsequent controls the absence of urachus permeability was evident.
