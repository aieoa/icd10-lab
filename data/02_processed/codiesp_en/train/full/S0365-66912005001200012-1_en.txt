In our clinic, we reviewed a young 40-year-old male patient with loss of visual acuity in the left eye (LE) for 2 weeks and metamorphopsia for two years. The patient's best corrected visual acuity was 0.3 and in the fundus, in addition to some myelin fibers in the superior temporal arch, an MER centered on the macular region was observed, producing an image of a macular pseudohole. A marked distortion of the retinal blood vessels could be observed around the macula. There was no DPV evidence. The right eye was normal. Flurescein angiography was performed in which no macular edema or diffusion of the retinal vessels was observed. The patient did not report any trauma or previous pathology in said eye, so he was diagnosed with idiopathic ERM and included on the waiting list to perform pars plana vitrectomy.
A month later he went to the clinic reporting marked improvement in visual acuity and disappearance of metamorphopsia. A better corrected visual acuity of the unit was observed and in the fundus the absence of the ERM, disappearance of the distortion of the retinal vessels and a dense prepapillary VPD.
