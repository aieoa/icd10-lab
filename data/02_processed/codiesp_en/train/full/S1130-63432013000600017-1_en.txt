A 36-year-old male, with no relevant medical history, attended the Emergency Department due to paresthesia in both upper extremities, accompanied by drowsiness. Two hours before his admission, he referred the application of 270 g (9 cans of 30 g) of EMLA® anesthetic cream (lidocaine 2.5%, prilocaine 2.5%) for laser hair removal on the back, upper extremities and lower. The patient was conscious and oriented, with a tendency to sleep and had cyanosis. He had a blood pressure of 144/83 mmHg, heart rate of 82 bpm and a baseline SaO2 of 91%, so oxygen therapy was started until SaO2 was 94%. The rest of the exploration was normal. In complementary tests (chest X-ray and electrocardiogram) no significant pathological alterations were found.
In the admission analysis (4 hours after the application of EMLA®, see Table 1), a methemoglobin level of 10.1% was noted, for which he was diagnosed with severe methemoglobinemia secondary to the topical administration of EMLA® (lidocaine 2.5%). , prilocaine 2.5%). Administration of 100 mg of methylene blue was started intravenously over 10 minutes. One hour after the administration of the antidote, the methemoglobin level remained elevated (2.7%), also presenting alkalosis, for which it was evaluated by the Intensive Medicine Service. After transfer to said unit, he continued oxygen therapy in a high-flow mask, improving skin-mucosal coloration and recovering SaO2 to 96%. After 24 hours after the application of EMLA®, the patient remained hemodynamically stable and the methemoglobin level decreased to 0.2%, so it was decided to transfer him to the Internal Medicine Service, where, given his favorable evolution, he was discharged, at three days after admission.
