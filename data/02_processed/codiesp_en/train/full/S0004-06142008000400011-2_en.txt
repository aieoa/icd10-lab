A 61-year-old patient with a history of radical prostatectomy with staging obturator lymphadenectomy for moderately differentiated prostate carcinoma (Gleason score 5), with limited microscopic involvement of the resection margins. Three months after prostate surgery, she consulted due to an increase in right testicular volume, with negative testicular tumor markers, a mass with irregular areas on ultrasound, and a decrease in vascular flow on a Doppler study. The preoperative evaluation had included an abdominal CT scan with no findings. The post-orchiectomy CT scan detects retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy from the inguinal area to the renal bifurcation.
After performing an inguinal orchiectomy, she was diagnosed with diffuse large cell type B testicular lymphoma.

A first course of cytoreductive chemotherapy was administered according to the R-CHOPx8 scheme, with complete remission after induction. He presented an early recurrence treated in the second line using the IFOVM/DHAP regimen, with initial remission after three cycles and subsequently a new recurrence, dying 14 months after diagnosis.
