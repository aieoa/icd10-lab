A 70-year-old patient, retired miner, with no known drug allergies, who presents as a personal history: old work accident with vertebral and rib fractures; underwent surgery for Dupuytren's disease in the right hand and left iliofemoral bypass; Diabetes Mellitus type II, hypercholesterolemia and hyperuricemia; active alcoholism, smoker of 20 cigarettes/day.
He was referred from Primary Care for presenting post-void macroscopic hematuria on one occasion and persistent microhematuria later, with normal voiding.
On physical examination, he was in good general condition, with a normal abdomen and genitals; rectal examination compatible with grade I/IV prostate adenoma.
In the urine analysis, the existence of 4 red blood cells/field and 0-5 leukocytes/field stands out; rest of normal sediment.
Normal blood count; In biochemistry, a blood glucose level of 169 mg/dl and triglycerides of 456 mg/dl stand out; normal liver and kidney function. PSA of 1.16 ng/ml.
Urine cytology is repeatedly suspicious of malignancy.
In the simple abdominal film, degenerative changes in the lumbar spine and vascular calcifications in both hypochondria and in the pelvis are assessed.
Urological ultrasound reveals the existence of simple cortical cysts in the right kidney, an unchanged bladder with good capacity, and a prostate weighing 30 g.
In the IVU, bilateral renal normality was observed, calcifications on the right renal silhouette and beaded ureters with images of addition in the upper third of both ureters, in relation to ureteral pseudodiverticulosis. The cystogram demonstrates a bladder with good capacity, but trabeculated walls in relation to the stress bladder. Abdominal CT is normal.

Cystoscopy discovered the existence of small bladder tumors, and transurethral resection was performed with the pathological result of superficial urothelial carcinoma of the bladder.
