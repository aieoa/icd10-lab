A 65-year-old Ecuadorian woman, with no personal or family history of interest, who attended the outpatient ophthalmology clinic due to a painless decrease in vision in the right eye (RE) of 3 to 4 months' duration. The maximum visual acuity (VA) in the RE was 0.5 and in the left eye (LE) was 1. Extrinsic and intrinsic motility was normal. In the RE, papilledema with some subretinal exudate was observed. CT and orbital-cerebral MRI, luetic serology, angiotensin-converting enzyme, Lyme serology, and chest x-ray were negative. The systemic study by devices was not significant. The mantoux (PPD), already in the first 24 hours, had an induration of 25 mm with areas of dermal necrosis. The chest x-ray was repeated and was also negative. In the CSF puncture the outlet pressure was 16 cm H2O with normal biochemistry and negative culture. CSF electrophoresis was performed, and no oligoclonal bands were detected. Urine culture and sputum culture were also negative for the growth of mycobacterium tuberculosis. Given the suspicion of ocular tuberculosis, a test was performed with 300 mg/day of isoniazid for 3 weeks with subjective improvement in RE vision although maximum vision was still 0.5. He was treated with isoniazid 300 mg (9 months) along with rifampicin 600 mg (9 months), pyrazinamide 15 mg/kg (2 months) and ethambutol 15 mg/kg (2 months). One year after the first consultation, the RE's visual acuity was 0.9 and its examination was normal.
