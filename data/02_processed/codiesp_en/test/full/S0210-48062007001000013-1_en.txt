15-year-old patient with no previous history of interest. He was treated in the emergency room due to vomiting and epigastric pain that had been going on for three days, and he reported losing 7 kg of weight in the previous two months. The physical examination revealed moderate mucocutaneous pallor, hepatosplenomegaly of two fingers, and epigastric pain on deep palpation. Scrotal examination revealed an enlarged left testis (three or four times more than normal) with a hard consistency, irregular surface, and no pain. When the patient was re-interrogated, he confirmed that for approximately a year he had noticed a progressive and painless increase in the testis.
The emergency analysis only revealed a hematocrit of 35%, a hemoglobin of 11.3 gr/dl and an LDH of 2802 U/L.
Abdominal and testicular ultrasound demonstrated the existence of a greatly enlarged left testis of heterogeneous echogenicity, with foci of necrosis and liquid areas; and at the liver level large images suggestive of metastasis.

Chest radiology showed the existence of multiple lung metastases and a discrete pleural effusion, which after thoracentesis revealed bloody pleural fluid.
Twelve hours after admission, a thoracoabdominal CT scan was performed, which demonstrated extensive metastatic involvement, with multiple bilateral pulmonary nodules, right pleural effusion, mediastinal and retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy, hepatomegaly with massive metastatic involvement, and possibly splenic and pancreatic metastases. There was no free intraperitoneal fluid.

Blood samples were obtained for testicular markers: alpha-fetoprotein 15,000 ng/ml and betaHCG 200,000 mIU/ml. and orchiectomy was scheduled for the next day.
Thirty-six hours after admission, rapid-onset hypovolemic shock occurred with pain and abdominal bulging, which required urgent laparotomy after an abdominal ultrasound that showed a large amount of intraperitoneal fluid. During the laparotomy, the existence of extensive metastatic involvement in the tail of the pancreas, spleen and liver was confirmed with rupture of splenic and hepatic metastases and massive hemoperitoneum due to active bleeding from them and with diffuse hepatic hematoma. A splenectomy was performed with suture and surgicel tamponade of the bleeding liver areas; At the same time, a left radical orchiectomy was performed.
The macroscopic pathology describes: "radical orchiectomy piece, weighing 233 g and measuring 8x9x5 cm, accompanied by 7 cm of cord; in serial sections the testicular parenchyma shows almost total replacement by a whitish tumor, with areas of necrosis and hemorrhage that does not seem to go beyond the testicular covers".
Microscopic histopathology of the testis showed a mixed germinal malignancy in which areas of embryonal carcinoma, endodermal sinus tumor, choriocarcinoma and teratoma were observed.

The spleen measured 15x7x3 cm and weighed 180 g. In the serial sections, six nodular, rounded, whitish formations with central necrosis were observed. Microscopy confirms that the splenic lesions are metastases from the testicular neoplasia.

Forty-eight hours after the intervention, the patient suffered a new condition of hypovolemic shock with obvious signs of intra-abdominal bleeding, for which a new laparotomy was performed, verifying the existence of hepatic burst at the level of the right and left lobe, with uncontrollable hemorrhage and death shortly after.
