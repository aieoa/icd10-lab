A 62-year-old woman who reported, for 13 years, multiple episodes of blurred vision in both eyes with subsequent complete recovery of visual acuity. The various outbreaks that she presented were treated with oral corticosteroid therapy, which were suspended after the respective remissions. She was diagnosed with serpiginous choroiditis based on clinical and angiographic findings.
As a family history, she presents a twin sister of her mother with a similar condition. She has no personal history of interest.
At the last ophthalmological examination, the disease remained inactive and visual acuity (VA) was unity in both eyes despite the significant chorioretinal atrophy and scarring that she presented in the posterior pole, including the macula. The anterior segment did not show relevant findings and the intraocular pressure was 14 mmHg in both eyes.
The fundus showed multiple geographic scar lesions, with variable atrophy and hypertrophy of the RPE throughout the posterior pole.

Fluorescein angiography showed multiple lesions with a mottled appearance due to atrophy and hyperpigmentation, with staining in late phases.

Four months earlier she began experiencing flatulent dyspepsia, vomiting and pruritus. In the laboratory studies performed, she presents an increase in transaminases (GOT 110 and GPT 90), GGT (gammaglutamyltranspeptidase) 110, hypergamma-globulinemia (IgG 1620), ANA (antinuclear antibodies) 1/320, AMA (antimitochondrial antibodies) negative. .
HBV surface antigen, anti-HBs antibody, anti-HBc antibody and anti-HCV antibody negative. A liver ultrasound was performed and was normal.
The patient had a good response to calculolytic bile acids. The suspected diagnosis was autoimmune hepatitis. It was decided not to perform aggressive diagnostic tests such as a liver biopsy due to the mild activity of the digestive system.
