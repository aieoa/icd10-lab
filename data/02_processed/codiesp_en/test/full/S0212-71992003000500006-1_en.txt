We present the case of a 26-year-old woman, with no relevant history, who, previously feeling well, began with mild odynophagia, afternoon fever of up to 38.5º that subsided well with paracetamol, and general malaise. No accompanying chills or sweating attacks. 4-5 days later, she noticed the appearance of lymph nodes in both side-cervical areas, so she started treatment with amoxicillin, 500 mg/8 h, on her own without observing improvement.
During the physical examination, she highlighted the presence of lymphadenopathy in both side-cervical chains, of medium consistency, rolling, non-adherent, 1-2 cm in diameter and painless. No lymphadenopathy at other levels or organomegaly was palpable. No goiter or pain on pressure on the thyroid region.
The analytical study showed predominantly lymphocytic leukocytosis, with an ESR of 22 mm/h. Liver function was preserved, there was no hypergammaglobulinemia and the mantoux test was negative. The chest x-ray was normal. Serology for the viruses studied was negative (anti-EBV, CMV, HIV, HTLV-1, HV-6, toxoplasma and lues). In the immunological study: ANA: 1/40 (80), antiDNA: 13.4 IU/ml (50), antiRNA: negative, ENA-RNP: 0.45 and anticardiolipin: negative. Thyroid function was assessed: T3: 1.31 mg/L (0.6-1.6), T4: 84 mg/L (46-93), TSH: 6.3 mU/L (<7), TGB (antithyroglobulin): 20.17 U/mL (<60), TPO (antiperoxidase): 1071 U/mL. The thyroid scan was normal.
The fever disappeared after 12 days, but the lymphadenopathy remained unchanged, so a biopsy of two lymph nodes in the middle area of ​​the right latero-cervical chain was performed, which was diagnostic. The histological examination was compatible with non-lymphocytic histiocytic necrotizing lymphadenitis or Kikuchi-Fujimoto disease.
In the evolutionary control of thyroid function 5 months after the onset of the condition, a decrease in the titer of antithyroid antibodies was observed (TGB: 7.6 U/mL; TPO: 870 U/mL), maintaining normal levels of thyroid hormones as well as TSH (2.95 mU/L). Our diagnosis was subacute lymphocytic thyroiditis associated with Kikuchi disease.
