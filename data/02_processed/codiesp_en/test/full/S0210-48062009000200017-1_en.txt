A 60-year-old man, with a history of repeated urinary infections, went to the emergency room with a mass in the lower abdomen, which had been going on for about six months, which bled spontaneously and caused intense pain.
During the physical examination, a plaque of bladder exstrophy in the hypogastrium and a complete epispadic penis were observed, which were not surgically corrected during his childhood. An exophytic formation stood out within the plaque, 10 cm in diameter and hard in consistency, with a friable surface that bled at the slightest touch. No inguinal lymphadenopathy was palpable and the rest of the examination did not reveal any other findings.

In the blood test, a creatinine of 2.2 mg/dL was observed, with the rest of the parameters being normal.
After hospital admission to complete the study, a bilateral renal ultrasound was performed in which grade III/IV dilation of both pyelocalyceal systems was observed. Intravenous urography showed functional annulment of the right kidney, with delay in the elimination of the left.
The suspicious mass was biopsied, obtaining the histopathological result of bladder adenocarcinoma.
Thoraco-abdomino-pelvic CT and bone scan showed no evidence of metastasis.
The patient underwent surgery, performing a radical cystoprostatectomy with lymphadenectomy plus Indiana shunt and closure of the abdominal wall defect with fascia lata. The histopathological analysis of the surgical specimen reflected the existence of an intestinal-type adenocarcinoma with some signet ring cells. The margins of the specimen were infiltrated, with extensive involvement of adjacent soft tissues and peritoneum, and two iliac lymph nodes infiltrated by the tumor were isolated. The tumor stage corresponded to T4bN2M0.
The Medical Oncology service rejected adjuvant treatment considering that it would not increase the patient's survival.
In the follow-up 6 months after surgery, a left inguinal adenopathy was palpated, painless and of increased consistency, together with induration of the edges of the surgical wound, a finding compatible with local recurrence of the tumor that was confirmed by biopsy of the area. suspicious. The bone scan at this time continued to be negative and the CT showed a heterogeneous mass with poorly defined edges in the surgical bed.
The patient died 2 months later due to sepsis of urinary origin.
