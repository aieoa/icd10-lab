A 78-year-old patient with a history of high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes mellitus, osteoporosis and repeated renal colic who, 6 days before admission, began to experience pain in the right renal fossa with irradiation to the ipsilateral knee, which was treated on an outpatient basis with dexamethaxone and lidocaine. , cyanocobalamin and thiamine without resolution. Subsequently, she began to feel dysthermic with a thermometered temperature above 38ºC, poor general condition, urinary symptoms and glycemic decompensation, for which she was admitted to hospital.
The physical examination revealed a marked affectation of the general condition, fever, pain in the sacral area with negative bilateral fist percussion and knees with phlogotic signs.
Laboratory tests showed mild leukocytosis without left deviation (12,100x103/mm3 with N 65%), predominantly direct hyperbilirubinemia (BT: 3.4 mg/dl, BD: 2.7mg/dl), alkaline phosphatase 215 U/L. (35-104). The rest of the analysis does not present significant alterations.
As complementary examinations, blood and urine cultures, puncture and cultures of joint fluid, CT scan of the abdominal and lumbosacral spine, magnetic resonance imaging of the lumbar spine and Ga-67/MDP-Tc-99 scintigraphy are performed.
In the CT scan of the abdominal and lumbosacral spine, air densities are observed in the right lumbar and psoas muscles and in the intraspinal and dorsal locations. Gas is also visualized in the bone canal that could correspond to a ruptured L4-L5 disc.

The magnetic resonance imaging of the lumbar spine confirmed images compatible with abscesses in the right paravertebral and psoas muscles, and two collections that suggested epidural abscesses located at L4-L5 and D12-L4. An alteration of the D12 body signal was observed after administration of gadolinium, resulting in bone edema compatible with spondylitis and probable spondylodiscitis.

In the combined body scan with Ga-67/MDP-Tc-99M, there is pathological hyperfixation of both tracers in the knees and shoulders, suggesting an inflammatory-infectious process as well as greatly increased gallium uptake in L4-L5 and in paravertebral soft tissues compatible with spondylodiscitis with Possible infection of adjacent soft tissues.
Upon admission, empirical antibiotic treatment with ciprofloxacin was started, subsequently confirming in microbiology (blood, urine and joint fluid cultures) the presence of Escherichia coli, with a similar antibiogram.
During the first days of treatment, the patient achieved a slight improvement in symptoms coinciding with the introduction of antibiotics and antipyretics. On the 10th day of admission, the fever reappeared and the general condition progressively worsened. On the 12th day of admission, the patient presented with atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response and cardiorespiratory arrest without response to resuscitation maneuvers.
The diagnosis was urinary infection due to E. coli complicated with polyarticular septic arthritis, spondylodiscitis, epidural abscesses, psoas and dorso-lumbar muscles.
