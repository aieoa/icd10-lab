A 38-year-old woman diagnosed with multiple sclerosis treated with interferon beta and ankylosing spondylitis treated with ibuprofen and metamizole. The relative who lived with her reported that she had not defecated for three days, with pain and abdominal distention. At around 1:30 a.m. she administered a 240 ml enema (of the so-called rectal laxatives) and she went to bed. At 5 a.m., her relative found her unconscious and with traces of dark green vomit on her face and around her. She attended the emergency medical service, which found her with signs of certain death and did not apply resuscitation measures.
Autopsy findings: height, 1.54 m; weight: 52kg; body mass index, normal (21.9 kg/m2); traces of dark green liquid in her nostrils, inside and around her mouth; abdominal distension; When opening the abdomen, great dilation of the stomach and duodenum that occupies most of the visible abdominal cavity, reaching the lesser pelvis; the remaining abdominal viscera are hidden by the stomach, which is completely filled with dark green liquid (bile and few blood remains in the form of "coffee grounds"); the walls of the stomach are thinned and with areas of hemorrhagic appearance in the mucosa; The stomach contains 3.5 liters of dark green liquid; gastric contents in the airway.

The histopathological study diagnosed, in her stomach, congestion in the vessels of the lamina propria and autolysis of the glandular epithelium; in the brain, inactive plaques of multiple sclerosis; and also bronchoalveolar aspiration.

Toxicological screening only detected subtherapeutic concentrations of metoclopramide, ibuprofen and diazepam.
