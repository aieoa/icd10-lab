A 50-year-old man who underwent a Scopinaro biliopancreatic diversion in January 2004 for morbid obesity. The patient presents hepatic steatosis and malabsorption syndrome. He is being treated with vitamin B12 for chronic anemia and vitamin K for decreased prothrombin activity.
In January 2006, a pyeloinfundibulonephrolithotomy with postoperative urinary fistula was performed.
In April 2006, the patient was referred to the ophthalmology service due to night blindness that had progressed over 3 months. The patient also reported a foreign body sensation and red eye. On examination, visual acuity was unity in both eyes. Slit lamp examination showed greatly decreased BUT, conjunctival xerosis, and Bitot spots in both eyes. Fine corneal conjunctive epithelial stippling could also be seen on fluorescein staining. In the Schirmer I test under anesthesia, values ​​greater than 15 mm were observed. Ophthalmoscopically, whitish mottling was observed in the mid-periphery without macular involvement. The plasma retinol level was 0.07 mg/l (normal level 0.30-0.8 mg/l).

Given the potential severity of the condition, oral treatment with vitamin A and pancreatic enzymes was started to promote its absorption while waiting to receive intramuscular treatment, with little improvement achieved.
Concomitantly, due to complicated nephrolithiasis (intrarenal abscess), the patient underwent a nephrectomy with intense bleeding, requiring five packed red blood cells. After this process, the patient reports a slight improvement in his symptoms, despite having discontinued oral treatment due to post-surgical fasting.
Finally, 300,000 IU of intramuscular vitamin A was administered, achieving an immediate improvement in subjective symptoms, although the ophthalmoscopic manifestations took three months to disappear.
