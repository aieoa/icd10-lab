This is a 45-year-old male patient, with a history of smoking and hypercholesterolemia, who suffers from severe acute coronary artery disease and who underwent quadruple aortocoronary bypass with extracorporeal circulation. Transesophageal echocardiography showed ejection fraction of 8-10%.
The patient was intubated and maintained on mechanical ventilation for 9 days in the intensive care unit (ICU). After his extubation, he began to have an increase in respiratory rate, reduced values ​​of oxygen saturation of oxyhemoglobin, so reintubation and reconnection to the mechanical ventilator were carried out, which were maintained until the twelfth day when they could be removed from final form. Once the condition is stabilized, he goes to cardiology.
On the eighth day of being on the ward, he began to experience dyspnea, stridor, intense labor of breathing and tachypnea, for which he was transferred back to the ICU. Given the symptoms of upper airway obstruction, urgent fiberoptic bronchoscopy was performed, which revealed complex tracheal stenosis. A cervical computed tomography (CT) scan showed 66% proximal tracheal stenosis approximately 2 cm from the glottis with peritracheal fibrosis and a 5 mm lumen.

It was decided to perform rigid bronchoscopy to place a tracheal prosthesis. Rigid bronchoscopy is combined with laser therapy with Nd-YAG laser on the lesion and subsequent mechanical resection with a rigid bronchoscope. After tracheal recanalization, a silicone prosthesis (Dumon 14/40) is placed, which is correctly anchored and permeable with the proximal end about 2.5 cm from the chords, and the distal end, about 7 cm from the carina. In the review carried out 7 days later, the prosthesis remains completely patent and the respiratory symptoms have definitively resolved.
