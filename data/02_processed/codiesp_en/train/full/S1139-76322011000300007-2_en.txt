A three-year-old male was brought to the emergency room after having had two episodes of approximately one minute in duration in the previous hour, consisting of environmental disconnection, lost gaze, and generalized hypertonia; both episodes gave way on their own. Two days before he had started with diarrhea and vomiting, he had not had a fever. He had no personal background of interest. His family history included only a six-year-old brother who had had two febrile seizures.
On physical examination he did not present signs of dehydration or neurological alterations. A complete blood count, biochemistry, and venous blood gas analysis were performed, which were normal.
He remained under observation for 20 hours without presenting new seizures and had good tolerance to oral hydration. Rotavirus was detected in the stool culture. Subsequent follow-up without presenting new crises and with normal psychomotor development.
