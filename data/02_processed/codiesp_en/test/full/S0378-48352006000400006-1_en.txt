This is a 71-year-old woman. Her background of interest is that she has been an ex-smoker for 25 years. She is diagnosed with hypertension, polymyalgia rheumatica, and temporal arteritis.
In 1988, an endometrial tumor was detected after a metrorrhagia study, and a hysterectomy plus double adnexectomy was performed. The pathological study revealed that it was a well-differentiated adenocarcinoma (grade I), with infiltration of the inner third of the myometrium, without evidence of infiltration of blood or lymphatic vessels, with negative extension study, therefore Stage Ib. She subsequently received radiotherapy with cobalt-60 to the pelvis and brachytherapy to the vaginal vault. She had regular check-ups with no evidence of relapse.
In 2003, she consulted for irritative cough and mild hemoptoic expectoration, and a simple chest X-ray showed a mass in the left upper lobe. With the suspicion of a bronchogenic neoplasia, the studies were completed by performing a thoraco-abdomino-pelvic CT, revealing said 8 cm necrotic mass in the LSI with atelectasis of said lobe without other alterations. A bronchoscopy was performed, which revealed a tumor in the anterior segment of the aforementioned lobe, with the BAS and bronchial biopsy being compatible with poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.
With the clinical diagnosis of Epidermoid Lung Carcinoma T2 N0 M0, it was decided to start neoadjuvant chemotherapy, with the Carboplatin (AUC 6) plus Paclitaxel (200 mg/m2) regimen every three weeks. After three cycles of treatment, a partial response was observed in the radiological studies and the patient underwent left upper lobectomy and lymphadenectomy. Analysis of the surgical specimen showed a tumor corresponding to adenocarcinoma with poorly differentiated areas, with some scaly areas, with a positive immunohistochemical result for the expression of estrogen and progesterone, and negative for TTF-1 and CK20. These confirmatory results changed the initial approach, not being a primary lung tumor as previously thought, but rather a lung relapse of her endometrial tumor.
The patient is being treated with megestrol acetate, with good tolerance, free of disease and currently being followed up.
