A male individual, 19 years old and in a military uniform, is found in a supine position with both upper limbs at the sides of the body, in an open night scene. On the left forearm, a weapon of military manufacture and use was identified: the Light Automatic Rifle -FAL, model PARA Nº 50-63, super compact with folding stock and flash hider, caliber 7.62 x 51 mm NATO, speed of 810 m/s. The stock was found resting on the left hand (it was in firing spasm) with the rifle positioned parallel to the body with the muzzle about 30 cm from the head and the magazine and firing device facing out. The scene and the victim's clothes were found without signs of violence. The deceased presented blunt tear-type injuries in his oral cavity, abundant epistaxis, otorrhagia, and a large blood stain under his head.

Once the protocols for fixing the scene of the incident had been carried out, the body was rotated to corroborate the existence of an alleged exit hole in relation to the large blood stain under the head, identifying a small wound with a circular diameter and regular edges. at cervical level. The presence of a cavity in the ground corresponding perfectly with the braincase and a perforation in the ground in direct connection with the alleged exit hole was confirmed. Probing this perforation made it possible to establish its depth at about 20 cm with an angulation of the path coinciding with that estimated between the oral cavity and the cervical os, right-left, bottom-up direction. The excavation of said hole favored the recovery of the projectile, embedded in its depth.

During the autopsy procedure in the morgue and given the oral-maxillo-facial involvement injuries, the damaging elements were described as follows:
1. Large blunt lesion in the oral cavity with a periorificial tear on both margins and a star shape, with accentuated destruction towards the right side of the individual. Deposit of grayish-blackish pigments throughout the oral mucosa, more marked in the left buccal mucosa. A large tear was identified throughout the lingual body with identical presence of the pigment. In the most posterior and left lateral sector at the level of the junction of the hard and soft palate and the isthmus of the fauces, a circular perforation of 1.5 cm in diameter and intense hemorrhage were observed. Regarding hard structures, a maxillary and mandibular fracture was confirmed at the level of the union of their respective central and lateral incisors, with displacement and significant mobility of fracture ends towards the right side. No direct or indirect dentoperiodontal lesions were found in maxillary teeth. Regarding the mandibular, the lower right central incisor was intact but avulsed and displaced towards the mandibular basal layer at the depth of the fracture line, while the lateral incisor on the same side showed longitudinal amelo-cementum-dentin fracture with loss of substance. . The missing tooth fragment was identified deep to the mandibular fracture line, on the same side of the avulsed central incisor.

2. Circular perforating lesion with regular edges, 1.5 cm in diameter, located in the midline slightly to the left at the posterior cervical level about 3 cm below the occipital eminence, partially hidden by hair. Macroscopic examination verified the absence of metallic particles.

3. Passive probing of the tract established communication between the perforation described in the depth of the oral cavity, in the left posterior sector, with the orifice at the cervical level, establishing a slightly rectilinear direction of right-left, front-back, bottom- above, involving the muscles of the pharynx, soft palate and tongue. During the exploration, no metallic residues were identified along this path.
Given the atypical morphology of both orifices, a tongue wedge was obtained, which was fixed in 10% formalin and processed with a routine paraffin embedding technique and hematoxylin eosin staining at the Laboratory of Expertise in Forensic Dentistry, Córdoba. (Argentina). The identification of particles due to gunpowder deflagration embedded between the lingual striated muscle bundles and the absence of these in the exit hole, allowed us to confirm the oral cavity as the point of opening and entry of the projectile.

Toxicology tests were negative for both alcohol and drugs. The absence of signs of struggle (both at the scene and on the subject's clothing), the location and number of the wounds, the direction and distance of the shot and the spasm in the left hand, compatible with the position of the weapon (reconstruction was carried out of the event), all elements listed by the literature as characteristic of suicide [7, 8, 11], and the collection of personal information that revealed a recent history potentially determining self-harm, determined the medico-legal etiology of suicide.
