A 74-year-old female patient was admitted with diffuse abdominal pain, hyporexia and asthenia for 2 weeks. She had been vomiting coffee grounds and melenic stools for 2 days. Her personal history included: hypertension being treated with amiloride/hydrochlorothiazide and dyslipidemia that she controlled with lovastatin. She was a non-smoker and she denied taking NSAIDs, alcohol, or oral contraceptives. Her family history was: mother died of gastric cancer, brother with liver cirrhosis of unknown etiology and sister with hepatocellular carcinoma.
- Physical examination: hemodynamic stability. Diffuse pain on abdominal palpation, without signs of peritonism.
- Analysis upon admission: hemoglobin: 13.5 mg/dl; hematocrit: 37.6%; platelets: 120,000 platelets/ml; bilirubin: 247 mg/dl; alkaline phosphatase: 220 IU/l; aspartate aminotransferase: 59 IU/l; alanine aminotransferase: 28 IU/l and gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase: 129 IU/l. An emergency gastroscopy was performed, which revealed medium-sized esophageal varices without warning signs of imminent bleeding [F2 CR(-) according to the classification of the Japanese Society for the Study of Portal Hypertension] and multiple erosions with fibrin at the gastric and duodenal without signs of recent hemostasis. In the computed tomography and abdominal ultrasound, the liver was of normal size, with slightly wavy edges with a slightly heterogeneous parenchyma, without space-occupying lesions. At the same time, splenomegaly, ascites and a small left pleural effusion were observed. On the other hand, there was a complete non-occlusive portal thrombosis at the level of the hepatic hilum and its branches, as well as another thrombus at the level of the superior mesenteric vein with the presence of collateral circulation in the gastrohepatic and perisplenic ligaments. The etiological study of the different causes of chronic liver disease included: hepatotropic viruses (HBV, HCV) and HIV, autoimmunity study, ceruloplasmin, ferritin and porphyrins in urine, all results being negative. Subsequently, a percutaneous liver biopsy was performed with a hemodynamic study that showed a portal pressure gradient of 12 mmHg with normal phlebography. The biopsy showed an alteration of the liver architecture due to hepatocyte hyperplasia in zone 1 of some of the acini, with a tendency to be nodular but without perinodular fibrosis septa. The portal tracts showed the usual duct-artery-vein triad, although the vein was sometimes indistinct and a paraportal vein was observed. They presented mild fibrous enlargement and contained mild lymphoplasmacytic inflammatory infiltrate with focal overflow through the periportal interface. Some inflammatory focus was also observed in the lobule, although there were no acidophilic bodies or other relevant findings. These mild fibroinflammatory changes, irregular nodular hyperplasia as well as the absence of perinodular fibrosis rule out the presence of cirrhosis and are compatible with non-cirrhotic portal hypertension. Given the possibility that all the liver changes were secondary to a primary portal thrombosis, a hypercoagulability study was performed.

This study demonstrated an antigenic free protein S (pS), total antigenic pS and anticoagulant free pS of 56, 107 and 44% respectively, which was suggestive of a type I protein S deficiency. The rest of the coagulation parameters (which included fibrinogen, antithrombin, protein C, anticardiolipin antibodies, resistance to activated protein C, factor V Leyden, factor II G 20210A and homocysteine) were within the normal range. This result was confirmed in a subsequent control.
The patient was placed on anticoagulant treatment with acenocoumarol and diuretics indefinitely. During the patient's follow-up, no new decompensations have been evident.
