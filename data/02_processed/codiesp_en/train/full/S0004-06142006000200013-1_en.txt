A 46-year-old patient had a hysterectomy for leiomyomas who came to the emergency room due to intense pain in the left renal fossa, general malaise, and repeated episodes of hematuria. Several urine cytological studies were negative for neoplastic cells. Imaging studies determined the patient's surgical intervention, observing an atrophic left kidney as well as a large 'tumor mass' that encompassed the theoretical exit of the left internal iliac artery, left ureter, pouch of Douglas, rectum and lateral peritoneal surface. Due to the technical difficulties involved in enucleating such a lesion, a ureteral sample was taken and sent for histopathological diagnosis with the suspicion of ureteral carcinoma. The macroscopic study of the submitted material showed an irregular whitish tissue with an elastic-firm consistency and a fibrous appearance that in serial sections showed a fine reddish dot that was difficult to appreciate. The histological study allowed us to observe a ureteral segment with the presence of multiple intramuscular dilated glandular structures lined by endometrial epithelium whose immunohistochemical staining was intensely positive for cytokeratins, estrogen receptors and progesterone receptors. Surrounding the ureteral wall, abundant disordered fibrous tissue with signs of hemorrhage was observed. With all these data, the diagnosis of intrinsic ureteral endometriosis was made, although the intense extraureteral tissue response and the clear signs of hemorrhage that accompanied it made us think of an accompanying extrinsic component although no glandular structures were identified at that level.
