We present the case of a three-day-old newborn, who went to the emergency room with his mother after discovering an increase in the left hemiscrotum, as well as a violet color to it; The newborn was feeling well at birth according to the parents, and in the last four hours he cried inconsolably.
He does not present fever or other additional clinical data. On examination we found an enlarged left hemiscrotum, painful on manipulation, wine-red in color.
Analytically, the patient does not present any alteration in the blood count, biochemistry, or coagulation study.
In the scrotal ultrasound we found a right testis with normal characteristics; The left testis is enlarged, with irregularities inside, with an anechoic area that may suggest necrosis, surrounded by a hyperechoic area suggestive of calcification; all of this compatible with evolved testicular torsion.

The patient underwent surgery, proceeding to the removal of the left teste and the fixation of the right teste, progressing favorably and being well two years later with the right teste of normal size and consistency.
