A 62-year-old man, ex-smoker with no other relevant history, was admitted to the ICU of our center due to seizure status. According to his family, in recent weeks he has been irritable and childish, in addition to having complained in recent days of heaviness and numbness in his right limbs. Seizures are controlled with IV phenytoin. There were no notable findings in the tests (analytical, ECG, brain CT) performed urgently, except for right basal atelectasis in the chest X-ray. A lumbar puncture was performed which showed acellular CSF but with hyperproteinorrachia (72 mg/dl of protein) and EEG with left temporal irritative activity. A brain MRI study showed a hypersignal lesion in the left temporal lobe (FLAIR and diffusion sequences), which did not change after the administration of paramagnetic contrast, nor did it present hemorrhage foci during the course.

Treatment with phenytoin, acyclovir (waiting for PCR against herpes simplex) and antibiotic therapy (pneumonia) with Tazocel and Teicoplanin were added to the treatment. He did not have seizures again and remained afebrile, and was transferred to the Internal Medicine ward. On admission there were no notable findings in the physical examination and in the neurological examination he appeared conscious, with nominal dysphasia, inappropriate mood, attention deficit and tendency to fabulate and mild right hemiparesis. In the following days he improved (the paresis recovered and his language became normal) although moria and Korsakoff syndrome persisted. Treatment with Acyclovir was completed (on the sixth day, a negative PCR test for HSV was received) and antibiotic therapy.
Analytical studies were irrelevant, except for a slight elevation of CEA (3.9 ng/ml). Anti-Hu antibodies, antithyroid antibodies, serologies (lues, HIV, neurotropic viruses) were negative. Screening for occult neoplasia was initiated starting with thoracoabdominal CT (normal except for LDI atelectasis/pneumonia), bronchoscope with bronchoalveolar lavage (negative), upper and lower gastrointestinal endoscopy, intestinal transit, urological evaluation, bone scintigraphy, and bone marrow biopsy. Given the normality of these studies, and with the diagnosis of PLD, a whole-body PET study was requested, which showed the presence of hyperuptake of fluoroglucose at the pharyngolaryngeal level, as well as small satellite lymphadenopathy. The finding of PE was confirmed with the CT of the neck and anatomopathological study of the lesion that was diagnostic of squamous cell carcinoma of the pyriform sinus.
