A 58-year-old male presented with sudden loss of vision in his LE. The patient had been followed up by Internal Medicine and Dermatology for 12 years due to PXE.
His BCVA in his RE was 0.6 and in his LE it was 0.4. No alterations were found in IOP or BMC of the anterior pole.
In the FO examination, bilateral AEs were evident. In the RE, a raised yellowish-grayish gliotic lesion stood out and, in the LE, a pigmented triradiate image bordered by orange areas in the macular area, together with a small juxtafoveal lesion, somewhat grayish and slightly raised, surrounded by abundant subretinal hemorrhages. especially nasal. The suspicion of a CNV membrane within a retinal dystrophy "in a reticular pattern" led us to perform an AFG, where a gliotic eschar was observed in the RE and, in the LE, a hypofluorescent image resembling a reticulum, in which area Superiorly, a hyperfluorescent lesion with lacy edges appeared that increased in intensity and size in the later stages, which confirmed the previous suspicion. OCT corroborated the diagnosis, as it revealed a slight elevation of the RPE-choriocapillaris band.

The FA of the LE revealed a curious image, since the patterned dystrophy appeared as the negative of the angiographic image; That is, the reticulum, which angiographically appeared hypofluorescent, in AF showed intensely hyper-AF due to the alteration of the RPE at that level.
In this case, the same treatment as in the previous patient was instituted according to the protocol already described. A higher VA was achieved, reaching 0.7. In the evolutionary OCT, a progressive decrease in macular thickness was observed and the control AFG revealed the improvement of the condition.
