Male patient, 40 years old, with health history. He went to the Urology consultation due to dysuria and 3 episodes of urethrorrhagia. On physical examination, the mucous membranes are moist and normally colored, cardio-respiratory auscultation is normal, the abdomen is negative, lumbar fossae are free, rectal examination, external genitalia and inguinal regions are normal. A urological ultrasound was performed, which was normal. The cystourethroscopy was normal, with only a slight ridge noted when the cystoscope passed through the anterior urethra. A voiding urethrocystography x-ray was performed where a filling defect was observed at the level of the anterior urethra.

For all of the above, we decided to perform an endoscopic study under anesthesia, detecting a polypoid tumor, reddish in color, well delimited, so it was decided to endoscopic resection. The pathological result of the surgical specimen was an inverted papilloma of the anterior urethra. He was discharged due to improvement, progressing satisfactorily with disappearance of dysuria and urethrorrhagia.
