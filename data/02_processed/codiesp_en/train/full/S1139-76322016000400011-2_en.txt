A 13-year-old girl, with no history of interest, who came to the clinic complaining of intense vomiting and abdominal pain that had been going on for five days. She presents a fair general condition, without signs of dehydration, with diffuse abdominal pain, without other accompanying symptoms. During her stay, she suddenly experienced chest pain and crepitus in the neck after vomiting. She is referred to the emergency room for evaluation. Chest x-ray shows signs of pneumomediastinum and subcutaneous emphysema in the neck and upper chest. She was prescribed intravenous fluid therapy and antiemetics, the vomiting subsided, so she was discharged without requiring admission. In a subsequent check-up in consultation five days later, we see the resolution of the condition, without complications.
