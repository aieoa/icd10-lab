A 65-year-old woman, with a previous diagnosis of bipolar affective disorder type II treated with lithium and olanzapine, attended the Emergency Department due to behavioral alterations of four days' duration. The patient has a long history of several admissions to psychiatric services. She lives alone and is autonomous in the activities of daily living although she receives home help. The family reports initial symptoms of mood swings and extravagant clothing that later progress to dysarthria, difficulty walking, and temporal-spatial disorientation. In the study carried out in the Emergency Department, arterial hypertension (174/110 mmHg), hypokalemia (3.09 mEq/l), fever (38.4ºC) and abdominal distension were observed, which was attributed to urinary retention, and 2 liters of fluid were removed. urine after bladder catheterization. The lithemia, ECG, CK and head CT are normal, as well as the simple abdominal and chest x-ray. Lumbar puncture was performed with normal cell count. The patient was admitted to the Internal Medicine Service with a diagnostic impression of confusional syndrome without an identified etiology. It is treated with antipyresis, broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy, and venous thrombosis prophylaxis with heparin. The study is expanded with a new lumbar puncture, MRI of the skull, thoracic and abdomino-pelvic CT, blood cultures and urine cultures that do not show alterations that justify the condition. The condition progresses in two days to mutism, akinesia, rigidity, distal tremor and decreased blinking. Fever and high blood pressure (HBP) persist, with hypernatremia (150mEq/l). Electrolyte alterations are interpreted in the context of diaphoresis and no fluid and food intake. After deterioration of the level of consciousness, admission to the Resuscitation Unit and placement of a nasogastric tube for nutrition of the patient is considered. A psychiatry evaluation was requested and urgent electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) was prescribed for the next day with a diagnostic impression of malignant catatonia. Two days after the first session, it was noted that the patient maintained coherent spontaneous speech and was feeding. Two more sessions of ECT are administered and lithium is reintroduced. The fever, diaphoresis, HBP, electrolyte disturbances and disorientation have subsided. The culture for Herpes Simplex Virus was negative, ruling out encephalitis due to this cause. The patient was discharged in 30 days with a diagnosis of TAB type II and malignant catatonia. At discharge the patient was euthymic.
