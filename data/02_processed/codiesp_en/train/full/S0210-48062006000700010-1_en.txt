A 56-year-old male patient with a history of positive serology for HIV and Hepatitis B presented with acute cholecystitis for which emergency laparoscopic cholecystectomy was performed.
During the late post-operative period, one month after surgery, the patient presents a bilioma which is evacuated percutaneously. At the same time, a biliary diversion is also performed using endoscopic cholangiography.
As a result of the above, the patient presents acute pancreatitis associated with a peri-pancreatic collection. This collection is evacuated through an initial percutaneous drainage, which proves to be insufficient, leaving the patient with a significant retroperitoneal collection. Without showing improvement, and with persistent drainage due to fever and deterioration of general condition, a computed tomography of the abdomen and pelvis was performed. This reports encapsulated fluid with an air-fluid level on the right flank adjacent to the abdominal wall extending 17 cm in the cranio-caudal direction and 11 x 3 cm in transverse diameter.

With a diagnosis of extraperitoneal right parietocolic abscess, drainage was performed using lumboscopy, evacuating abundant necrotic and purulent material.
Two pure silicone drains were placed facing each other, creating a continuous washing system, using physiological solution with a slow drip.

The patient progressed favorably with few signs of fever. He had a positive culture for Pseudomona Aureaginosa, which was treated with Meropenem during hospitalization. On the fourth postoperative day, continuous lavage was replaced by a hemosuctor system and he was discharged.
During the controls, the patient continued to be afebrile and the drain was removed after it had stopped draining.
