This is a 47-year-old man, with no history of interest, with a history of headache and occasional vertigo in the last four years, who consulted for a nonspecific vision disorder. The neurological examination turned out to be normal, except for the detection of right inferior homonymous quadrantanopsia in the campimetric study. There were no associated malformations or anomalies at the level of the central nervous system or outside it. The computed tomography (CT) scan was normal. An MRI study was performed that showed, in T1 sequences and in parasagittal projection, two hypersignal foci located in the quadrigeminal cistern and in the upper lip of the left calcarine fissure compatible with the diagnosis of lipomas.
No surgical intervention was performed, and the patient remained with identical symptoms two years later.
