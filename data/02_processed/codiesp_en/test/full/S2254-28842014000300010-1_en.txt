A 57-year-old man with a history of arterial hypertension and dyslipidemia, presents with ischemic heart disease and acute lower myocardial infarction in 2004, peripheral arterial disease with bifemoral aortic bypass in 2006, suffers in 2010 from acute ischemia of the left lower extremity due to occlusion. by-pass that required urgent revascularization with thrombectomy. He has been on a hemodialysis program since December 2010 for chronic renal failure possibly secondary to nephroangiosclerosis.
After failure of the left radiocephalic AVF, a temporary left jugular catheter was placed at the end of August 2013, which was dysfunctional from the beginning but was maintained until the use of a new left humeral cephalic AVF.
One month after the catheter was placed, the patient promptly referred postural low back pain to nursing, although the patient himself attributed it to exertion.
After removing the catheter (43 days after insertion), in several subsequent sessions he presented general malaise and fever, a bacteriological control of the monitor was carried out, which was negative, and vancomycin 1 g was administered empirically. Along with an antipyretic, the patient remains stable for the following month.
Subsequently, he again reported very intense low back pain that barely allowed him to walk. He went to the emergency room up to four times in which, after abdominal examination, X-ray, and ultrasound, they always diagnosed mechanical low back pain.
Given the deterioration of his general condition and after verifying analytical alterations (increased C-reactive protein and erythrocyte sedimentation rate), he was admitted to the internal medicine unit for study. Following positive microbiological results for S. aureus, targeted antibiotic treatment with cloxacillin was initiated. After performing a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), D10-D11 spondylodiscitis was diagnosed, which is associated with a paravertebral abscess that does not cause compromise of nervous structures.
Analgesic treatment was initiated with first-level drugs associated with transdermal opiates, with progressive dose titration throughout the hospital stay and acceptable pain control.
The patient remains afebrile throughout his admission, with a progressive decrease in acute phase reactants and negative blood cultures. He completes five weeks of targeted intravenous antibiotic treatment, transitions to outpatient oral therapy, and is discharged from the hospital. At this time, the patient remains stable without further intrahemodialysis symptoms with self-controlled analgesia and awaiting new control MRI.
