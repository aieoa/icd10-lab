A 17-year-old male with a history of paraplegia secondary to gunshot wound at the T1 vertebra level. He died at home without medical assistance, so a judicial autopsy was ordered.
a) External examination: atrophy of all four extremities and ankylosis of the hands, pressure ulcers with necrotic tissue at the bitrochanteric and sacral levels. Tracheostomy cannula at cervical level. He had a urinary catheter connected to a collection bag that contained urine of a striking purple color.

b) Internal examination: brain with notable weight increase (1,875 g) with a pattern of cerebral edema (flattening of gyri and gyri). Heart of normal weight (325 g) with slight dilation of the right heart chambers and presence of mural thrombi. Right pleural and interlobar adhesions and pulmonary thromboembolism in the left branch of the pulmonary artery. Lungs of slightly high weight (D: 640 g; I: 600 g) with a pattern of moderate to severe congestion and edema. The presence of pulmonary infarction was not evident. Kidneys of normal weight (D: 180 g; I: 130 g) with poor cortico-medullary differentiation, hemorrhage in the medullary area and areas of violet color with purulent material leaking through the renal pelvis. In the urinary bladder, 400 cc of cloudy urine was quantified, with a foul odor associated with sediment in its greenish-cream-colored background with a purulent appearance.

c) Complementary explorations:
- Negative toxicological analysis in blood, urine and vitreous humor samples.
- Microbiological analysis. In the culture of the urinary sediment, growth of Proteus vulgaris was observed.
d) Cause of death: pulmonary thromboembolism was considered to be the immediate cause of death, urinary tract infection as an intermediate cause, and paraplegia as the fundamental cause.
