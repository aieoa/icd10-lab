A 78-year-old male, carpenter, ex-smoker for 22 years and with a history of hypertension, dyslipidemia and ischemic heart disease due to acute myocardial infarction that required double coronary bypass. The patient underwent surgery for colon neoplasia with the pathological result of stage III adenocarcinoma (pT3pN2M0). Given the patient's pathological history, the use of fluoropyrimidines is not recommended. For this reason, adjuvant chemotherapy treatment was initiated according to the TOMOX scheme at adjusted doses (raltitrexed 2.5 mg/m2 and oxaliplatin 100 mg/m2 every 3 weeks). A total of 8 cycles are planned. The tolerance is correct. In the follow-up visit prior to the seventh cycle, the patient reported having received antibiotic treatment the previous days for a lower respiratory tract infection diagnosed by his family doctor. He is afebrile, with grade II asthenia and mild deterioration in kidney function, so it is decided to postpone the cycle. At that time, the total cumulative doses of raltitrexed and oxaliplatin are 26 mg and 1,040 mg, respectively.
48 hours after this last visit, the patient went to the emergency room with a fever of 38.5oC and dyspnea on minimal exertion. Arterial blood gas analysis shows parameters compatible with severe respiratory failure. The chest x-ray shows bilateral pulmonary infiltrates and doubtful condensation in the right lower lobe. The patient was admitted with suspicion of severe pneumonia, and empirical broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy and non-invasive mechanical ventilation were initiated. Echocardiography rules out pulmonary thromboembolism. After 48 hours, due to respiratory worsening, the patient was transferred to the Intensive Care Unit (ICU) requiring endotracheal intubation. It is oriented as severe respiratory failure secondary to community pneumonia without isolated germ vs pulmonary toxicity due to cytostatics associated with adult respiratory distress syndrome. Interactions with the patient's chronic medication are ruled out. The results of microbiological studies (blood and sputum), as well as serologies for atypical microorganisms, were all negative. Computed tomography of the chest shows a component of diffuse interstitial pneumonitis. Given these results, and given the patient's clinical course, it was decided to start high-dose corticosteroid treatment (prednisone 1 mg/kg/day), under suspicion of pulmonary toxicity to cytostatics. The patient's evolution was torpid, with death after 17 days of admission to the ICU.
