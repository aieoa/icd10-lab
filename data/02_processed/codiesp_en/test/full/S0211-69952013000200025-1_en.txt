Female patient, 37 years old, black, history of sickle cell anemia and end-stage renal failure, on hemodialysis for 6 years. She previously had several episodes of sickle cell crises requiring multiple transfusions. In November 2010 she underwent a kidney transplant from a deceased donor, sharing a DR and an A, with a cold ischemia time of 15 hours. She underwent induction with alemtuzumab and methylprednisolone boluses. During the intraoperative period, the patient was hypotensive, requiring dopamine. Renal graft perfusion was slow, with recovery during the surgical procedure. Postoperatively, the patient became anuric, with elevated nitrogen levels and metabolic acidosis; The peripheral blood smear reported hypochromia, anisocytosis, microcytosis and target cells, high-density lipoproteins 677, leukocytes 17,800, neutrophils 90%, lymphocytes 8%, hemoglobin 7.1, hematocrit 21%, platelets 179,000, corrected reticulocytes 5.2%. . Doppler of the graft reported good vascular perfusion, but high resistance indices. Vaso-occlusive crisis of the graft was suspected and management was initiated with transfusions, hydroxyurea and hemodialysis. On the fifth day, a biopsy of the kidney graft is performed; The most important findings observed were areas of infarction in peritubular capillaries with the presence of neutrophils and trapping of erythrocytes, some of them sickle-shaped and C4d positive in 100% of the peritubular capillaries. With these findings, a diagnosis of acute humoral rejection and sickle cell crisis of the graft is made; Treatment is performed with pulses of methylprednisolone, plasmapheresis, tacrolimus, mycophenolate and exchange transfusion, the latter as management of sickle cell crisis. Ten days after the transplant there is improvement in renal function and renal replacement therapy is suspended. The last control creatinine, four months later, was 1.3 mg/dl.
