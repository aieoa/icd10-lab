We present the case of a three-year-old preschooler who came to his health center because of a bitemporal, daily, evening headache lasting for hours, which subsided after analgesia. The pain does not appear during sleep nor does it interfere with your daily activities. There is no aura, sonophobia or photophobia. There is no associated vomiting or other symptoms. Uninteresting personal history. Normal psychomotor and pondostural development. Physical and neurological examination within normal limits. He is referred to Ophthalmology, where pathology is ruled out. An analysis is requested in which ferritin stands out, at 8 ng/ml, with the blood count and the rest of the biochemistry being unremarkable. After this, treatment with oral iron is started.
Two months later, the patient consulted due to persistent headache, so he was referred to Neuropediatrics (normal magnetic resonance imaging [MRI] of the brain) and an analytical control was performed to assess the previous iron deficiency, determining IgA antitransglutaminase antibodies (> 128 U /ml) and IgA (normal). Subsequently, a confirmatory biopsy of celiac disease with Marsh type II-III villous atrophy was obtained, and low-resolution histocompatibility system (human leukocyte antigen [HLA]) typing was performed, obtaining a DQ2 haplotype of celiac susceptibility in cis. After the diagnosis, a gluten-free diet was started, presenting a favorable evolution with disappearance of the headache and correction of ferritin levels.
