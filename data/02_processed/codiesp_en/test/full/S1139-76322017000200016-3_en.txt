Nine-year-old boy who comes to the clinic with a circinate erythematous lesion, with raised, erythematous edges, noticeably more active than the center of the lesion, measuring about 10 cm, located on the right thigh. Treated with hydration it disappears in a few weeks. However, a few months later she presented similar smaller lesions scattered on the chest, neck and thigh.

A culture of the lesions was performed, with negative results. Given the intense pruritus and spontaneous resolution with hydration, the dermatologist responds that the most likely etiology is atopic eczema. Therefore, she recommends treatment with topical corticosteroids.
