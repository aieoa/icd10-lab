A 42-year-old man, a drinker of more than 100 g of ethanol per day, with no other relevant history, who reported a 3-day history (coinciding with an increase in habitual alcohol intake) of low-grade fever, jaundice, and abdominal pain. in the right upper quadrant and abdominal distension. The physical examination highlights: frank muco-cutaneous jaundice, grade I encephalopathy and ascites. There is no increase in the liver or spleen. Cardiorespiratory auscultation was normal. In the admission analysis, the following results were obtained: leukocytes 26,820 per µl (neutrophils, 77%), prothrombin activity 40%, plasma creatinine 1.9 mg/dl; urea 91mg/dl; total bilirubin 21mg/dl; AST 164 U/l; ALT 103 U/l; GGT 152 IU/dl. Negative liver viral markers. The chest x-ray showed a right basal alveolar infiltrate and the abdominal ultrasound showed a small liver, with increased echogenicity and homogeneous structure; normal bile duct; Patent portal vein, splenomegaly, abundant ascites. In the computed axial tomography (CT) of the abdomen, a small liver with a lobulated outline could be seen.
With the clinical judgment of acute alcoholic hepatitis over chronic liver disease with severity criteria and a Maddrey score of 71, he was admitted to the digestive service. Treatment with corticosteroids, enteral nutrition and empirical antibiotic therapy is instituted. On the fifth day of stay, a progression of hepatic encephalopathy to grade III and acute renal failure was observed in the context of type I hepatorenal syndrome. The analysis revealed a worsening of renal function with plasma creatinine values ​​of 4.19. mg/dl; urea 140 mg/dl and total bilirubin 35.3 mg/dl, with elevated transaminase values ​​and alteration in prothromin time persisting.
Pentoxifylline, (as anti-TNF therapy), terlipressin and albumin, (as treatment for HRS type I) and MARS are added to the treatment, after requesting informed consent.
We carry out 3 sessions, on alternate days, with the patient admitted to the Intensive Care Unit (ICU) for each session. The approximate duration of each session was 12 hours or until the filter coagulated. Femoral venous access was used, used exclusively for this purpose. The associated renal replacement technique was continuous veno-venous HDF. Anticoagulation was variable with epoprostenol sodium 5 ng/kg/minute, with Na heparin 5U/kg/hour or with both. The patient was hemodynamically stable during the sessions, without notable incidents.
