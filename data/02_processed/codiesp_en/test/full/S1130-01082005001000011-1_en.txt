A 58-year-old woman was referred for abdominal discomfort. She underwent surgery for breast carcinoma in 1996. A year later, in a follow-up abdominal ultrasound and CT scan, a multicystic pancreatic mass was discovered, with puncture aspiration biopsy negative for malignant cells. She was asymptomatic until 2000 when she started with dull abdominal pain. The physical and analytical examination was strictly normal. Tumor markers in blood (CEA, Ca 125 and Ca 19.9) were negative. Abdominal ultrasound: multiple cystic formations throughout the pancreatic cell, multi-seated, with a solid component and some with microcalcifications, of various sizes, the largest being 3 cm, and without flow inside. All of this is compatible with giant pancreatic cystadenoma. Abdominal CT and contrast-enhanced MRI confirm these findings. Fine needle aspiration was performed on one of the cysts, and no malignant cells were observed. Tumor markers in the cystic fluid show Ca 125: 786 IU/ml (vn: 0-35), Ca 19.9: 174 IU/ml (vn: 0-37), and normal CEA. He was diagnosed with giant pancreatic cystadenoma, probably serous. The patient has not presented clinical or radiological changes since the first imaging study 6 years ago. After discussing it with the interested party and the Surgery Service, both reject surgical treatment for the moment.
