Recently, we treated a 46-year-old man in our hospital with a history of Down syndrome and severe mental retardation, who came to the emergency room of our hospital with diffuse abdominal pain and progressive abdominal distension that had been going on for 24 hours. He had a history of constipation and no bowel movements for the past 2 days. As a history of interest, the patient had presented, 13 years previously, a gastric volvulus that was operated on urgently, performing a surgical gastropexy. During the operation, a dolichocolon was evident, with the absence of fixation ligaments, but no procedure was performed on it. The current physical examination revealed only abdominal distension and bloating, accompanied by diffuse abdominal pain. Rectal examination failed to demonstrate feces in a rectal blister. The blood count and biochemistry were normal. Plain abdominal radiography demonstrated massive dilation of the proximal colon. This distension could also be seen in the computed tomography (CT). The subsequent barium enema demonstrated the typical "bird's beak" image. Although with difficulty, the barium passed into the dilated segment of the splenic flexure. A therapeutic colonoscopy was performed, with which the colonic segment was unwound. The patient eliminated a large amount of feces and gas, remaining asymptomatic after 24-48 hours, for which he was discharged from the hospital and referred to the surgery service. Two days later, symptoms reappeared and he was diagnosed with a recurrence of volvulus of the splenic flexure of the colon. The colonoscopy was repeated, once again resolving the urgent process. The patient underwent scheduled surgery, with resection of the redundant segment of the colon. The postoperative period passed without incident.
