A 42-year-old woman attended the consultation of the Maxillofacial Surgery Service of the Marqués de Valdecilla University Hospital in Santander (Spain) due to discomfort in the right temporomandibular joint (TMJ) that had been going on for several days.
The patient reports right TMJ clicking for years with no other accompanying symptoms.
On physical examination, no dentoskeletal abnormalities were observed. She had a painless maximum oral opening (MAO) of 45 mm, reciprocal TMJ clicking (at the beginning of opening and at the end of closing). Lateral deviation and protrusion greater than 10 mm. Selective pain on palpation of the right masseter and pterygoids.
A panoramic radiograph was performed in which the left condylar duplication was observed.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of both TMJs demonstrated the presence of anterior disc displacement that was reduced to the opening in the right TMJ. In the left TMJ, the morphology of the condyle was striking: anteroposterior bifidity and the presence of grade II joint effusion. To complete the imaging study, a computed tomography (CT) of both TMJs was requested, which confirmed the existence of a left anteroposterior bifid condyle without evidence of degenerative changes.

The patient was treated with conservative treatment using a unloading splint, muscle relaxants, anti-inflammatories, local heat and a soft diet. With this treatment she improved clinically and is currently followed up in routine check-ups in outpatient clinics of our service.
