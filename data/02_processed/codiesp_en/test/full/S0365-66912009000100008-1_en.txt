28-year-old male patient who suffered blunt trauma to his left eye during an assault. In the initial emergency examination, a significant reduction in VA was seen in said eye due to a hyphema, the IOP was 8 mmHg, and Berlin's edema was seen in the fundus. The patient is treated with relative rest, cycloplegia, and topical and systemic corticosteroids. After resorption of the hyphema, VA improved to 1, but IOP was 4 mmHg, so treatment was continued with restriction of physical efforts, 1% atropine and topical corticosteroids, despite which ocular hypotonia persists (4 -6 mmHg), VA is reduced to 0.4 and retinochoroidal folds and papilledema develop. The withdrawal of topical corticosteroids did not improve the clinical picture either.
The gonioscopic study shows a grade IV open angle without other alterations. Ten months after the trauma, an ultrasound biomicroscopy (BMU) was performed, which revealed a tear of the iris root in the nasal area with 360° ciliochoroidal detachment. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) shows foveal thickening (326 microns).

Given the lack of response to conservative treatment, it was decided to perform a transscleral cyclopexy with a contact diode laser using the G probe of the Oculight SLx diode laser (Iris Medical Instruments, Mountain View, California). The procedure was performed under retrobulbar anesthesia and 2 rows of 10 impacts each (power 3 watts, exposure time 3 seconds) on the cyclodialysis area described with the BMU.
In the first 24 hours, no changes were observed in VA or IOP, but after a week a gradual increase in IOP was observed, reaching 12 mmHg at the 6th month, which was accompanied by improvement in VA and progressive reduction of papilledema, chorioretinal folds and foveal thickness. Using BMU, the closure of the cyclodialysis was verified through a peripheral anterior synechiae and the disappearance of the choroidal detachment, which justified the normalization of the IOP. At one year of follow-up, VA was 1 and IOP was 14 mmHg.
