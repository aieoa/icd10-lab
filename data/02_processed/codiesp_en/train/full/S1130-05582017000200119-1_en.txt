A 21-year-old woman who attended a maxillofacial surgery consultation due to a painless tumor at the left parotid level that had been present for about 8 months. The physical examination revealed a solid lesion at the level of the parotid gland, not adherent to deep planes. An ultrasound with fine-needle aspiration (FNAC) was requested, in which a well-defined parotid nodule of approximately 25mm in maximum diameter, predominantly cystic, with a 9mm solid pole inside was detected. It did not show vascularization with color Doppler; the FNAC was inconclusive. Given these ultrasound findings, the study was completed with a computed tomography (CT) of the neck with intravenous contrast. The CT showed a well-defined tumor of about 22mm, located in the superficial lobe of the left parotid, which contained fat inside, and an 8mm rounded solid nodule on its posterior wall. All of this was compatible with parotid cystic teratoma by imaging.

Given these results, surgical intervention was proposed to the patient, which she accepted. A conservative suprafacial parotidectomy of the facial nerve was performed with en bloc resection of the lesion. At the end of the surgery, all branches of the facial nerve were monitored to check their integrity and function. The surgical specimen obtained after resection presented a fragment of epidermis with hair follicles.
The pathological result confirmed the definitive diagnosis of benign mature cystic teratoma of the left parotid gland, in which epidermal and mesenchymal cells, adipocytes, sebaceous glands and mature chondrocytes were found. No atypia was observed.

The patient presented mild paresis postoperatively, completely recovering facial mimicry 4 months postoperatively. Currently, 14 months after surgery, she has no recurrence of the lesion.
