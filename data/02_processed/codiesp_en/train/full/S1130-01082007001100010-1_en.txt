74-year-old cirrhotic woman with HCV associated with Child's stage B8 hemochromatosis with multiple admissions with the diagnosis of recurrent severe hepatic encephalopathy (17 admissions in 20 months). Conventional abdominal ultrasound revealed a heterogeneous liver parenchyma without space-occupying lesions compatible with chronic liver disease, a 13-mm patent portal vein, and a homogeneous spleen that was slightly enlarged (13 cm). On the other hand, the Doppler study demonstrated the reversal of flow in the portal and splenic veins, without visualizing a clear portosystemic shunt. A CT angiography was then performed, which revealed the existence of marked collateral circulation and an associated splenoid-renal shunt. The patient maintained persistently high ammonium levels. Using interventional radiology, partial embolization of the shunt was carried out with microcoils, achieving a notable slowing of blood flow through the shunt. Post-embolization abdominal ultrasound demonstrated hepatopetal portal flow with persistence of hepatofugal flow in the splenic vein and decreased perisplenic collateral circulation.
After embolization, the patient experienced immediate clinical improvement and ammonium levels returned to normal. In the 18 months following shunt embolization, she has required hospitalization on three occasions, in relation to mild hydropic decompensation, hepatorenal syndrome and grade I hepatic encephalopathy.
