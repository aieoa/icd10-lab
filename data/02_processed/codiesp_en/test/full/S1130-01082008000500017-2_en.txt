This is a 27-year-old male patient, also a native of Senegal and with no previous history of interest, who presented with vomiting and abdominal pain. The patient reported symptoms of asthenia and anorexia that had been going on for a month, with diffuse discomfort in the abdomen since the beginning of the condition. Given the progression of the symptoms and the appearance of vomiting, he went to the emergency room. On physical examination, he was in an acceptable general condition, highlighting only great pain on palpation in the epigastrium and right hypochondrium, with an area of ​​filling compatible with a mass at that level being palpated. There were no signs of peritoneal irritation, although he did present voluntary defense. Laboratory tests showed moderate hypertransaminasemia and mild coagulopathy.
An ultrasound was performed, revealing the presence of a large liver mass (approximately 12 cm in diameter) with numerous satellite nodules. MRI confirmed the hypervascular nature of these lesions. Finally, viral serologies revealed chronic HBV infection and alpha-fetoprotein was 12,687 ng/ml, so the patient was diagnosed with giant HCC with satellitosis and HBV infection. Symptomatic palliative treatment was instituted, and he died a few months later.
