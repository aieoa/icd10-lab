Female patient, 13 years and 7 months old, who presented with abdominal pain and vomiting for 2 days.
Family history: no interest. Personal background: Bolivian origin. She arrived in Barcelona 3 weeks ago for family reunification (her mother has been in Barcelona for 5 years). She had menarche 4 months ago. No previous relevant pathologies.
Current illness: abdominal pain of 2-3 days of evolution, diffuse, radiating to the epigastrium, which is sometimes accompanied by vomiting. No alteration of depositional rhythm. She went to an emergency department where she was prescribed ranitidine and omeprazole. She comes because the condition persists. The patient reports that, while she was in her country, she has presented 2 cases of isolated abdominal pain in the last 2 months, which she has related to menstruation. She expects menstruation these days. It is therefore a recurring abdominal pain.
Physical examination: afebrile, good general condition, good hydration of skin and mucous membranes, good color. Truncular obesity. Soft and depressible abdomen. Diffuse abdominal pain on palpation in the epigastrium and flanks. No abdominal masses are palpable. Normal peristalsis. Lumbar percussion fist: negative. Normal cardiorespiratory auscultation. ENT: normal. Normal neurological.
Complementary examinations: once the case has been oriented with the data from the clinical history, the tests will help us eliminate or confirm the organic cause. In our case, the study began with a blood test and abdominal ultrasound that showed: Hct 37.8%, Hb 13.4 g/dl, platelets 338,000/ml, leukocytes 7,700/ml (N 46%); glucose 79 mg/dl, creatinine 0.55 mg/dl, protein 77 g/l, albumin 41 gl/l, total bilirubin 2.1 mg/dl, cholesterol 206 mg/dl, ALT 475 IU/L, AST 215 IU /L, GGT 243 IU/L, amylases 427.8 IU/L, lipase 1298 IU/L. Abdominal ultrasound: poorly distended bile duct with image of 15 mm lithiasis. Pancreas not evaluable due to interposition of intestinal gas.
Final diagnosis and evolution: this is therefore a case of acute pancreatitis secondary to gallstones. He was admitted to hospital and discharged after a week. Five days after discharge, she presented a new episode of the same condition, so she was admitted again and the scheduling of a cholecystectomy was accelerated, after which the patient has not had any further episodes of abdominal pain.
