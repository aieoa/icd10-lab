30-year-old woman, smoker of about six cigarettes a day, with a history of abortion two and a half years before being studied in the Gynecology Service for infertility. In this context, and during the vaginal ultrasound, an intravesical exophytic lesion of approximately 24 mm in diameter was observed, located in the right posterolateral wall, with septations inside.

She was referred to the Urology Service, reporting certain urinary discomfort coinciding with menstruation, without macroscopic hematuria or other accompanying manifestations. The blood analysis showed values ​​within normal limits, while the elemental urine only showed the presence of 18 red blood cells/mcL. An intravenous urography was requested, but no other significant findings were observed; Cystoscopy was also performed, which allowed us to observe an exophytic lesion, approximately one endoscopic field, located in the bladder fundus, and with a pseudocystic surface.
Transurethral resection of the described lesion was indicated and was achieved completely, with good postoperative evolution. The result of the histopathological study revealed a bladder wall covered by focally hyperplastic endothelium, with glandular formations of predominantly tubal morphology being seen in the lamina propria and muscle tissue, with associated components - to a lesser extent - of an endometrial and endocervical nature, all of which is compatible with a diagnosis of Bladder mullerianosis.

After one year of follow-up, the patient has remained asymptomatic, and with no evidence of endoscopic recurrence in the follow-ups performed.
