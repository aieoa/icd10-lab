54-year-old male patient with no personal history of interest.
He attended the Emergency Department of our hospital in September 2005 due to right frontoparietal headache and lateral gaze diplopia of 2 weeks' duration as well as right eyelid edema for a week.
He was initially evaluated by the Ophthalmology Service showing moderate exophthalmos in the right eye, moderate inflammation, hyperemia and swelling of the upper and lower right eyelids, limitation in all gaze positions and diplopia. He had no alteration in visual acuity. The intraocular pressure (IOP) of the right eye was 20 mmHg, the IOP of the left eye was 10 mmHg. The fundus examination was normal.
An orbital CT scan was requested, revealing an increase in the size of the sphenoid bone with a permeative pattern and hyperostosis of the orbital face with a soft tissue mass that bulged the lateral rectus muscle. Likewise, occupation of the ethmoidal cells occurred.

The patient was admitted to the Neurosurgery Service and a cranial MRI was requested, which was reported as a possible hyperostotic meningioma in the right periorbital plate of the greater wing of the sphenoid without being able to rule out sphenoidal fibro-osseous lesions.
It was scheduled to perform a surgical biopsy of these lesions. The anatomopathological result of the biopsy was connective tissue, striated skeletal muscle and isolated bone trabeculae with nests and cords of epithelial cells with extensive cytoplasm that focally formed glandular lumens. Metastasis of adenocarcinoma with immunohistochemical positivity for Prostate Specific Antigen was seen in the bone. (PSA).
Given these findings, the Urology Service was consulted. Taking a directed anamnesis, alterations in urination in recent months (frequency and nocturia) and hemospermia were discovered.
Physical exploration
Rectal examination: prostate volume II/IV, stony consistency in both lobes, fixed, suspicious for prostate cancer.
Supplementary tests
PSA: 389 ng/ml
Transrectal prostate biopsy: Gleason 4+4=8 prostatic adenocarcinoma that extensively affects both lobes.
Bone scanning with Tc99m: the images showed multiple areas of hyperuptake affecting the right zygomatic and right internal orbital region, both rib cages, dorsolumbar spine, iliac blade, right acetabular and ischiopubic region, left iliac spine and acetabular region, and middle third of the diaphysis. left femoral.

Diagnosis
Gleason 4+4=8 prostate adenocarcinoma with multiple bone metastases.
Treatment
Treatment was initiated with complete hormonal blockade (Bicalutamide 50 mg/24h and Goserelin 10.8 mg/12 weeks) and bisphosphonates (Zoledronic Acid 4 mg IV/4 weeks).
Evolution
After the establishment of androgen blockade, there was a progressive improvement in the ocular symptoms. During the first 7 months of follow-up, the patient remained asymptomatic.
After 8 months, the patient was admitted to the Urology Service with symptoms of hematuria, which, since it did not improve with continuous bladder lavage, required bladder TUR, revealing a large prostatic neoformation process that invaded the bladder floor. Pathological anatomy of the resection fragments: foci of prostatic adenocarcinoma with involutional changes secondary to hormonal treatment.
Nine months after the initial diagnosis, the patient was admitted to our service again due to obstructive voiding syndrome, decreased diuresis, deterioration of renal function and dyspnea. A thoracoabdominopelvic CT was performed, revealing a right pleural effusion with compressive atelectasis, multiple mediastinal lymphadenopathy, multiple lung metastases, ureteropyelocalyceal dilation of the left kidney and the already known multiple blast-type metastatic bone lesions.
Right pleurocentesis was performed (pleural fluid cytology was positive for malignant tumor cells). Percutaneous nephrostomy placement was ruled out.
The patient is monitored by the Palliative Care Unit. He died 10 months after the initial diagnosis.
