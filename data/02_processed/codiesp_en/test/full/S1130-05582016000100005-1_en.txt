We present the case of a 24-year-old man referred to the Maxillo-Facial Surgery Service of our institution for evaluation of cleft lip and palate. The patient had no history of interest except surgery of an unspecified type for his underlying disease years before, and with secondary malar hypoplasia. On physical examination, the patient had dysmorphia of the nasal pyramid. Cephalometric analysis and imaging studies including multidetector computed tomography (MDCT) confirmed the presence of maxillary hypoplasia. A 2-phase surgical management was planned based on the clinical and radiological findings. In the first phase, a LeFort type 1 osteotomy was performed with placement of internal maxillary distractors. After the correction of the maxillomandibular relationship, a second phase would proceed, which would probably include extraction of teeth and rehabilitation using dental implants. The first phase of treatment was performed without immediate surgical complications.
The patient attended the emergency department 27 days later due to epistaxis, for which bilateral anterior tamponade and admission were indicated. It was decided to perform MDCT after administration of intravenous contrast (IVC) for vascular study of the facial mass, in which a pseudoaneurysm of approximately 8 × 9 × 8 mm (anteroposterior, transverse and cranio-caudal diameter) was evident in the pterygo-caudal fossa. right palatine with probable origin from the ipsilateral internal maxillary artery.

During admission, the patient presented significant episodes of oral and nasal bleeding with important hemodynamic consequences, so subsequent tamponade with Foley catheters was performed. One day later, selective arteriography of the right external carotid artery was performed with therapeutic intent, which confirmed the presence of the pseudoaneurysm and its dependence on the internal maxillary artery. It was decided to perform embolization in the same intervention with 50% cyanoacrylate gum. The patient tolerated the procedure well, so he was transferred to the maxillofacial surgery ward. As there were no new episodes of bleeding or hemodynamic alterations, 6 days after embolization, the patient was discharged.
