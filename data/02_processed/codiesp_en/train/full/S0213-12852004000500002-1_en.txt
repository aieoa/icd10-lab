A 57-year-old man, with no relevant medical history, presented with an asymptomatic tumor of nine years of evolution at the level of the left buccal mucosa, coinciding with the edentulous area of ​​the upper second molar. The growth of the lesion was slow and at no time did he present ulcerations. Intraoral examination showed an exophytic mass with a nodular appearance and well delimited, 2 The tumor had a pale pink color and a firm consistency on palpation. After causing the outflow of saliva through manipulation maneuvers of the left parotid gland, we verified that the duct was permeable and the saliva was clean and fluid.

The treatment consisted of locating and channeling the Estenon duct through its caruncle, using a pediatric intravenous catheter, followed by biopsy-removal of the tumor mass with a cold scalpel, respecting both the path of the duct and its drainage caruncle. We then leave active intraoral drainage through the caruncle for a week and apply external pressure to the parotid gland every 48 hours.

The histopathological examination showed fibrosocollagenized tissue oriented in bundles in all planes of space, without atypia and covered by stratified squamous epithelium, compatible with fibrous hyperplasia.

One month after surgery, the patient's upper jaw was rehabilitated using a removable metal-resin prosthesis. After a year of evolution, no recurrences have appeared and the drainage of the left parotid gland is normal.
