63-year-old male with no relevant history. He consulted due to a nodular lesion in the right dorso-lumbar quadrant that had been developing for a month and was rapidly growing, associated with asthenia and anorexia for three months, with a weight loss of 10 kg. He did not report any other type of symptoms. The physical examination revealed only slight hepatomegaly. At the right dorso-lumbar level, the 5x3 cm nodular lesion was observed, with a purplish color, not adhered to deep planes but which caused pain on friction and movement.

Analytically, only a urea of ​​74 mg/dl was notable, with creatinine of 1.93 mg/dl and GGT 83 UI/L with normal GOT/GPT. A chest x-ray was performed, which revealed elevation of the left diaphragm and aortic elongation, with no other findings. A biopsy of the skin lesion was performed with a pathological diagnosis of adenocarcinoma metastasis. Given these findings, a thoraco-abdomino-pelvic CT scan was performed, which showed thickening of the esophago-gastric junction, with left gastric lymphadenopathy >1cm in diameter in addition to liver lesions suggestive of metastasis. Gastroscopy showed a lesion compatible with cardia neoplasia. The biopsy gave the result of adenocarcinoma. Given the diagnosis of metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma, the patient began chemotherapy treatment with cisplatin (70 mg/m2 every 21 days) and 5-fluorouracil in a continuous 48-hour weekly infusion (3 gr/m2, days 1,8,15). This scheme is one of the first-line ones used in this tumor and is included in the guidelines of the Spanish group for the treatment of digestive tract tumors (TTD). After the second cycle he presented significant hematological toxicity with febrile neutropenia. He developed septic shock and died in that context.
