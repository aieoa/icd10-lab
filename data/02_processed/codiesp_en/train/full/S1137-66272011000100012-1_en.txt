A 69-year-old woman comes to the clinic due to recurring respiratory infections. The patient does not report allergies or toxic habits, she is hypertensive under treatment with amlodipine and has hypothyroidism treated with levothyroxine. She had a hysterectomy with a double adnexectomy 20 years earlier. She reports a 10-year clinical history of frequent respiratory infections, predominantly winter but also present in summer, consisting of cough, dyspnea, wheezing and purulent expectoration. She also reports orthopnea. On physical examination, she presented rhythmic cardiac auscultation, without murmurs or extratones, and pulmonary auscultation with some isolated rhonchi. Perimaleolar edema can be seen on her extremities. Spirometry was performed with the following result: FVC 67%; FEV1 56%; PEF 73%; FEV1/FVC 68%, positive bronchodilator test. Pulmonary gas diffusion is slightly reduced, being 72.6% of the reference value. The chest x-ray showed kyphoscoliosis without other alterations. In the computed tomography, areas of emphysema and bronchiectasis are observed in both bases, more evident in the middle lobe. In the blood analysis study, only an AAT deficiency (30 mg/dl) was observed. Fiberoptic bronchoscopy was performed without significant findings. With these results, an AAT phenotype is made, which demonstrates the ZZ type. Diagnosed with asthma and D-AAT, treatment for asthma was instituted with significant improvement. Initially, D-AAT replacement treatment is not performed to maintain acceptable lung function.
