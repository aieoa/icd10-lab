We present the clinical case of a 28-year-old man, with no personal history of interest, who came to the clinic because of an indurated area on the penis close to the balanopreputial groove for about 10 days, with what he associated as the only symptoms of discomfort during erections in recent days. In the anamnesis, the only data of interest was the increase in sexual activity in the days preceding the appearance of the injury, without these relationships having been with a risk partner nor having received any trauma to the referred area. He also did not report urinary symptoms or fever.
On physical examination, a subcutaneous indurated cord was palpable, about 2 cm long, close to the balanopreputial sulcus, mobile, non-painful and without associated alterations at the level of the penile skin or inflammatory signs. Inguinal lymphadenopathy was not palpable either.
Among the complementary tests, a study of urine and sediment, urine culture, culture of prostate secretion and urine after prostate massage were requested, which were strictly normal, as well as determination of serology for venereal diseases, which were negative.
An expectant attitude was maintained and sexual abstinence was recommended, with the process spontaneously regressing after 4 weeks.
