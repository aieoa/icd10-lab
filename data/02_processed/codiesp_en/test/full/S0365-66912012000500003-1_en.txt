A 51-year-old woman, with no personal history of interest, was referred to the Uveitis Unit of our center due to episodes of ocular pain and progressive decrease in visual acuity in both eyes for 3 years, which had partially resolved with corticosteroids.
On examination, he had a best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) of 0.7 in the right eye (RE) and 0.5 in the left eye (LE). The intraocular pressure was 28 and 19mmHg, respectively. Biomicroscopy of the anterior segment in the RE showed slight redness of the temporal sector.
The right fundus showed inferior peripapillary splinter hemorrhage, superior temporal retinochoroidal folds, and lipid exudates, with the retina applied.

The image obtained with B-mode ultrasound shows diffuse scleral thickening along with fluid in the adjacent Tenon's capsule, giving rise to the “T” sign.

An interconsultation is made with Internal Medicine, which carries out an examination of devices and systems, and biochemical and blood markers are also requested (ESR, CRP, ANA, ANCA, FR, B27, infectious serology), which rule out the presence of disease. associated systemic. A magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and a computed axial tomography are also requested, which rule out other types of ocular or orbital lesions.
In view of the results, the patient was diagnosed with idiopathic posterior scleritis and ocular hypertension secondary to corticosteroid pharmacological treatment, and treatment with oral prednisone together with ocular antihypertensives was again instituted.
Quarterly check-ups are carried out, during which the patient suffers from recurrent episodes of ocular pain and redness in both eyes, with only subtle signs evident both in the ultrasound (mild sclerochroid thickening) and in the fundus. In order to assess inflammatory activity in these doubtful situations, we used OCT, showing the presence of folds at the level of the inner retina/RPE/choriocapillaris as indirect signs of scleral inflammation, in such a way that OCT allowed adequate monitoring of the patient until achieve a stable clinical situation, manifested in this case by the absence of tomographic anomalies.

Given the chronicity of the process and the need to require prolonged corticosteroid treatment, maintenance treatment with methotrexate was introduced, with corticosteroid rescue.
Currently the patient is stable, with no inflammatory signs observed in the ophthalmological examination or in the tomographic or ultrasound tests.
