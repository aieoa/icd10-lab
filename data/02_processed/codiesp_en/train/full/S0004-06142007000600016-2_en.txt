A 35-year-old male patient with a tumor in the upper pole of the right testis found by chance during a self-examination, which is why he went to a urology consultation where a physical examination was performed, observing a mass of approximately 1cm in diameter dependent on the epididymis, and a testicular ultrasound. , which is reported as a solid nodular lesion in the head of the right epididymis. MRI is performed. Confirming a nodular mass, with the adenomatoid tumor of the epididymis being the first diagnostic possibility.

In both cases, it was decided to perform surgical resection of the nodular tumor in the right epididymal tail, without performing a subsequent orchiectomy.
In both cases, anatomopathological examination of the surgical specimen was performed. Macroscopic histological findings: nodular formation of 1.5 cm (case 1) and 1.2 cm (case 2) of firm consistency, whitish in color and well delimited. Microscopically, tumor proliferation is observed consisting of tubular structures in which the cellularity shows rounded and elongated nuclei without cytological atypia and that occasionally shows vacuolated cytoplasm, all of which is compatible with an adenomatoid tumor of the epididymis.
