A 52-year-old male patient came to the emergency room due to loss of vision in his right eye that had been going on for a week. His medical history was: right nephrectomy for renal carcinoma seven years earlier and right pneumonectomy for lung metastasis three months earlier. At that time he was not following any treatment.
In the ophthalmological examination, his visual acuity was 0.1 in his right eye and unity in his left eye; the anterior pole and intraocular pressure were normal. In the funduscopy of the right eye, a uniform whitish choroidal mass was observed at the level of the lower temporal arch of approximately four papillary diameters, with associated retinal detachment (RD) affecting the macula, without signs of intraocular inflammation. The left eye was normal. A B-mode ultrasound was performed, which showed a 3x6 mm domed choroidal mass of medium-high reflectivity, without choroidal excavation. Angiography revealed a mass with early hypofluorescence, late hyperfluorescence, and a slightly mottled pattern.
Given the patient's history, a consultation was requested with the medical oncology service, which performed the appropriate tests to rule out progression of the process. During that time, the size of the lesion and the associated RD increased, affecting the macula. Initially, radiotherapy treatment was proposed to the patient, but he rejected it and opted for the surgical option. Enucleation was performed one month after diagnosis. The pathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of renal carcinoma metastasis, without surpassing the choroid and without infiltrating the optic nerve. During one year of follow-up, carried out by the Medical Oncology service, no progression of the process was observed and the patient only received immunotherapy with interferon. One year after the diagnosis of choroidal metastasis, signs of progression have appeared and the patient has also received hormone therapy and chemotherapy, without clear improvement.
