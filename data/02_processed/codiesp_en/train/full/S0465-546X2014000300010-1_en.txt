Woman, 27 years old, nursing professional in the Internal Medicine hospitalization area, suffers a work accident such as a puncture with an abbocath during the work day on 11/23/1989. Therefore, a review of the clinical case of the source patient and the respective confirmatory tests is carried out, without finding information in the clinical history of data on communicable diseases. Likewise, the tests carried out on the patient and the worker were negative for viral markers for Hepatitis. 15 days later, she presented a thermal increase quantified at 40º, abdominal pain located in the right hypochondrium, of mild intensity, continuous, without irradiation, does not subside with postural changes or outpatient treatment, accompanied by nausea, vomiting and asthenia. She denies choluria, acholia and jaundice, so she went to the emergency department and it was decided to admit her. During her stay, a serial analysis of liver enzymes was performed, showing hypertransaminemia and serology for hepatitis A and B were negative. (See Table 1). She began medical treatment with Konakión®, Lisozima®, Nervobión® and Polyvalent Human Gammaglobulin, in the same way she was vaccinated against Hepatitis A and Hepatitis B. With good clinical and laboratory progress, she was discharged with follow-up. doctor.

Later in 1996, she underwent an occupational medical examination, and the analytical results found that she was positive for HCV and negative for HIV. In 2004, in view of the worker's personal history of a biological accident in 1989, the source patient was located, repeating the serological control, proving positive for the same virus, and the event that occurred 15 years ago could be declared as work accident and occupational disease, by demonstrating the relationship between the source patient and the worker.
