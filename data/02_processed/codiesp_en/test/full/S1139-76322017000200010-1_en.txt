A two-year-old, ten-month-old girl with a history of atopic dermatitis came to the emergency room due to skin lesions on the trunk and generalized itching that had been going on for 12 hours. Afebrile, without other symptoms. On physical examination, non-confluent erythematous lesions were observed on the trunk, assessed as whey and evanescent, as well as the presence of tonsillar exudate. The rapid pharyngeal streptococcus test is negative. The diagnosis was acute parainfectious urticaria and pharyngotonsillitis of probable viral etiology.
After 36 hours of evolution, he returned due to progression of the skin involvement, with pruritic erythematodesquamative lesions in the periocular and perioral regions and in scattered plaques on the trunk and large folds, along with areas of superinfection and scratching lesions on the trunk. The pharyngeal exudate persists, he remains afebrile and reports pain when picking it up. An outbreak of atopic dermatitis with superinfection was diagnosed and treatment with oral deflazacort was initiated.

After 72 hours of evolution, he came again due to worsening of the lesions, associating low-grade fever, weakness, and pain on palpation of the skin. Complete blood count with leukocytosis of 17,500/mm3 without neutrophilia (neutrophils: 51%, lymphocytes: 34.8%) and C-reactive protein at 4.3 mg/l. Finally, scalded skin syndrome was diagnosed, and admission was decided for intravenous antibiotic treatment with clindamycin and cloxacillin. Promotions with 2% sodium borate and antibiotic ointment are applied to eyelid and perioral lesions. Methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus is isolated in conjunctival and pharyngeal exudate and in a skin lesion; The blood culture is sterile. The patient progressed favorably and was discharged six days after admission.
