A 61-year-old man with a history of NIDDM was admitted for elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy performed with 4 trocars. The postoperative period progressed normally, with a cough on the third day after the intervention followed by a bulging of the entire right anterior rectum, very painful and hard in consistency. An urgent CT scan was performed showing increased attenuation and volume of the rectus abdominis with an antero-superior diameter of about 6 x 20 cm in the craniocaudal direction, compatible with a large volume hematoma in the right anterior rectus possibly secondary to trocar injury. of the epigastric vessels. Initially, it is managed conservatively by monitoring the hematocrit, which drops to two points without the need for transfusion, and finally drains spontaneously through the supraumbilical trocar orifice. The patient is discharged 28 days after the intervention. Diagnosis: large abdominal wall hematoma.
