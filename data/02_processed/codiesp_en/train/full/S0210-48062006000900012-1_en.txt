57 year old male. With a history of right nephroureterectomy for transitional cell carcinoma in 1991 and radical cystoprostatectomy with Bricker-type urinary diversion in 1995 (pTis No Mo, performed due to bladder carcinoma in situ refractory to BCG). He came to our office to consider converting his urinary diversion, as he had stoma prolapse and reported that his life was very limited by the external self-adhesive device.
A thoraco-abdominal CT scan was requested as a restaging study, and no signs of tumor activity were found. His baseline creatinine was 1.5 mg/dl. An exploratory laparoscopy was performed in May 2003, with complete dissection of the pelvis, identifying the urethral stump with a Benique. We resected the urethral stump and an intraoperative biopsy ruled out tumor infiltration. The neobladder (Studer) was constructed after externalizing 45 cm of terminal ileum through the stoma orifice. The urethra-neobladder anastomosis was performed laparoscopically. The total surgical time was 480 minutes, with an estimated bleeding of 100 cc. The patient was discharged on the sixth postoperative day without complications.

The evolution since then (29 months) has been favorable, with no evidence of metabolic alterations or signs of recurrence of the underlying disease.
