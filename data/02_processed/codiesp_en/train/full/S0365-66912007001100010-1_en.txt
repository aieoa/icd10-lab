A 63-year-old patient reported visual acuity (VA) deficit in the right eye (RE) of several months' duration.
Her personal history included post-traumatic epilepsy of ten years' duration, high blood pressure and type II diabetes mellitus.
She was being treated with 1.5 g of vigabatrin and 200 mg of carbamazepine daily since the trauma.
The VA was 0.3 in the OD and 0.8 in the left eye with correction.
Previous biomicroscopy and tonometry were normal.
Ophthalmoscopy revealed paleness of both papillae and a macular epiretinal membrane in the RE.

Humphrey 30/2 white-white and blue-yellow computerized perimetries were performed, showing severe concentric field reductions in both eyes (Humphrey instruments, model 745, Carl Zeiss Inc).

She had no relevant family history, so hereditary optic neuropathy was ruled out.
The anamnesis and analytical determinations of mean corpuscular volume, vitamins B1, B6, B12 and folic acid ruled out a nutritional etiology.
The diagnosis of retinotoxicity due to vigabatrin was established by referral to the neurology service.
At one year of follow-up, field loss and VA loss persisted.
