A 41-year-old man with no toxic habits and a history of lactose intolerance, diagnosed in 2003 with Crohn's disease with an inflammatory pattern and ileal involvement, which debuted two years earlier with an episode of uveitis. He is currently in clinical remission without any maintenance treatment.
He has had a cold in the upper respiratory tract for two months, accompanied in recent days by cough, whitish expectoration and a fever of 38ºC. Initially diagnosed with right basal pneumonia, he received treatment with antibiotics and mucolytics with little improvement. Subsequently he presented dyspnea, purulent expectoration, asthenia and loss of 8-10 kg. After not improving, he was referred to the hospital, entering the pulmonology service.
The physical examination revealed pale skin, Tª: 37.2 ºC, and lung auscultation showed crackles at the right base.
-Complementary explorations:
• Systematic: ESR: 66 mm 1st hour, leukocytes: 6,900/ul with normal formula, red blood cells: 4,570,000/ul; Hb: 11.9 g/dl; Hct: 35.6%; platelets: 366,000/ul. Biochemistry: no significant findings. Coagulation: normal. Sat O2: 95%.
• Blood cultures negative for anaerobes and aerobes. HIV serology: negative. Negative sputum bacteriology. Bacterial Ag in urine: negative for pneumococcus and legionella.
• Chest X-ray on admission: alveolar infiltrates in the right base and minimal infiltrates in the left base, which in subsequent radiological controls become migratory to segment 6, also affecting the right middle lobe. Days later, condensation was observed in the right upper lobe, reducing the basal infiltrates.
• Spirometry: FVC: 2.52 l (56%); FEV1: 2.23 l (60%); FEV1/VC: 78%; MMEF25-75: 1.96 l (46%): non-obstructive ventilatory failure.
• Chest CT: extensive parenchymal consolidation in the right upper lobe; resolution consolidation and volume loss in the right middle lobe; consolidation in partial resolution in the right lower lobe with image of alveolar infiltrate and partial resolution image in the posterior segment of the left upper lobe.

• After the results, fiberoptic bronchoscopy with bronchioalveolar lavage (BAL) and transbronchial biopsy (BTB) was performed: no endoluminal lesions or mucosal alteration were observed. BAL microbiology: normal upper respiratory tract flora with negative Legionella, Gram, AFB and fungal culture. BAL: 80 cells/mm3, 65% macrophages, 25% lymphocytes (increased), 5% eosinophils and 5% PMN. Lymphocyte populations: CD4/CD8 ratio: 0.87. Transbronchial biopsy: widened interalveolar septa due to chronic inflammation and fibrosis. Alveolar cell hyperplasia with desquamation of pneumocytes in the lumen and PAS-negative granular exudate. Myxoid granulation tissue emerging from terminal bronchi into the lumen. No granulomatous lesions or hyaline membranes. All of the above is compatible with organizing pneumonia with bronchiolitis obliterans.
Treatment was started with prednisone at a dose of 1 mg/kg/day, the fever subsided and the rest of the symptoms improved. After one and three months the patient is asymptomatic and radiological controls are normal.
