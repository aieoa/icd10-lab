This is a 5-year-old female patient, referred to our service with the diagnosis of oronasal fistula. Among his history we find that he underwent bilateral cheiloplasty and palatoplasty of the soft palate at the age of 6 months and palatoplasty of the hard palate at the age of 4 years, with the latter procedure presenting a dehiscence (oronasal fistula) that caused rhinophonia and nasal reflux of the food. In the intraoral examination we observed an oronasal fistula at the junction of the primary and secondary palate on the left side measuring approximately 12mm x 25mm.

It was planned to close the fistula with an anteriorly based lingual flap due to the dimensions of the defect.
Technique
Under general anesthesia with nasotracheal intubation, the palatal defect is incised contouring, taking a tissue margin of approximately 8mm. The mucosa that lined and surrounded the fistula is unfolded and its edges are smoothed to create a nasal floor.
An aluminum template is made with the dimensions of the fistula, with which a slightly larger flap is designed. The lateral edges of the tongue are sutured to a horseshoe-shaped acrylic plate, which has the function of stabilizing and supporting the tongue to facilitate taking the flap.

Once the lingual flap is obtained, primary closure of the bloody lingual area is performed. Subsequently, the flap is positioned over the oronasal fistula and the suturing begins from back to front.

Twenty-one days after surgery, a perfusion test was performed; no signs of ischemia were observed, so the pedicle was removed under local anesthesia.
A satisfactory closure of the fistula was achieved; a small dehiscence occurred in the immediate postoperative period, which had a spontaneous closure three months later.
