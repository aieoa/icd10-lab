A 9-year-old female patient who, starting at 3 years of age, presents with protrusion at the level of the nasal root. In the last year, prior to admission, an increase in said deformity is detected, which causes cosmetic alteration and anxiety with schooling problems.
The physical examination only revealed a frontal bulge over the nasal root without pain on palpation or other relevant pathological data.

The study with cranial computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) demonstrates deformity of the frontal sinus, without occupation of it; sinus walls of normal thickness. No findings of intracranial lesions. In addition, there is a concomitant right parietal lesion compatible with a focus of fibrous dysplasia.

The hormonal study reveals levels within normal limits for age.
The patient underwent surgery, making a coronal incision that exposed the frontal bone and nasal root, and a bulging of the frontal sinus could be seen. Multifragmentation and remodeling of the anterior wall of the sinus is performed, preserving its mucosa, using an ultrasonic scalpel. The bone defect is reconstructed with resorbable mesh (polylactic and polyglycolic acids). On the other hand, resection and remodeling of the parietal lesion is performed, replacing the internal cortex.

The patient has no post-surgical complications and has a favorable evolution. The subsequent cosmetic result was optimal. The histological study of the parietal lesion was non-pathological. Clinical follow-up of the patient was carried out for only one year, due to moving home for family reasons, with no data of recurrence or discomfort in the intervention region.
