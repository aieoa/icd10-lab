We present the case of an 85-year-old man, with a history of prostate adenocarcinoma treated with LHRH analogues, maintaining adequate biochemical control of the disease, and who consulted for a 2-month history of an increase in the size of the hemiscrotum. left, pain, fever or other symptoms being absent. On physical examination, a left hemiscrotum larger than normal, under tension, with a mass that prevents identification of the left testicle was observed. Blood tests were requested, proving beta-HCG and alpha-fetoprotein within the reference ranges, and a scrotal ultrasound, reported as: "large cystic mass at the level of the left scrotum, measuring 10x8.3 cm, which compresses and displaces the testicle, compatible with a large cyst dependent on the epididymis, although other possibilities, such as cystadenoma, cannot be ruled out. With the clinical data, and evaluating the differential diagnoses and the patient's situation, he is offered bilateral orchiectomy, the right subalbugineal and the left inguinal, which he accepts.

The surgical piece weighed 550 g and measured 11x11x9 cm. and had a brownish-hemorrhagic coloration; The cut revealed a cystic cavity that compressed the testicular parenchyma. The microscopic pathological study included sections of the cyst wall and the rest of the testis and revealed a cystic lesion lined with flat epithelium, with areas of ulceration, hemorrhage and necrotic tissue; The testicular parenchyma had intense signs of atrophy, fibrosis and vascular congestion, with an almost total absence of germ cells in the seminiferous tubules.

The anatomical-pathological diagnosis was: benign testicular cyst compatible with cystic dilation of the rete testis.
