A 60-year-old male patient, with a Björk-Shilley type metallic mitral prosthesis and tricuspid annuloplasty 20 years ago due to rheumatic valve disease, chronic atrial fibrillation (CAF), HTN, congestive heart failure (CHF) with a NYHA Functional Grade I/IV. He was receiving treatment with acenocoumarol. He was admitted to our service due to fever, chills, and worsening of his usual dyspnea, with orthopnea and edema appearing in MMII. The clinical examination was compatible with CHF (engorged jugular veins, arrhythmic pulse at 100 bpm, gallop rhythm, bilateral basal pulmonary crackles, 2-fold hepatomegaly with positive hepatojugular reflex and pretibial pitting edema in the MMII). In the analysis, he had a hemoglobin of 12.4 g/L, leukocytes 17,810/ml, with 87% neutrophils, the rest of the usual parameters being within normal limits. Chest x-ray showed signs of CHF. Serial blood cultures were performed in which Neisseria Sicca was persistently isolated, so treatment was initiated with intravenous Penicillin G sodium 24 million units in continuous infusion and Gentamicin 80 mg I.V. every 8 hours (first 2 weeks), leaving the patient afebrile on the third day after starting treatment. During the admission, reddish and painful lesions appeared on the pads of the fingers of both hands compatible with vasculitic phenomena, therefore, the diagnosis of Infectious Endocarditis (IE) was made according to Durack's clinical criteria (4). Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE) and Transesophageal Echocardiography (TEE) demonstrated a normal functioning mitral prosthesis and absence of vegetations. On the 9th day of treatment, the patient presented a sudden episode of loss of strength in the left hemisphere, with 3 hyperdense images at the temporal-parietal and occipital levels of both hemispheres compatible with hemorrhagic foci being visualized on a cranial CT scan. At that time in I.N.R. was 2.45, despite which, anticoagulation was suspended. Four days later, and after stabilization of the patient, it was decided to restart anticoagulation with IV sodium heparin, completing 6 weeks of antibiotic and heparin treatment. In the control brain CT scan after treatment, the occipitoparietal hematoma persisted, with the other hemorrhagic foci having disappeared. Given these findings and the possibility of rebleeding, the patient was discharged with bemiparin 10,000 IU. subcutaneous injection once a day, for 3 months, after which a new echocardiographic study and cranial CT scan were performed, which demonstrated the normality of the mitral valve and the practical resolution of the cerebral hematoma, so oral anticoagulation with acenocoumarol was started again, finding the three months of it, asymptomatic.
