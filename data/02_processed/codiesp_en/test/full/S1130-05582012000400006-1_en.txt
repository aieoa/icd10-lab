18-year-old woman referred to the Emergency Department of the 12 de Octubre University Hospital from another hospital after suffering a traffic accident with direct craniofacial impact. The patient did not report any personal history of interest and upon admission she had a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) of 15. Physical examination revealed significant edema and bilateral periorbital hematoma with crepitation, right otorrhagia, and rhinorrhea from the left nostril. Assessed by the Ophthalmology Service, there was no evidence of decreased visual acuity, diplopia, or alterations in extrinsic eye movements, although there was left macular edema. There were no initial cranial nerve alterations except paresthesias in the V1 territory. On palpation a significant offset was observed in the left supraorbital rim.
With the diagnosis of suspected skull base fracture with associated cerebrospinal fluid fistula, a craniofacial CT scan was requested, which revealed a comminuted left frontobasal fracture-subsidence with associated involvement of the ipsilateral temporal squama and pneumocephalus. Fracture of the right petrous bone in its most cranial portion lateral to the superior semicircular canals. Fracture without displacement of the anterior wall of the right carotid canal. At the level of the brain there were multiple contusive hemorrhagic foci mainly located in both frontal lobes, parieto-temporal endocranial bone fragments and minimal subarachnoid hemorrhage in the tentorium with right occipital epidural hematoma, probably due to a contracoup injury.

After the diagnostic confirmation of cerebrospinal fluid fistula (beta-2 transferrin + test) associated with a complex frontobasal fracture, a decision was made together with the Neurosurgery Service to perform surgical treatment:
Bicoronal and left subcranial approach with excision of impacted frontobasal fragments. Repair of basal dural tears using duraplasty and fibrin adhesive after aspiration of a bruised left frontal base. Obliteration of the frontal sinus after obturation of the duct with calvarial bone chip and DBX (demineralized bone matrix) and curettage of the sinus mucosa. Reconstruction of the left orbital roof with titanium mesh and bone fragments fixed to it. Reduction and osteosynthesis of frontotemporal fractures and left supraorbital rim. Temporal muscle pexia.

The patient is evaluated in periodic post-surgical check-ups without presenting symptoms. In control imaging tests, postsurgical changes and complete obliteration of the frontal sinus appear.

Eighteen months after the intervention, the patient again presented occasional unilateral rhinorrhea from the right nostril. After requesting imaging tests (high-resolution CT and MRI), a post-traumatic sphenoidal meningocele with a 1.5cm by 1.5cm paramedial cranial base defect was observed. Associated right frontobasal cerebrospinal fluid cystic cavity.

Given the characteristics of the lesion, transnasal endoscopic treatment was decided. Opening of the anterior wall of the sphenoid sinus with visualization of the herniated meningocele. Intracranial dissection and monopolar coagulation until progressive intracranial reduction. The defect is outlined at the cranial base and fibrin adhesive is applied. The sphenoid sinus is obliterated with abdominal fat. The lumbar drainage was maintained until the 5th postoperative day and the anterior nasal packing was removed the same day without evidence of intranasal fluid leakage.

Control magnetic resonance imaging is performed postoperatively, showing absence of previous meningocele and adequate obliteration of the cranial defect with autogenous fat. At present, the patient is in the third postoperative year after the last intervention without recurrence of meningocele or symptoms of cerebrospinal fluid fistula.
