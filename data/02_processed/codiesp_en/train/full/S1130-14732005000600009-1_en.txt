Male, 53 years old, who began with pain and helplessness in both lower limbs, numbness in his legs with paresthesias, and in recent weeks difficulty walking. The symptoms were partially relieved when lying down and did not worsen while sitting. The predominant symptoms were sensitive, with intense pain in the lower limbs.
The pain increased with Valsalva maneuvers and he suffered cyclical worsening.
The whole situation began in September 2002, a year before being seen in the Neurosurgery consultation.
The patient's only history was back pain and bilateral inguinal hernia surgery years ago, under general anesthesia.
In the Magnetic Resonance images, a polylobulated, intraspinal, extradural collection was observed that compressed the dural sac by 90% of its volume. It was reported by the radiologist as a L2-L3 synovial cyst.

Spinal angiography was not performed, although neither the MRI images nor the surgical procedure showed any type of vascular malformation.
Intervention
A laminectomy is performed from L4 to L2. At L3 and L2, fragments of bone and cartilaginous tissue begin to be obtained, infiltrated by a dark liquid that, in addition, spills into the operating field with an appearance similar to "fuel oil." The tissue in the area is removed, decompressing the dural sac, and the incision is closed.
Pathological anatomy
The Pathological Anatomy report describes both extemporaneous and postoperative samples as osteocartilaginous tissue, muscle and fibrous tissue, with areas of hemorrhage and macrophages in the periphery loaded with hemosideric pigment.

No other types of lesions or any malignant or atypical cells were observed.
Postoperative result
The postoperative period was an excellent recovery without complications and a total improvement in symptoms.
After one year of follow-up, the patient remains completely asymptomatic.
