A 29-year-old woman, single, lives with her parents, with no history of organic or psychiatric pathology or substance abuse, admitted to neurology due to behavioral alterations, disorientation and lack of connection with the environment. The family reported that it was the first episode of these characteristics, that for 2 weeks they had seen the patient discouraged, not very communicative (they did not identify any trigger or decompensatory factor, the relationship with the patient was good, she had a good social support network, work performance, etc.), sometimes he stared blankly, speaking incoherently ("the veins in his head were dry"), there was no alteration of biological rhythms. All of this was a cause of great family distress, so they went to the family doctor, and she was treated as a depressive illness and was prescribed sertraline 50 mg/day (1 week before admission). Her evolution was torpid, with episodes of psychomotor agitation and disorientation, which is why the family decided to take the patient to the medical emergency room. There was no family history of major psychiatric or organic illness. During the examination, apart from spatio-temporal disorientation and agitation, nothing striking was found, and there was no neurological focality.
The blood tests (hemogram, coagulation, liver, kidney, thyroid, urine, folic acid and vitamin B12 function tests) and brain tomography were normal. Urine toxicants were negative. Lumbar puncture (LP) showed 26 leukocytes/mm3. In the emergency room she presented a generalized tonic-clonic seizure, and she was admitted with the presumptive diagnosis of viral encephalitis, and treatment with acyclovir and valproic acid was initiated.
During her admission she was evaluated by Psychiatry. The family again reported that the patient had been apathetic for 2 weeks, repeating that she had "dry veins in her head." She was classified as having psychotic depression and was prescribed venlafaxine 75 mg/day. Serology for HIV, Brucella, Cytomegalovirus, Herpes Simplex, Syphilis, Toxoplasmosis and Epstein Barr were negative.
During the first week of hospitalization, the patient was confused, she presented soliloquies and unmotivated laughter. The PL was repeated (34 leukocytes/mm3, lymphocytes 100%, serology and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) culture for Brucella, Lúes and Borrellia were negative). Brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was normal. She stopped the antidepressant and was prescribed tiapride.
During the second week, the electroencephalogram (EEG) showed signs of diffuse slow encephalopathy of moderate degree. The patient did not respond to simple commands, she was mute, stuporous, so the tiapride was discontinued, leaving her on intramuscular haloperidol in case of agitation. Due to her torpid evolution, she was admitted to the ICU, stopping valproic acid and starting levetirazepam. The patient began to present involuntary movements and dystonia. Transthoracic echocardiography was normal. Megadoses of corticosteroids and hemin were added (due to suspicion of acute intermittent porphyria). Due to the more frequent complex tonic crises, sedation and endotracheal intubation were decided. No uroporphyrins were detected, coproporphyrins and zinc were within normal limits (so hemin was discontinued). PL was repeated, with the polymerase chain reaction for mycoplasma and mycobacteria being normal.
During the fourth week, she was withdrawn from sedation: she responded to simple commands, had facial myoclonus and buccolingual movements, and was extubated. The EEG continued to show signs of diffuse encephalopathy. Prion disease was ruled out (negative for protein in CSF). Gynecological ultrasound showed a slightly enlarged ovary.
During the sixth week, ceruloplasmin, serum copper, and abdominal ultrasound were normal. The patient was more conscious and reactive. The possibility of encephalitis in relation to unobserved teratoma was raised. The LP was repeated looking for the presence of paraneoplastic antibodies (anti-Hu, anti-Yo, anti-Ri, anti-CV2, antiphysin, anti-Tr and anti-calcium channels), which were negative, except for anti-NMDA antibodies. . Psychiatry was called again due to psychomotor agitation and self-referential delusional ideas, and ziprasidone was started at 40 mg/day, reaching 160 mg/day with progressive improvement, with the final diagnosis being encephalitis against N-methyl-D-aspartate receptors (ECR). NMDA) and psychotic disorder secondary to organic pathology (ECR-NMDA).
