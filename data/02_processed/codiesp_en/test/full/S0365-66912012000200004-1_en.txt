A 62-year-old man presented with a painless decrease in visual acuity (VA) in the left eye (LE) for 2 days. He had presented 2 months earlier for red eye, diagnosed with conjunctivitis, and treated with a fixed combination of dexamethasone and tobramycin. So the VA was 1 and the IOP was 14mmHg in both eyes. The personal history was diabetes mellitus, arterial hypertension, obesity, hepatic steatosis, hyperthyroidism, ischemic heart disease, and kidney transplant due to chronic renal failure secondary to chronic pyelonephritis. Within his systemic medical treatment we highlight: prednisone 5mg/24h, (Prednisone Alonga®, Sanofi Aventis), tacrolimus (Prograf®, MSD) and mycophenolate mofetil (Cellcept®, Roche).
The initial examination was VA of 1 in the right eye (RE) and 0.1 in LE, normal biomicroscopy in RE, in LE there was intense conjunctival injection and dull cornea predominantly central with slight edema and negative staining with fluorescein, without Tyndall, synechiae , keratic precipitates or secretion. The IOP was 18mmHg in the RE and 46mmHg in the LE. The fundus was difficult to visualize due to corneal edema, but the papilla and macula were normal, with the retina applied without peripheral lesions. On gonioscopy the angle was open, without synechiae or neovessels. During the examination, the patient's drowsiness and some respiratory difficulty drew attention. He also had short stature, moderate obesity and a short neck.
The initial diagnosis was ocular hypertension in the LE, and treatment was initiated with the fixed combination of 0.2% brimonidine and 0.5% timolol maleate (Combigan®, Allergan). He was referred to the glaucoma clinic and to the pulmonology service, which subsequently diagnosed severe apnea-hypopnea syndrome.
One week later, the VA was still at 0.1 and conjunctival hyperemia, mild corneal edema, and mild superficial punctate keratitis persisted, with IOP of 40mmHg. Pachymetry was 512µm in the RE and 574µm in the LE (increase justified by corneal edema). The differential diagnosis was considered to be a carotid-cavernous fistula or an orbital apex syndrome, for which a computed axial tomography (CT) scan was requested. Treatment was changed to brinzolamide (Azopt®, Alcon Cusi), tafluprost (Saflutan®, MSD) and diclofenac sodium (Diclofenaco-lepori®, Angelini Farmacéutica). Two days later, the IOP was still at 30mmHg, so acetazolamide (Edemox®, Chiesi-Spain) and prednisone 60 mg (Prednisona Alonga®, Sanofi Aventis) were added orally.

Hyperemia progressively decreased, and corticosteroids were gradually reduced, but IOP remained above 26mmHg. The CT scan ruled out any orbital and/or cranial disease. After 5 weeks, a peripheral ulcer with a pseudodendritic appearance appeared with positive fluorescein staining and infiltration of the edges; Corneal scraping was performed for culture and detection of herpes virus C-reactive protein and treatment was initiated with topical acyclovir (Zovirax®, Glaxo Smith Kline) and oral (Virex®, Biogen). Microbiological analysis confirmed positivity for HVS. With this treatment, the corneal lesion healed, leaving a small peripheral leukoma, the IOP normalized, and visual acuity reached 0.8.
