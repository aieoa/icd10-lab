Male patient, 10 years old, referred to the Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery Service of the Piñero Hospital to be evaluated for an increase in volume in the region of the right mandibular angle of two weeks' duration; with no relevant medical history. The swelling was not painful on palpation and there was no report of paresthesia.

The intraoral examination revealed bulging of the buccal and lingual table of the lower jaw. The teeth were healthy and without mobility; normal-looking mucosa.

Radiographically, a radiolucent, unilocular lesion with sclerotic edges was observed. Root resorption of tooth 37 was also evident.

The result of the biopsy was ossifying fibroma, for which enucleation of the lesion was scheduled under general anesthesia. The pathological anatomy confirmed the result of ossifying fibroma.

The last clinical-radiographic control was carried out after eight months, with a good evolution and radiographic evidence of new bone formation, and increased thickness of the mandibular basal layer. The patient remains under follow-up to rule out tumor recurrence.
