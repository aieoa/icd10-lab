22-year-old male (68 kg, 172 cm), active military man, with no relevant history, who was hit by a projectile (caliber 9 mm), due to an accidental pistol shot, at close range, on the right thigh.
After the primary assessment and initial care, he was evacuated to the HGD "Orad y Gajías", where a fracture of the distal third of the right femur was diagnosed, open Gustilo type III, comminuted, and without displacement. In the rest of the examination, no injuries were observed.
Exhaustive cleaning and debridement of the wounds on the right thigh were performed, and immobilization of the right lower limb using a posterior cruropedic splint. Antibiotic prophylaxis, tetanus prophylaxis and antithrombotic prophylaxis were instituted.

In the tertiary evaluation of the patient at the "Gómez Ulla" HCD, an axial CT of the right femur was performed, with multiplanar and volumetric reconstructions. A comminuted fracture of the distal third of the right femur was determined, presenting multiple small fragments displaced to the posterior compartment. After the presentation of the case in a clinical session of the service, surgical treatment was decided using an external fixator.

The surgery was performed by placing an XCaliber external fixator (Orthofix®) with four femoral pins, in a static and monoplanar configuration under intradural regional anesthesia and sedation. No surgical or anesthetic incidents were recorded during the intervention.

The injured man presented a good postoperative evolution, beginning partial weight bearing 24 hours after the intervention. Five days after hospital admission, the patient was discharged home, with subsequent clinical-radiological follow-up on an outpatient basis.
Two months after the intervention, the patient had limited knee joint balance and delayed union at the fracture site. In the review carried out after four months, a CT study confirmed an absence of consolidation of the fracture focus, and knee joint stiffness with a rigid stop at 30° of flexion.
After removal of the external fixator at 18 weeks, the patient underwent scheduled reoperation, under intradural regional anesthesia and sedation, to refresh the fracture focus and internal fixation by means of retrograde femoral intramedullary nailing using a T2 nail (Stryker®), and to To treat the knee extension contracture, a quadriceplasty was performed according to the modified Judet technique, after which a gain in joint balance was observed by flexing the knee against gravity until reaching 110°.

The immediate postoperative period was uneventful, and the patient began early rehabilitation treatment scheduled by the Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation service of the HCDGU.
In the controls after surgery, the patient had a knee joint balance of 110° of flexion with full extension, walking normally and without pain.
Currently, the patient is fully returned to his usual activities.
