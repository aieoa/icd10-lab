A 66-year-old woman was referred to our Service because of uveitis in the right eye for a year. The patient reported decreased vision in said eye but she did not have myodesopsias or metamorphopsias. Her medical history highlighted that twelve years ago she suffered an intracranial hemorrhage that required surgery and a transfusion of two units of packed red blood cells.
In the ophthalmological examination, the best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) was 8/20 in the right eye and 10/20 in the left eye. In the slit lamp examination, cellular remains were observed in the vitreous without signs of activity. In the fundus examination, epimacular membrane was observed in both eyes; no signs of retinal vasculitis or the presence of snowballs or snowbanks were found. Optical coherence tomography confirmed the epimacular membrane. She was diagnosed with intermediate uveitis of unknown etiology.

In the following months, the patient developed progressive weakness in her lower limbs, leading to inability to walk. Likewise, she consulted for an episode of vitritis ++ that she resolved with transseptal triamcinolone (TRIGON depot).
In the etiological study, after ruling out other causes of the neurological condition, with normal nuclear magnetic resonance, given the association of uveitis and idiopathic myelopathy with patellar hyperreflexia, serology for HTLV-1 was requested, which was positive by enzyme immunoassay (EIA) for detection. of antibodies against HTLV-1 (ABBOTT HTLV-1/HTLV-2, Innogenetics N.V.). The EIA samples were analyzed using a strip immunoblot (INNO-LIATM HTLV I/II, Innogenetics N.V.) which confirmed the presence of antibodies against HTLV-1. The diagnosis of tropical spastic paraparesis and intermediate uveitis due to VLHT-1 was then established. She was prescribed treatment with oral prednisone, improving her neurological symptoms as well as her BCVA, achieving vision of 14/20 in the right eye and 18/20 in the left eye.
