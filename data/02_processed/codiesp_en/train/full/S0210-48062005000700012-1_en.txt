A 42-year-old man, resident in Mérida (Extremadura), with a personal history of type I diabetes mellitus and chronic prostatitis; He consulted three weeks after ingesting an unhygienic dairy product due to fever and general malaise. Complementary tests showed a complete blood count, biochemistry, coagulation tests and conventional radiology without alterations. Blood cultures in conventional and Ruiz Castañeda media were negative. The serological study for Brucella resulted: Rose Bengal: positive, sero-agglutination: positive (1/20) and anti-brucella-coombs: positive.
Treatment was started with Rifampicin 900 mg/day plus doxacycline 100 mg/12h for 6 weeks.
After five months he suffered a relapse and presented lower back pain plus fever. On examination, the left iliac region is pasted together with a positive psoas sign. A computerized axial tomography (CT) was performed, which revealed L5-S1 spondylodiscitis with involvement of surrounding soft tissues and a small bilateral psoas abscess. It was decided to resume previous antibiotic treatment to which ciprofloxacin 400mg/12h was added. After a month, given the poor response to treatment, transcutaneous drainage of the psoas collections was performed.
Given the patient's poor progress, a new CT scan was performed in which the existence of large bilateral psoas abscesses and involvement of surrounding tissue could be observed along with spondylodiscitis at L5-S1. The patient was referred to our service where he underwent joint surgery by the urology and neurosurgery services, performing drainage of both muscles and L5-S1 discectomy. The postoperative period passed without incident, so he was discharged with antibiotic treatment doxycycline 100 mg/12 hours along with septrim forte 1/12 hours for four months.

The patient is asymptomatic with good general condition until the last check-up three years after surgery.
