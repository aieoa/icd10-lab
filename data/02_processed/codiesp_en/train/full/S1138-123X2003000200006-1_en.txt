We offer a case of a complicated crown and root fracture, in which intraalveolar transplant was used using Ledermix paste as provisional intracanal medication, mixed with calcium hydroxide, following its evolution for four years.
Case description: In 1996, a thirteen-year-old boy came to the clinic with a fracture of the upper right central incisor, which had been going on for a week, without having received dental treatment. The examination revealed a complicated crown-root fracture that reached up to two millimeters below the gingival marginal edge. Surgical extrusion of the root was chosen. After anesthesia with 4% Mepivacaine, an incision was made in the periodontal ligament and the root was dislocated with an elevator, placed on the mesiopalatine and distopalatine sides, respectively. The root was removed and inspected for additional fractures. The apical fragment was repositioned, seeking to ensure that the tooth was stabilized and the fracture was optimally exposed with minimal extrusion. The tooth was rotated to a position 90º from the original, the one considered most stable, and subsequently splinted to the adjacent teeth. To avoid interference during occlusion, part of the crown was removed.

One week after the initial treatment, the pulp was removed and filled with Ledermix and calcium hydroxide in equal parts, and the crown was provisionally restored with composite. After six months, in the absence of signs of root resorption, the canal was permanently filled with gutta-percha, but the crown had suffered discoloration, acquiring an intense gray color. The non-vital teeth whitening technique was performed, using a mixture of carbamide peroxide and glycerin (Endoperox®, Septodont France). The patient has been monitored for four years, with no signs of root resorption evident in this period of time.
