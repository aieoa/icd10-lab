We present the case of a three-year-old girl who came to Primary Care for three days of toothache and discreet dental phlegmon, without fever.

During the examination, a caries was seen in the lower right second primary molar, with slight inflammation and jaw pain. It was decided to start antibiotic treatment with high doses of amoxicillin and anti-inflammatories and it was indicated that she go to the dentist3.
It is reviewed in consultation after four days. Phlegmon and regional lymphadenopathy persist, but she has no pain. It is decided to continue with treatment and control.
After a week of treatment, the phlegmon is more defined and painful, so it is referred to maxillofacial surgery. That same afternoon she developed a fever, so she was referred to the Emergency Department, where they decided to treat her with amoxicillin plus clavulanic acid and go to the dentist.
After 20 days in Primary consultations she presented an abscess on the right submandibular edge, red, painful and suppurative. She is referred again to the Emergency Department for drainage. There it is drained and samples are taken for culture and pathological anatomy of the ulcerated cervical lesion. Treatment is given with azithromycin and ciprofloxacin.

The result of the pathological anatomy describes granulation tissue with acute and chronic inflammation and central necrosis, without observing microorganisms (with Grocot, Gram and Ziehl-Neelsen techniques) and no lymph node remains are identified.
The evolution of the ulcer is torpid, with treatment every two days at the health center. One month after the drainage, the extraction was performed, obtaining definitive improvement after two months of evolution. The large scar on her face begins to regress.
