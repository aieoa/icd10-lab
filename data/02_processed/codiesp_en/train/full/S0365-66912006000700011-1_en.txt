A 73-year-old patient, ex-smoker, with a history of hyperlipidemia and intervention with triple aorto-coronary bypass eleven years earlier, was admitted for symptoms of one month's duration with dyspnea and angina with minor exertion. For this reason, coronary angiography was performed by cardiac catheterization, which showed three-vessel coronary disease with non-revascularizable obstruction of the marginal saphenous to obtuse bridge. Prior to the procedure, the patient was being treated with an antiplatelet agent and had moderate renal failure (creatinine of 3.7 mg/dl) that did not contraindicate the study.
Since the catheterization, the patient presented a general syndrome with asthenia and anorexia, with evidence of a worsening of his renal function in the analysis carried out two weeks later, with a creatinine of 7.5 mg/dl and an eosinophilia of 13%, so he was admitted with suspected acute renal failure due to CES after cardiac catheterization. Due to the progressive increase in creatinine, he required hemodialysis, and as new signs, purple spots appeared in the lateral area of ​​the foot (livid reticularis), whose biopsy did not reveal cholesterol emboli.
Referred to ophthalmology after 40 days, for a fundus study, he had the following ophthalmological examination: visual acuity (VA) was 0.8 in both eyes, intraocular pressure (IOP) was 13 mm Hg and in the In the right fundus of the eye (RE), a microhemorrhage was observed above the papilla and four cholesterol emboli, and in the fundus of the left eye (LE), two other emboli located in the temporal and nasal branches were observed.
Subsequently, the patient entered heart failure, accompanied by signs and symptoms of acute peripheral ischemia, with worsening of general condition and progressive obtundation until his death 73 days after coronary angiography. At necropsy, numerous cholesterol emboli were observed in the vascular structures of the thyroid, pancreas, heart, spleen, liver and kidneys.
