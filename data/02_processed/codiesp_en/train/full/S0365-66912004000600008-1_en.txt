A 72-year-old man was diagnosed with primary open-angle glaucoma and underwent trabeculectomy of both eyes in February 2001 with good blood pressure control. One year later, the patient underwent cataract surgery using phacoemulsification plus posterior chamber intraocular lens implantation, which proceeded without complications, preserving the posterior capsule intact and achieving a visual acuity (VA) of 0.6 in OD and 0.5 in I HEARD. Two years after filtering surgery, the visual field showed progression in OD and an intraocular pressure of 23 mmHg, so treatment with travoprost 0.004% (Travatan®, Alcon, Fort Worth, Texas) was initiated. One month after starting treatment, the patient reported a decrease in VA in his OD, the examination being compatible with the development of cystic macular edema that had reduced VA to 0.2. The diagnosis was confirmed by an OCT (OCT 3000, Humphrey Zeiss Instruments, Dublin, CA) which revealed the existence in the retinal thickness of low intensity spaces corresponding to cystic spaces, and an accumulation of underlying subretinal fluid, reaching a thickness at foveal level of 437 DE 21 microns. Travoprost was suspended, and treatment was initiated with oral acetazolamide (edemox®, Chiesi wassermann, Madrid, Spain) 125 mg every 6 hours, ketorolac (acular®, Allergan, Madrid, Spain) 1 drop every 6 hours and timolol maleate 0.5 % (timofthol 0.5%®, Madrid, Spain) twice a day. Two weeks later, VA had improved to 0.6, and OCT showed normal macular thickness (213 SD 9 microns), with disappearance of the cysts and recovery of the foveal depression.
