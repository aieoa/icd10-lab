The patient is a 12-year-old male, the result of a full-term delivery with the use of forceps for stationary dilation. Apgar and other postpartum examinations were normal. Rest was indicated during the last trimester of pregnancy due to threatened labor. During the postnatal period he presented delay in psychomotor development. At 3 years old he presented absence seizures without subsequent epileptic seizures or need for treatment. He currently presents with moderate mental retardation, learning difficulties, severe behavioral alterations on the autism spectrum, deficits in sphincter control and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, treated first with methylphenidate without improvement and, subsequently and to date, with antioxidants and improvement of the disorder. He also presents social anxiety and extreme phobias of noises and animals, hypersensitivity to sounds, textures and certain foods, as well as difficulties in chewing and secondary vomiting, generalized hypotonia, lax joints, valgus feet that have required surgical intervention, and a significant alteration of the sleep treated with melatonin. He does not present cardiological or dysautonomic alterations, nor characteristic facial features.
The patient has a healthy older brother, but has a second cousin on the maternal side who is 33 years old and diagnosed with FXS at age 13 after a genetic study. At that time, the entire maternal family branch was genetically studied and it was communicated orally, without a written report, that both the case patient's mother and her sisters were healthy carriers. This condition was not sufficiently valued by the family in the face of successive healthy children of the sisters and the healthy brother of the case patient. The mother of the case patient did not receive genetic counseling or indications for prenatal diagnosis in either of her two pregnancies. Genetic analysis was only performed in the postnatal period in both cases and at her request.
