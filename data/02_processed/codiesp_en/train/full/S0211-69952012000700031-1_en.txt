53-year-old male, retired, who lives in a rural area. He was diagnosed 10 years ago with inflammatory bowel disease, which is controlled only with high doses of steroids. Two years earlier he had been admitted to the Intensive Surveillance Unit for sepsis of intestinal origin with multiple organ failure; Since then he has been on periodic hemodialysis as there has been no functional recovery. The renal biopsy showed tubulointerstitial nephropathy.
One month before admission, he had erythematous skin lesions on indurated, pruritic and painful areas on the back of his right foot and inner thighs, with a poor response to antibiotic treatment (initiated empirically due to suspicion of cellulitis), which They evolved towards peeling and subsequent ulceration. A skin biopsy was performed and was reported as cryptococcosis. Direct examination with India ink and Gram stain revealed abundant large, spherical yeasts with marked budding and encapsulation. The culture yields yeast fungus, urease positive, compatible with cryptococcus neoformans, variety neoformans. Positive serum cryptolatex 1/2048. Pulmonary and neurological involvement was ruled out by computed axial tomography and lumbar puncture.

We started treatment with voriconazole 200 mg/12 hours, presenting after 10 days a striking alteration of the liver tests (total bilirubin: 9.59 mg/dl; direct: 9.41 mg/dl; glutamic-oxaloacetic transaminase [GOT] 176 U/l; glutamic-pyruvic transaminase [GPT]: 226 U/l) which required its replacement by amphotericin B at a dose of 100 mg/day. In addition to this, the dose of prednisone was decreased. With this, after 2 weeks there was a notable improvement in the lesions with almost complete disappearance of the ulcerations and normalization of the liver tests.
