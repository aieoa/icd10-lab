13-year-old boy with no personal history of interest. His relevant family history included a brother who died of Wilms tumor. He consulted for urinary symptoms (dysuria, frequency) without other symptoms. His urine test strip was negative. He was started on treatment with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole until the urine culture result was obtained, which was reported as sterile. Given the lack of improvement after a week, he went to the Emergency Department. In the anamnesis he associated voiding symptoms and anuria of 18 hours duration, along with abdominal pain located in the hypogastrium and the right iliac fossa. He reported no history of surgery or fever since the onset of the condition. On examination, he was in good general condition with no signs of dehydration, and his vital signs were within normal values. Cardiopulmonary auscultation was normal. Regarding the abdominal examination, palpation was painful at the level of the hypogastrium and the right iliac fossa, with local signs of peritoneal irritation. He did not have a bladder balloon. The rest of the examination showed no relevant findings.
Analytical tests did not show findings of interest: leukocytes 5400 μl, neutrophils 2370 μl (44%), hemoglobin 13.9 mg/dl, C-reactive protein <2.9 mg/l. An abdominal x-ray was performed, which showed no alterations, and an abdominal ultrasound showed no findings in the urinary tract, with a practically empty bladder.
However, an 8 mm thickened appendix with free periappendiceal fluid and thickening of the adjacent fat was visualized; findings compatible with acute appendicitis.

An infusion of intravenous fluids was performed in an expansion regime, obtaining adequate diuresis in a short time. Given the diagnosis of acute appendicitis, he underwent surgery under general anesthesia, phlegmonous appendicitis was identified and an appendectomy was performed. The subsequent evolution was favorable, with no postoperative complications occurring.
