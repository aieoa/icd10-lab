32-year-old woman, diagnosed with SLE and antiphospholipid syndrome 6 years ago, monitored in Rheumatology outpatient clinics, treated with prednisone (15 mg per day), ASA (100 mg per day), calcium (1 g), vitamin D (800 u) and omeprazole (20 mg per day). She reported fever (40 ºC) predominantly in the evening, lasting a week, without other accompanying symptoms. On physical examination, only hepatosplenomegaly draws attention, without palpable lymphadenopathy. Regarding complementary examinations, blood cultures, urine cultures, and mantoux are negative; Echo-Dopler and chest x-ray, within normal limits; Normal thoraco-abdominal CT scan, except hepatosplenomegaly without apparent focal lesions. In the analysis, Hb 6.7, leukocytes 1,000 (400 neutrophils, 500 lymphocytes), platelets 60,000, ANA 1/1,280, antiDNA 42.3, rheumatoid factor 176, CRP 11.9, C3 122/C4 11.2, proteinogram ( gamma globulin 44.2) and normal serologies. Given a possible outbreak of SLE, a decision was made to transfuse two packed red blood cells, daily injections of C-GSF, boluses of 1 g of methylprednisolone for three days, and amikazine and ceftazidime as empirical treatment for neutropenia. After four days with the treatment described, a fever of 40 ºC remains, with similar analytical values: in new analysis Hb 8.5, leukocytes 1,000 (400 neutrophils, 500 lymphocytes), platelets 32,000, thinking that the condition does not correspond to a disease outbreak. It was decided to perform a sternal puncture, revealing abundant hemoparasites of the genus Leishmania, confirming the suspected diagnosis, we began treatment with liposomal amphotericin B 200 mg per day for 5 days, remaining afebrile on the second day. Control analysis after 10 days of treatment: with Hb 11, leukocytes 2600 (1,700 neutrophils) and platelets 97,000 at discharge.
