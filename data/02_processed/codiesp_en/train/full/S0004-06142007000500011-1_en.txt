11-year-old girl, white, past health history. She was admitted when her parents noticed an increase in abdominal volume for approximately 12 days, accompanied by weight loss and lack of appetite. On physical examination, the mucous membranes are pale, and a tumor measuring 8-10cm is palpable on the right abdominal flank, with defined edges, firm and non-painful consistency, which passes the abdominal midline. Routine examinations show a hemoglobin of 90mg/lt and an erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) of 130mm/1 hour. The chest displacement of the right kidney downwards and outwards due to the tumor and the ultrasound shows a solid, echogenic mass, located on the right abdominal flank above and towards the lower pole of the right kidney, but without infiltrating it, 11cm in diameter. Surgical treatment was decided with the preoperative diagnosis of Neuroblastoma, after blood replacement by transfusion. During the operation, several tumors were observed in the middle of the transverse colon, the largest measuring 7cm, which were resected and removed in their entirety. In the macroscopic examination of the surgical specimen, whitish-looking tumors are observed, with apparently good delimitation, a firm consistency and swirling appearance on section, with focal calcifications. The microscopic study showed that the intestinal tumor was made up of a proliferation of fibroblastic cells, with characteristics in some areas similar to smooth muscle cells, foci of calcification and necrosis, making the histopathological diagnosis of myofibromatosis.
