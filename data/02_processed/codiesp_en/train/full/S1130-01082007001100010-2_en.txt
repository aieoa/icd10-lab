A 49-year-old man with alcoholic cirrhosis associated with HCV infection, Child's stage B7, was admitted due to altered level of consciousness. Ammonium levels during admission remained persistently high. An abdominal ultrasound was performed, which showed a very dilated and tortuous collateral vessel at the peripancreatic level, a heterogeneous liver parenchyma without space-occupying lesions, and a homogeneous splenomegaly of 15 cm. Subsequently, CT angiography was performed, which confirmed the existence of abundant collateral circulation associated with a porto-systemic splenoid-renal shunt.

Using interventional radiology techniques, partial embolization of the shunt was carried out with microcoils. Postembolization abdominal ultrasound confirmed the disappearance of the splenoid-renal shunt. After embolization, the patient showed rapid clinical improvement (disappearance of drowsiness) and laboratory analysis (normalization of ammonium levels). He has not developed gastroesophageal varices or ascites. He has not required hospital admission again during the 10 months of follow-up.
