Male patient, 64 years old, with a history of type 2 diabetes mellitus of 20 years of poorly treated evolution, systemic arterial hypertension diagnosed 6 months ago without treatment, heart failure diagnosed 6 months ago and diagnosis of chronic renal failure, also done 6 months ago, treated with peritoneal dialysis.
He presented with symptoms of 2 weeks' duration, after being hospitalized for metabolic decompensation, where a transurethral catheter was placed for 2 weeks; He reported abdominal pain in the hypogastrium, of moderate intensity and oppressive type, radiating to the glans, and use of self-prescribed topical treatments without improvement. The pain intensified to the point of being disabling 3 days before his admission, so he came to our service. He denied fever or any other accompanying symptoms.
On physical examination, the abdomen was unchanged, the penis had necrosis in the glans and purulent secretion through the urethral meatus, as well as areas of necrosis in the first and second toes of both feet.
Laboratory tests showed: hemoglobin 9.9 g/dl, leukocytes 11,100, platelets 304,000, glucose 138 mg/dl, BUN 143.8 mg/dl, creatinine 7.71 mg/dl, calcium 7.79 mmol/l, phosphorus 7.08 mmol/l, potassium 5.75 mmol/l, pH 7.17, pCO2 47 mmHg and HCO3 17.1 mmol/l.
He underwent a phallectomy, initially partial, but intraoperatively it was decided to be complete due to lack of vascularity to the base of the penis; In addition, a perineal meatus was performed.
The pathology report was: distal ischemic necrosis of the glans and foreskin associated with microthrombosis and bacterial proliferation, resection edges with dystrophic calcification of the arterial media.
The secretion culture result was Escherichia coli, for which he received antibiotic therapy for 7 days. He was hospitalized for 9 days and was discharged in good general condition; He is currently under surveillance.
