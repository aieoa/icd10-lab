An 83-year-old man with a history of prostate neoplasia treated with radiotherapy, currently asymptomatic untreated radiation proctitis, hiatal hernia and asymptomatic gastroduodenal ulcer on maintenance treatment with omeprazole 20 mg/24 h, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and untreated pneumoconiosis. after 40 of evolution.
He went to the emergency department with pain in the left hemithorax that had been going on for 24 hours, continuous, dull and non-pleuritic. He maintained his appetite, oral tolerance, and daily bowel habits. He reported no previous trauma or previous invasive surgeries or studies. He maintained a good general condition, was apyretic and hemodynamically stable. He presented a normal neurological, cardiological and abdominal examination; no lymphadenopathy was found. Some isolated bilateral pulmonary rhonchi were heard. The electrocardiogram, blood count, leukocyte formula, coagulation study, blood glucose, ionogram, serial troponins, and kidney and liver function tests were normal.
Simple chest x-ray showed a bilateral basal reticulo-nodular pattern. The abdominal X-ray showed bilateral pneumoperitoneum, with a rectified segment of small intestine in the mesogastrium in the absence of intestinal obstruction.
Emergency thoraco-abdominal computed tomography (CT) was performed, which revealed a segment of small intestine with a dissection of its wall due to intramural gas, presence of gas in the subsidiary mesenteric vessels and the portal vein, and bilateral pneumoperitoneum.
