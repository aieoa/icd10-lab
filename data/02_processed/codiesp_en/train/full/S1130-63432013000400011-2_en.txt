A 62-year-old man with long-standing type 2 diabetes mellitus secondary to chronic calcified pancreatitis with a tendency to hypoglycemia due to the administration of rapid insulin, multiple admissions for urinary infections and functional deterioration.
Upon admission, he suffered from neglect of personal hygiene with uncontrolled elimination habits, malnutrition, chronic renal failure, diabetic ketoacidosis, and a grade III sacral ulcer.
The culture of the ulcer exudate showed that it was positive for PAMR and methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and the antibiogram showed that the PAMR was sensitive only to colistin (MIC < 2) and intermediate to amikacin (MIC = 16). MRSA was sensitive to cotrimoxazole (MIC < 1/19) and intermediate to clindamycin (MIC = 1.9).
Treatment was started with colistin cream 0.1% with a daily application in the treatment and cotrimoxazole 800/160 mg was administered orally every twelve hours for the treatment of MRSA. After one month of treatment, a favorable clinical evolution of the ulcer was observed, with growth of edges and granulation tissue, which had not been observed prior to topical treatment.
The patient died due to chronic renal failure exacerbated by urinary sepsis, which is why a post-treatment culture was not available.
