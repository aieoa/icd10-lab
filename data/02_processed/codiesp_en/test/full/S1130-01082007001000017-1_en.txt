A 37-year-old male, with no relevant medical history, went to the emergency room guarded by police officers from the airport for suspected ingestion and intra-abdominal transport of drug packages (BP). During the journey of a regular flight, the patient presented a seizure episode and subsequent expulsion of three foreign bodies orally. Upon his arrival, the patient presented: blood pressure 157/88 mmHg, heart rate 108 beats/min, temperature 37 ºC and O2 saturation 94%. On examination, his abdomen was distended and dull on percussion, not painful, with palpation of multiple foreign bodies in the mesogastrium and no peritonism. On a neurological level he showed slightly mydriatic pupils, Glasgow 15 and absence of neurological focality. Abdominal radiography confirmed the presence of multiple radio-opaque intra-abdominal foreign bodies. The most relevant analytical findings were: leukocytes 14.1 x 109/l (neutrophils: 86%), creatinine 1.7 mg/dl and glucose 131 mg/dl. The electrocardiogram showed sinus tachycardia. Urinalysis was positive for cocaine and its metabolites and benzodiazepines. Evacuative treatment was started with polyethylene glycol solution (Casenglycol®) and strict clinical observation. Two hours after admission, the patient suffered an episode of psychomotor agitation and tachycardia (120 beats/min), followed by a decreased level of consciousness, motor incoordination, mydriasis and tonic-clonic crisis, for which he was transferred. to an intensive care unit. Clonazepam (1 mg) and diazepam (10 mg) were administered e.v. and it was decided to perform an urgent laparotomy. After entering the abdominal cavity, multiple hard foreign bodies were palpated throughout the entire digestive tract. A transverse gastrotomy was performed at the antral level, a longitudinal ileotomy 15 cm from the ileocecal valve and two longitudinal colotomies (at the level of the transverse colon and sigmoid colon), with extraction of multiple foreign bodies. Anterograde lavage of the rectum-sigma was performed with physiological saline through a colotomy, managing to evacuate another four bundles located distal to the promontory. A total of 96 cylindrical foreign bodies measuring 5 cm in length were extracted with multilayer latex wrapping, smooth, compact and apparently resistant in manufacture. None of the packages showed visible signs of breakage. The patient remained under observation for 24 hours in an intensive surveillance unit. He started peristalsis and oral diet on the second postoperative day. The patient presented a correct subsequent evolution, being discharged from the hospital on the fourth postoperative day.
