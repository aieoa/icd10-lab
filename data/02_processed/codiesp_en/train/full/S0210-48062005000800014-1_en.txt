A 60-year-old male patient, smoker, severe alcoholism, with a medical history of arterial hypertension and an episode of acute alcoholic pancreatitis in November 2003, upon admission, an abdominal CT scan was performed, which revealed diffuse pancreatic inflammation with exudates, as well as the presence of two tumors in the anterior leaflet of the left kidney, one of them with tomodensitometric characteristics that suggested a hemorrhagic cyst or one with high protein content, without being able to rule out a solid tumor.

After recovery from the acute inflammatory condition, the case was discussed with us, and we continued the study of said tumor by performing a new control abdominal CT in February 2004, observing the persistence of both kidney lesions. Given the radiological characteristics of the tumor, we indicated surgical intervention. On March 29, 2004, we performed left renal tumorectomy via lumbotomy.

The pathological anatomy result of the specimen turned out to be steatonecrotic granuloma with abundant ferric pigment, characteristic of fatty necrosis secondary to pancreatitis.
