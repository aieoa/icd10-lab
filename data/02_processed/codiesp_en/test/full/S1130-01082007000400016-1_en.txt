We refer the case of a 73-year-old patient who consulted for jaundice and constitutional syndrome of less than 2 months' duration. In the Emergency Department, a large painful hepatomegaly was evident and the laboratory tests revealed leukocytosis with neutrophilia and a mixed alteration of the liver profile, predominantly cholestatic (alkaline phosphatase and GGT more than 10 times the normal value and ALT and AST less than 3 times the normal value). normal value), without liver failure.

Twenty-four hours after admission, she began to experience confusion that quickly evolved into coma and fever greater than 39ºC. Abdominal ultrasound and CT were performed, revealing multiple space-occupying hepatic lesions, hypodense, solid and distributed in both lobes, occupying practically the entire organ, suggestive of MTS. No infectious focus was found, several blood cultures were sterile and, using CT, the presence of brain lesions had been ruled out.

Analytically, the liver profile deteriorated, presenting a significant elevation of transaminases in the range of acute hepatitis (ALT and AST greater than 20 times the normal value with a significant increase in LDH) and data of liver failure with progressive deterioration of kidney function.
Despite intensive treatment with antiencephalopathy measures, vasoactive drugs, broad-spectrum antibiotics at high doses (to cover the CNS as a possible focus) and transfusion of fresh frozen plasma, the patient died 5 days after admission as a result of multiple organ failure. , without establishing the nature of the liver lesions or their origin due to rapid evolution. For this reason, a clinical autopsy was performed.
The necropsy confirmed the existence of extensive hepatic MTS. The primary tumor was a cecal adenocarcinoma measuring 3 x 2 cm in diameter, Dukes stage D and Astler-Coller stage IV. There was infiltration of serosal and perivascular fat and lymphatic and venular infiltration. MTS in infradiaphragmatic locoregional nodes and lung parenchyma. In addition, lesions of associated ischemic hepatitis and cholangiolitis and extensive pancreatic and peripancreatic autolysis were observed.
