This is a 55-year-old man, allergic to tetracyclines and beta-lactams, with a history of high blood pressure, being followed up by the Chronic Pain Unit for a clinical picture of low back pain and left lumbosciatalgia of spondyloarthritic origin with an associated myofascial component.
Medical treatment is instituted with tramadol, pregabalin and duloxetine at usual doses and a diagnostic-therapeutic infiltration is performed with local anesthetic and corticosteroids of deep muscles (left psoas and quadratus lumborum). After a clinical improvement of more than 50%, a new infiltration was scheduled, according to the hospital protocol, of the psoas and left quadratus lumborum muscles, with botulinum toxin serotype A (Dysport®) 200 IU per muscle in the same session and without incident. Four months later, the patient presented a self-limited episode of eyelid ptosis without consulting for it. Since then, he has reported episodes of ptosis, showing improvement after infiltration with local anesthetic and corticosteroids of trigger points in the shoulder girdle. Six months later, he presented an episode of eyelid ptosis associated with diplopia and jaw weakness, which is why he consulted and was studied in the Neurology Service of the hospital and was diagnosed with myasthenia gravis (MG), starting corticosteroid and pyridostigmine treatment with improvement in the clinical condition. .
