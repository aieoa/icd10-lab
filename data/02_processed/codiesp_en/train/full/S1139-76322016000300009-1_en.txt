Ten-year-old boy who went to the Emergency Department with testicular pain that had been going on for four hours. He reported that, having previously been well, he progressively began to have discomfort in his left testicle. He did not report fever, previous trauma or voiding syndrome. He had no relevant family or personal history.
The clinical examination was normal, except for the left testicle, which showed a slight increase in size, erythematous and uncomfortable on palpation, but with relief of pain with testicular rise. Cremasteric reflex present. Both testes were palpated in a normal position, with no skin lesions observed.
Given the acute scrotal pain, a testicular Doppler ultrasound was requested to complete the study, observing images compatible with left epididymitis and ruling out testicular torsion. As a chance finding, an image of 1.5 mm right intratesticular calcification was observed.

Analgesia measures and recommendations were prescribed to minimize discomfort (scrotal elevation and rest), explaining the warning signs in which to consult again. He evolved favorably.
Given the image of intratesticular calcification, a Pediatric consultation was scheduled for evolutionary follow-up.
In the first two years of follow-up, the patient has remained asymptomatic and no changes have been observed regarding the number or distribution of testicular microcalcifications, nor have any changes been reported in the echogenicity of the testicular parenchyma. We will continue to perform periodic clinical and ultrasound checks to assess his progress.
