A 12-month-old female infant who came to the clinic due to persistent crying, a slight cough, and respiratory distress that had lasted for about 10 hours. She associated two vomitings, partial refusal of food and rhinorrhea. Her personal history included three previous episodes of bronchospasm. As a family history worth highlighting, her mother suffered from asthma.
The clinical examination showed: weight of 8.1 kg (P3), height of 72 cm (P25), temperature of 37.4º C, blood pressure of 101/85 mm Hg, heart rate of 148 beats per minute, heart rate respiratory rate of 68 breaths per minute and oxygen saturation of 90%. She had a general good appearance, she was irritable and did not calm down in her mother's arms. She had labored breathing, with subcostal and intercostal indrawing. Respiratory auscultation revealed good air entry with bilateral expiratory wheezing and prolonged expiration. Cardiac auscultation was normal. Her oropharynx was hyperemic, with abundant mucus in the cavum. Otoscopy revealed bilateral tympanic hyperemia with preserved light reflex. The rest of the exploration was normal.
Anteroposterior and lateral chest x-rays were performed, which revealed air trapping with the presence of anterocardiac air, a continuous diaphragm sign, and minimal subcutaneous emphysema in the neck. She was diagnosed with pneumomediastinum associated with bronchospasm and was admitted to the hospital. She was administered oxygen treatment via nasal prongs as needed, inhaled salbutamol, and oral prednisolone. The clinical evolution was good, on the third day there were no signs or symptoms of respiratory distress, and the control chest x-ray showed a decrease in the size of the air in the mediastinum, so she was discharged. The sweat chloride test was normal.
