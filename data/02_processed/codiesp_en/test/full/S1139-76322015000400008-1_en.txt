Eight-year-old boy with diffuse intermittent abdominal discomfort in recent years, attributed to acute banal processes. No personal or family history of interest. No urine infections or other urinary symptoms.
Coinciding with an episode of abdominal pain in the right hemiabdomen, an abdominal ultrasound was performed, which revealed mesenteric adenitis and mild dilation of the right renal pelvis, with suspicion of stenosis of the UPU. Normal urine sediment and urine culture.

The symptoms of abdominal pain disappeared spontaneously, but he was referred to Pediatric Nephrology for follow-up.
In the control ultrasound, being asymptomatic, dilation of the urinary tract with mild-moderate dilation of the right renal pelvis was still visible, so an isotopic renogram was requested (suspected stenosis of the pyeloureteral junction). The renogram confirmed obstructive ectasia in the right kidney. In the renal scintigraphy study, an increase in right kidney size was observed, with evident hypouptake throughout the medial edge, signs of duct dilation, with a renal function of 58.8% in the left kidney and 41.2% in the right kidney. .
Given the dilation of the urinary tract due to obstructive causes, he was referred to Children's Urology for surgical intervention. A nephrostomy catheter was placed for a few months to recover and prevent deterioration of renal function5,6. After removal of the nephrostomy catheter, imaging, clinical and analytical controls continued, gradually returning to normal.
