44-year-old male patient, whose personal history includes testicular trauma in childhood that did not require surgery. He presented due to an increase in the size of the right hemiscrotum associated with mild testicular discomfort for 7 months and self-palpation of a right paratesticular mass.
The physical examination revealed a penis with no findings of interest, a left testis of normal size, mobility and consistency, and an enlarged right hemiscrotum with a normal testes and an adjacent structure in the upper pole separated from the theoretical right testes. indurated or painful to the touch.
For a first approach to diagnosis, germinal tumor markers are requested, with normal results, and a testicular ultrasound that describes a structure adjacent to the right teste in its upper pole differentiated from it, smaller in size and sonographically similar to the teste, associated with hydrocele. right, so the initial suspicion is polyorchidism with associated hydrocele.

A CT scan was also performed showing two similar-looking structures in the right hemiscrotum, one of them smaller than the other, with no other exploratory findings of interest.

An exploratory scrototomy was performed where a right hydrocele and two related structures were observed, compatible with the testis, sharing the epididymis and the deferens. A biopsy is taken of the smaller structure and the vaginal eversion is performed to resolve the hydrocele. The pathological anatomy resulted in preserved testicular tissue without signs of atrophy.
