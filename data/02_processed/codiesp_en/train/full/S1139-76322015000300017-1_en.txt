Four-year-old patient, born in Mali and adopted at six months of age, without significant pathological history and correct immunizations. She consulted due to the appearance of papular skin lesions and crusted nummular plaques with a tendency to converge on the anterior aspect of the right thigh. Initially, topical fusidic acid was prescribed, without clinical improvement and with the appearance of similar lesions on the trunk, the roots of the extremities and on the face. She had no palmoplantar or mucosal involvement. The lesions were not pruritic.

Upon re-interrogation and examination of the patient again, she highlighted an isolated lesion on the dorsum of the right foot, oval in morphology, hyperpigmented with a perimarginal scaly collar, which had preceded the rest of the lesions and was oriented as a herald plaque. The final diagnosis was Gibert's pityrasis rosea in a black patient. Symptomatic treatment was started with good evolution.
