Patient aged 13 years and 11 months with a personal history of thyroglossal cyst operated on at 2 years old, obesity under study, which manifested 2 months before admission: asthenia, headache, anorexia, frequent epistaxis and tachycardia after physical exercise. The examination revealed pallor of the skin and mucosa, laterocervical and submandibular lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly of 6 cm and hepatomegaly of 2 cm. Various complementary examinations are requested, among which the following stand out: Hct: 28.3%; Hb: 7.2 g/dl; leukocytes: 235,600 mm3 (L 83.8%, M 1.1%, N 2%, E 0.1%, B 7.9%); platelets: 24,000 mm3; ESR: 70 mm/h; LDH: 1,686 U/l; uric acid: 8.3 mg/dl; ferritin: 151 ng/dl. Given the patient's clinical and analytical evolution, a hematological neoplastic process is suspected and a bone marrow aspirate is performed in which 97% blasts are observed, with medullary infiltration of blast elements. After completing the cytogenetic, molecular, serological, microbiological and immunological study, the diagnosis of acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), FAB type L2 is reached. Chemotherapy is started according to SHOP 99 protocol for very high-risk patients, achieving complete remission at the end of the induction phase. Four months into the consolidation phase, the patient relapses, and the fourth phase of treatment, maintenance therapy, is necessary to keep the child in remission.
The treatment of ALL is carried out according to established protocols (4), and it is common that throughout the entire disease treatment process, the child has to undergo multiple lumbar punctures for the administration of chemotherapy and spinal aspiration punctures to control the disease (5).
After obtaining parental consent and explaining the treatment to the child, the sites for lumbar puncture and bone marrow aspiration were chosen. The patient was premedicated with midazolam (Dormicum®) 5 mg p.o. and OTFC transmucosal oral fentanyl citrate (Actiq®) 400 mcg p.o., and an EMLA® cream dressing was placed on the puncture sites. Oxygen was administered through nasal prongs at 4 l/m, and the electrocardiogram, capnography, pulse oximetry, and bloodless blood pressure were monitored from the beginning of the process to discharge from the post-anesthesia recovery unit, in accordance with the recommendations of the Spanish Society. of Anesthesiology and Resuscitation.

After 30 minutes, the skin of the puncture site was infiltrated with 2-5 ml of 1% lidocaine to subsequently begin the treatment. On all occasions (4 lumbar punctures and 1 bone aspiration puncture), these could be performed without complications, being well tolerated by the patient. The pain assessment according to the Visual Analog Scale was 2.8 mm (0-10) and according to the verbal categorical scale was 1 (0-4). The overall assessment of the effectiveness of the analgesic treatment was 'good' in all situations, except the day the lumbar and spinal cord puncture was performed, which was classified as 'fair'. The parameters monitored during the procedure were within normal limits. There was no nausea, vomiting or pruritus, and the patient was discharged from the ward 60 minutes after the procedure.
