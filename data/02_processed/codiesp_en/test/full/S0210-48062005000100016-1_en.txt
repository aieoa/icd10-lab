A 78-year-old male patient, diabetic on treatment with oral antidiabetics, with a history of kidney stones and recurrent nephritic colic, and with a malignant neoplasm of the neck treated with RT two months previously, who came to the emergency room due to irritative voiding syndrome with frequency. , urinary urgency and dysuria of two months' duration. Two days before, he was in an emergency department where he was diagnosed with UTI, and antibiotic treatment with Amoxicillin-clavulanic acid was instituted without improvement.
On physical examination he had an axillary temperature of 38.2º C. The rest of the examination was completely normal, including the rectal examination which revealed a size III/IV prostate, not indurated and slightly painful on palpation. In the blood test carried out, attention was drawn to a significant leukocytosis of 41,800 with neutrophilia (93%), a blood glucose of 337 mg/dl, a platelet count of 394,000, a fibrinogen of 771 and a decrease in the Quick Index ( 68%). Pyuria and microhematuria were evident in the urine sediment and no pathology appeared in the simple x-rays of the chest and abdomen performed. An ultrasound of the abdomen was also performed at the Emergency Department, which did not reveal any pathology.
With the suspicion of UTI, he was admitted to the Urology Service and intravenous antibiotic treatment was instituted after obtaining a urine culture. The regimen chosen in this case was the combination of ceftriaxone 1 g plus tobramycin 100 mg every 12 hours. On the third day after admission and given the patient's clinical and analytical improvement, it was decided to switch from intravenous to oral antibiotic therapy, continuing with cefuroxime-axetil. On the seventh day, and coinciding with the arrival of the blood and urine cultures taken upon admission and which were reported as negative, the patient presented a feverish peak of 39º C. and a new increase in the number of leukocytes. We decided to reinstate intravenous antibiotic therapy and perform a vesico-prostatic ultrasound in which the patient's large prostate (400 cc.) caught our attention.

Given the suspicion that it could be acute prostatitis in the context of BPH. It was decided to continue with intravenous antibiotic therapy and schedule a Millin-type adenomectomy when the infectious symptoms subsided. Despite antibiotic therapy, the evolution continued to be unfavorable, with fever peaks persisting, so the treatment regimen was changed by introducing levofloxacin i.v. instead of ceftriaxone and a fractional urine culture was requested. Despite the change in antibiotic treatment, the patient continued to present fever and worsening general condition, so it was decided to perform a transrectal ultrasound to rule out the presence of any complications, in which a poorly defined area appeared in the medial and cranial periphery of the right lobe. , with heterogeneous echogenicity, although predominantly hypoechoic, and with absence of flow in the Doppler method suggestive of prostatic abscess.

Given the ultrasound findings, it was decided to perform an ultrasound-guided transrectal puncture of the abscess, obtaining about 20 ml. of purulent material that was sent to microbiology. The culture of the prostatic exudate and pus were positive for E. coli. Despite performing the puncture, the patient continued to have septic symptoms and reported no improvement, so it was decided to perform a CT scan to confirm the diagnosis and rule out complications of the abscess. The CT showed an increase in the size of the prostate gland with a multiseptate fluid collection, located caudal to the gland itself.

Given the CT findings, and having failed the transrectal puncture, it was decided to perform a transvesical prostatectomy. In the intervention, after opening the capsule, we were able to see the total destructuring of the gland, with the presence of multiloculations and purulent material inside as well as extensive areas of tissue necrosis that compromised the entire prostatic urethra at the apical level.
The pathological anatomy showed two small foci of microacinar adenocarcinoma, very far from the limits of resection, with a Gleason 2 + 3 and chronic and acute abscessed prostatitis with large areas of necrosis along with nodular hyperplasia.

The postoperative evolution was satisfactory, with rapid clinical and analytical improvement, and the patient was discharged from the hospital 21 days after the intervention. Fifteen days after discharge, the patient was seen in our outpatient clinic presenting with stress urinary incontinence. Six months after the intervention, the patient continues to have mild-moderate incontinence and requires absorbents for daily hygiene.
