Female patient, 19 years old, who consulted for mild recurrent pain in the left hemimandible, associated with paresthesia in the aforementioned area, especially at the level of the lower lip. She also describes mild pain when chewing on molars and premolars on the affected side.
In the physical examination, a slight facial asymmetry was observed due to an increase in volume located in the most posterior area of ​​the mandibular body and right ascending ramus. The increase in volume is painless. Intraorally, a bulging external bone table is observed at the level of the second molar area. A similar situation, but with expansion of both tables, is investigated by palpation of the mandibular ascending ramus. Clinically absent third molar.
With the diagnostic hypothesis of mandibular odontogenic cyst or odontogenic tumor, an imaging study is requested prior to biopsy. The panoramic radiograph reveals an extensive radiolucent lesion with clear limits, uniloculated and involving the entire ascending ramus of the mandible. Associated with the injury, the included third molar is observed in a low position and in distoversion.

An incisional biopsy is scheduled during which, and according to the clinical characteristics of the lesion, a decompression cannula is installed. The histopathological report reveals a unicystic ameloblastoma of intramural growth, type IIIa of Ackermann9.

After 10 months of clinical-radiographic follow-up, with the presence of a decompression cannula whose length is gradually reduced, the almost total reduction of the ameloblastic cystic lesion was confirmed, so enucleation and curettage surgery of the residual cavity was performed. in addition to extraction of the third molar involved 15.
