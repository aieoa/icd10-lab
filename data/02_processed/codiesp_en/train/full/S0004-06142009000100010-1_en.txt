A 9-year-old girl was admitted to the pediatric service from the emergency room due to the second episode of macroscopic hematuria in 20 days.
She has no family history of interest, a personal history of facial hemihypertrophy due to congenital torticollis, chickenpox, infectious mononucleosis, nocturnal enuresis up to 7 years of age, pneumonia at 9 months, and trauma to the coccyx.
Physical examination: slight skin pallor, weight within the 25-50th percentile and height within the 50-75th percentile. No more findings.
Complementary tests: blood count, biochemistry, coagulation, urine culture, kidney function, enolase, alpha feto protein, chest x-ray and normal ECG.
Urinary sediment: macroscopic hematuria.
Abdominal ultrasound: mass 5cm in diameter in the left kidney, complex with cystic areas/necrosis.
The pediatric service made a consultation with our service and decided to perform, after an ultrasound finding, an abdominal CT with contrast with the finding of a mass dependent on the left kidney, on the anterior side, with cystic content with irregular endoluminal thickenings. It presents a thick and irregular septum with a density similar to that obtained in the internal irregularities described. No inter-cavoaortic or periaortic lymph nodes were observed.
The mass is compatible with Wilms tumor or adenocarcinoma.
It was decided to perform a left nephrectomy: the mass measures 4.5 cm. diameter.

The pathological diagnosis is cystic mesoblastic nephroma (multilocular renal cyst).
