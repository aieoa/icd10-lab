A 25-year-old Colombian patient was admitted to our Obstetrics Section due to rupture of membranes during 24 weeks and 5 days of pregnancy.
She did not report any family or personal history of interest. Gynecological history: menarche at age 14; FM: 3-4/32. In a routine pregnancy examination performed at 20 weeks of gestation, she was diagnosed with vaginal condylomatosis. Examination upon admission: apyretic patient. Upon inspection of the external genitalia, multiple vulvoperineal condylomas were observed. TV: cervix formed, posterior and closed. Abundant clear waters flowed. Ultrasound: fetus in longitudinal cephalic view. Posterior placenta, ILA 8 (amniotic fluid index). F.C.F. positive. Estimated fetal weight: 641 g.

Treatment with intravenous antibiotics was started and a series of complementary tests were performed (complete analysis with PCR, serology; vaginal and rectal cultures and amniotic fluid; and hybridization test for HPV), the results of which are reflected in Table 1, and which allowed us to reach the diagnosis of chorioamnionitis.

Delivery was triggered a week later and due to the presence of chorioamnionitis and fetal immaturity, it was allowed to progress. The delivery was spontaneous, with a live male fetus weighing 690 g, artery pH 7.24 and vein pH 7.48. Apgar test at 1-and 5-out of 5. Placenta of 167 g, hypermature with signs of chorioamnionitis. After delivery, biopsies of the vagina and perineum were performed, the results of which were: condyloma acuminatum of the vagina and condyloma acuminatum of the perineum that respected the edges.
The newborn died at 40 days of life.
