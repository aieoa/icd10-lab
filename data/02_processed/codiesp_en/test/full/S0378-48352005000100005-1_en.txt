An 80-year-old woman who reported the presence of a tumor in her left breast for sixteen years that she had not wanted to consult about for fear of the diagnosis and possible treatments. Although she reported that at first the growth had been slow and indolent, in the last two years she had progressed to the point of self-amputation of the breast. She had finally attended the consultation encouraged by her family after presenting back pain and progressive dyspnea, which had become more evident in the previous weeks. At that time, she had ipsilateral axillary and supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. Her chest x-ray revealed signs of pleural effusion and probable carcinomatous lymphangitis. The bone scan showed multiple uptakes. The clinical diagnosis was confirmed by puncture of a nodule that protruded above the breast bed; Cytology was compatible with infiltrating ductal carcinoma. The patient began treatment with tamoxifen. Two days later she suddenly experienced a significant worsening of her back pain, so it was necessary to increase analgesia. In the following two weeks, the patient herself gradually withdrew the analgesics until she stopped them. After a transient elevation, both CA153 and alkaline phosphatase decreased until they reached normal levels. After six months, a complete response of the lymphadenopathy had been obtained, although a small remnant persisted in the breast bed; The scintigraphy showed a certain attenuation of the hyperuptake areas and a clear improvement in lung involvement was seen. The patient maintained the response for 22 months. After progression she received several lines of hormonal treatment. She died 56 months after diagnosis.
