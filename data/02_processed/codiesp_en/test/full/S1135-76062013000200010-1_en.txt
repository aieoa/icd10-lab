A 38-year-old woman, with a 40-week primigravida, with a history of controlled gestational diabetes, was admitted to the hospital due to high blood pressure, with figures of 150/95 mmHg. Treatment is performed with intravenous infusion of labetalol. After the failure of labor induction with prostaglandins, she underwent a cesarean section, during which intense bleeding occurred due to atony, so it was decided to perform a subtotal hysterectomy. In a situation of intense hemodynamic instability and with signs of coagulopathy, he was admitted to the intensive care unit, where an abdomino-pelvic computed tomography (CT) was performed, which reported a hepatic subcapsular hematoma measuring 6.7 × 15 × 19 cm with suggestive areas. of recent bleeding, which compresses the right hepatic lobe. Due to the indication for surgery, it is treated with conservative measures, thus achieving a stable hemodynamic situation for the patient, with a progressive decrease in the hepatic subcapsular hematoma according to CT control. An analysis is performed that reveals: decrease in hematocrit, 18%; total bilirubin, 5.1 mg/dl; GOT, 815 U/l; and thrombocytopenia (55,000 platelets/ml), for which a platelet transfusion is performed. Given the favorable clinical and ultrasound evolution, it was decided to transfer the patient to the ward, and the day before being discharged, 11 days after delivery, while lying down and talking to her husband, she began to feel unwell, with a sensation of dyspneic and loss of consciousness. After performing cardiopulmonary resuscitation maneuvers, the patient died and it was decided not to certify her death due to sudden death in a stable patient.
Autopsy findings
On external examination, the corpse was very pale with mild lividity. The internal review revealed:
- Hemoperitoneum of liquid blood of 950 cm3.
- Liver increased in size and weight, 2,390 grams, which presented a tear of Glisson's capsule in the upper portion of the anterior surface of segments VII and VIII of the right lobe.

- Large subcapsular hematoma extended to almost the entire upper and posterior surface of the right hepatic lobe, with approximate dimensions of 22 cm in longitudinal diameter, 15 cm in transverse diameter and about 3 cm in maximum thickness.
- Generalized visceral pallor.
The entire liver was sent for histopathological study, revealing a very altered liver parenchyma with destruction of the normal architecture, periportal and intraparenchymal necrosis, and intrasinusoidal fibrin deposition. All findings are compatible with spontaneous liver rupture probably associated with HELLP syndrome.

Therefore, the cause of death was established as hemorrhagic shock secondary to spontaneous liver rupture, caused by a subcapsular hematoma in the context of HELLP syndrome.
