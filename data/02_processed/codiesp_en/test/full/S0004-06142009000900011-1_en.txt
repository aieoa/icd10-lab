A 53-year-old male patient came to the urology clinic for a left testicular mass. During the physical examination, palpation of a lesion in the upper pole of the left testicle was notable, with a firm, non-painful consistency.
A testicular ultrasound was performed which described an area of ​​2 x 2 cm, of heterogeneous echogenicity, with multiple cystic areas, suggestive of possible neoplasia. Tumor markers are negative. Given the high suspicion of a testicular tumor, we decided to perform a left radical orchiectomy.
In the histological study of the surgical specimen, macroscopically a well-defined tumor measuring 1.8 cm was described. in diameter, yellowish in color and stony in consistency. Microscopically, it is made up of horny scales and nests of squamous epithelium in which the "shadow" epithelial cells are recognized. Most of this material is calcified and ossified, and a fibrous reaction is observed around the lesion that determines the formation of a pseudocapsule that delimits the tumor. In this fibrous pseudocapsule, bundles of smooth muscle are focally identified. The testicular parenchyma shows moderate interstitial edema. The epididymis and spermatic cord do not present relevant histological alterations. The anatomical-pathological diagnosis reports testicular dermoid cyst (testicular mature teratoma), pseudopilomatrixoma variant.

After orchiectomy, an extension study was performed using chest x-ray and abdominopelvic CT, with no evidence of systemic extension of the disease. Testicular tumor markers (alpha-fetoprotein, beta-HCG and LDH) are negative.
The patient is currently asymptomatic and disease-free after 6 months of follow-up.
