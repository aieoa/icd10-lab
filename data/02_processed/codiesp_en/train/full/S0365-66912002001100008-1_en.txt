We present the case of a 27-year-old woman who was visited in the emergency department complaining of metamorphoppsies in her RE. His VA was 0.6 in the RE and 1.0 in the LE and the funduscopic examination showed several rounded whitish foci scattered in the posterior pole of both eyes, one of them juxtafoveal in the RE and of a more grayish color with a perilesional neurosensory detachment, microhemorrhages and hard exudation that raised suspicion of the existence of a subretinal membrane. There were no inflammatory signs in either the anterior chamber or the vitreous.

In the FGA (fluorescein angiography), a very early blockage of fluorescence was seen in all the foci, which quickly turned into frank hyperfluorescence, more notable in late phases in the focus suspected of having an associated membrane (NVSR). In some foci, a small area of ​​central blockage persisted. In the AVI (indocyanine green angiography), a blockage of fluorescence was observed from early phases and throughout the angiogram, except in the case of the suspected focus of RSNV, where a hot spot appeared early within a hypofluorescent plaque. which in late phases caused plaque hyperfluorescence that confirmed the diagnosis of NVSR.

In both the AGF and the AVI we found more foci of activity than those observed in the fundus.
A complete blood study was performed that included biochemistry and banking, an HLA study was requested (B7 and DR2: for PHOS and AMPPE, A29 for Birdshot), which was negative; Likewise, a chest radiological study was carried out to rule out tuberculosis and serology to rule out syphilis and borreliosis, which was normal in all cases.
Treatment with corticosteroids is instituted at a dose of 1.5 mg/kg body weight/day. VA improved to 0.8 and remained stable until the last review 3 months later. The foci progressively evolved towards pigmentation.
