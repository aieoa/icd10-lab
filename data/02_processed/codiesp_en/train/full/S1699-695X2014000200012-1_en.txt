This is a 68-year-old man, a pack-a-day smoker for the last 40 years, with no known drug allergies. He is not hypertensive, nor diabetic, nor does he have dyslipidemia. He underwent cataract surgery.
Our patient began to be studied in internal medicine, a year earlier, due to sciatica and edema in the lower limbs that had been going on for a month. During the clinical examination, two hard, painless masses were accidentally palpated in both parotid glands, which is why an ultrasound of these glands and a FNA of the lesions detected in them were requested. His family doctor assumed the performance of both techniques (ultrasound and FNAC).
• Ultrasound report: "At the level of the superficial lobe of the right parotid gland, two hypoechoic nodular lesions with a solid appearance are identified, one more lateral of 32 mm and with well-defined edges and another more medial and deep of about 38 mm. At the level of the superficial lobe of the left parotid, a hypoechoic and heterogeneous nodular lesion (solid/microcystic) is identified, with mamelonated contours and with microcalcifications inside, vascularized and measuring about 32 mm.

• PAAFs report:
- FNAC of the left parotid area: negative cytology for malignant cells, compatible with Warthin's tumor.
- FNAC of the right parotid area: negative cytology for malignant cells, compatible with Warthin's tumor. (Bilateral).
• Nuclear Magnetic Resonance Report: "Multiple heterogeneous nodular lesions are observed in both parotid glands. Some of these lesions present multicystic areas, with hyperintense content, in relation to proteinaceous/hematic content and all of them present intense enhancement. Compatible with multicentric and bilateral Warthin tumor".

The patient is awaiting surgery by the maxillofacial surgery service for a multifocal and bilateral Warthin tumor.
