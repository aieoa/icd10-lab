A 51-year-old male attended the Dermatology consultation because he had had nodular, erythematous-violaceous and painful lesions on his legs and the back of his feet for 6-7 days, accompanied by swelling of the ankles.

The patient was in good general condition, with no other accompanying symptoms. His personal history included three episodes of acute pancreatitis secondary to chronic alcoholism, the last one a year ago. In the magnetic resonance cholangio-resonance performed at that time, chronic pancreatitis had been detected, as well as two pancreatic pseudocysts, for which he was being monitored by the Digestive Service. A skin biopsy and analytical study were performed and treatment was started with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and omeprazole. Histopathology of one of the skin lesions showed an intense inflammatory infiltrate in the fatty lobule consisting of polymorphonuclear neutrophils, soaps secondary to fat necrosis, and areas of hemorrhage.

The analytical study showed pancreatic amylase values ​​of 469 IU/L. Given these findings, compatible with the diagnosis of pancreatic panniculitis, the patient was admitted for study and monitoring for a possible exacerbation of his chronic pancreatitis. The evolution of the skin lesions was good, despite the fact that the patient had suspended anti-inflammatory treatment due to epigastralgia. However, the epigastralgia did not subside and even became more continuous and intense, later accompanied by nausea and vomiting. The symptoms and amylase levels were controlled during admission using conservative measures, but in the control computed tomography (CT) an increase in the size of the head of the pancreas was observed, with dilation of the Wirsung duct, and partial destruction of the pancreatic gland. , peripancreatic, celiac and gastrohepatic ligament lymphadenopathy, along with portal vein thrombosis, which required anticoagulant treatment. Five months after the diagnosis of pancreatic panniculitis, the follow-up CT scan reported the resolution of the pancreatic pathology, with portal cavernomatous transformation and persistence of small lymphadenopathy without clear pathological significance, so the condition was finally interpreted as a exacerbation. of his chronic pancreatitis.
