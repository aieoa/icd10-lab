A 78-year-old woman was referred from the general surgery service to the pain clinic due to chronic abdominal pain secondary to intestinal angina. No history of chronic degenerative diseases. She underwent 2 laparotomies in 6 months and 1 diagnostic laparoscopy for episodes of mesenteric ischemia.
- Diagnosis: mesenteric ischemia corroborated by computed tomography with venous and arterial contrast.
- Current illness: patient with chronic, generalized, severe abdominal pain, lasting 6 months, that does not resolve with regular analgesics. Crampy pain, VAS 10/10, EVERA severe, disabling, episodic, sometimes accompanied by nausea and vomiting, without factors that increase or decrease it.
- Physical examination: weight 45 kg; size 1.50 m; blood pressure 90/50 mmHg; heart rate 65; respiratory rate 18. Conscious, oriented, hydrated, with pale mucous membranes and integuments, cachectic. Face, head and neck without alterations, cardiopulmonary without compromise, abdomen with generalized pain in all quadrants, severe, on superficial and deep palpation, peristalsis present and with normal characteristics, without palpable masses. Rest of normal physical examination.
- Algological diagnosis: chronic abdominal pain of severe visceral type.
- Etiological diagnosis: intestinal angina (mesenteric ischemia).

Clinical evolution
Therapeutics with powerful opiates (hydromorphone, methadone, oxycodone, morphine and transdermal fentanyl) and adjuvants, such as calcium antagonists, nitrates, etc., carrying out different rotations and management schemes according to their clinical evolution. Not a candidate for intervention due to clinical status and anticoagulation with acenocoumarine, and the use of antiplatelet agents.
