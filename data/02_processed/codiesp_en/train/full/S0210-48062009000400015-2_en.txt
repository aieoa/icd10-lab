A 75-year-old man was found injured on a public road, with head trauma, fractures in the right upper and lower extremities and ribs, with intense subcutaneous emphysema, and mild hematuria. He is admitted to the Intensive Care Unit for ventilatory support and pelvic imaging studies reveal a closed fracture of the pelvic bones with bone fragments imprinting on the bladder. A reconstruction of the CT images seemed to rule out penetrating bladder injury. However, once the vital signs were stabilized, cystoscopy was performed, which demonstrated perforation of the anterior bladder wall by the bone chip. Through a suprapubic access, the bladder was repaired and the bone fragment was removed, without sequelae and with subsequent good healing of the fractures.
