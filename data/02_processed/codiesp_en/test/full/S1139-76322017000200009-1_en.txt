We present the clinical case of a five-year-old boy affected by atopic dermatitis from the first months of life, with no other personal or family history of interest. As an infectious history, it should be noted that he had suffered from chickenpox a year earlier without complications and an injury compatible with cold sores in his father a few days before.
They consulted the Emergency Department due to vesicular, erythematous skin lesions that had been present for two days. Some of the lesions showed signs of impetiginization without signs of cellulitis. The skin lesions were distributed on both lower and upper limbs and trunk. The rest of the examination was unremarkable. Fever of 38 °C maximum from twelve hours before. He had no other symptoms. There was no family environment of infection at the time of consultation.

Given the suspicion of Kaposi's varicelliform rash, it was decided to admit him to start intravenous treatment with acyclovir at 20 mg/kg/day. Treatment was associated with oral amoxicillin-clavulanate at 50 mg/kg/day and daily treatments with chlorhexidine.
An analytical study was performed upon admission with a complete blood count and biochemistry, without elevation of acute phase reactants, and serology was obtained for herpes simplex viruses 1 and 2, Coxsackie, cytomegalovirus, toxoplasma, Epstein Barr virus, parvovirus and human herpes virus type 6. .
The clinical evolution was favorable, with fever disappearing in the first 24 hours of admission and without the appearance of systemic or skin complications.
From the third day onwards, the lesions were all in the scabbing phase with progressive detachment of the crusted lesions without incident.
Discharge was decided on the fourth day of admission, completing oral treatment with acyclovir for seven more days.
A week after admission he showed no traces of skin lesions except for his already known atopic dermatitis. The serology result was negative for everything studied except IgM positive for herpes simplex virus type 1.
