A 34-year-old man, diagnosed with Klinefelter syndrome, came to the emergency room due to loss of visual acuity in the left eye (LE) for 2 days. Visual acuity in the right eye (RE) was 1 and in LE 0.2. The anterior pole showed no alterations and the angle was open grade IV 360o with little pigmentation. The intraocular pressure (IOP) in the RE was 34mmHg and that in the LE was 30mmHg. The central corneal thickness in RE was 516 microns and in LE was 520 microns. Funduscopic examination revealed a marked increase in papillary cupping in both eyes with a cup/vertical disc ratio of 0.8. In the LE, an oval depression was observed corresponding to a congenital pit of the optic nerve in the temporal sector with an area of ​​peripapillary atrophy associated with a macular serous detachment. The study of the retinal nerve fiber layer using optical coherence tomography (OCT) (Stratus OCT Carl Zeiss Ophthalmic Systems, Inc) showed significant thinning in both eyes, more marked in the RE although it must be taken into account that the Actual thickness of the fiber layer of the left optic nerve may be increased by macular serous detachment. Computerized perimetry (Humphrey 24-2) showed a superior arcuate defect in the RE and a central defect in the LE due to the maculopathy. Fluorescein angiography characteristically showed initial hypofluorescence of the pit. OCT on the macula revealed the separation of the inner layers of the retina in communication with the pit and a macular neurosensory detachment not connected to it. We also see a lamellar macular hole in the outer layers of the retina.
