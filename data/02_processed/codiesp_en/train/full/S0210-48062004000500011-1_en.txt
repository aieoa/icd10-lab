In July 2000, from the Nephrology Service where he was being studied for chronic renal failure secondary to nephroangiosclerosis, a 77-year-old man was referred to us due to the ultrasound finding of a 6 cm renal mass affecting the left kidney.
As notable personal history, he had COPD, HTN and duodenal ulcer with pyloric stenosis.
To complete the studies, we performed an abdomino-pelvic CT, which was reported as 'infiltrating lesion affecting the upper 2/3 of the left kidney with involvement of the pericapsular space and poorly defined borders'. Analytically, the presence of a monoclonal gammopathy with IgM of 4640 g/l (38-231) kappa by immunofixation was very notable. Other analyzes: positive benzene proteinuria, bone marrow aspirate with 12.5% ​​atypia and bone series without lytic lesions.

With the first diagnosis of a renal mass associated with IgM monoclonal gammopathy (without meeting IgM myeloma criteria), a left radical nephrectomy was performed in August 2000.
The pathological result was primary renal non-hodking B lymphoma type MALT (mucosal associated lymphoid tumor).
The patient presents a good post-operative evolution and is discharged with outpatient check-ups. In December 2000, a first control was carried out, showing an IgM of 3430 g/l in a first analysis and 4340 g/l in a second, so it was decided by the Hematology Service to begin treatment with Chlorambucil. A control CT scan was performed and was reported as 'thickening of the perinephric fascia with fraying of fat compatible with inflammatory lesions or similar'. Since there was no complete evidence of recurrence, it was decided to strictly follow up the patient with analytical and radiological controls and maintaining treatment with Chlorambucil. In January 2002, a new CT scan was performed in which no signs of recurrence were found.
Currently the patient remains asymptomatic, with stable IgM levels (last control 48 g/l) treated with Chlorambucil.
