A 25-year-old man underwent surgery for a left varicocele six months ago, who consulted for a problem of repeated urinary infections without sexual pathology. A retrograde urethrography and a voiding cystourethrography were performed, which indicated the presence of an incomplete urethral duplication that originated in the bulbar urethra and ran along the ventral side.
The accessory tract was surgically resected via the perineal route. Subsequent imaging controls showed the existence of a normal bulbar urethra. Urination symptoms have not recurred in the two years since the intervention.
