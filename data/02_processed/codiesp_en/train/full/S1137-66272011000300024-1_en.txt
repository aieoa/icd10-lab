This is a 25-year-old patient, originally from Ukraine, diagnosed with diabetes secondary to pancreatic agenesis for 5 years, admitted to our service from the Emergency Department due to non-acidotic ketotic hyperglycemic decompensation. Since diagnosis, the patient has had acceptable metabolic control with HbA1c less than 7% with dietary treatment and exercise.
Among her family history, she has a diabetic great-grandfather and healthy parents and siblings.
In the anamnesis, upon admission the patient reported poor metabolic control for two months, coinciding with the onset of frequent episodes of urinary infections, and recently, aggravated by vulvovaginal candidiasis and paronychia in the fingers, with symptoms of polydipsia, intense polyuria. and loss of 6.4 kg of weight during this period.
In the physical examination she stands out: height 167 cm; weight 38.6 kg; BMI 13.4 kg/m2; BP 120/80 mmHg; HR 116 bpm; saturation 97%. Lantern jaw. Thyroid grade 0. Normal AC and AP. Abdomen soft and depressible, not painful, without masses or megalias. MMII without edema and preserved peripheral pulses. Vibratory sensitivity, with preserved monofilament and deep tendon reflexes. Erythematous and pruritic lesions in the vulvar and perianal region suggestive of candidiasis. Paronychia in the fourth finger of the right hand. Extensive diabetic dermopathy on legs and arms.
Complementary tests: Normal blood count, ESR 35 mm/h, glucose 552 mg/dL, Cr 0.8 mg/mL, total cholesterol 205 mg/dL, LDL-C 125 mg/dL, HDL-C 58 mg/dL. Normal liver profile except GOT 54 IU (7-32), GPT 63 IU (5-31) and GGT 59 (7-32). Albumin/creatinine ratio: 0.6µg/mgr. Urine glucose 5567 mg/dL. Methyl ketone in urine 50 mg/dL. HbA1c 16.2%. Basal C peptide 166 pmol/L (298-2350). Anti-GAD 0.2 KU/L (0-0.9), anti-IA2 0.2 KU/L (0-1) and anti-insulin (IAA) 77.2 nU/mL (N<40) antibodies . IgA antitransglutaminase antibodies 0.82 KU/L (0-10). Anti-TG 149 (0-40) and anti-TPO 13.3 (0-35) antibodies. TSH 3.1
Gynecological examination: bicornuate uterus.
Fundus examination and normal retinography.
Abdominopelvic CT with contrast: dorsal agenesis of the pancreas.
Abdominal ECHO: in the left kidney, multiple cortical cysts smaller than 1 cm, diffuse throughout the left renal parenchyma without evidence of dilation of the pyelocalyceal system or other findings. Normal right kidney. Absence of pancreatic body and tail.
Exudate culture from the whitlow grew Klebsiella oxytoca and the vulvovaginal culture was positive for Candida albicans.
With the suspicion of MODY type diabetes, a genetic study was requested using semiquantitative fluorescent PCR, detecting a heterozygous deletion of at least exons 1 to 8 of the HNF1 β gene, which is associated with type 5.
Treatment was started with insulin glargine and insulin aspart 1.3 U/kg. Oral fluconazole 100 mg/day (10 days) and a single-dose vaginal suppository of clotrimazole 400 mg were administered, and the fungal lesions disappeared.
In subsequent controls, HbA1c levels of less than 6% were detected with a progressive decrease in insulin to 0.5 U/kg 6 months after admission. Eight months later, the diabetic dermopathy disappeared and 18 months after admission, the patient has regained her initial weight and insulin requirements remain at 0.5 U/kg. Kidney function has remained within normal limits and negative microalbuminuria. It only maintains a slight increase in transaminases.
