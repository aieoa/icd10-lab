A 59-year-old woman with no relevant history was admitted for pain in the right epigastrium-hypochondrium radiating to the scapula along with jaundice and nutritional intolerance for 5 days. On examination she had a heart rate of 80 bpm, afebrile (36.3º), with jaundice of the skin and mucous membranes and on palpation her abdomen was soft, depressible and not painful. The emergency analysis showed a total bilirubin of 11.63, mainly due to direct bilirubin (7.12), amylase of 135, GPT 221, LDH 557, leukocytosis of 14,400 and eosinophilia of 5,480 (normal value: 0-0. 8). An abdominal ultrasound was performed, which revealed a well-defined cystic lesion measuring 6.9 x 6 cm in the right hepatic lobe, compatible with a hydatid cyst, along with multiple gallstones. Gallbladder, body of pancreas, kidneys, spleen and bladder normal. No free peritoneal fluid. Given the findings, the patient began treatment with albendazole. After one day of admission, the patient presented an episode of anaphylactic shock with dyspnea and desaturation (pO2 50 mmHg, SatO2 86.9), fever (38º), bilateral wheezing, erythematous skin lesions and eosinophilia. The emergency abdominal CT did not show new data compared to the ultrasound and after administering 80 mg i.v. of urbason and 1 ampoule i.v. of Polaramine®, she underwent emergency surgery for anaphylaxis with the following findings: partially calcified hydatid cyst in segments V-VII and VIII, closely adhered to the diaphragm. Intraoperative ultrasound showed a cyst in contact with the inferior vena cava and right suprahepatic vein. Puncture, aspiration and sterilization with hypertonic saline were performed in addition to cholecystectomy and choledochotomy. After clinical and analytical improvement after 15 days, the patient was discharged on treatment with albendazole.
