A 36-year-old man presented with chronic diarrhea and weight loss.
An analytical study was performed, which consisted of general biochemistry, blood count, TSH, immunological study, celiac profile and fecal parasites, which were normal. C-reactive protein was elevated. A colonoscopy was performed up to the cecum, which was normal, and no alterations were identified in the barium intestinal transit.
The abdominal ultrasound revealed a left renal mass in the upper pole of more than 4 cm in diameter, rounded, isoechoic with hypoechoic areas inside that could be compatible with necrotic areas.

The CT scan confirmed the existence of this 4.3 cm, hypodense lesion, limited to the kidney, without affecting the perinephric fat or the vascular pedicle. The patient was referred to the Urology service and underwent a left laparoscopic nephrectomy, macroscopically visualizing a lobulation in one of his poles, with an intact Gerota capsule and without other significant macroscopic alterations. In the sections, a rounded, well-defined lesion measuring 4.8 x 4.5 cm was identified at said pole, with yellow, brownish parenchyma, with small millimetric cavities and friable when cut, remaining very close to the kidney capsule. He was diagnosed with Delahunt and Eble type 2 papillary renal cell carcinoma. Currently the patient is asymptomatic, regressing after the intervention, from the diarrheal condition for which he consulted.
