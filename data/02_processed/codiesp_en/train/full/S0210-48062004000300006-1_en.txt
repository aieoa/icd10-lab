A 69-year-old man comes to us for a review of prostate syndrome being treated with an alpha-blocker. His medical history includes having undergone right hip surgery, hearing loss and repeated angina. Episode of left costolumbar trauma about 30 years ago. The urological history was hematuria on two occasions, the last time a year ago.
On examination, the prostate has fibroadenomatous characteristics, volume II/IV, without nodulations. Abdominal palpation reveals a large mass located in the left hypochondrium and void, painful on palpation.
Analytical: Hb 14.6 gr/dl, Hct. 43.1%, ESR 9 mm/h and biochemistry within normality. PSA: 3.1 ng/ml.
Simple X-ray: great mass effect located in the left hypochondrium and void, completely calcified ovoid in shape.

Given these findings, a differential diagnosis is proposed with any mass located in the retroperitoneum that presents this type of calcification:
- Primitive retroperitoneal tumors
- Adrenal tumors
- Kidney tumors
- Metastatic tumors
- Infectious pathology
Blood determinations of plasma renin activity are performed before and after ambulation, as well as aldosterone and cortisol at 8 and 20 hours, with the results being within normal limits. Urinary levels of 17-ketosteroids and corticosteroids, as well as catecholamines, metanephrines, and vanilmandelate were normal. All of the above ruled out a functioning adrenal tumor.
Staining for acid-fast bacilli in urine and sputum was also performed and was negative, as well as the Mantoux test.
The possibility of a calcified hydatid cyst was raised, but serology was negative. Likewise, the left costolumbar trauma, previously suffered, was considered as a possible cause of the calcified adrenal mass.
With the diagnosis of an unknown retroperitoneal mass, an exploratory laparotomy was performed, finding a prominent lesion in the left upper quadrant, hard on palpation, with fixation to deep planes and diffuse lesions distributed throughout the peritoneum, the intraoperative pathological report of which showed infiltrated peritoneal fat. due to a malignant tumor of probable mesenchymal origin. It was decided to close the abdominal cavity as it was a disseminated malignant process. The definitive pathological report is of pleomorphic liposarcoma, with immunohistochemical techniques being: S100: positive; Oil-Red: positive; vimentin: positive; PS 3: positive; desmin: negative.

The patient was discharged on the seventh day after the intervention, and later died two weeks later at his home.
