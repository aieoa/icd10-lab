This is a 20-year-old man diagnosed in September 2003 with pre-B acute lymphoblastic leukemia, who received intravenous induction chemotherapy treatment and intrathecal prophylactic chemotherapy using LP. After applying various treatment regimens for 8 months, complete remission of the disease was not achieved. In this period of time, 4 lumbar punctures were performed to administer intrathecal chemoprophylaxis, without complications. Repeat cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analyzes were normal on all occasions. The platelet counts prior to these punctures were 34,000, 118,000, 338,000 and 161,000 platelets/mm3 respectively. Prothrombin (PT) and activated partial thromboplastin (APTT) times were normal, and platelet transfusion was not performed in any case prior to LP. In May 2004, the patient was admitted to the hospital again for a new cycle of chemotherapy and to subsequently schedule a bone marrow transplant. A new LP was performed without incident, but on this occasion several platelet concentrates had to be previously transfused because the patient had thrombocytopenia of 26,000/mm3. In the previous 2 months he had also required several transfusions due to the progressive tendency towards thrombocytopenia (below 20,000/mm3). PT and APTT were normal. A few hours after this last LP, the patient required attention for isolated right sciatica. After about 48 hours, radicular pain was associated with loss of strength in both MMII, which quickly evolved to bilateral paraparesis, preserving only partial motor strength (3/5) in flexion-extension of the feet. No alterations in sensitivity or sphincters were observed. An emergency thoracolumbar MRI was performed, which showed an image suggestive of extraparenchymal intradural ventral hematoma that compressed the conus medullaris and cauda equina from D12 to L4. After intravenous gadolinium was administered, no contrast enhancement suggestive of underlying injury was observed. Subsequently, a decompressive laminectomy was performed from L1 to L4 and a longitudinal durotomy, after which the cauda equina was observed under great tension, contained by the intact arachnoid membrane. A small amount of subarachnoid blood clot dorsally compressed the conus medullaris. Examination of the lateral areas demonstrated the presence of a large ventral hematoma also contained by the arachnoid membrane. Next, a longitudinal incision of the arachnoid was made and after separating the dorsal roots of the cauda equina, the clot was accessed. It was only possible to achieve a subtotal extraction of it, since it was partially organized and adhered to the nerve roots. No macroscopic lesions were observed that could be the origin of the hematoma. The surgical act was completed with closure of the dural plane using a freeze-dried plasty to expand space. The postoperative course was uneventful. The patient achieved a progressive recovery of strength, being able to walk on the fourth day, without sphincter alterations or radicular pain and reporting only a slight residual swelling in the lower limbs. In the following weeks, the oncological treatment and bone marrow transplant were continued, but The patient developed a set of systemic complications that finally caused multiple organ failure and his death, one month after the surgical intervention. A post-surgical lumbar MRI could not be performed due to the patient's poor clinical condition.
