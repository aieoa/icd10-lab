A 50-year-old patient with a history of recurrent kidney stones who consulted due to recurrent hematuria and feeling of discomfort. Serial cytological study of urine demonstrated the presence of atypical cells suspicious of malignancy. Imaging studies revealed a heterogeneous lesion that occupied almost the entire right renal pelvis. A total nephrectomy and partial ureterectomy were performed, revealing a kidney measuring 10.5 court. This lesion, relatively well defined, did not invade the parenchyma or the renal sinus and was limited to the pyelocalyceal system although it compressed the cortex in one of the renal poles with expansive margins. The histological study showed that the lesion was made up of well-defined tumor nests without evidence of invasion of the renal parenchyma, encompassing a central mucinous matrix which constituted more than 50% of the tumor volume. Within this matrix, loose neoplastic cells and nests of smaller size than those located at the peripheral level were found floating. The tumor cells showed polygonal morphology with central nuclei without apparent nucleoli and little atypia as well as eosinophilic and clear cytoplasms. In the delimiting areas with the mucinous matrix, a timid glandular differentiation was observed, coinciding with the observation of some nucleoli and a slight increase in nuclear atypia. The tumor cells showed intense immunopositivity for EMA, CK 7 and mild for CK20; This, together with the morphology, confirmed the urothelial nature of the neoplasia.
With all the data presented, the diagnosis of low-grade transitional cell carcinoma, with intense mucinous differentiation, of the renal pelvis (colloid carcinoma) was made.
