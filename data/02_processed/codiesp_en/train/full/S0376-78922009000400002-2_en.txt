A 65-year-old man with mediastinitis caused by Staphylococcus aureus. After aortic reconstruction surgery with a Dacron® prosthesis, he presented exposure of the same 12 days after the intervention, with a high risk of dissemination of the local infection and the possibility of rupture due to desiccation. Since it was decided to open the surgical wound, washings with saline solution were performed every 8 hours and dressings with silver sulfadiazine. The patient required continuous intubation from the day of the intervention, maintaining hemodynamic stability thanks to the use of vasoactive drugs.
It was decided to perform restitution of the chest wall with pectoral muscle flaps. After curettage of the sternum in the midline, a window was created at the upper end of the sternum (left margin), at the level of the 3rd and 4th ribs, to wrap the aortic prosthesis with the left pectoral muscle. The right pectoral muscle was advanced over the sternum once new osteosynthesis with wires and subsequent direct closure of the skin flaps were performed. The patient remained in the ICU for 2 days with antibiotic treatment, and was then discharged to the ward without incident. Two years later he remains asymptomatic.
