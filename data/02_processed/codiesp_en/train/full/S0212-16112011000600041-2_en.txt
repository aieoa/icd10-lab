A 41-year-old male, with a personal history of treated pulmonary tuberculosis, with no other personal history of interest except for being a moderate drinker of alcoholic beverages. Referred to our center due to a gradual increase in abdominal perimeter since the previous month, along with the appearance of edema in the lower extremities. No other accompanying symptoms. Analyzes revealed altered coagulation (prothrombin time 43%), creatinine 0.58 mg/dl, cholesterol 77 mg/dl, albumin 1,610 mg/dl. An abdominal ultrasound was performed where the diagnosis of hepatic steatosis and portal hypertension was confirmed, along with collateral circulation and ascites, all in probable relation to excessive consumption of alcoholic beverages (virological study for hepatitis negative). Gastroscopy revealed grade III esophageal varices, a small hiatus hernia, and gastropathy secondary to portal hypertension.
Given the clinical-analytical findings, protein-losing disease was suspected, so A1AT clearance was performed, being 45 ml/day (> 24 ml/day) [A1AT in blood: 190 mg/dl; A1AT in feces: 0.38 mg/g; stool weight in 24 hours: 225 g], being diagnosed with the same, in relation to his portal hypertension. He was treated with alcohol abstinence, oral hyperprotein nutritional supplements and diuretics, improving clinically and analytically, so he was discharged for follow-up in outpatient clinics at his reference center.
