A 42-year-old woman was studied in Gastroenterology Consultations since January 2005 for presenting scant rectal bleeding at the end of a bowel movement along with tenesmus and anal pain for two years. The patient had no relevant family or personal history, nor did she follow any regular medical treatment. During the physical examination, only an indurated area on the left lateral wall of the rectum was observed on digital rectal examination. A complete analysis was performed, which was strictly normal. Given this finding, it was decided to perform a colonoscopy, finding two millimeter polyps in the rectal ampulla, internal hemorrhoids (one of them prolapsed) and on the left lateral side of the rectal ampulla an erythematous and slightly raised area, approximately 2 x 2 cm, with edges geographical and with an indeterminate appearance from which biopsies were taken. The differential diagnosis between inflammatory bowel disease (ulcerative colitis), sterchorrhea ulcer, and solitary rectal ulcer syndrome was initially proposed. The pathological anatomy showed a thickening of the mucosa, with elongation and distortion of the glands and an edematous lamina propria with a large amount of collagen and fibroblasts, all compatible with the diagnosis of solitary rectal ulcer syndrome. Anorectal manometry was performed, which showed a slightly reduced maximum tolerable rectal volume and a minimally lengthened balloon expulsion time.
