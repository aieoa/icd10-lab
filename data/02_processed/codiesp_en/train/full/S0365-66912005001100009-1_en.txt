A 73-year-old male patient diagnosed with advanced GCCA was referred to our center. He had undergone two trabeculectomies that had failed early. Given the stage of glaucoma and the existence of a nuclear cataract, it was decided to perform a combined procedure of phacoemulsification and valve implantation. Twenty-four hours after surgery, the intraocular pressure was 48 mmHg and the anterior chamber was flattened with peripheral iridoendothelial contact. The iridectomies were observed to be patent, ruling out the possibility of pupillary blockage. Fundus examination and ultrasound were normal, so malignant glaucoma was diagnosed. A capsulotomy and hyaloidotomy were performed with an Nd-YAG laser through the pupil, observing a slight deepening of the anterior chamber when perforating the anterior hyaloid, which progressively increased in the following hours. Three hours after treatment, the intraocular pressure was 14 mmHg and iridoendothelial contact had disappeared. One year after surgery, intraocular pressure (IOP) was 16 mmHg with brinzolamide 0.1% (Azopt®, Laboratorios Alcon-Cusi SA, El Masnou, Barcelona).
