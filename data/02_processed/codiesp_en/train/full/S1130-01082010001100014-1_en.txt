A 63-year-old woman with no relevant history presented with epigastric discomfort, non-radiating and unrelated to eating, of two months' duration. The physical examination was normal and the only thing that stands out in the laboratory data is a mild microcytic anemia and hypogammaglobulinemia. Gastroscopy was performed where an excrescent, whitish, ulcerated lesion in the center of the duodenum was evident in the second portion of the duodenum, friable and elastic in consistency when biopsies were taken. The pathological examination revealed a dense lymphocytic infiltrate with a centrocytoid habit that destroyed some of the glands and penetrated the epithelium. The immunophenotype of the proliferating cells was CD 20 and CD 43 positive with CD 3, CD 5 and cyclin D1 negative. All of this led to confirming the presence of duodenal MALT lymphoma. The patient was referred to the oncology service where the extension study was completed and, after verifying the absence of locoregional and metastatic involvement, treatment with cyclophosphamide was prescribed with a good response.
