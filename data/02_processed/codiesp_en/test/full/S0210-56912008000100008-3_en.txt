A 60-year-old man comes to the hospital with chest pain that lasts two hours. The ECG revealed a subepicardial lesion current consistent with an inferior MI, a pronounced J-point depression along with a descending D-ST in leads V1-V2 and a horizontal D-ST in V3 to V5. Fibrinolytic treatment with tenecteplase and lidocaine was administered for extrasystolic ventricular tachycardias. An hour and a half later the ECG, recorded without pain, revealed a marked J wave in leads V1-V2 and absence of ST segment shift. One day later, the ECG revealed an inferior inversion of the T wave, a J wave in leads V1-V2, and a concave E-ST with positive T waves in leads V2 to V4. Creatine kinase rose to 1,143 U/l (with a MB fraction of 102). An echocardiogram revealed inferobasal akinesia. The hospital clinical evolution had no significant incidences. Four years after discharge, the ECG showed a J wave in the inferior and anterior leads, and a concave E-ST with a positive T wave in leads V2 to V4, suggestive of PRS.
