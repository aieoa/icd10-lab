A 13-year-old boy was referred to the Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery Service from the Pediatrics Service due to a 4-day history of asymptomatic swelling on the right cheek. He does not report any other symptoms nor has there been any weight loss.
Physical examination revealed a solid, elliptical mass between the body of the malar bone and the anterosuperior part of the right maxillary bone. There was a boundary between the lesion and the surrounding muscle and it did not infiltrate the skin. Intraoral examination revealed a firm, mobile, painless mass in the gum at the bottom of the vestibule of the right upper hemiarch. No other findings were found on head and neck examination.
A biopsy was performed with intraoral access. Histologically, the mass consisted, for the most part, of a compact proliferation of small cells. At higher magnification, it was observed that these cells were made up of round and uniform nuclei, which revealed granular chromatin with peripheral condensation, as well as scant and slightly eosinophilic cytoplasm. The stroma was scant with occasional fibrous tissue tracts. Through immunohistochemical analysis, positivity for CD-99 was observed.

Subsequently, a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was performed, which showed the presence of a well-defined, encapsulated solid tumor measuring approximately 24 × 13 mm located anterior to the right maxillary sinus, with anterior displacement of the zygomatic muscles. The lesion did not present infiltration of neighboring structures and showed intense enhancement with gadolinium after its administration, suggesting high cellularity of the lesion. No underlying bone alterations or significant adenopathic growths were identified.

Prior to treatment, distant metastases were ruled out by biopsy and bone marrow aspiration, in addition to performing a full-body PET-CT.
The treatment for SEE was administered following the Euro-EWING 99 protocol. The control MRI showed the disappearance of the lesion, although with persistence of a thickening that compromised the affected facial muscles (inferior aspect of the orbicularis oculi and levator labii). superior), along with minimal enhancement after contrast administration.
After this, the remaining lesion was surgically treated, performing a wide resection of the affected facial muscles and soft tissue. It was deepened to the plane of the maxillary bone, without resecting it because no tumor infiltration was observed clinically or radiographically.
The patient was discharged on the second postoperative day in good general condition and without postoperative complications.
Histological analysis of the surgical specimen revealed the absence of malignant cells and negative margins.
The control MRI findings were described as post-surgical changes from the baseline study, without criteria for underlying tumor rest or recurrence.
