A 32-year-old man was referred to our service due to a pedunculated lesion and clinical diagnosis of pyogenic granuloma, 1 cm high and 0.6 cm in diameter at its base, reddish in color, with vascular engorgement, located at the edge. free of the middle third of the right upper eyelid, without symptoms of pain or bleeding, and one year of evolution. The rest of the ophthalmological and systemic examination was normal.

It was surgically excised by creating a full-thickness pentagon that included the lesion. The histopathological diagnosis was pilomatricoma due to the description of a solid, encapsulated mass, consisting of a mass of pilomatric cells arranged in the periphery, large masses of keratin and isolated areas of ghost cells, with a granulomatous reaction to an adjacent foreign body. At higher magnification, small basaloid cells without cytological atypia are identified. In the most central area, the cells are larger, with eosinophilic cytoplasm with loss of the nucleus (phantom). Between these two predominant cell types there are transitional cells.

After 4 years of follow-up, no recurrence or malignancy has been observed.
