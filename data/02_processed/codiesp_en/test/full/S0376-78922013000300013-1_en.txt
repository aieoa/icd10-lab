A 56-year-old woman, hypertensive, with a history of poliomyelitis at one year of age and sequelae in the left lower limb that required multiple interventions to enable adequate walking, as well as lumbar spine surgery at 42 years of age. Diagnosed with spinal pathology of D11-D12 and L3-L4, posterolateral arthrodesis L1-L5 plus lamina graft and bone substitute was performed, presenting a poor evolution, with infection of the surgical wound and necrosis of the paravertebral muscles that required 3 new interventions to debride nonviable tissue. After the third intervention, a vacuum therapy system was placed and she was referred to our Service, a reference center for Reconstructive Surgery. The time elapsed between the first spine surgery and the time of referral to our Service was approximately 18 days.
The patient was transferred on a stretcher since she was unable to walk and had significant atrophy in both lower limbs, as well as a defect in the dorso-lumbar area of ​​approximately 20 x 5 cm in diameter, with exposure of the spine and osteosynthesis material. . She had no signs of infection.

We performed a study of the extension of the defect using Computerized Axial Tomography (CT) which reported instrumented arthrodesis from L1-S1 with resection of posterior elements and posterior soft tissue augmentation in the surgical bed, with air remains and soft tissue mass suggesting changes. postsurgical, without evidence of abscesses. We also performed dorso-lumbar angioCT to determine the viability of the musculocutaneous paravertebral perforators.

We kept the vacuum therapy system in the surgical wound for 20 days, while imaging and preanesthetic studies were completed, to contribute to wound clearance.
Planning of the surgical procedure included debridement of scar tissue, coverage of deep bony structures with well-vascularized tissue, and tension-free closure of the skin defect.
Under general anesthesia, with the patient in the left lateral decubitus position, we performed excisional debridement of the edges of the inverted skin and the fibrous scar tissue in the lumbar region. We designed and raised a reverse latissimus dorsi muscle flap based on the secondary pedicles, with its transposition to the lumbar defect by tunneling and fixed in the perilesional healthy muscle tissue. We placed 4 expiratory drains, 2 in the donor area and 2 in the recipient area, leaving one in the submuscular plane and the other in the subcutaneous plane.
The closure of the donor area was direct, in planes, without tension. Closure of the skin of the recipient area was performed after Friedrich of the edges, without tension and without compressing the muscle flap.

Five days after surgery, we observed an increase in right paravertebral volume after abrupt mobilization of the patient in bed. We performed an ultrasound that reported the presence of a right paravertebral intramuscular hematoma measuring 15 x 2 cm in size. Under general anesthesia, we evacuate a clotted hematoma and check the viability of the muscle flap, re-closing the wound.
After 18 days, the patient began to walk with the help of a walker, recovering the functionality of her lower limbs and was discharged on postoperative day 22.
During the 2-year follow-up after surgery, the patient showed normal ambulation and excellent local evolution of the flap.
