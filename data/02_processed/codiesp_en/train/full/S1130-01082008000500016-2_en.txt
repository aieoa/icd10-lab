A 66-year-old male, active smoker, with a history of partial gastrectomy for ulcer disease and chronic liver disease due to HCV in periodic follow-up. He was admitted with digestive bleeding in the form of melena accompanied by pain in the right hip that had been going on for several weeks, radiating from the anterolateral aspect of the thigh to the knee; He also reported constitutional syndrome with a weight loss of 6 kg. The physical examination revealed pain on palpation in the right femoral head and slight hepatomegaly of 2 cm, while the basic biochemical and hematimetric data were normal, except for slight anemia; serum αFP levels were 4.4 ng/ml. Abdominal CT showed an 8 x 7 x 5 cm mass in the right hepatic lobe with heterogeneous contrast enhancement, suggestive of HCC; Conventional radiology of the pelvis and hip, for its part, revealed a lytic image of a moth-eaten pattern at the level of the neck and right femoral head. The bone scintigraphy (99mTc) confirmed the pathological accumulation of radiotracer at that level, as well as in the left supraorbital region. Finally, the histological study by biopsy of the lesion in the femoral neck confirmed the diagnosis of HCC metastasis. Chemoembolization of the primary tumor was performed, associated with palliative radiotherapy to the coxofemoral joint (total dose of 2,400 cGy), with evident symptomatic improvement. Six months later, the patient presented a pathological subtrochanteric fracture that required osteosynthesis using a PFN nail, and died 20 weeks after the intervention.
