43-year-old mixed-race woman with a history of augmentation mammoplasty performed in June 2009: periareolar incision, transglandular dissection, subfascial pocket, placement of 290 cc textured cohesive gel anatomical implants, (McGhan® Medical Corporation, Santa Barbara, California, USA) In the same intervention, she also underwent abdominoplasty and liposuction of the lower back area. Aspirational drains were placed and removed 24 hours after the intervention. The postoperative period was uneventful and the result was satisfactory.
One year after the procedure, the patient presented discomfort and edema in the right breast that resolved with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs at a dose of 120 mg per day for 7 days. The subsequent evolution was satisfactory until 2015, that is, 5 years after these symptoms and 6 after the intervention, when the patient again presented discomfort and edema in the right breast, as well as the appearance of galactorrhea. She underwent magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) imaging studies documenting the presence of abundant periprosthetic fluid in the affected breast.

In view of the situation, an ultrasound-guided puncture was performed, obtaining 270 cc of serous material, without debris inside, dark yellow in color and without a characteristic odor. A sample was sent for cytological study, which reported chronic mastitis with granulomatous reaction and negative culture. However, the gradual enlargement of the breast continued in the following months, with 2 new punctures being performed without success. In the second puncture, fluid was sent again for a malignancy study due to the suspicion of a possible ductal carcinoma. The patient was evaluated again through ultrasound and MRI imaging studies without finding any abnormalities in the breast parenchyma, therefore, it was decided to remove the implant, with capsulectomy and review of the breast tissue through intraoperative biopsy. The surgery was carried out in conjunction with an oncological surgeon, who examined the mammary gland and took samples from the different quadrants.
The intraoperative and definitive study of both the sent breast tissue and the periprosthetic capsule were negative for malignancy. However, the periprosthetic fluid evacuated during surgery was positive for neoplastic cells in the cytological study. This liquid was also studied by making a cell block and processed with histological and immunohistochemical techniques. The latter obtained a positive result for ALCL, T immunophenotype, positive for CD 45, CD3, CD 30 and EMA (epithelial membrane antigen), negative for CKAE1/AE3 (cytokeratin), CD 20 and ALK (anaplastic lymphocyte kinase) and proliferation index 80%.

The Hematology Service proposed performing a positron emission tomography (PET) study, however the patient decided not to continue with the staging studies despite the final diagnosis of lymphoma, and to date has refused to follow any type of treatment. , not even the removal of the contralateral implant. Currently (May 2016) she is asymptomatic.
