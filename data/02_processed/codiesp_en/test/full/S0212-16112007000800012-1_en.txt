We present the case of a 35-year-old patient. She was referred to our Unit after her third hospital admission in the previous 18 months for recurrent chylous ascites. With no history of interest, she was admitted for the first time with symptoms of ascites and mild edema in the lower limbs for a month and a half, and an exploratory laparotomy was performed due to suspicion of ovarian neoplasia. 12 liters of ascitic fluid with chylous characteristics were evacuated, with no ovarian anomalies or peritoneal carcinomatosis observed.
She was discharged with diuretic treatment with spironolactone 100 mg and prednisone 40 mg in a descending regimen. After 5 months she was readmitted, extracting 9 liters of chylous fluid by evacuating paracentesis. In a new admission, 6 months later, during which the symptoms had been progressively reappearing, 14 more liters of peritoneal fluid were evacuated.
Throughout her stay in hospital, she underwent the following complementary tests: blood count, with slight leukocytosis and normal formula, iron study without alterations; biochemical profile, with hypoproteinemia and hypoalbuminemia, hypocalcemia. Determinations of celiac profile, HBsAg, HCVAc, tumor markers, and Mantoux were negative. Levels of ASLO, C-reactive protein and serum α-1 antitrypsin were normal. Study of thyroid function within normality. The serum proteinogram showed decreased albumin and gamma globulin values.
The extracted ascitic fluid presented characteristics of exudate and chylous appearance. Her culture was negative.
The thoracoabdominal CT revealed a normal chest, ascites in all peritoneal compartments, and edema in loops of the small intestine. The abdominal ultrasound showed abundant peritoneal ascitic fluid, with normal liver, portal vein, great vessels, gallbladder and spleen. Fibroenteroscopy, reaching the cecum, shows from the bulb to the sections of jejunum explored, irregularity in the villous pattern, fold edema and numerous punctate lymphangiectasias. Ileoscopy was performed, and the terminal ileum presented an extremely irregular mucosa, with whitish plaques that were friable when biopsies were taken. Capsuloendoscopy shows diffuse involvement of the small intestine, with punctate "millet grain" formations and edematous and congestive folds. The intestinal mucosa biopsy was reported as lymphoid follicular hyperplasia, focal lymphangiectasia; and the gastric, duodenal and jejunal mucosa did not show significant alterations.
Treatment with diuretics and intravenous albumin was started, presenting a favorable clinical evolution, and she was discharged with the diagnosis of primary intestinal lymphangiectasia, and home treatment was prescribed with Furosemide 40 mg, Spironolactone 100 mg and Prednisone 30 mg, and she was referred to the Nutrition Unit. Clinic and Dietetics for assessment and follow-up.
The examination in the Consultations shows a height of 1.65 m, weight 53.5 kg, with BMI 19.3 kg/m2, after evacuation of 14 L of ascites fluid during admission; Subjectively, she is thinner than before the onset of symptoms. The patient presents pale skin, long fingers, and discrete symmetrical malleolar edema. The rest of the physical examination is normal.
He did not report the usual gastrointestinal symptoms, except for 1-2 stools a day that looked somewhat greasy. Eat a varied diet, always avoiding excessively fatty foods, due to intolerance. Deny toxic habits. Nuligesta.
Analytically, serum values ​​of total proteins were 3.4 g/dL, albumin 2.1 g/dL, and calcium 7.3 mg/dL. The rest of the basic biochemistry, lipidogram, and blood count were normal.
We decided to institute dietary treatment. A personalized diet is prepared, with 2,200 kcal in 24 hours and the following distribution of nutrients: 52% carbohydrates, 30% lipids, 18% proteins. The contribution of fats from food is restricted, and lipids are provided in the form of MCT oil, using the amount of 85 ml per day, introduced into the diet progressively to avoid intolerances. The protein contributions of the diet are completed with 400 ml of high protein formula for enteral nutrition and 20 g of powdered protein module. In addition, a vitamin-mineral supplement is added.
The patient is cooperative and shows strict compliance with the recommended guidelines, with good adaptation and excellent tolerance.
After 11 months of follow-up, she has achieved weight gain, weighing 59.3 kg and BMI 21.4. The analytical parameters have experienced a significant improvement, with serum values ​​of total protein 5.2 g/L, albumin 3.7 g/L, calcium 8.5 mg/dL. She has no gastrointestinal symptoms. The waist circumference is 78 cm, and in a control abdominal ultrasound, no free fluid is observed in the abdomen. She has not had new episodes of ascites nor has she required hospitalization during this period.
