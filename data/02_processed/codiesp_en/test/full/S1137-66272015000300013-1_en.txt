This is a 59-year-old man, diagnosed with T2N2b squamous cell carcinoma of the larynx, which was initially treated with three cycles of chemotherapy without remission, so three months later a supraglottic laryngectomy was performed, with modified incomplete radical dissection of the left side. The pathological anatomy showed an infiltrating squamous carcinoma in the left hemiepiglottis with 8 of 11 positive lymph nodes in the internal jugular chain, subdigastric and left supraclavicular lymph nodes. He subsequently received chemotherapy and radiotherapy to the supraglottis and hypopharynx and bilateral lymph node areas.
Nine months after surgery, he consulted for a three-week headache associated with binocular diplopia and blurred vision in the right eye, showing ophthalmoparesis of the right cranial nerves III, IV, and VI. A brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed a mass in the posterior and lateral portion of the right cavernous sinus, with a protrusion towards the prepontine cistern, well delimited, with homogeneous gadolinium uptake and dimensions of 11x14.4x12.2 mm. In the bone window of the CT scan, neither lytic lesions nor invasion of the wing or sphenoid sinus were observed. Thoraco-abdomino-pelvic CT was negative. The radiological differential diagnosis was schwannoma, meningioma or metastasis.

An open biopsy was performed with a transylvian approach to the lateral wall of the cavernous sinus where tumor invasion of the intradural space was observed. The pathological result was squamous cell carcinoma. Palliative radiotherapy was subsequently administered. Three weeks after surgery, the patient consulted due to back pain without neurological symptoms. An MRI study of the spine showed focal lesions in the bodies D4, D6, and D10 suspicious for metastatic implants.
With radiotherapy treatment, the headache was relieved, but he developed ophthalmoplegia, preserving only abduction of the right eye. The patient died nine months after the diagnosis of metastasis in the cavernous sinus.
