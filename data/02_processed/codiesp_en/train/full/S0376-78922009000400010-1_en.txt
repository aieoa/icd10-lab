A 12-year-old woman reported having suffered a left facial abscess of dental origin. The predominant symptoms at the time of our evaluation were inability to open the mouth. The most significant finding in the CT and Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (MRI) was the absence of the left parotid gland and the mandibular deformity with fusion of its posterior wall to the upper jaw and left pterygoid process, with the consequent anterior displacement of the mandibular condyles. due to severe dysplasia of the temporomandibular joint.
Given the clinical situation described, tracheostomy was required to proceed with the extra-articular release of the maxillomandibular ankylosis, extraction of affected teeth and coverage with a standard mucosal flap. In the immediate postoperative period, fixation of the oral opening was performed by 2 cm using a retromolar wedge.

At the present time the patient maintains a bucomandibular opening of 2.4 cm which allows solid feeding by mouth. Confirmation of this entity was carried out through an anatomopathological study that reported changes compatible with chronic osteomyelitis.
