A 68-year-old woman with a history of high blood pressure and hypothyroidism, came to the emergency room with acute pain in the left hypochondrium, with no history of trauma, of a constant nature, radiating to the epigastrium with approximately 3 hours of evolution, accompanied by dizziness and weakness. The physical examination revealed hypotension and tachycardia that persisted despite the administration of intravenous fluids; The abdomen is painful with predominance in the hypochondrium and left lumbar fossa without signs of peritoneal irritation. Ultrasound and computed tomography (CT) were performed, demonstrating the presence of a rounded retroperitoneal tumor measuring approximately 10 x 12 cm in front of the upper pole of the left kidney, vascularized, with irregular contrast enhancement and with small calcifications inside; Furthermore, an increase in the density of the retroperitoneal space compatible with bleeding is observed. With the aforementioned findings, it was decided to intervene surgically, revealing a large left retroperitoneal hematoma that extends to the midline and the left iliac fossa and a tumor approximately 12 cm in diameter above the upper pole of the left kidney, encapsulated, with active bleeding. due to a fissure in it, which is extracted preserving part of the adrenal gland and the left kidney. The evolution is satisfactory. The subsequent evaluation revealed the absence of arterial hypertension and no pathological findings were observed in the abdominal ultrasound. The biopsy describes a nodular tumor measuring 10 cm in diameter and weighing 457 g. of reddish color with a yellowish peripheral margin, diagnosed in the microscopic study: Adrenal endothelial pseudocyst.
