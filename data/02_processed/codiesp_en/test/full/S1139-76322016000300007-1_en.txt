Six-month-old female infant. Controlled pregnancy with a normal course and no family history of interest. Delivery by scheduled cesarean section due to breech presentation. Artificial breastfeeding without incident. Correct vaccination according to schedule. Start of complementary feeding at five months (gluten-free fruits and cereals). His parents consult because they have noticed, since the introduction of the fruit porridge, the appearance of erythema on both temples a few minutes after beginning the intake. They report that the condition occurs when eating any type of fruit and that the erythema increases in intensity as the amount ingested increases. The girl does not reject fruits and even takes them with some eagerness. This erythema usually disappears after about ten minutes without being accompanied by any other symptoms.
Given that at the time of the examination the patient was asymptomatic, it was decided to perform a controlled provocation test in the office. In this way, it is seen how, a few seconds after starting the intake, erythematous plaques suddenly appear, of a faint red color and with poorly defined limits, located in the bilateral temporal region, being more evident on the right side.

By relating the anamnesis, the physical examination and the provocation test, Frey syndrome is suspected and its benign and self-limiting nature is explained to the parents. An expectant attitude is maintained without the need for additional tests or treatment and maintaining the usual complementary feeding regimen. After a month the symptoms subside, although the erythema reappears after 12 months with the consumption of natural yogurt, also with acidic characteristics, to disappear definitively after several weeks.
