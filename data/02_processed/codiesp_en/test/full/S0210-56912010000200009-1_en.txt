This is a 70-year-old woman with a history of HTN and recently diagnosed with lower rectal neoplasia, treated with chemotherapy (5-fluorouracil analogue) and preoperative radiotherapy completed 2 months before surgery. She was admitted to the ICU after surgery (abdominoperineal amputation) due to suspicion of aspiration during anesthesia induction with oximetric repercussions and ventilatory difficulties. She is sedated, intubated and connected to MV. Radiologically, no infiltrates indicative of parenchymal lung involvement were observed. She had a good initial evolution, so she was extubated without incident after 48 hours. It presents progressive worsening in the following 2 days at radiological level (right basal parenchymal involvement), blood gas (non-invasive mechanical ventilation (NIMV) was required to maintain pulse oximetry at more than 90%) and clinical level (persistent tachypnea with poor respiratory mechanics). so it is reintubated. She begins with a persistent fever above 39 oC, resistant to antipyretics and physical measures. Microbiological samples are extracted (blood culture, urine culture and bronchial aspiration). The antibiotic treatment started in the operating room is maintained. At an analytical level, a significant inflammatory reaction stands out with C-reactive protein of 50 mg/dl without being accompanied by a leukocyte reaction (<10,000×103/μl). She remained intubated and connected to MV for 7 days during which fever persisted with peaks greater than 39oC, with radiological improvement without infiltrates and persistently negative microbiological samples; Abdominal CT is performed and complications derived from surgery are ruled out. The afebrile patient is extubated, with compensated blood gases and a good response to spontaneous respiratory tests; Despite optimal conditions at the time of extubation, she had to be reintubated within 12 hours due to respiratory failure. Once again, he began with a high fever and with repeated negative cultures, so fiberoptic bronchoscopy was performed with collection of bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) and samples were sent to microbiology (culture and PCR for herpes, CMV and respiratory viruses) and pathological anatomy. Percutaneous tracheostomy is performed and the BAL results are received, in which early positive HSV-1 PCR is confirmed (qualitative test) and, at a histological level, the presence of cells that show multinucleated nuclei, with ground glass chromatin and intranuclear inclusions; Immunocytochemically, nuclear positivity for herpes virus was confirmed. Antiviral treatment was started with acyclovir 10mg/kg/8h i.v. with clinical improvement, disappearance of fever and progressive disconnection from the respirator until full respiratory autonomy.
