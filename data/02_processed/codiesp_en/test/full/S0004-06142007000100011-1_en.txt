A 39-year-old black male patient with a history of recurrent oral aphthous lesions and vision disorders (progressive loss), who presented with painful ulcerative lesions in the genital region (penis and scrotum), with a diameter of 3 and 10 mm, with cut edges and yellowish bottom, covered with a pseudomembrane. We performed complementary tests thinking about a sexually transmitted disease, but everything was within normal limits. So we decided to perform excision and biopsy of the lesions. Reporting leukocyte infiltrate in the superficial dermis, epidermal necrosis and presence of polymorphonuclear leukocytes (not tumor lesion).

When he goes to consult again he comes with total vision loss and arthralgias. The joint case of Urology, Dermatology and Ophthalmology is discussed, and with the criteria of:
- Recurrent oral aphtosis. - Signs of uveitis (vision loss). - Genital ulcers.
And performing the skin pathergy test, which consists of injecting 1 cc of intradermal physiological saline into the patient's forearm. Where it was considered 'positive' (he developed a pustule on an erythematous base), we concluded that this patient had Behcet's disease.
