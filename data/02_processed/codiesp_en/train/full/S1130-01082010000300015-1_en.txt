An 83-year-old woman with a history of non-insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus, arterial hypertension, dyslipidemia and chronic atrial fibrillation presented with progressive abdominal pain and constitutional syndrome for one month. On physical examination, she was afebrile and hemodynamically stable, with a globular, soft, depressible abdomen, painful on palpation with a tumor on the left flank. The analysis showed a hemoglobin of 9.3 g/dl, leukocytes 11,300 /mm3, urea 109 mg/dl, creatinine 1 mg/dl, total bilirubin 2.6, alkaline phosphatase 139 U/l, with the rest of normal liver function. Abdominal computed tomography (CT) found a mass measuring 20 x 20 x 15 centimeters of heterogeneous density that causes displacement of the renal vessels and pyelocalyceal system without causing obstructive uropathy or metastatic involvement. The surgery involved a left radical nephrectomy with complete excision of the mass. The patient died a month after surgery due to acute lung edema. The pathological anatomy showed a 3.5 kilogram tumor with a mucoid appearance with areas of hemorrhage affecting 30%, and in its center the rejected nephrectomy piece but without infiltration of the parenchyma, compatible with a mixed liposarcoma (dedifferentiated and myxoid). with a mitotic index of 2/10 high power fields.
