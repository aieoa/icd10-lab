A 68-year-old woman with no relevant history came to the emergency room due to bleeding from a tumor located in her left arm. The tumor is large (21x15 cm) and occupies the entire distal half of the arm, it is ulcerated, with necrotic areas with great skin fragility and bleeding. She reported having noticed a small diameter tumor that had been progressively increasing in size in the last 2 years and for which she had never consulted.

In the emergency room, electrocoagulation of the bleeding point is performed under local anesthesia and a tissue sample is taken for pathological study.
She is admitted to the Hospital and undergoes study. She undergoes preoperative tests, as well as imaging tests and a tumor extension study. X-ray of the arm shows a soft tissue mass without bone invasion. The extension study (thoraco-abdomino-pelvic CT) shows a pulmonary nodule with well-defined borders and juxtapleural location, located in the upper segment of the left lower lobe, measuring about 6 mm, suggestive of metastasis; It also shows ipsilateral axillary lymphadenopathy.

The biopsy of the tumor results in an area with massive infiltration of the entire dermis by a fibrohistiocytic proliferation that appears to be low grade and another necrotic area, which suggests a high grade proliferation. Tumors such as malignant fibrohistiocytoma, fibromyxoid sarcoma or myxofibrosarcoma are proposed as differential diagnoses.
Once the studies were completed, the patient was evaluated in a joint clinical session by the Plastic Surgery, Oncology and Thoracic Surgery Services to decide the therapeutic approach to take. We opted for surgical treatment of the tumor and application of adjuvant chemotherapy to the pulmonary nodule, which is not considered a subsidiary of surgical removal.
The intervention consisted of complete resection of the tumor with wide margins and direct closure of the underlying defect. The adjuvant chemotherapy treatment was 6 cycles with Ifosmamida, Mesna, Adriamycin and Dacarbazine.

The pathological study of the excised tumor macroscopically revealed a piece measuring 21x16x15cm, partially covered by skin with important areas of deep ulceration and an expansive growth edge; The cut showed extensive central areas of necrosis, a firm elastic consistency and a whitish color in the non-necrotic areas and a myxoid appearance in other areas. The microscopic study determined a global nodular pattern, although in many areas it was lost due to the large size of the nodules. Histologically there were very variable appearances, with areas of sarcomatoid appearance predominating with elongated cells with ovoid nuclei and discretely acidophilic cytoplasm; In other areas there were variable amounts of mucoid material, areas of predominantly myxoid and even clearly cartilaginous appearance; The other clearly identified component was epithelial, glandular and often cylindromatous. The immunohistochemical study was positive for Cytokeratins (AE1-AE3) in the epithelial areas and negative in the sarcomatoid areas, Vimentin positive in sarcomatous areas and negative in pure epithelial areas, s-100 protein positive in sarcomatoid areas and negative in the epithelial areas, EMA positive in epithelial areas, very high MIB I (proliferation index) (50%), negative Actin and Desmin, negative C-Kit. The definitive histological diagnosis was a tumor of epithelial origin of high malignancy, called malignant chondroid syringoma.

The patient was followed up in the Plastic Surgery outpatient clinic, showing good progress of the scar, with no evidence of recurrence after 2 years. Magnetic Nuclear Resonance (MRI) of the arm without evidence of local recurrence. Thoraco-abdominal control imaging studies show a decrease in axillary lymphadenopathy and pulmonary nodule (4mm). Currently, quarterly checks continue to be carried out by the Plastic Surgery and Oncology Services.
