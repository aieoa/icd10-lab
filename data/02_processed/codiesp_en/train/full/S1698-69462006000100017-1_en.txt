67-year-old patient, smoker of 6 cigarettes/day. Previously diagnosed with breast cancer (T2 N0 M0) treated by surgery associated with chemotherapy and radiotherapy. Due to the metastatic progression of the breast carcinoma, treatment with bisphosphonates was initiated (pamidronate at a dose of 90 mg IV for 20 cycles). After the first 9 cycles of pamidronate, the patient is referred to our Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery clinic by her oncologist due to a burning sensation and diffuse gingival pain with common gingivorrhages, presenting an ulcerous lesion 5 mm in diameter at the edge. Very painful right side of the tongue.
Extraction of the molar associated with the lesion due to lingual decubitus and of four other teeth without viability was indicated, with an injury due to bone exposure appearing approximately one month after each extraction at the site of the extractions. Limited debridement of bone necrosis was performed, complemented by antibiotic therapy (amoxicillin-clavulanate, clarithromycin) and chlorhexidine gel. The evolution was favorable in all areas except the mandibular molar, in which the exposure of the bone perpetuated the lingual decubitus, requiring a second, more aggressive ostectomy. Despite this exposure not completely disappearing, correct control of the patient's pain was achieved when the tongue lesion disappeared.
