A 54-year-old man is admitted to the hospital due to an hour-long episode of chest pain. The ECG showed Q waves along with E-ST in inferior leads and D-ST in leads I, aVL and V4-V5. In the right precordial leads, a marked J wave could also be observed, followed by a convex and descending D-ST that ended in a negative T wave. With the diagnosis of inferior AMI, he received fibrinolytic treatment with tenecteplase. One hour later the ECG, recorded with slight precordial pain, revealed absence of lower E-ST, a negative T wave in aVL and an elevation of the J point > 0.2 mV with descending E-ST and negative T wave in leads V1- V2, which suggested an electrocardiographic pattern of SB. Five hours later the ECG showed a prominent J wave with convex and saddle E-ST in leads V1 and V2, respectively. There was also a J-point elevation associated with an E-ST and positive T wave in leads V3-V4. Three days after admission, the ECG revealed a lower qR pattern along with a negative T wave in leads III and aVF, a manifest J wave in V1-V2, a concave E-ST with positive T wave in leads V2-V3, and absence S wave in the left leads. While he was hospitalized he had no ventricular arrhythmias. The maximum value of creatine kinase and troponin I were 1,398 U/l and 23.2 ng/ml, respectively. Coronary arteriography revealed stenosis (95%) of the proximal right coronary artery and a stent was placed. After being discharged, 6 months later he underwent a provocation test with flecainide (2 mg/kg intravenously in 10 minutes) which was negative in order to make a diagnosis of SB.
