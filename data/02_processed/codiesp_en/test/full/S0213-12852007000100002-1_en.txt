In March 2005, a 30-year-old patient presented for observation at the Department of Odontostomatological Surgery of the Università degli studi di Milano-Bicocca, at the Hospital San Gerardo di Monza, for a consultation about dental element 2.8 ( upper left third molar). The patient did not present relevant clinical symptoms, except for mild pain, reported in the area of ​​the third molar.
The extraoral examination showed no relevant signs and palpation of the neck lymph nodes demonstrated a normal situation.
The intraoral examination revealed partial mucosal inclusion of element 2.8, without particular variations in color and consistency upon palpation of the area under examination.
Radiographic analysis with orthopantomography, element 2.8 presented increased radiolucent areas.

The patient, who had no systemic pathologies, in March 2005 received the extraction of element 2.8 and biopsy of the adjacent tissues, measuring approximately 1.0 x 1.0 x 1.0 centimeters.
The diagnosis based on the histological report was plexiform ameloblastoma.

Upon deepening the radiographic diagnosis through computed axial tomography with Dentascan software, it demonstrates the involvement of a large area of ​​the left maxilla that reached the base of the orbit.

In May 2005, under general anesthesia, a hemi-maxillectomy was performed with wide safety margins starting from the first upper left premolar, according to the principle, supported by many authors, which inspires the therapy of malignant tumors, of "resecting apparently healthy tissues.

Further histological analysis of the surgical specimen confirmed the diagnosis of plexiform ameloblastoma.
Regarding the porotic treatment, before surgery impressions of the jaws were made and after casting a simulation of the surgical intervention was carried out on the plaster models.
With the help of CT, the margins of the resection were established, with particular attention to the anterior margin, which was important so as not to compromise the correct positioning of the palatal prosthesis.

The palatal filling prosthesis was mucosa-supported and tooth-retained with hooks in elements 2.3 and controlaterally 1.4 and 1.5 and 1.7 and 1.8. At the end of the intervention, the adaptation of the prosthetic device was found to be adequate.

Figure 9 demonstrates how, at 6 months, normotrophic and well-vascularized tissues are observed in correspondence of the resection area.

The prosthesis has been periodically controlled and adapted to the conditions of the tissues according to healing and the possibility of adding teeth will be estimated, keeping them out of occlusion, until the reconstruction phase with implants.
