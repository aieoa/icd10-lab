A 43-year-old man was admitted to the hospital due to intense chest pain that lasted an hour. The ECG showed slight inferior E-ST, prominent J wave in V1-V2 and marked D-ST in leads V1 to V5. Diagnosed with inferoposterior AMI, the patient received thrombolytic treatment with reteplase. One hour later the ECG revealed an incomplete RBBB, a manifest J wave (R') in leads V1-V2 and absence of ST segment deviations. There were also high R waves affecting the initial part of the QRS complex and acuminate T waves in leads V2-V3, which suggested the diagnosis of superimposed posterior AMI. Eight hours after admission, the ECG revealed an inversion of the T wave in leads III and aVF, a J wave in V1-V2, and a concave E-ST with a positive T wave in leads V1 to V4. Three days later, the ECG revealed a J wave, a concave E-ST, and a positive T wave from V2 to V5 suggesting PRS. Creatine kinase rose to 4,999 U/l (with a MB fraction of 254). An echocardiogram revealed inferobasal and apical akinesia. A coronary arteriography revealed a 90% stenosis in the middle third of the right coronary artery and a 60% stenosis in the proximal portion of the circumflex artery. After the placement of a stent in the right coronary artery, the patient has been asymptomatic for three years.
