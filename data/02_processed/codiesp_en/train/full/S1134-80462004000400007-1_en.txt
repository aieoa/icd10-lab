This is a 75-year-old woman with a history of type II DM on insulin treatment with chronic complications such as polyneuropathy, HBP under treatment and who is obese, who is scheduled for TKA surgery (total knee prosthesis). In the preoperative data there is nothing notable apart from what was described, the anesthetic technique was a combined epidural/intradural procedure at the L3-L4 level, 3 ml of 0.5% bupivacaine without vasoconstrictor were administered through the intradural needle and left an epidural catheter was placed for postoperative analgesia. The postoperative period was uneventful and he was taken to the ward with a PCAE (epidural PCA) loaded with 0.125 bupivacaine and fentanyl 4 micrograms per ml, with a schedule of 3 ml.h-1, 3 ml boluses every 30 minutes. At 4 a.m. Approximately, the change of PCA occurs, causing the error and administering a PCA epidurally with medication and programming for the IV route. (in 100 ml, 50 mg of morphine plus 10 g of metamizole).
After the daily visit of the PADU, the nurse of said unit detects the error. The patient is conscious and oriented with excellent analgesia, without signs of respiratory depression, neurological alterations, or other side effects. The amount of drug administered was 4 mg of morphine and 160 mg of metamizole. The PCA and the catheter were removed and she was admitted to the Resuscitation Unit for control and follow-up, where the patient did not present any side effects and remained stable at all times, so she was discharged 24 hours later. ward and after 2 weeks she was discharged home, after being reviewed by the anesthesiologist. Periodic follow-up was carried out for up to 18 months without any incident.
