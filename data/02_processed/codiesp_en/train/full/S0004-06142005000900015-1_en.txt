A 36-year-old male, with no history of interest, was studied in the internal medicine clinic for presenting a painful left inguinoscrotal mass on palpation of two months' duration, without weight loss or voiding syndrome.
On examination, the testes were of normal size and consistency, with an indurated and very painful left spermatic cord. The testicular ultrasound was normal. CT of the abdomen-pelvis revealed a 6 x 3 centimeter mass in the path of the left spermatic cord without showing images of retroperitoneal involvement. With the diagnosis of left paratesticular tumor, he underwent surgery, finding a mass in the spermatic cord and performing an intraoperative biopsy reported as neoplastic proliferation with a non-lymphomatous myofibroblastic appearance, for which a regulated left radical orchiectomy was performed. The pathological anatomy was pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma of the spermatic cord, normal testis and epididymis, and negativity of the resection margins.

Subsequently, the patient received several cycles of chemotherapy with adriamycin and ifosfamide + MESNA. In the control imaging tests four months after surgery, no tumor recurrences were observed.
