A 13-year-old male, with no personal history of interest, who visits the health center because for a few months he has noticed an injury in his left leg that does not cause any symptoms and has not changed in size. He reported possible trauma to that area two years earlier after a fall on skates.
He is in good general condition and is asymptomatic. A prominence of approximately 1.5-2 cm of bony consistency is palpated about 3-4 cm above the left medial malleolus. There was no local inflammation, hematoma or pain on palpation. There are also no distal neurovascular alterations or functional impotence.

A simple x-ray of the affected leg, anteroposterior and lateral, is requested, where a bone injury is visualized. The radiological report gives the diagnosis: "The anteroposterior and lateral x-ray of the ankle shows a well-defined bone relief, at the expense of the cortex, without apparent soft tissue involvement, on the medial surface of the distal third of the left tibia compatible with osteochondroma."

Given that it is an asymptomatic mass and has remained stable while maintaining its size, it was decided to follow the evolution in the consultation and, in the event of possible changes, perform an imaging test and/or schedule it in trauma consultations for review.
