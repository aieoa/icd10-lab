A 47-year-old patient with a history of gastric ulcer, smoker of 10 cigarettes/day, vasectomized, who consulted for a moderate increase in left scrotal volume for years but with an increase during the last month, accompanied by dull discomfort, without acute pain. No urinary symptoms or fever. The physical examination highlights the presence of a medium-sized hydrocele that prevents exploration of the testicle and epididymis. General blood and urine tests were normal. A scrotal ultrasound was performed, reporting a left intratesticular nodular image with well-defined borders, suggestive of testicular neoplasia and hydrocele with anechoic content. Tumor markers (AFP and ß-HCG) normal. A left inguinal orchiectomy was performed with placement of a silicone testicular prosthesis. After a normal immediate postoperative period, an abdominal CT scan was performed, which was normal. The surgical specimen macroscopically showed an infarcted area of ​​testicle alternating with another apparently normal area, confirming the presence of testicular infarction in the microscopic study with the absence of tumor.
