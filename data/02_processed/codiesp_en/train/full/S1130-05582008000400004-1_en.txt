In 1993, a 73-year-old man was admitted to the internal medicine service with a provisional diagnosis of bone metastasis from an unknown primary tumor. He had lost 15 kg in 18 months and manifested anorexia, constipation, left thigh pain, and elevated blood concentrations of calcium, bilirubin, and acid and alkaline phosphatases. Bone scintigraphy demonstrated multiple areas of increased uptake and hypertrophic osteoarthropathy, suggesting metastatic disease.

The patient's personal history did not offer anything notable, except for having undergone surgery for a maxillary tumor at the age of 18 (1938). Histological diagnosis of this tumor was not available.
During admission, elevated levels of urinary parathyroid hormone and blood calcium were found. Cervical ultrasound showed an increase in the size of the right inferior parathyroid gland.
The patient underwent right hemithyroidectomy with excision of the enlarged parathyroid and ipsilateral gland; Calcemia was immediately normalized. The histopathological study showed infiltrating carcinoma of the inferior parathyroid and hyperplasia of the superior parathyroid.
Two years later, bone pain and constipation occurred again with elevated levels of calcium and parathyroid hormone in the blood. In a new intervention, a hemithyroidectomy, left parathyroidectomy and thymectomy were performed. Calcemia normalized but the patient died two years later from unrelated causes.
