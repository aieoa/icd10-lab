The first case is a 25-year-old woman diagnosed with Laurence-Moon-Biell syndrome that manifests itself with hypogonadism with obesity, mental retardation, polydactyly, retinitis pigmentosa and kidney failure. She started HD treatment at age 11, she received her first kidney transplant the following year, which failed due to venous thrombosis, so she returned to HD and required a transplantectomy 9 months later.
Due to vascular access difficulties, she needed to be referred to PD. During her evolution, the patient suffered several episodes of peritonitis and suffered from severe hyperparathyroidism. Its peritoneal membrane was classified as medium-high transporter. After 12 years in the PD program, the patient shows a poor general condition and peritonitis, caused by two germs (Staphylococcus epidermidis and Pseudomona aeruginosa), along with tunnel infection due to staphylococcus. During the examination, a "mass effect" was seen in the area of ​​the subcutaneous tunnel, not fluctuating, without inflammatory signs, with a hard consistency and a mamelonated contour, producing a peritoneal eventration in the catheter hole.
The radiological images showed sclerosis and calcification of the entire peritoneal membrane, compressing all abdominal visceral structures. Specific antibiotic treatment, removal of the PD catheter, and transfer to HD were performed. Later, the patient had difficulty swallowing due to vomiting. Once on HD, she developed two new episodes of ascites, secondary to Echerichia coli peritonitis, which required evacuation by paracentesis. Subsequently, she presented acute ischemia in the right lower limb, due to femoropopliteal obstruction.

Surgical treatment of peritoneal calcification and amputation of the ischemic limb were ruled out. The evolution was unsatisfactory and he died shortly after.
