A 56-year-old woman with moderate congenital mental retardation, dyslipidemia and hypothyroidism, who came to the clinic due to progressive constipation, an increase in abdominal circumference and a weight loss of 8 kg in the last 4 months. During the physical examination, palpation of a hard, poorly defined, central hypogastric mass measuring approximately 10 x 8 cm was notable. Analytical determinations, including acute phase reactants and digestive and gynecological tumor markers, as well as gynecological ultrasound, are normal. An abdominopelvic CT scan was performed, revealing a giant dilation of the rectum and left colon, occupied throughout the lumen by compacted fecal remains, which produces displacement of all abdominal organs and bilateral hydronephrosis without associated renal failure. Colonoscopy up to the cecum with biopsies and pelvic MRI are normal. Anorectal manometry was performed, which demonstrated conservation of the inhibitory rectoanal reflex. Treatment was started with oral polyethylene glycol and lactulose enemas, with notable clinical improvement, and maintenance therapy with high doses of oral lactulose is currently underway with weight gain and a bowel movement every 24-48 hours.
