We present the case of a 45-year-old patient admitted to the Hematology Service for MM Ig G Kappa stage III-A with hepatic infiltration. He did not have any personal or family history of interest.
The patient was referred to the Ophthalmology Service due to a 24-hour red right eye (RE) without any other visual or ocular symptoms.
Visual acuity was unity in both eyes and a painless, vascularized, superior nasal subconjunctival mass was seen in the RE at the slit lamp. The rest of the examination, including the fundus, was normal.
The lesion progressed in 48 hours, infiltrating the upper eyelid and producing RE ophthalmoplegia.
A computed tomography (CT) scan was performed, showing an increase in soft tissue without bone lesions. The biopsy of the lesion demonstrated infiltration by plasma cells.
The patient was undergoing CT using: Vincristine, Uracil, Cyclophosphamide, Melphalan and Prednisone followed by Vincristine, Uracil, Adriamycin and Dexamethasone. Upon confirming the biopsy, local RT was added.
Despite the treatment of CT and RT, the infiltration continued to progress through the eyelids and conjunctiva, developing acute post-trabecular glaucoma with intraocular pressure (IOP) of 50 mm Hg that did not respond to oral (carbonic anhydrase inhibitors) and topical (beta) treatment. blockers and prostaglandins).
RT was suspended and QT was replaced by Cyclophosphamide, Dexamethasone, Bortezomid and Adriamycin, followed by Methotrexate, Cytarabine and Methylprednisolane. This second line of CT produced remission of the infiltration in two weeks, normalizing the IOP with normal visual fields, although with residual ptosis and restriction of ocular motility. Despite the remission of the ocular symptoms, the patient died six months later.
