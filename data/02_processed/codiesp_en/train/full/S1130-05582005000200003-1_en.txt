A 28-year-old male patient came to our Service with left hemimandibular pain and fever. He has no pathological history of interest. The physical examination does not reflect any specific sign, but it does reveal pain on palpation of the pericoronal tissues of the lower left third molar. An emergency analysis was performed, observing a slight increase in the number of leukocytes, with no other notable alteration. In the orthopantomography (OPG) both included third molars are visualized, in a mesioangular position, with respective follicular sacs, the right one being larger.

With suspicion of possible odontogenic infection (pericoronaritis), antibiotic treatment is instituted, with complete resolution of the condition. Extraction of both lower wisdom teeth is recommended. The histological study confirmed the presence of a follicular sac associated with the wisdom tooth, formed by non-keratinized polystratified epithelium. After the extraction of the lower left third molar, the patient does not come to complete the treatment. After 20 months he consulted again due to pain in the lower right third molar. The OPG shows a large radiolucent image around the crown of the lower right wisdom tooth.

With the suspected diagnosis of a dentigerous cyst associated with the third molar, surgical intervention was performed with extraction of the tooth and cystectomy. The pathological study confirmed the presumptive diagnosis, as a lesion with a wall of connective tissue lined on the inside by a stratified epithelium of keratinized ciliary cells and cells with abundant mucin. In the three-month review, the adequate active ossification process (with formation of bone trabeculae) of the residual cavity generated by the cyst was verified.
