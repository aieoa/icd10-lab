A one and a half month old girl who presents with fever (38 °C) of 48 hours of evolution and irritability, associated nasal mucus; On examination he had an erythematous rash of small elements and the rest of the examination was normal.
In an initial analysis: leukocytes 6310/µl (segmented 67%, arched 6%, lymphocytes 19%, monocytes 8%), hemoglobin (Hb) 10.9 g/dl, hematocrit (Hct) 29.6%, platelets 492,000 /µl, C-reactive protein (CRP) 8.9 mg/l. Lumbar puncture was performed: glucose 51.3 mg/dl, protein 133.9 mg/dl (hemorrhagic fluid).
Given the suspicion of a serious bacterial infection, the patient was admitted for intravenous antibiotic treatment with cefotaxime and ampicillin (200 mg/kg/day), adding acyclovir (60 mg/kg/day) in the next 24 hours to cover the herpes simplex virus. Fever after 48 hours. On the fourth day he presented irritability, poor color and general condition, with persistence of evanescent rash, and respiratory difficulty; Control analysis was performed with findings of: leukocytes 2670/µl (segmented 31%, segmented 3%, lymphocytes 47%, atypical lymphocytes 5%, monocytes 11%), reticulocytes 0.66%, Hb 8.0 g/dl, Hct 25.3%, platelets 131,000/µl. Capillary blood gas analysis: pH 7.32, PCO2 43.3 and HCO3 21.8 mmol/l. Chest x-ray: bilateral alveolointerstitial infiltrates, more striking on the left side, suggestive of acute pulmonary edema. Upon discovery of pancytopenia, samples were taken for serology of parvovirus and enterovirus, as well as influenza A. High-flow oxygen therapy was initiated and the patient was transferred to the pediatric intensive care unit of a tertiary hospital, where he remained for five days.

Immunoglobulin M (IgM) antibodies to parvovirus B19 are positive and IgG are negative, making a diagnosis of parvovirus B19 infection, respiratory failure, and associated pancytopenia. The patient showed progressive improvement on the fifth day of admission, and was discharged on the twelfth day without further complications. The girl's brother later presented with infectious erythema.
