The case of an eight-month-old patient admitted with a seven-day history of fever up to 40oC, refusal to eat, and vomiting is presented. She had no relevant family or personal pathological history, nor any known allergies. The physical examination only revealed small laterocervical lymphadenopathy, lymph nodes and pharyngeal hyperemia.
In the complementary study requested, she had a blood count with a normal erythrocyte sedimentation rate; C-reactive protein, 4.48 mg/dl; and normal sodium, potassium, chlorine, magnesium, calcium (Ca), phosphorus (P), bilirubin, proteins and blood gases. Blood, urine and stool cultures were negative and Parainfuenzavirus 3 was isolated from the throat swab.
An analytical finding that attracted attention was an ALP figure of 5252 IU/l. The parents were asked again in search of any cause for this elevation of FA, with special emphasis on the consumption of drugs, history of fractures or previous illnesses, both in the patient and in her family, without obtaining new data.
A determination of FA was requested again to verify that it was not a laboratory error, as well as GGT, vitamin D, parathyroid hormone (PTH) and FA isoenzymes. In this analysis, the elevation of FA (6070 IU/l) was confirmed, with the rest of the requested parameters (GGT, FA isoenzymes, vitamin D and PTH) being normal.
On the seventh day of admission, once the febrile process had resolved, the patient was discharged with the diagnosis of Parainfuenzavirus 3 infection and hyperphosphatasemia under study. It was controlled clinically and with periodic AF determinations in outpatient clinics.
In the subsequent controls, she presented a progressive decrease in ALP, she remained asymptomatic and her physical examination was strictly normal, so she was definitively discharged six months later, after presenting two ALP determinations within normal values ​​for her age, with the diagnosis of transient benign hyperphosphatasemia of infancy (HTBI).
