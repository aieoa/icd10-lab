A 43-year-old man presents to our service for evaluation of a 6-month-old left frontal tumor with no previous trauma at that level. The patient did not report pain in the tumor, only a feeling of heaviness and left fronto-occipital headache. Physical examination revealed a 1 cm diameter tumor with minimal perilesional erythema. On palpation it appears as a hard mass, not adhered to the bone plane without alteration of the frontal branch of the facial nerve. He also reports having a similar nodule in the right costal region. A CT scan and ultrasound-guided fine needle aspiration (ECO-FNA) of the costal tumor are requested. The CT revealed a well-defined mass of approximately 0.7 cm in the left frontal region that did not invade adjacent tissues and did not present bone destruction. The ECHO-FNA of the right costal region revealed a lipoma.

It was decided to perform a biopsy-excision of the tumor under local anesthesia. Intraoperatively, a lesion was observed firmly adhered to the fascia of the frontal muscle and the pericranium. A subperiosteal dissection was performed including the frontal muscle and pericranium; no erosion of the underlying frontal bone was observed. Closure of the surgical defect was direct and simple. The postoperative course was uneventful and there has been no recurrence after 18 months of post-surgical follow-up.
The pathological study describes at a macroscopic level an irregular white-brownish nodular fragment measuring 0.9 × 0.7 × 0.4 cm. At a microscopic level, a connective tissue is observed with a circumscribed, poorly delimited nodular lesion, composed of a proliferation of oval-fusiform elements that are arranged in a loose matrix with a swirling-storiform pattern together with osteoclastic-type giant cells, some extravasated red blood cells and little dispersed mononuclear inflammatory component. Immunohistochemically, it showed positivity for the CD68 antibody and the vimentin protein in the giant cells and with lower intensity in the oval cells. Negativity for S-100 protein. Positivity for the Ki 67 antibody representing a proliferative index of less than 5%.

The study concludes with the diagnosis of benign neoplasm of mesenchymal origin type FN.
