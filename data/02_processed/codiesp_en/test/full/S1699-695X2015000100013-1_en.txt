A 22-year-old male presented to the Primary Care (PC) clinic with a personal history of functional murmur in childhood, seasonal allergic rhinitis, smoker of 10 cigarettes a day, and weekend drinker. Uninteresting family history. After performing an ECG in the Primary Care consultation required prior to traveling abroad to study at a private university in the United Kingdom, a generalized negative T wave was observed from V2 to V6 and on the inferior surface, with isolated ventricular extrasystoles. On physical examination, blood pressure levels were 140/70 mm Hg, and cardiopulmonary auscultation was normal at 60 beats per minute with no other data of interest. With these electrocardiographic findings, he was referred to the Cardiology consultation.

The complementary studies requested by the PC are normal (analysis with blood count, biochemistry and coagulation; lateral and posteroanterior chest x-ray). The echocardiogram detected a significant dilation of the right ventricle (55 mm), with a hypokinetic lateral aspect as well as mild tricuspid insufficiency. Holter monitoring shows sinus rhythm and a migratory atrial pacemaker with frequent ventricular extrasystoles and some ventricular triplets. Cardiac MRI shows an enlarged right ventricle and thin-walled moderator band hypertrophy with some hyperintense areas inside suggestive of fatty infiltration, with small dyskinetic sacculations. The left ventricle, valves and pericardium are normal.
With a suspected diagnosis of ARVC, treatment with beta-blockers is instituted. The patient remained asymptomatic following periodic controls. After his return to Spain two years later, after playing a basketball game, he developed syncopal symptoms, so it was finally decided to implant an ICD. One month after admission he is referred to the Rehabilitation Service to consider starting a Cardiac Rehabilitation Program (CRP). The patient continues an active life today, one year later, with follow-up from Primary Care and reviews by Cardiology.
