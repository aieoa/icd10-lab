A 36-year-old male, with no personal history of interest, who in an ultrasound performed due to colicky pain in the right renal fossa, was reported as an 8 - 10 cm intrarenal mass with echogenic material inside, located in the lower pole of the right kidney. suggesting a complicated renal cyst. In a CT scan performed later, a solid mass was reported in the lower pole of the right kidney with a maximum diameter of 10 cm, which caused compression of the kidney's excretory duct, with no evidence of tumor thrombus in the renal vein or cava, suggestive of hypernephroma. A bone scan with Tc99m-MDP was performed without observing pathological bone accumulations.
With the data obtained, a right radical nephrectomy was performed, revealing as an intraoperative finding, a mass adhered to the lower pole of the right kidney. (The tumor and the kidney, although close, were clearly independent upon dissection).
The anatomo-pathological report of the specimen resulted in a solitary fibrous tumor. The tumor was well delimited although not encapsulated, without infiltrating the kidney or the renal vessels that were displaced by the large tumor mass. The immunohistochemical study showed positivity for CD34, CD99 and Vimentin, while it was negative for Desmin, Protein S-100, and CAM5.2.

The patient underwent surgery in November 2001. Since then he has been undergoing outpatient follow-up in our office with biannual ultrasounds and annual CT scans, without any recurrence.
