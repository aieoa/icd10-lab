A 53-year-old woman diagnosed with tuberous sclerosis at age 29 due to the presence of multiple facial lesions (sebaceous adenoma). History of fibrocystic breast mastopathy and colloid goiter. Her parents and children do not show signs of the disease. Two years ago, she reported two eyelid lesions that caused itching and tearing in her right eye. On examination, he presented multiple facial lesions with a distribution in butterfly wings, flat in appearance, well delimited (some overlap), reddish and non-scaly, measuring approximately 0.4 to 0.8 cm in long axis. At the periungual level, both on her toes and fingers, she has fibromas (Koeman's tumors), and a hypopigmented spot on her chest. In the left lower eyelid she has two lesions that affect the free edge of the eyelid, nodular, cystic in appearance and yellowish-white in color, located in the middle 1/3 and external 1/3 of the eyelid, measuring 0.5 and 0.3 cm in diameter. . The clinical diagnosis was epidermal inclusion cysts. The rest of the ophthalmological (fundus) and systemic (cranial, thoracic and abdominal CT) examinations were normal. The surgical treatment consisted of resection of the two eyelid tumors using two wedges that encompassed the lesions, with good aesthetic and functional results. The histopathological diagnosis was angiofibroma due to the presence of a pronounced collagen proliferation associated with angiomatous hyperplasia. After 18 months of follow-up, no recurrence has been observed.
