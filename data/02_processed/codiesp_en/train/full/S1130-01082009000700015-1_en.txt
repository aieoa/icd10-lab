A 33-year-old woman presented with abdominal pain of two months' duration located in the right iliac fossa without other accompanying symptoms. The patient attended several hospital centers, where she was discharged with normal basic complementary tests. She went to the emergency room of our hospital due to the persistence of her symptoms.
An abdominal Doppler ultrasound was performed, in which a hypervascularized nodule measuring one centimeter in diameter was observed in the right iliac fossa. The patient was admitted to our service with a presumptive diagnosis of endometrioma to complete studies. The computed tomography performed during admission revealed a single 1 x 1.5 cm nodule located in the appendix.
The patient underwent surgery, performing exploratory laparoscopy, which identified the appendix without inflammatory signs, with the nodule described above. The pathological examination of the extracted piece macroscopically presents a nodule of hard consistency at the tip of the appendix that is reported as a granular cell tumor. The lesion extends through the subserous adipose tissue of the appendix.

Histologically, the proliferating cell is medium to large in size with a large cytoplasm and small nuclei with a patent nucleolus, highlighting the presence of eosinophilic granules in the cytoplasm. Immunohistochemical techniques show positivity for the S100 protein.

Once the histological diagnosis is known, upper and lower digestive endoscopies are performed, which do not detect other lesions.
