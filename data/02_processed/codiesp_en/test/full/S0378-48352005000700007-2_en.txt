34-year-old female patient with a deceased maternal grandfather's history of lung carcinoma. Sinusitis seven years ago. She consulted her ENT because of a 14-month history of rapidly progressive rhinorrhea and epistaxis. Through rhinoscopy, a polypoid-like mass was observed in the right nostril, very bleeding on contact, which occupies the entire right nostril, appears to connect with the septum and middle turbinate, extending to the choana, and occupying the nasopharynx. The biopsy was positive for esthesioneuroblastoma.
A CT scan of the head and neck was performed, showing a 4 centimeter (cm) polypoid mass with a large pedicle in the right nostril and cavum. MRI of the head and neck reveals a space-occupying lesion that strikingly obliterates the cavum and extends from the level of the nasal cavities backwards, coming into contact with the prevertebral muscles, without infiltrating it, and well delimited.

A chest CT scan and a bone scan without pathology were performed as an extension study. It is classified as Kadish Stadium C.
She underwent functional endoscopic surgery with the pathological anatomy being esthesioneuroblastoma. The postoperative CT scan revealed a polypoid thickening of the mucosa at the level of the right maxillary sinus, without evidence of malignant characteristics, at the level of the right frontal sinus.

External radiotherapy was decided as adjuvant treatment, using a Linear Accelerator with 6 and 15 MV photons, with tissue compensation wedges after virtual simulation and 3D planning on individualized helical CT. The tumor bed received a global dose of 60 Gy in 30 fractions, with a scheme of 2 Gy/fraction, 5 fractions per week. Tolerance was good, with maximum G1 skin, G2 mucosa and G2 oropharyngeal toxicity. Currently, 6 months have passed since irradiation, and the complete response is maintained.
