A 40-year-old man goes to the emergency room due to the appearance of edema. He has a family history of a mother with unknown renal amyloidosis. The mother's renal biopsy report refers to resistance to permanganate, which seems to rule out AA amyloidosis, and leads us to consider, at the time the diagnosis was made, the possibility of AL amyloidosis.
The patient does not receive any pharmacological treatment. The physical examination revealed a blood pressure of 135/75 mmHg and bimalleolar edema, with no other data of interest. Laboratory tests confirm the presence of nephrotic syndrome (serum albumin of 2.5 g/dl, cholesterolemia of 275 mg/dl and proteinuria of 6 g/24 h), without alterations in the sediment and normal glomerular filtration rate. The electrophoretic spectrum in blood and urine, the immunological study and the renal ultrasound are normal. A renal biopsy was performed, observing 19 glomeruli with a structure completely distorted by a hyaline deposit in a nodular arrangement, with positivity for Congo red and thioflavin; The tubules and vessels do not show significant alterations, the result being compatible with amyloidosis. The study on a paraffin sample showed marked positivity for AP protein, which is a part of the amyloid substance common in all types of amyloidosis, with negativity for light chains and AA proteins, B2 microglobulin and transthyretin. A complementary study was performed with antibodies against fibrinogen and lysozyme, resulting in deposits of amyloid substance positive for the A alpha chain of fibrinogen (Afib). However, the genetic study did not find any mutation in the Afib chain gene, and a previously undescribed mutation was demonstrated in the apolipoprotein AI (apo AI) gene. Given this discrepancy, the mother's biopsy is analyzed, which contains glomerular deposit in the cortex and massive deposit at the medullary level. The deposit at the medullary level is not described in Afib amyloidosis, being very characteristic of apo AI. It is concluded that the first results of the immunohistochemistry are not reliable because they were not carried out under the appropriate conditions, and the presence of apo AI was subsequently confirmed using the same technique.
An extension study was performed, with echocardiogram and electromyogram, with no evidence of cardiac involvement or autonomic neuropathy. During the course, a progressive increase in transaminases is detected, suggestive of liver involvement. These results are consistent with this type of familial amyloidosis, with predominantly renal and hepatic involvement. Despite initial normality, kidney function progressively deteriorates until requiring renal replacement therapy within 2 years. The patient receives treatment with peritoneal dialysis for 4 years, until he undergoes surgery and undergoes a kidney transplant from a deceased donor. He presented a complicated postoperative period with splenic rupture, in the context of his illness, with amyloid deposit in the spleen, requiring an urgent splenectomy. Finally, the patient is discharged in a stable condition.
