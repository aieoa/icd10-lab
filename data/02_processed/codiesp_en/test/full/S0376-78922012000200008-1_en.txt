A 46-year-old woman underwent left radical mastectomy with axillary lymphadenectomy in October 2005 due to an infiltrating ductal carcinoma in the upper-outer quadrant with positive hormone receptors and lymph node metastasis in 1 of 13 isolated lymph nodes.
In the same surgical procedure, immediate reconstruction was performed by placing an expander prosthesis (Becker anatomical) with a layer of 300 cc textured silicone gel. of volume, beginning expansion 2 weeks after surgery and reaching maximum expansion volume 1 month later.
As complementary treatment, chemotherapy was instituted according to a sequential scheme of adriamycincyclophosphamide and taxane, receiving a total of 24 sessions during the period between November 2005 and May 2007, followed by trastuzumab. Complementary hormonal treatment with letrozole is maintained for a period of approximately 3 years.
The second stage of the reconstruction was carried out in July 2006, by replacing the expander prosthesis with a 320 cc textured silicone gel breast implant. in size at the same subpectoral location.
In the follow-up clinical controls, a depression was evident at the level of the medial edge of the mastectomy scar, in the shape of an axe, which displeased both the patient and the surgical team itself, so it was decided to perform a remodeling in March 2007. of the same using a fat graft from adipose tissue obtained from the abdominal area and processed using the Coleman technique, after infiltrating the scar with 3 cc. local anesthetic (1% lidocaine with adrenaline); It was carried out without incident, infiltrating a total of 5 cc. of fatty tissue in the area of ​​the defect.
The patient continued to be monitored by the Gynecology-Oncology Service, undergoing control imaging tests according to the diagnosis and treatment guide for malignant tumors of our hospital. Specifically, 4 annual mammograms and 2 breast ultrasounds were performed between May 2006 and April 2009, with no evidence of alterations that suggested damage to the integrity of the prosthesis.
In March 2010, a new breast ultrasound was performed in which a previously unreported ovoid cystic image was visualized on the anterior surface of the interior of the breast prosthesis, so the radiologist decided to perform an MRI. This procedure was carried out a month later and a nodular image was observed in the prosthesis, located in the internal interquadrant area, about 2.5cm in diameter, which impressed as an image of liquid content, without inflammatory signs or signs of prosthetic breakage. In different sections it seemed as if an elongated, hook-shaped structure was inserted inside the prosthesis, similar to how a finger is inserted into a half-inflated balloon. The patient did not report any symptoms.

Given such data in the images, we decided to perform surgical exploration taking into account the possibility of being forced to remove the breast prosthesis. At first it was thought that perhaps the fatty infiltrate could have entered the interior of the prosthetic capsule and pushed the prosthesis, without breaking it, creating a deep concavity inside, like a tunnel.
During the operation, it was found that the prosthesis appeared to be intact, and that there was no type of tissue inside the capsule that could alter the shape of the implant. In the area of ​​the capsule that was in contact with the place where the fat infiltration occurred, there were no alterations, but we nevertheless decided to take a biopsy of the area of ​​injected fatty tissue; This biopsy corresponded in the histological study with fibrous tissue without signs of malignancy.
When removing the prosthesis to examine it, we were struck by the fact that several opaque spots appeared inside, scattered in different places, without a linear distribution, which could make us think that they were needle punctures. However, at a point close to the largest of them, after applying strong compression to the prosthesis, we saw that the cohesive silicone of the implant herniated slightly. We decided then, once it was removed, to open it and send samples of the material that was inside. The replacement of the removed implant was done with another one with the same characteristics.

The anatomopathological study reported the sample analyzed from the inside of the prosthesis as blood material.
