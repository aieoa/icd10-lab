A 75-year-old man whose history only highlights high blood pressure and benign prostatic hyperplasia.
As a result of presenting mild symptoms of the lower urinary tract, a simple abdominal x-ray revealed a right paravesical pelvic annular calcium image.
Given these findings, an abdominal CT was performed and, in a retrovesical situation, two masses were observed, one 3 cm and the other 1.5 cm in diameter, with calcified walls, compatible with a retrovesical hydatid cyst, with no other findings in the abdomen or on chest x-ray.
The patient, from the beginning, has been asymptomatic, which is why periodic CT checks have been established, and has remained unchanged over time; A 2.5 cm diameter, heterogeneous mass persists in the pelvic cavity, which sits above the left seminal vesicle, along with another similar, somewhat smaller, completely calcified mass on its periphery on the right side, in relation to its pelvic hydatid disease. .
