28-year-old woman, nursing assistant, who goes to the Emergency Traumatology Service because she reports that for two days she has been experiencing pain in the subpatellar region of the right knee that has been worsening and currently has difficulty mobilizing said joint. .
On physical examination, an erythematous lesion was observed in the right subpatellar region, with pain on palpation and a small incised-blunt wound just a few millimeters long. Active and passive mobilization are partially limited by pain, with no other accompanying symptoms.
The worker states that in childhood she had an osteochondroma in the same knee, currently cured and without sequelae, and the last control x-ray was performed two months ago, which was completely normal.
The worker relates this episode to a work accident suffered two days ago, in which while she was carrying a box with 32 mercury thermometers in her hand, she slipped and fell to the ground. Some of the mercury thermometers fell on her right knee and caused an incised-contused wound, which we observed in the examination of her still in a healing state.
The nursing assistant recognizes that she was overloaded at her workplace. The box in which she transported the thermometers was not closed, the worker was not wearing gloves and her work clothes were not removed after the work accident. Subsequently, no hygienic-sanitary measures were carried out nor did any adequate action be taken against the mercury spill.
In the Emergency Department, the next step was to perform an x-ray of the knee, revealing a metallic foreign body in the patellar bursa, corresponding to mercury.

After the diagnosis of chemical bursitis due to mercury, and taking into account the toxicity of the metal, the patellar bursa was urgently removed in the operating room under scopy, to prevent traces of mercury from remaining in the worker's joint.
Currently, the assistant does not have any sequelae and she has fully recovered from her injury.
