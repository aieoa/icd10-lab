He was a 27-year-old man, who had suffered pneumonia as a child and who was not a smoker, nor did he have an alcohol habit. He was diagnosed with extrinsic bronchial asthma and allergic rhinoconjunctivitis with hypersensitivity to grasses and olive trees. He required inhaled corticosteroids sporadically. After a significant physical effort (going out as a bearer in a procession during some local festivals and having slept very little), in the summer of 2003 he began to experience pain in the right hemithorax that sometimes radiated to the right upper limb and that increased with inspiration. deep Subsequently, he developed fever and a feeling of shortness of breath, so he went to the hospital emergency room. During the clinical examination upon admission, he was found to be in poor general condition and a lesion in the scarring phase on the abdominal wall that could correspond to a boil. The vesicular murmur was decreased at the right base. Cardiac and neurological examinations were normal. There were no organomegaly.
He presented leukocytosis (from 12,000/cubic mm to 18,700/cubic mm) with neutrophilia, with the rest of the hematological parameters being within normal limits. There was no eosinophilia. During admission, creatinine increased to 1.6 mg/dl and urea to 61 mg/dl. Other biochemical values ​​were: total bilirubin: 2.19 mg/dl, direct fraction: 1.33 mg/dl, LDH: 687 IU/l, GOT: 61 IU/l; GPT: 111 IU/l, GGT: 521 IU/l, alkaline phosphatase 509 IU/l. The sedimentation rate reached 71 mm in the first hour. Serology for HIV and hepatitis B and C viruses were negative. Serial blood cultures were performed and were positive for Staphylococcus aureusmethicillin-susceptible.
Transthoracic echocardiography showed normal images without pericardial effusion. Ten days after being admitted, a hot, erythematous lesion appeared in the right parasternal region that disappeared with digit pressure and was about 5 x 5 cm. Fluid was aspirated from the lesion, the culture of which was also positive for methicillin-sensitive S. aureus. The abdominal ultrasound was normal. On the chest CT there was a left pleural effusion with interstitial prominence. Three small nodular images were observed in the right upper lobe, left upper lobe and another subpleural location smaller than one centimeter in size, compatible with small staphylococcal abscesses (Fig. 1). The abdominal CT scan was normal, with no lymphadenopathy or fluid in the free cavity. Bone scintigraphy showed intensely increased osteogenic activity at the level of the sternal joint compatible with arthritis at that level. An MRI of the sternum was performed. There was gadolinium uptake in the joint of the manubrium with the sternal body, the uptake extending to the middle of the manubrium and the proximal third of the sternal body, with an increase in size and uptake also in the adjacent soft tissues, both anterior and posterior. These findings were compatible with arthritis at that level and highly suggestive of osteomyelitis. There were no signs of bone destruction. The clinical evolution of the condition was good with antibiotic treatment consisting of intravenous (IV) cloxacillin for 15 days at a dose of 2 g/6 hours and IV gentamicin for 7 days at a dose of 5 mg/kg/24 hours. At no point during his evolution did he require intensive care. Subsequently, oral treatment based on rifampicin (dose of 600 mg/24 h) and levofloxacin (dose of 500 mg, every 24 h) was continued for two months. The parasternal dermal lesion took five weeks to disappear. To rule out the existence of a primary immunodeficiency, screening was performed with the following results: complement levels, lymphocyte subpopulations and markers, oxidative capacity of granulocytes and normal lymphocyte functionality (the latter with expression of activation markers in response to different stimuli). Protective levels for specific IgG against tetanus toxoid, Haemophilus By capsular polysaccharide of pneumococcus. Slightly elevated levels of IgE and IgG4 with normality in the rest of immunoglobulins and IgG subclasses (IgE = 170 IU/l, normal range: 0-100 IU/l; Ig G4 = 3,910 mg/l, normal range: 80 -1,400 mg/l). Isohemagglutinin titers could not be evaluated due to lack of samples. This study was carried out while the patient was discharged and two months after the onset of the disease.
