A 60-year-old woman underwent left laparoscopic radical nephrectomy for a lower pole renal tumor measuring 9.7x7.5 cm. During the intervention, para-aortic peritumoral adhesions and to the mesocolon were found, where some thick lymphatic vessels were ligated or coagulated, a careful dissection of the hilum was performed and the distal ureter was clipped, without intraoperative incidents and with bleeding of less than 100 ml. The anatomical-pathological study showed clear cell renal carcinoma, solid and cystic, with areas of intratumoral bleeding, Fuhrman grade 2-3, stage pT2 pN0. The postoperative period was uneventful, a drop in hemoglobin of 1.5 g/dl (from 13.2 to 11.7) was detected, and she was discharged from the hospital five days later.
One month after discharge, she consulted due to an increase in abdominal perimeter that caused dyspnea and discomfort, and imaging studies revealed diffusely distributed ascites, suggestive of chylous ascites. The analyzes showed Hb of 11.1 g/dl, normal leukocyte formula and lymphocyte count, normal kidney and liver function and mild hypoproteinemia - albumin of 2.9 g/dl (N: 3.4-5), proteins totals of 5.8 g/dl (N: 6.4-8.2). She was treated conservatively, without performing diagnostic or evacuative paracentesis, with a diet low in salt and lipids, protein supplements and spironolactone 50 mg/day, with improvement in symptoms and abdominal perimeter, discharged from the hospital six days later, and with complete recovery. confirmed by abdominal ultrasound one month later. Six months later she has no symptoms or signs of recurrence, and the abdominal ultrasound is normal.
