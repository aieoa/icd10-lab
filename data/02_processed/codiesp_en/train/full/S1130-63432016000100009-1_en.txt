A 52-year-old man, weighing 240 kg and BMI of 74 kg/m2, was admitted to the hospital with a diagnosis of ischemic stroke. As a pathological history of interest, the patient presented deep venous thromboembolism in the past, which is why he was prescribed home anticoagulant therapy with Rivaroxaban.
The Emergency Service considers the possibility of giving the patient fibrinolytic treatment with alteplase. However, after the examination, this intervention was rejected, since the patient was at the limit of the temporal interval, had 23 points on the NIHSS scale (National Institute of Health Stroke Scale) and the time of the last intake of Rivaroxaban was unknown. .
After his admission to the ICU for neurological monitoring and surveillance, it was decided to start treatment with enoxaparin as anticoagulant therapy. To this end, the Intensive Medicine Service contacted the Pharmacy Service in order to know the most appropriate dosage given the situation of extreme obesity2 (BMI > 40 kg/m2).
For safety reasons, given the scarcity of data in this regard, it was decided to start treatment at a dose of 0.7 mg/kg/12 h subcutaneously (enoxaparin 160 mg/12 h) to avoid bleeding and because it was the maximum dose evaluated. in various clinical trials3-5. In addition, anti-Xa factor monitoring was established to establish the final dose6.

Four hours after the administration of the third dose, the concentration of anti-Xa factor in blood obtained was 0.37 IU/mL. When it was between 0.35-0.49 IU/mL, the dose was increased by ≈10% (170 mg/12 h). Four hours after the next dose, the concentration of anti-Xa factor was 0.59 IU/mL, and at 24 hours it was 0.48 IU/mL. In view of this result, the dose was increased, reaching 180 mg/12 h. Finally, the next determination obtained a value of 0.84 IU/mL, placing us within the therapeutic range and setting the optimal dose for our patient at 0.75 mg/kg/12 h.
