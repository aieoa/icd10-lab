36-year-old patient admitted for cerebral hemorrhage, open to the ventricles. Neurosurgical evacuation is ruled out. She initially presents ICP greater than 20mmHg; Sedoanalgesia, relaxation and osmotics begin. At 72 hours she began progressive respiratory deterioration, attributed to neurogenic pulmonary edema. After 48 hours with FiO2 of 1, PD was tested on the eighth day of admission. There is no relevant respiratory improvement and ICP rises to 20mmHg. After 12 hours she switches back to DS. After another 12 hours, respiratory improvement begins, allowing a decrease in FiO2 to begin. On the twelfth day, coinciding with a transfer to the CT room, ICP monitoring is lost and a new sensor is placed, which offers values ​​less than 20mmHg with a CPP greater than 65mmHg, which remain similar on successive days. Two days later she deteriorates again respiratoryly. She is placed on PD early, which allows a decrease in FiO2 in 3 hours. After 12 hours on PD, she was placed on DS and the improvement was maintained. No deterioration of ICP or CPP is observed. The subsequent evolution is favorable until a normal level of consciousness is recovered, with left hemiparesis.
