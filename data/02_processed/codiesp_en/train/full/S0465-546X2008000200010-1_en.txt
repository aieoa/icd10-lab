A 48-year-old male, with no history of medical interest or toxic habits, and no known allergies.
While working in the warehouse of a nuclear power plant, he was required to unload a truck transporting 55% hydrazine hydrate. One of the drums accidentally fell to the ground, spilling about 25 liters. The company's emergency services were notified and they diluted the surface of about 3 m2 where the spill had occurred with water. The patient actively participated in the waste collection, without using any personal protective equipment; When he finished the task he did not change his clothes or take a shower.
Several hours after the accident he began to notice oral redness with chelitis of the lower lip, oral thrush, and diffuse abdominal pain. He was treated by a medical service, where he was prescribed symptomatic treatment. Six days after the accident, he presented erythematous-scaly lesions on his face and arms (areas that were not covered during the accident). He therefore went to a hospital where a general analysis and simple x-rays of the chest and abdomen were performed, which were normal, and he was prescribed treatment with antihistamines.
Two weeks later he went on his own initiative to the Toxicology Unit of the Barcelona Clinical Hospital, due to persistence of erythema-scaly lesions, abdominal discomfort and dysgeusia. The physical examination was normal and the blood and urine analysis only revealed a total bilirubin of 2.5 mg/dl (VN: up to 1.2 mg/dl) with an indirect bilirubin of 2.1 mg/dl. The abdominal ultrasound study was normal. A check-up was carried out after one and three months, and he was asymptomatic although the bilirubin remained slightly elevated, and he was discharged from the hospital.
