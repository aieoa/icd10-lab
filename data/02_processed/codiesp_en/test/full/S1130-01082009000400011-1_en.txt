A 54-year-old woman presented to the Emergency Department with a 5-day history of progressive pain starting in the right iliac fossa, associated with hyporexia and without alterations in intestinal transit, voiding symptoms or previous local trauma. Her history includes arterial hypertension and non-insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus treated with enalapril and acetylsalicylic acid. On examination, she had a distended abdomen, painful at the level of the right iliac fossa without signs of peritoneal irritation. The analysis showed no alterations except for an increase in acute phase reactants (fibrinogen 811 mg/dl and CRP 12 mg/dl). Computed tomography (CT) showed a 4 x 3 x 5 cm mass adjacent to the cecal pole with an 8 mm appendix, suggesting acute appendicitis versus a complicated neoformation process. A laparoscopic approach was initially used, finding a hematoma with a mass effect at the level of the cecum and bloody free fluid, so it was decided to convert and perform a right hemicolectomy with an uneventful postoperative period. The pathological anatomy was reported as an area of ​​blood extravasation at the level of subserosal tissues of the cecum with foci of abscesses without histological signs of malignancy.
