14-year-old adolescent admitted for treatment of a cervical disc herniation at the C4-C5 level. A month earlier she had begun to suffer from right cervicobrachialgia and cervical muscle contractures, without neurological deficits. The pain improved with conservative treatment, but days later she began to have difficulty abducting her right upper limb. On physical examination she noted a loss of strength in the right deltoid and biceps (grades 2/5 and 4/5 respectively). No sensory alterations or pyramidalism were observed in the lower limbs. Cervical spine radiographs showed loss of height in the vertebral bodies of C4-C5-C6, as well as calcification at the level of the C4-C5 disc. Cervical MRI revealed a voluminous right C4-C5 disc herniation that compromised the ipsilateral root. A cervical computed tomography (CT) scan showed that the hernia was calcified. A complete analytical study was performed, and no alterations were observed in the blood count, erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), or phosphocalcium metabolism. A scintigraphy ruled out pathological uptakes.

This patient had been diagnosed at the age of 10 with idiopathic intervertebral calcifications following a radiographic study motivated by neck pain. This study showed the existence of a calcification at the C4-C5 level and several calcifications at the dorsal level. An MRI ruled out any disc herniation. Until the current episode she had remained asymptomatic, except for occasional back pain.
Given the neurological deficit that the patient presented, surgical treatment of the herniated disc via anterolateral approach was chosen. During surgery, the existence of an intervertebral disc with fine calcifications was observed. When the posterior common vertebral ligament was opened, a whitish, pasty material was released, which was under moderate tension. The pathological report of the material obtained was fibrocartilage with foci of calcification. One month later the patient had completely recovered from her deficits.
