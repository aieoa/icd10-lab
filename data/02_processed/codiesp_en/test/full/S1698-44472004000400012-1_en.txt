A 54-year-old man was referred to our service in May 2003 due to dental pain related to an erupted lower right third molar. Upon oral inspection, it was possible to observe the existence of a swelling that expanded the vestibulo-lingual cortices, in the region of the right mandibular third molar, carious distally. The oral mucosa was intact, and no cervical lymph nodes were palpable. The patient reported the existence of hypoesthesia in the distribution territory of the mental nerve for fifteen days. In the orthopantomography, the presence of a radiolucent image, with poorly defined contours, was evident in the right mandibular body. Two days later, under local anesthesia, the third molar was extracted and curettage-biopsy of the underlying tissue was performed. During the operation, intense hemorrhage occurred, which could be stopped with the use of Surgicel (Johnson & Johnson, New Brunswick, NJ) and by packing bone wax in the alveolar bed.

The postoperative blood count and coagulation study did not show significant alterations. The histopathological diagnosis of the material submitted was: mandibular metastasis due to well-differentiated hepatocellular carcinoma. The neoplastic cells did not stain with cytokeratins 19 and 20, nor with alpha-fetoprotein, but they did show intense cytoplasmic granular staining with the hepatocyte marker N.

The patient's personal history included an 8-year history of type II diabetes, controlled with oral hypoglycemic agents. It was a 130g drinker. ethanol/day since age 30, and smoker of 2 packs of cigarettes/day since age 20. One year before our evaluation, the patient had been diagnosed with liver cirrhosis (Child Pugh stage A: 5/15), and multicentric hepatocellular carcinoma affecting segments II, III, and IV, undergoing partial liver resection. one month after his diagnosis. Previously, a complete study had been performed that included bone scintigraphy with technetium 99-MDP and computed tomography of the skull, lung, abdomen and pelvis, without detecting the presence of metastasis. Serum alpha-fetoprotein values ​​were, in postoperative controls, normal (3.6 ng/ml).
15 days after our intervention, a marked increase in alpha-fetoprotein levels was observed (221 ng/ml), with bone scintigraphy and magnetic resonance imaging showing the existence of multiple metastases that affected the left occipital bone, sternal body, pelvis, and multiple vertebrae. The patient underwent palliative radiotherapy on the vertebral lesions (10 Gy per week of Co 60) without improvement, with death six months after our biopsy.
