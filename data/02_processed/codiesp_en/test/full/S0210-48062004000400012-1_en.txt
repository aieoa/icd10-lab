A 66-year-old man was referred by the Gastroenterology Service due to the discovery of a 25 cm retroperitoneal mass, in an abdominal ultrasound performed for the study of dyspepsia. His personal history included: old pulmonary tuberculosis and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. On physical inspection, a large mass was observed that deformed the right anterior hemiabdomen, extending from the subcostal area to the pubis. On palpation, said mass was painless, firm in consistency, with no signs of peritoneal irritation and dull on percussion. On rectal examination, a size II/V prostate was palpable, adenomatous, smooth and well-defined. The blood analysis, sediment and urine culture were normal, and the PSA level was 1.2 ng/ml.
Abdominal ultrasound showed a cystic lesion 25 cm in diameter, with abundant internal echoes, extending from the lower border of the liver to the groin.
In i.v. urography (IVU) distortion of the right renal silhouette and pelvis was observed, with a significant displacement of the lumbo-iliac segment of the right ureter, which surpassed the abdominal midline, as well as slight ectasia of the ipsilateral upper urinary tract.

The abdominopelvic CT performed with oral and intravenous contrast revealed a right retroperitoneal polylobulated cystic mass measuring 25 cm in cranial-caudal diameter, which extended from the subhepatic region to the groin, compressing and displacing the right kidney, the iliac psoas and ascending colon, posteriorly and medially. Punctate calcifications were also observed in the cystic wall. In the medial and inferior portion of said mass, a tubular structure 2 cm in diameter and 7 cm in length was evident, with progressive tapering caudally, ending in a complete stop. The right kidney appeared functionally normal. The CT findings were interpreted as a possible dysplastic right lower hemikidney with partial agenesis of the ureter.

Given the diagnostic doubts that existed with the radiological studies carried out, it was decided to perform a percutaneous puncture-biopsy, which was reported as a cystic lesion wall, and urine cytology, which did not show malignant cells.
Subsequently, surgery was performed through a right pararectal approach, observing a cystic mass related at its cranial end to the right hepatic lobe and the lower pole of the kidney, and at its caudal end to the space of Retzius and the internal inguinal orifice. No infiltration of any intra-abdominal organ was observed. A careful resection of the cystic mass and the cecal appendix, which was in close relation to the caudal portion of it, was carried out.

In the pathological study of the specimen, a dilated appendix was seen, covered by a cytologically benign mucinous epithelium, which formed papillary-type structures. These findings were diagnostic of mucinous cystadenoma of the appendix.

The post-operative period was normal. In the IVU performed three months after the surgical intervention, good bilateral renal function was observed, with lateral hypercorrection of the right ureteral tract and disappearance of the right renal distortion.

In the review carried out after 20 months, the patient is asymptomatic from a urological point of view, and in the control CT scan there are no abdominal lesions suggestive of pseudomyxoma peritonei.
