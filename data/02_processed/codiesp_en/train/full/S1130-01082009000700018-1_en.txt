A 66-year-old man, of Vietnamese origin and currently residing in the United States, came to the emergency room of our center after presenting with sudden pain in the epigastrium, which he associated with heavy eating. The pain radiates to the left shoulder. On examination, sweating and mucocutaneous pallor appear with preserved distal pulses.
The patient is hypotensive and tachycardic, his ECG is normal and given the urgency of the condition, an abdominal ultrasound is requested, which informs us of a mass in the VI liver segment suggestive of hepatocellular carcinoma and the existence of free intracavitary fluid; Next, a CT scan of the abdomen with contrast was performed, which reported very abundant hemoperitoneum and a lesion in liver segment VI compatible with hepatocellular carcinoma, probably ruptured, and with a hyperdense focus next to the lesion that suggested active bleeding. The analysis shows hemoglobin figures of 8.8 g/L, hematocrit of 0.249 L/L and 14,310 leukocytes with neutrophilia.
Given the patient's instability and the findings of the imaging tests, we decided to urgently undergo surgery.
During the laparotomy, the lesion described in the ultrasound and CT scan is identified and it is decided to perform segmentectomy, lavage and drainage of the cavity.
After 7 days (4 of them in the Intensive Care Unit) the patient is discharged from the hospital.
