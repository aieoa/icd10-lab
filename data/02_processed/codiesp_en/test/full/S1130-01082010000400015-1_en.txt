We present an 85-year-old patient with angiosarcoma of the cecum. The patient was admitted to our hospital with rectal bleeding. On admission she had hypotension and tachycardia and her physical examination showed mucocutaneous pallor and abdominal distension. Her digital rectal examination showed bright red blood with no tumor identified. The analysis revealed a hemoglobin of 4.3 g/dl, so a lower digestive endoscopy was performed in which a 1 cm reddish mass was identified in the cecum with active bleeding and which was initially controlled with hemostatic clips after surgery. obtaining biopsies. Twelve hours later, the patient presented a new episode of digestive bleeding with hypotension, so an urgent laparoscopic right hemicolectomy was performed. The patient was discharged on the fifth postoperative day. The extension study performed after surgery was negative.
On macroscopic analysis, the cecum contained a 2 cm exophytic ulcer. Microscopic analysis showed multiple anastomotic vascular channels dissecting the collagen and smooth muscle sheets to the lamina propria and muscularis propria. Immunohistochemistry demonstrated great reactivity of the tumor cells against CD31 and vimentin. Stains for CD34, cytokeratin 20, and cytokeratin AE1-3 were negative. Based on these results, the diagnosis of epithelioid angiosarcoma of the colon was made. The margins were free of tumor as well as the 12 lymph nodes analyzed. The patient did not receive adjuvant treatment and in subsequent follow-up she is still alive and without evidence of disease two years after surgery.
