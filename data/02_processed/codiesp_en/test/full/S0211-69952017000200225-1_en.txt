A 41-year-old man was referred for evaluation due to the finding of proteinuria. Diagnosis of PNH in the context of hemolytic anemia. He did not require blood transfusions and had no thrombotic events. Laboratory tests showed persistent hemolytic activity, elevated LDH (1,800-3,000 U/l) and undetectable haptoglobin, hemoglobin 8.5 mg/dl, mild leukopenia and thrombocytopenia, normal ferritin 25 ng/ml.
He had recurrent hemolytic crises with fatigue, dysphagia, abdominal pain, and dark urine. The episodes usually lasted 1-3 days, every 2-3 months.
Kidney function was normal, serum creatinine 0.9 mg/dl and GFR (MDRD) > 60 ml/min. Persistent hemoglobinuria with normal urinary sediment, and proteinuria of 1.3 g/day with albuminuria 170 mg/day and non-selective aminoaciduria, indicating DTP. Acid-base, phosphate, and uric acid status were normal. The possibility of DTP due to hemosiderin deposition was considered, so an MRI was performed with the finding of bilateral renal cortical hypointensity compatible with iron deposition. Initiation of therapy with eculizumab was considered, but was rejected due to the lack of transfusion requirement and normal GFR.

Two years later, he presented a severe hemolytic crisis with acute renal failure, which was attributed to hemoglobinuria. The patient was treated with conservative measures, presenting recovery of kidney function in 18 days. After this episode, treatment with eculizumab was started. Within a few weeks, LDH and hemoglobin almost normalized, maintaining low haptoglobin levels. In the following months, tubular proteinuria progressively normalized.
Fourteen months after initiation of eculizumab, a repeat MRI showed an improvement in signal hypointensity in the renal cortex of 34 and 51% in the left and right kidneys, respectively, indicating partial removal of iron stores in the kidney.
