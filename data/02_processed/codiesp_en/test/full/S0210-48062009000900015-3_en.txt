Male patient, 54 years old, with a history of type 2 diabetes mellitus since he was 30 years old, chronic renal failure for 2 years, treated with hemodialysis. A supracondylar amputation of the right pelvic limb was performed 4 years ago.
He came to our service with a 1-month history of symptoms, characterized by the presence of asymptomatic bullous lesions on the glans penis, which progressed to ulcers a week later; Subsequently, he reported intense pain in the glans and hypochromia, which is why he consulted.
On physical examination, a hypochromic, hypothermic glans was observed, with a necrotic lesion of approximately 1.5 cm on the ventral side, in addition to transurethral purulent discharge and induration of the distal third of the shaft of the penis.
The blood count reported: hemoglobin 10.1 g/dl, leukocytes 4,890, platelets 283,000; blood chemistry: glucose 55 mg/dl, creatinine 7.88 mg/dl, BUN 65.5 mg/dl; Serum electrolytes: sodium 145.3 mmol/l, potassium 4.81 mmol/l, chloride 99 mmol/l, calcium 8.3 mmol/l and phosphorus 9.21 mmol/l. Liver function tests reported: TGO 18, TGP 14 and alkaline phosphatase 64.
A penile Doppler ultrasound was performed with Caverject, thus demonstrating the total absence of flows in 100% of the penis.
Surgical management was proposed to the patient and he decided to go to another institution.
