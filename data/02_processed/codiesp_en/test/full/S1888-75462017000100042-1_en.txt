A 34-year-old man presented with pain with paresthesia in the ulnar edge of the hand and right fourth and fifth fingers for two weeks, after a tennis championship.
He had diffuse edema in the volar aspect of the ulnar area of ​​the wrist and hand, with positive Tinnel over the pisiform.
Radiology and ultrasound were normal. After infiltrating a corticosteroid with local anesthetic, he was immobilized with a wrist brace in a neutral position. The neurophysiological study showed a compressive neuropathy of the ulnar nerve in Guyon's canal. The patient received symptomatic treatment with cryotherapy, orthoses, vitamin B complex analgesics, and physical therapy.
In the Computerized Axial Tomography (CT) of the wrist, requested due to persistence of pain after nine months, he showed a pseudoarthrosis of the uncinate process of the hamate, with a large fragment. He underwent surgery using a palmar approach, dissection of the ulnar neurovascular bundle, abrasion of the nonunion bed and osteosynthesis with two 1.5mm diameter Compact Hand titanium screws (Synhes®).

He maintained an antebrachial splint, including his fingers, for three weeks, after which he began physical therapy.
Four months after surgery, the patient was free of symptoms, except hypoesthesia of the fourth finger, with complete mobility, recovered strength and return to his work (strength) and sports activities.
The CT scan at two months, and the X-ray at three, showed a partial fusion. A new CT scan four months later demonstrated complete union.
