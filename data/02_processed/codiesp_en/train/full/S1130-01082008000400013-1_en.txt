A 33-year-old man with no relevant history and no known family history of thrombosis came to the emergency room due to intermittent diffuse abdominal pain that had been going on for several days with a feeling of nausea and some alimentary-bilious vomiting. The physical examination revealed diffuse tenderness on abdominal palpation without signs of peritoneal irritation. The most relevant analytical data were: glucose 84 mg/dl, urea 30 mg/dl, creatinine 0.9 mg/dl, amylase 29 U/l, lactic dehydrogenase 241 U/l, aspartate aminotransferase 26 U/l, bilirubin total 0.9 mg/dl, C-reactive protein 4.1 mg/dl, hemoglobin 14.4 mg/dl, platelets 301,000 mm3, leukocytes 6,700 mm3, prothrombin activity 85%, fibrinogen 518 mg/dl and D-dimer 5,871 ng/ml. An abdominal ultrasound showed a portal vein of increased caliber (1.6 cm in diameter), with no flow inside it in the Doppler study. An abdominal computed tomography showed thrombosis of the portal vein and the superior mesenteric vein, without other abnormalities. The patient received treatment with enoxaparin (1 mg/kg every 12 hours) for 10 days, with a favorable evolution, subsequently continuing treatment with acenocoumarol. The thrombophilia study identified heterozygosity for the G20210A mutation of the prothrombin gene, with factor V Leiden, resistance to activated protein C, lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin antibodies IgG and IgM, proteins C and S, antithrombin and plasma homocysteine. An MRI performed 10 months later demonstrated the existence of a 15 cm splenomegaly and a portal vein of increased caliber but patent with cavernomatous transformation (with small periportal collateral vessels). An oral digestive endoscopy performed 14 months after the initial presentation was normal. After 15 months of follow-up on treatment with acenocoumarol, the patient remains asymptomatic.
