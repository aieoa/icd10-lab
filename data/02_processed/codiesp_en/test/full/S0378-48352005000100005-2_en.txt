In 2000, at 47 years of age, she underwent a mastectomy for stage IIIB infiltrating ductal carcinoma of the breast (T4a N1). Hormone receptors were positive, as was the Herceptest (+++). She received complementary treatment with chemotherapy (CMF), radiotherapy and tamoxifen. In May 2003, a cervical bone recurrence was detected that caused spinal cord involvement. She was treated with surgery and local radiotherapy. She subsequently began treatment with letrozole. In November 2003, bone and lymph node (supraclavicular) tumor progression was confirmed. She then began palliative cytostatic treatment with epirubicin, docetaxel, and trastuzumab. In the following weeks she presented a significant but transitory symptomatic worsening; The evolution of these symptoms is described in some notes from the patient herself that she later provided to us. Simultaneously, an elevation of CA153 and alkaline phosphatase was found, which subsequently decreased until normalizing (Graph 1). From a clinical point of view, the uptake in the bone scan did not undergo relevant changes but the supraclavicular adenopathy achieved a complete response. The duration of the response was ten months, after which she presented bone and liver progression. She is receiving a new line of chemotherapy with apparent stabilization of the disease.
