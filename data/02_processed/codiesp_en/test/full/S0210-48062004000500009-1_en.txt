This is a 70-year-old patient, who reports lower urinary tract symptoms in the form of increased nocturnal urinary frequency, slight dysuria, as well as a slight decrease in urinary caliber.
Given this incipient prostate symptomatology, he is referred to his local urologist, who performs a rectal examination (adenoma II), and requests a PSA determination and an abdominal ultrasound for the next consultation.
The patient's only history was an intervention on the left eye as a result of trauma, and on a urological level, a ureterolithectomy of the upper third of the ureter 35 years ago in another center, through a lumbotomy.
In the next consultation, and with the patient being completely asymptomatic, the ultrasound study determined the existence of a parahilar mass in the left kidney of about 6 cm in diameter, a normal right kidney, a 35 cc prostate, as well as a PSA. within normal ranges.
Given these findings and the suspicion of a renal tumor, a CT scan was performed with the result of a left parapelvic image, 5-6 cm in diameter, solid in appearance, extending from the renal hilum to below the lower pole, with compression of the kidney and with a doubtful fatty plane of separation with it, calcifications inside and very low vascularization.

With the diagnosis of renal mass, an extension study is performed, which is negative, and surgical revision and excision of the mass and/or nephrectomy are considered depending on the findings.
Fifteen days later, a left lumbotomy was performed.
Once the kidney dissection maneuvers began, the mass compressing the kidney was clearly identified, but with a clear dissection plane between them. Given doubts about the nature of the mass, an intraoperative biopsy was performed, obtaining textile fibers.
Given the evidence that it was a gauze from the previous intervention, the entire tumor was excised, preserving the renal unit.
Once the mass has been excised, it is opened, revealing frayed fragments of one or more gauze pads inside, with cheesy-looking material around them.

The pathological analysis of the tumor wall showed hyaline material with a tendency to collagenization, and an inflammatory reaction, especially at the expense of histiocytes.
