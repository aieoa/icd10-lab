A 5 year and 7 month old patient, with no personal or family history of interest, was admitted to our hospital on June 12, 2002 due to a 10-day history of headache predominantly in the evening, to which strabismus was later added. convergence of the left eye and diplopia. In the neurological examination we found an awake, collaborative and environmentally connected girl, who presented convergent strabismus of the left eye with preserved ocular motility and horizontal nystagmus when forced gaze to the right.
Blood analysis, biochemistry and chest x-ray were normal. CT and MRI of the skull showed a well-defined mass located inside the right lateral ventricle at the level of the atrium and temporal horn, of homogeneous density and measuring 6 by 2.5 cm, with significant perilesional edema and which showed intense uptake after surgery. administration of intravenous contrast.

The patient underwent surgery using a trans-temporal approach through the middle temporal gyrus with macroscopically complete removal of the tumor.
The histopathological study showed a meningothelial neoplastic proliferation with a predominantly meningotheliomatous pattern and high cellularity, classifying the tumor as an atypical meningioma. What was striking was the presence of a high mitotic activity that, in some areas, reached 2-3-mitosis in 10 high magnification fields, with the proliferating cells presenting more voluminous nuclei and a prominent nucleolus. The activity of the Ki-67 antibody, measured randomly in the entire tumor, ranged around 18-20%.

The girl's clinical evolution was favorable, and she was discharged from the hospital ten days after the surgery with minimal convergent strabismus in the right eye. Despite not having undergone complementary treatments, the patient is currently asymptomatic and no signs of recurrence have been seen in control imaging studies.
