A 76-year-old patient who came to the emergency room due to right frontal TBI with loss of consciousness, caused by a casual fall at home. The patient reported loss of vision in the right eye (RE) upon regaining consciousness.
Her history included type 2 diabetes mellitus being treated with oral antidiabetics and osteoarthritis. She did not report any other personal or family history of interest.
On examination, she had an incised-contused infraciliary wound and a diffuse right fronto-orbital hematoma. Visual acuity (VA) was no light perception with a relative afferent pupillary defect (RAPD) in the RE and 0.6 in the left eye. The anterior pole was within normal limits, as well as intraocular pressure and fundus.
Computed tomography (CT) of the skull with orbital sections was normal and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed increased perineural fluid in the right ON sheath.
Given the persistence of the symptoms, a sagittal reconstruction of the optic canal of the helical CT was performed, revealing a linear fracture of the roof of the right orbit that extended to the lesser wing of the right sphenoid, in the CO region, with minimal collapse of the lateral fragment that reduced the internal diameter of the CO.
Visual evoked potentials were performed using monocular stimulation (Medelec® Synergy. Oxford. Instruments, England) with Googles at 2 cycles per second, showing a great asymmetry in the morphology of the responses to the stimulation of both eyes, suggesting absence of conduction of the visual stimulus. in the right NO.
The patient was diagnosed with traumatic optic neuropathy (NOT) with a mixed production mechanism as it was a closed frontal trauma that was transmitted indirectly to the CO and a direct injury to the NO when the lesser wing of the sphenoid was displaced inside the CO.
Treatment was initiated with megadoses of corticosteroids (1 g of methylprednisolone followed by 250 mg every 6 hours for 48 hours), with no improvement.
