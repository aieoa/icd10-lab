A 50-year-old man, diagnosed with Crohn's disease with complex ileal and perianal involvement on immunosuppressive treatment, attended the emergency room due to fever, perianal suppuration, and diarrhea for 1 week. The patient had been on therapy with salicylates and azathioprine (2.5 mg/kg/day, adjusted according to TPMT levels) for 2 years and had received 6 doses of infliximab, a biological treatment that he interrupted 6 months before the current admission, for work reasons. .
On physical examination, the patient appeared to be in poor general condition, being febrile (39 ºC), hypotensive (BP 80/40 mmHg), tachycardic (104 bpm) and tachypneic (22 bpm). A "mass effect" of increased consistency was palpable in the right iliac fossa and a fistulous orifice was observed in the left buttock over an indurated, erythematous, hot and painful area. The urgent analysis showed anemia (Hb 7.9 g/dl, Hct 29%), renal failure (creatinine 4 mg/dl) and elevated acute phase reactants (CRP 127, platelets 424,000/ml). With the probable diagnosis of sepsis and multifactorial renal failure (salicylates, dehydration,...), treatment with serum therapy and broad-spectrum antibiotics was initiated. During the next 24 hours, the patient presented disorientation, agitation and deterioration of his renal function with evident metabolic and hydroelectrolyte alterations (urea 68 mmol/l, creatinine 6.9 mg/dl, sodium 138 mmol/l, potassium 6 mmol/l , total calcium 7.5 mmol/l, inorganic phosphorus 5.1 mmol/l, LDH 17,481 U/l, urate 44 mg/dl). In view of these results, a renal Doppler ultrasound was performed, which was unremarkable, interpreting the renal failure as having a multifactorial origin aggravated by the precipitation of uric acid crystals, for which treatment with hyperhydration, alkalinization of the urine, and hemodialysis was instituted. , allopurinol and rasburicase with a marked decrease in urate levels (urate 1.8 mg/dl). However, given the progressive clinical worsening of the patient's general condition, an abdominopelvic CT scan was performed, which showed a retroperitoneal mass measuring 13 x 10 cm and a lesion on the right flank measuring 15 x 8.6 cm, which seemed to correspond to a conglomerate of fixed loops with thickened wall, without being able to rule out an underlying neoplasia. Multiple lymphadenopathy was also seen in the mesenteric, iliac and inguinal chains, as well as a left perirectal lesion, probably related to his history of abscess and perianal fistula. This last lesion was the only radiological finding observed, two months before the current episode, in an abdomino-pelvic MRI to monitor his perianal disease.
Despite the supportive treatment established, the patient continued to have fever, decreased level of consciousness, progressive dyspnea and anuria, dying three days after admission to the ward. The definitive diagnosis was obtained after performing a necropsy: plasmablastic plasmacytoma with predominance of lamda-type light chains that infiltrated the terminal ileum and cecum, root of the mesentery, abdominal lymph nodes and bone marrow.
