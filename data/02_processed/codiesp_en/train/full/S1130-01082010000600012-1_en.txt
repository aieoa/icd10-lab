We present the case of a 58-year-old patient with a personal history of obesity, hypertension, fibromyalgia, and D3-D4 disc herniation. She was studied in another hospital center for symptoms of several months of evolution consisting of lumbosciatica refractory to usual analgesic treatment, sensation of retrosternal knot, progressive dysphagia and weight loss, where she performed:
- Cervicothoracoabdominal CT: observing mass effect in the upper third of the esophagus, not being able to accurately define its nature.
- Upper gastrointestinal endoscopy: a brown-black mass is seen in the upper third of the esophagus, submucosa, not ulcerated, with an intense vascular network.
- CT and dorsal MRI: multiple vertebral lesions (in D7 and from D9 to D12) compatible with osteoporotic vertebral crushes.
- Bone scan and tumor markers: negative.
Diagnosed with lumbosciatica due to multiple vertebral lesions of osteoporotic origin in the dorsal column and esophageal mass, she was referred to our service to complete the study.
Upon arrival, the patient presented with low back pain that made ambulation impossible, not yielding to opioid treatment, and dysphagia to liquids. On examination, she was in very poor general condition, hemodynamically stable and afebrile. She highlights an abdomen in which hepatomegaly of 3 cm in diameter and very significant pain on palpation of the dorsal column is palpable. At this time we perform the following complementary tests:
- Ultrasound endoscopy: lesion located 18 cm from the dental arch, measuring 40 x 35 x 18 mm, heterogeneous density, which infiltrates and breaks serosa. No peripheral lymphadenopathy is seen.
- Abdominal ultrasound: highlights the existence of liver metastases in segment IV and II of 22 and 26 mm in greatest diameter, respectively. Rest of the examination without significant alterations.
- Positron emission tomography: uptake in the form of a tumor mass in the upper third of the esophagus, with mesenteric implants, multiple liver and bone metastases in the axial skeleton and peripherally.
- Pathological anatomy: malignant melanoma -HMB45: (+), MELAN A: (+), CKPAN: (+)- with areas of necrosis.
