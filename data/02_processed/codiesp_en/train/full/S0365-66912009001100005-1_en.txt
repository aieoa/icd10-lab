A 39-year-old male patient attended the Emergency Department due to proptosis of one year's duration in both eyes (AO), without a decrease in visual acuity (VA). He did not present any other symptoms of note or any history of interest. On examination, the patient had a VA of 0.5 in the right eye (RE) (referred to as amblyopic by the patient) and 0.8 in the left eye (LE). Hertel exophthalmometry was 25 mm in the OD and 23 mm in the LE. The biomicroscopic and tonometric examination was normal. In the fundus study, he had papilledema in the RE. The Farnsworth color test was normal. In the campimetric study, the LE was normal, but in the RE there was an inferonasal defect, which was not strictly quadrantanopsia. A computed tomography (CT) was requested and was reported as ectasia/thickening of the sheaths of both ON in the context of bilateral exophthalmos more marked in RE. Suspecting the presence of bilateral meningocele, it was decided to periodically observe the patient without performing any additional diagnostic-therapeutic action. Currently, 16 years later, the patient has a VA of 0.6 in the RE and 0.8 in the LE. Exophthalmometry and campimetry have remained stable. In the fundus the papillary appearance has hardly changed. He continues with his periodic reviews.
