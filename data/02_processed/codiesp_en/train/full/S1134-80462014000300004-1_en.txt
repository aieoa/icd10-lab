11-year-old girl when the diagnosis of left ankle sprain treated in Pediatric Traumatology was made. She was diagnosed with CRPS of the left ankle and foot by Pediatric Rheumatology and began pharmacological treatment; When she did not get relief, she was referred to the Pain Unit.
First, she was treated pharmacologically without achieving adequate pain relief, so it was decided (with prior information and parental consent) to implant, in the operating room and under general anesthesia, a lumbar epidural catheter, initiating, through it, a continuous infusion of local anesthesia. During two weeks. At that time the pain relief was complete, but it stopped when the administration of local anesthesia was stopped. For this reason, it was decided (with prior information and consent from the parents) to implant, in the operating room and under general anesthesia, an octopolar electrode in the posterior spinal cords for stimulation of the left leg. The system was programmed once the patient was awake, achieving a very good localization of the paresthesias. Two weeks later, the patient was reviewed and continued to have a good response to both the pain and the rest of the symptoms, so it was decided to implant an implantable and programmable pulse generator and connect it to the electrode. Currently she continues treatment with spinal cord stimulation with total remission of the condition.
