46-year-old male patient, diagnosed with classic Hodgkin lymphoma, mixed cellularity variety, stage IV-B with spinal, hepatic and splenic involvement, undergoing chemotherapy treatment (escalated BEACOPP). In the induction phase of chemotherapy (day 11) he begins with fever, chills and melena. In the analysis he showed neutropenia (100 leukocytes/mm3) and plateletopenia. He was admitted to the ICU due to acute respiratory failure and during his admission he suffered lower gastrointestinal bleeding with hypovolemic shock, which required intubation and transfusion of blood products. Upper endoscopy identified, in the proximal jejunum, a large 2-3 cm ulceration, with smooth edges, with a necrotic background, without active bleeding. The anatomopathological study of the biopsy taken from the edges of the ulcer showed several fragments of small intestine mucosa, some ulcerated, with a dense mixed inflammatory infiltrate, with numerous foreign body giant cells, identifying abundant hyphae in its cytoplasm and among the infiltrate. thick non-septate lesions compatible with mucormycosis. CT identified a thickening of the intestinal wall in the jejunum, 4.5 cm in diameter, and multiple hypodense lesions in both liver lobes and lytic involvement in vertebral bodies at the dorsal and lumbar levels.
Treatment with amphotericin B was started, with glycemic control, and eradication surgery was considered. The patient underwent surgery, revealing a plastron located in the jejunum with involvement of the transverse mesocolon, with a double perforation covered by the jejunum. The anatomopathological study of the resection of the jejunum and ileum showed ulceration and intense acute and chronic inflammation with double perforation and adhesion of loops, secondary to infection by Zygomycetes of the mucor type, with extensive fibrinoid necrosis of the medium-sized vessels of the fat. subserosa. The hyphae are thick, of variable diameter, they branch at different angles and in the coronal sections they show a clear and reinforced membrane. These hyphae were positive with the PAS and methanamine silver techniques for fungi. The patient did not suffer any complications derived from the surgery. After a year of follow-up, the patient is still alive and his lymphoma is in remission.
