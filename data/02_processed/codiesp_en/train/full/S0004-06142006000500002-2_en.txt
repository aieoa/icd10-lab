A 46-year-old patient presented with pain in the hypogastrium, painful ejaculation, hemospermia, and a sensation of weight in the testicles, until then attributed to a right varicocele that had been known for a year. His personal history included an episode of acute prostatitis a year before the consultation.
On physical examination, the patient was in good general condition, had a right varicocele, and no masses were palpable in both testicles. The rectal examination showed an irregular prostate, slightly enlarged with indurated areas and somewhat painful on examination.
A comprehensive urological ultrasound was requested, which showed a hypoechoic nodular image in the right testis with surrounding hypervascularization and a simple cyst in the left testicle.
In the abdomino-pelvic CT, several images were observed at the liver level that could correspond to metastases or hemangiomas. An MRI performed later confirms that they are hemangiomas. The chest X-ray showed no alterations.
Tumor markers and PSA had values ​​within normal limits. Alpha-feto-protein: 6 ng/ml, beta-HCG: 0.1 ng/ml, PSA: 1.5 ng/ml.
Given all these findings, it was decided to perform a right inguinal radical orchiectomy. Macroscopically, the piece presented a nodular formation of identical color to that of the testicular pulp measuring 2.5 x 1.8 x 1.5 cm at the upper pole of the testicle. The histopathological report was of a diffuse Leydig cell tumor that infiltrated perineural spaces, adjacent capsular vascular channels and smooth muscle; moderate atypia and few mitoses.
After 10 years of evolutionary controls, the patient is asymptomatic, no metastases have been observed and the tumor markers have remained negative.
