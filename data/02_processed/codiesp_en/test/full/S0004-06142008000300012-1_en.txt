A 41-year-old woman with no relevant history presented with diffuse abdominal pain with greater intensity in the epigastrium for 1 week along with nausea, bilious vomiting and poor general condition. The pain increases with cough and inspiration. She does not report fever or dysthermic sensation. No coluria or acholia.
On physical examination, the patient is conscious and oriented, affected by pain and appears to be in serious condition. Mucocutaneous pallor. Afebrile and normotensive patient. Cardiopulmonary auscultation without findings. Abdomen without masses or megalias, with generalized abdominal pain on palpation, more intense in the epigastrium, without signs of peritoneal irritation (Murphy negative). No collateral circulation is appreciated. No edema in lower limbs. No signs of deep vein thrombosis (DVT).
Laboratory tests show normal biochemistry with increased transaminases (GOT: 146 U/L, GPT: 171 U/L). Hemogram with hemoglobin: 11.4 g/dL, hematocrit: 34%, leukocytes: 12700 without neutrophilia and platelets: 291000. Hemostasis with INR: 1.3 and cephalin time 53.3 sec.
A CT scan was performed where a thrombus with fatty density was observed extending from the left kidney to the left renal vein (LRV), inferior vena cava (IVC) and ostium of the right atrium. Free suprahepatic veins. Non-complete occlusion of the IVC lumen. Nonspecific abnormality of the head of the pancreas.

Due to this last CT finding along with the intense epigastralgia, it was decided to perform an abdomino-pelvic ECO, finding: normal liver and intra- and extrahepatic bile duct. Lipoma in IVC. Ultrasound signs of cholecystitis, probably perforated and possible pancreatitis. Free fluid in the pelvis, perihepatic, perisplenic and right perirenal spaces (in greater amounts than in previous CT).
A differential diagnosis is proposed between bilio-pancreatic pathology vs. Budd-Chiari syndrome, so it was decided to perform a second CT scan, revealing bilateral pleural effusion, thickening of gastric folds with increased contrast uptake, and angiomyolipoma (AML) vs. renal lipoma that extends to the IVC and ostium of the right atrium, with contrast passage throughout the venous structures, with patent portal and suprahepatic veins. There are images suggestive of hepatic congestion: hepatomegaly, increased caliber of the portal and suprahepatic veins with delay in the arrival of contrast to them, periportal edema, brindle spleen and pancreas, and abundant free fluid.

With a presumptive diagnosis of Budd-Chiari syndrome secondary to caval thrombus, paracentesis is performed under ultrasound control, in order to exclude with greater certainty an infectious/tumorous peritoneal process as the cause of said syndrome. An analytical result of transudate is obtained, ruling out peritoneal process. In transesophageal ultrasound, the patient has good ventricular function without intracavitary thrombus, the tricuspid is free and there is pericardial effusion.
Finally, with a diagnosis of Budd-Chiari Syndrome secondary to cava thrombus (AML vs. Lipoma), and given the patient's clinical worsening with poor general condition, urgent surgical intervention was decided 48 hours after her arrival at the Service. of Emergencies.
SURGICAL INTERVENTION. It is a thrombus with surgical level IV as it presents a supradiaphragmatic situation reaching the ostium of the right atrium. A Chevron-type incision is made and access to the retroperitoneum is made by opening from the fixed loop to the Winslow hiatus to allow complete ascending release of the intestinal package, including ligation of the inferior mesenteric vein. Liver mobilization is achieved by sectioning the round, coronary, right and left triangular, and falciform ligaments. Next, the left kidney and its corresponding hilum are released, starting then with the release of the vena cava, starting at the infrarenal level and ascending to the suprahepatic level with control of the right renal vein (RV) and section of the rest of the branches except the suprahepatic veins that They are proven free of tumor thrombus and are the only ones that fix the cava to the liver. The diaphragmatic hiatus is expanded and the thrombus is squeezed to the infradiaphragmatic level. Clamping of the hepatic pedicle (Pringle maneuver), proximal and distal IVC and VRD was performed with subsequent cavotomy and VRI opening, complete extraction of the thrombus and left nephrectomy.

PATHOLOGICAL ANATOMY: predominantly lipomatous angiomyolipoma of the renal sinus with vascular infiltration.
