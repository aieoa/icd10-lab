An 18-year-old female patient who underwent surgery for cloacal exstrophy (reconstruction of the intestinal and urinary tracts) in another healthcare center, and who arrived at our Service presenting absence of anterior abdominal wall, unstable skin, pubic eventration, numerous scarring sequelae and an incontinent urinary reservoir.

She is scheduled for wall reconstruction, eventhroplasty and urinary stomaplasty, working as a team with the Children's Surgery and Urology Service.
1st surgical time:
It consisted of the placement of 2 1000cc rectangular expanders. (with expansion capacity up to 1500cc) and 15.2 cm. long x 7.3 wide with remote valve (Silimed®) below the abdominal cellulose tissue and above the aponeurosis of the oblique major muscle in the subcostal and lateral position. The expansion was carried out at a rate of between 50 and 100 cc of physiological solution with intervals of 1 week to 20 days, depending on the patient's tolerance, until reaching 1300cc.

2nd surgical time:
Once the expansion is completed, surgery of the urinary wall and stoma is scheduled.
The Children's Plastic Surgery team removed the expanders and the urological surgeon performed the plasty of the urinary stoma. Both surgical teams then proceeded to reconstruct the upper and middle thirds of the wall using the remaining tissues of the rectus abdominis sheath, rectus abdominis muscle, and remaining scar tissue. In the lower third and pubis, due to the absence of tissue, it was decided to use a 15 x 10 cm polypropylene prosthetic mesh (Prolene®) above the loose scar tissue that covers the eventration, anchoring it to the bones. pubic muscles and the major oblique muscle on both sides. The reconstruction of the skin and cellulose-fatty tissues is achieved with the advancement of the expanded lateral tissues.

The patient progressed favorably without complications, and vaginal reconstructive surgery was performed a year later.

Despite the pubic diastasis of almost 17cm. The patient has no alterations in gait and by her own decision no treatment has been performed.
