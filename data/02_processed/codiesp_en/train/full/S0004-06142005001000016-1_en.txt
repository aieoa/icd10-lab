A 65-year-old man presented with infravesical obstructive syndrome along with a soft tumor in the right hemiscrotum, which decreased in size during urination.
The physical examination revealed the presence of an enlarged right scrotal sac and a reducible inguinal hernia. A rectal examination revealed a medium-sized, bilobed prostate with adenomatous characteristics.
Among the complementary examinations, a total PSA was performed, which was normal, a vesico-prostatic ultrasound that revealed a 57-gram prostate, and a testicular ultrasound with an image suggestive of bladder herniation.

Finally, retrograde cystography showed the presence of a massive bladder hernia in the right hemiescrotum.

The treatment was surgical and consisted of resection of the herniated portion of the bladder, which was peritoneal, and correction of the inguinal hernia with Marlex mesh. In a second stage, a prostate TUR was performed.
Controlled after six months, the patient remains asymptomatic.
