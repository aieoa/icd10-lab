A 68-year-old woman, as personal history of interest, reported being a smoker of 20 cigarettes/day for 40 years, and as surgical interventions: cholecystectomy, tonsillectomy and bilateral adnexectomy (for uterine polymyomatosis). She was studied two years ago in digestive consultations for moderate dysphagia to solids, without other accompanying symptoms, an upper digestive endoscopy was performed with the discovery of a hiatal hernia, recommending treatment with proton pump inhibitors, with considerable improvement in symptoms. Subsequently and during the period of review in consultations, he presented a picture of intense epigastric pain of a few months of evolution, so a second endoscopy was repeated in which a lesion was evident at the level of the middle third of the esophagus, biopsied and reported as Ulcerated and infiltrating oat-cell small cell carcinoma. Given these findings, studies are completed on the patient, performing different examinations with the fundamental objective of finding the primary tumor (thinking of a pulmonary origin given the histological species of the neoplasia) and staging the disease.
On examination, the patient was in a preserved general condition, afebrile, with no findings of lymphadenopathy, nor relevant data on cardiopulmonary auscultation, with a normal abdomen except for the right subcostal scar and infraumbilical continents, and without neurological alterations.
At an analytical level, she did not present any relevant alteration. The barium esophagogastroduodenal study showed, 5 cm from the esophagogastric junction, a flat, ulcerated lesion about 5 cm long that discreetly reduced its lumen, with the rest of the study being normal.

The thoraco-abdominal CT scan shows a neoplasia at the level of the middle third of the esophagus, which causes a decrease in its lumen, with lymphadenopathy in the subcarinal, left supraclavicular area (1.5 cm); in the abdomen lymphadenopathy in the retroperitoneum, retrocrural area and right iliac chain; images suggestive of bilobar liver metastases and a large splenic cyst. There is no evidence of space-occupying lesion at the lung level. No pathological findings were found in fiberoptic bronchoscopy. A transthoracic puncture aspirate was performed with positivity for oat-cell carcinoma, which could indicate pleural invasion.

The ultrasound endoscopy showed a vegetative and ulcerated esophageal tumor, which extended from 30 to 35 cm and infiltrated 2/3 of its sides, but which allowed the passage of the endoscope. Between 28 and 32 cm there was a well-defined retrocardiac extrinsic mass that did not infiltrate the esophagus. Multiple celiac lymphadenopathy were found. And the biopsy of the lesion is reported as small cell carcinoma of the oat-cell type. Cranial CT did not demonstrate any pathological findings.
Given the extension of the neoplasia, surgery was ruled out, considering chemotherapy as the main therapeutic option and, at the end of this work, he was undergoing treatment in the Oncology Unit of our hospital (schedule with CDDP plus etoposide).
